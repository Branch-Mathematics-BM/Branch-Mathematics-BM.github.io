1. Purpose of the Changelog

This is the formal mathematical revision ledger. It records what changed, why it changed, what the corrected form is, which dependencies are affected, and what status follows.

\[ \boxed{ \text{previous form} \longrightarrow \text{mathematical reason} \longrightarrow \text{corrected form} \longrightarrow \text{dependency update} \longrightarrow \text{new status}. } \]

Informal drafting chronology and cosmetic edits do not belong in the mathematical record.

2. Revision Principles

\[ \boxed{ \text{correction}\neq\text{erasure}. } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical truth} \neq \text{historical novelty} \neq \text{physical realization} \neq \text{external validation}. } \]

A correction should make the framework more precisely typed, scoped, and testable. Historical entries remain visible when they explain why the current form exists.

4. September 4, 2026 — Corpus Synchronization

September 4, 2026
BMGURFCORPUSSYNCHRONIZATION

Definitions, Examples, Theorems, Equations, Structures, Passports, Roots, and Research synchronized

The principal public mathematical registries were rebuilt against the current BM/GURF architecture. The goal of the pass was not cosmetic uniformity; it was elimination of cross-page mathematical drift.

\[ \boxed{ \text{one current object} \Longrightarrow \text{one current definition, equation, theorem status, structure, example role, and research status}. } \]

Affected public pages: definitions.html · examples.html · theorems.html · equations.html · structures.html · passports.html · roots.html · research.html

The public Branch structure now consistently exposes sector, type, carrier, admissibility, laws, native Operations, and Passport/Interface obligations.

6. Interaction Domains Moved to Admissible Branch Domains

September 4, 2026

Supersedes / refines: \(D_I\subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n}\) when that notation was used as the governing current form

68. What Belongs in This Changelog

  • definition changes;
  • theorem/proof/counterexample changes;
  • domain or assumption corrections;
  • equation generalizations or restrictions;
  • semantic-sector corrections;
  • ownership migrations;
  • candidate promotions, demotions, freezes, closures, and supersessions;
  • research-status or realization-status changes;
  • important numerical or experimental verdicts;
  • prior-art findings;
  • cross-page mathematical synchronization.

69. What Does Not Belong Here

  • informal drafting transcripts;
  • casual brainstorming chronology;
  • typographical edits with no mathematical consequence;
  • decorative layout changes;
  • unverified discovery claims;
  • speculation presented as theorem-status change.

71. Central Changelog Principle

\[ \boxed{ \text{A mathematical framework should become more precise when it is corrected, not merely different.} } \]

The changelog therefore preserves the path from weaker or overbroad formulations to better-typed, better-scoped, and better-tested ones.
