No-Broadcasting and Quantum Access Geometry

Classical information can often be copied onto several independent routes. A genuinely quantum noncommutative task cannot. No-broadcasting therefore forces a distinctive geometry on which subsets of a distributed quantum system may simultaneously be authorized.

Volume II development Uses established no-broadcasting and operator-algebra results
Abstract

For a distributed quantum encoding, let Auth(A) be the subsets that can exactly recover a declared task algebra A. If two disjoint subsets both exactly recover the same finite-dimensional C*-algebra, that algebra must be commutative. Hence every exact authorized family for a genuinely noncommutative task is intersecting: no two authorized sets are disjoint. In a symmetric k-of-m threshold structure this forces 2k>m. Minimal authorized sets determine the access hypergraph, while the task-relative erasure distance is the hitting number of that family. This page develops the distinction between classical redundancy and quantum access, exact access geometry, threshold consequences, graceful task weakening, and approximate questions that remain open.

1. Distributed recovery is not the same as copying

Suppose a logical quantum task is encoded into physical subsystems:

1,2,…,m.

A subset S may be able to recover the declared task algebra even when other subsets cannot.

This creates an access structure:

Auth(A)={S⊆[m] : A is exactly recoverable from S}.

The geometry of this family depends on whether A is commutative or genuinely noncommutative.

2. Commutative and noncommutative tasks behave differently

Commutative task

Represents jointly measurable classical information. Exact copies can, in principle, exist on disjoint subsystems.

Noncommutative task

Contains genuinely quantum observable structure. Exact recovery on disjoint regions is constrained by no-broadcasting.

3. Exact no-broadcasting constraint

Disjoint Exact Recovery Implies Commutativity

If two disjoint subsystems both admit exact recovery of the same finite-dimensional C*-algebra A, then A must be commutative.

This is the central structural fact behind BM quantum access geometry.

4. Intersecting authorized families

Suppose A is genuinely noncommutative. Then two authorized sets cannot be disjoint.

S,T∈Auth(A)  ⇒  S∩T≠∅.
Quantum Access Intersection Principle

The exact authorized family of a genuinely noncommutative task algebra is an intersecting family.

5. Why this differs from classical routing

In a classical network, one can often duplicate the same task bit onto two separate routes for resilience.

For a genuinely quantum task, two disjoint routes cannot both hold exact independently recoverable copies of the same noncommutative algebra.

Permanent firewall: classical redundancy logic cannot be imported unchanged into quantum access structures.

6. Symmetric threshold structures

Consider a symmetric threshold design with m physical shares. Every subset of size at least k is authorized.

If:

2k≤m,

then two disjoint k-subsets exist.

That would give two disjoint exact recoveries of the same task algebra.

Threshold Constraint for Genuine Quantum Tasks

For a symmetric threshold access structure carrying an exactly recoverable noncommutative task algebra,

2k>m.

7. The threshold rule is task-relative

If the declared task is only a commutative algebra, disjoint authorized sets are not forbidden by the same no-broadcasting argument.

same physical encoding + weaker task ⇒ potentially different access geometry.

Thus the threshold restriction belongs to the task algebra, not merely to the code hardware.

8. Minimal authorized sets

An authorized family is upward closed: if S can recover the task, then any superset can discard extras and use S's recovery.

S∈Auth(A), S⊂T ⇒ T∈Auth(A).

The essential combinatorial data therefore lie in:

MinAuth(A)=minimal authorized subsets.

9. Access hypergraph

The family MinAuth(A) can be viewed as a hypergraph on the physical shares.

For a genuinely quantum task, its hyperedges must be pairwise intersecting.

E,F∈MinAuth(A) ⇒ E∩F≠∅.

This gives a direct combinatorial signature of exact noncommutative recoverability.

10. Erasure distance as a hitting number

An erasure set F defeats the task exactly when every minimal authorized set loses at least one member.

Thus F must hit every hyperedge in MinAuth(A).

Task-Relative Erasure Distance
dA=τ(MinAuth(A)),

where τ is the minimum hitting-set size.

11. Why the hitting-set formulation is useful

It separates two questions:

QuestionObject
Which subsets can recover the task?Authorized hypergraph
What is the smallest erasure that defeats every recovery route?Hitting number

The second is a purely combinatorial property once the exact access structure is known.

12. Example: three shares

Three-share intersecting family

Let the minimal authorized sets be:

{1,2}, {1,3}, {2,3}.

Every pair intersects. No two authorized 2-subsets are disjoint.

The minimum hitting-set size is:

τ=2.

So two erasures are needed to intersect all minimal authorized sets, while any one surviving pair remains authorized.

13. Example: a forbidden genuinely quantum threshold

2-of-4 exact quantum task

A symmetric 2-of-4 access structure would authorize:

{1,2} and {3,4}.

These sets are disjoint.

Therefore a genuinely noncommutative task algebra cannot have exact recovery from every 2-subset of 4 shares.

2k=4=m ⇒ violates 2k>m.

14. Classical task on the same shares can be different

If the receiver only needs one classical logical bit represented by a commuting observable algebra, two disjoint authorized groups can be possible.

This gives a sharp BM distinction:

physical shares do not determine access geometry alone; the Passport does.

15. Full-state access is one special Passport

Full-state quantum recovery corresponds to the full logical algebra:

A=B(HL).

Task-relative access allows a proper subalgebra:

A⊂B(HL).

Different subalgebras can induce different minimal authorized sets and different erasure distances.

16. Access structure under task weakening

If:

A1⊂A2,

then every set authorized for the stronger task A₂ is also authorized for the weaker task A₁.

Auth(A2)⊂Auth(A1).

Weakening the task can only enlarge the exact authorized family.

17. Graceful degradation as access expansion

As noise or erasure increases, the strongest recoverable algebra can shrink.

At the same time, if the receiver accepts a weaker task algebra, more subsets may remain authorized.

damage ↑ ⇒ maximal exact task algebra ↓
task requirement ↓ ⇒ authorized family ↑

This is one way to describe graceful degradation geometrically.

18. Maximal recoverable algebra and access geometry

For a specific noise or erasure pattern F, let:

Amax(F)

be the maximal exactly recoverable algebra on the surviving shares.

A declared task Areq survives exactly when:

Areq⊂Amax(F).

This turns each damage pattern into a point in a partially ordered capability landscape.

19. More erasure gives less exact capability

If F₁⊂F₂, then the second pattern erases at least as much as the first.

Amax(F₂)⊂eq Amax(F₁).

Exact capability is antitone under erasure.

20. Quantum access and secret-sharing language

Authorized and unauthorized sets are standard language in secret sharing and quantum coding.

BM does not claim these notions as new. The BM placement is receiver-relative:

authorized for which declared observable algebra?

The same physical shares can support different access structures for different Passports.

21. Correlations can alter authorization

A subset may reproduce all local task observables yet fail to preserve the declared joint correlations with a reference or external subsystem.

Permanent firewall: local observable recovery does not imply recovery of every contextual correlation.

The access structure must therefore be built from the full contextual operator system required by the task.

22. Quantum access is not ordinary route redundancy

Classical network

The same payload can often be duplicated onto disjoint routes to improve resilience.

Quantum noncommutative task

Exact independently recoverable copies on disjoint routes are forbidden by no-broadcasting.

Quantum resilience must instead use encoded correlations spread across overlapping or jointly authorized share sets.

23. Approximate access structures

At positive tolerance, a subset can be approximately authorized when some CPTP recovery achieves task error at most ε.

Authε(A)={S : δA,S≤ε}.

This family can change continuously or discretely as tolerance varies.

Firewall: exact no-broadcasting geometry does not automatically provide a complete theorem for approximate disjoint recovery.

24. Approximate disjoint recovery tradeoffs

Two disjoint subsystems can each carry imperfect information about a quantum task without violating exact no-broadcasting.

The research problem is to quantify the joint tradeoff:

δA,S, δA,T   with   S∩T=∅.

For noncommutative tasks, both errors cannot generically be driven to zero simultaneously, but the sharp task-relative tradeoff depends on the algebra and physical model.

25. Approximate authorized families need not have one clean threshold

Different subsets can have different recovery errors:

δA,S≠δA,T.

As ε increases, authorized sets can enter at different tolerances.

The resulting access geometry can therefore have several transition scales rather than one exact threshold.

26. Access geometry and semantic width

Each authorized subset must support a carrier large enough to represent the declared task algebra physically.

Thus access structure and carrier width are linked but distinct:

who can recover?   ≠   how large a carrier is required?

A subset may be authorized but only with a large carrier, while another subset may support a more efficient realization.

27. Access geometry and locality

In many-body systems, only shares inside a task-active causal collar may be capable of influencing the required observables within the deadline.

Native locality can therefore restrict the candidate authorized family before QEC structure is even tested.

causal eligibility → quantum authorization.

28. A practical exact-access workflow

declare task algebra → include contextual correlations → test each surviving subset with OAQEC → build Auth(A) → reduce to MinAuth(A) → verify intersection constraints → compute hitting number → analyze graceful degradation.

29. What BM contributes here

No-broadcasting, operator-algebra QEC, access structures, threshold codes, and hitting-set combinatorics are established mathematics.

The BM synthesis is to make access geometry explicitly task-relative: the authorized hypergraph belongs to a declared operator algebra and context; noncommutativity determines whether exact disjoint recovery is possible; erasure distance is read from the minimal authorized family; and graceful degradation is represented by changes in the surviving algebra and its access geometry.

30. Open directions

Important open problems include sharp approximate no-broadcasting tradeoffs for task algebras, efficient computation of approximate authorized families, access geometry under spatial locality, dimension-aware carrier bounds for minimal authorized sets, and adaptive task downgrading when the strongest algebra is no longer recoverable.
