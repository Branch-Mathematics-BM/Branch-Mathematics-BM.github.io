Quantum Semantic Carriers

A quantum representation need not preserve the full state when the receiver only requires a declared family of observables or correlations. The carrier must preserve exactly the quantum structure needed by that task—and it must do so through physically admissible quantum channels.

Volume II development Uses classical quantum information, operator systems, and QEC
Abstract

A quantum semantic carrier is a task-relative quantum representation whose Heisenberg adjoint preserves the declared observable system and required correlations. If a task operator system S_A^ctx must factor through a k-dimensional quantum carrier, then the carrier operator space has dimension at most k², giving the lower bound log₂k ≥ ½log₂dim(S_A^ctx). Exact locality can reduce the required operator system to a backward circuit collar, while Lieb–Robinson-type estimates can support approximate collars under native assumptions. Abstract operator recovery is not enough: physical closure and recovery require completely positive trace-preserving channels. This page develops exact carriers, contextual operator systems, dimensional bounds, local quantum collars, correlation preservation, access structure, and the firewall between task-relative quantum width and entanglement entropy.

1. What must a quantum carrier preserve?

Let the native quantum system have Hilbert space:

H.

A receiver may not need arbitrary state reconstruction. It may need expectation values of a declared family of observables:

X∈S,

where S is an operator system or task algebra selected by the Passport.

The representation problem becomes:

preserve the declared observable response, not automatically the full density operator.

2. Schrödinger and Heisenberg views

Let:

E:B(H)→B(K)

denote a quantum channel from the native system to a carrier Hilbert space K.

In the Schrödinger picture, E acts on states. In the Heisenberg picture, its adjoint:

E*:B(K)→B(H)

acts on observables.

Exact task sufficiency asks whether each required native observable can be represented through the carrier adjoint.

3. Heisenberg factorization

Task Observable Factorization Quantum BM placement

For every declared task observable X∈S, exact carrier sufficiency requires a carrier observable YX∈B(K) such that:

E*(YX)=X.

Equivalently:

S⊆im(E*).

Then every carrier state E(ρ) contains enough information to reproduce the expectation of every task observable in S.

4. Full-state recovery is stronger

If S=B(H), the task asks for the entire observable algebra and therefore full quantum information.

If S is a proper operator subsystem, the task can be strictly weaker.

Full-state carrier

Must preserve all observables of the logical system.

Task-relative carrier

Must preserve only the declared observable system and any required contextual correlations.

5. Why correlations must be declared explicitly

Preserving local expectation values does not automatically preserve every joint correlation with another subsystem.

If the receiver task depends on observables:

XA⊗ZC,

then the Passport must include the relevant joint operator system, not merely the local span of XA.

Permanent firewall: local quantum sufficiency does not imply joint continuation sufficiency.

6. Contextual operator system

Let:

SActx

denote the linear operator system generated by every observable on subsystem A whose expectation or correlation can affect an allowed receiver context.

This is the quantum analogue of a contextual semantic profile.

more allowed contexts ⇒ potentially larger SActx.

7. Carrier dimension lower bound

Suppose the carrier Hilbert space has dimension:

dim(K)=k.

Then:

dim B(K)=k².
Contextual Operator-System Dimension Bound Volume II

If the task operator system factors through the Heisenberg image of a k-dimensional carrier, then:

dim(SActx)≤k².

Hence:

log₂k ≥ ½ log₂ dim(SActx).

This is a structural lower bound on exact carrier size.

8. The bound is not always tight

The dimension inequality only says the carrier operator space is large enough linearly.

It does not guarantee:

complete positivity, correct multiplicative structure, preservation of joint correlations, locality, or existence of a suitable physical encoding.

Firewall: operator-space dimension sufficiency is not physical carrier sufficiency.

9. Physical carrier maps must be CPTP

A physically admissible state encoding must be a completely positive trace-preserving map.

E CPTP.

This restriction matters because abstract linear factorizations can exist even when no quantum channel realizes them.

Permanent firewall: algebraic or operator recovery does not imply CPTP recovery.

10. Quantum dynamic closure

Let:

Φ

be the native quantum channel and:

E

the carrier encoding.

Exact physical closure asks whether there is a CPTP carrier evolution:

Ψ

satisfying:

ΨE=EΦ.

This is the quantum analogue of exact dynamic closure with the added requirement of complete positivity and trace preservation.

11. Choi feasibility in finite dimension

In finite-dimensional quantum systems, existence of Ψ can be written as a semidefinite feasibility problem in the Choi matrix of the candidate channel.

The constraints are:

J(Ψ)≥0,   TroutJ(Ψ)=I,   ΨE=EΦ.

Thus exact physical closure is algorithmically checkable in finite explicit models through standard semidefinite machinery.

12. Range invariance is not enough

It is tempting to ask only whether:

EΦ(ρ)

lies in the carrier state range whenever E(ρ) does.

But setwise range invariance does not guarantee a linear CPTP update on the entire carrier state space.

Firewall: range invariance is weaker than CPTP dynamic closure.

13. CPTP right inverse as a sufficient special case

If the encoding E admits a CPTP right inverse R on the relevant carrier range:

ER = id,

then:

Ψ=EΦR

is a sufficient construction for a physical carrier evolution on that range.

This is a useful special case, not a necessary condition in general.

14. Approximate physical closure

When exact closure fails, define the physical closure defect:

δQ = minΨ CPTP ||EΦ-ΨE||⋄.

In finite dimension, the feasible CPTP set is compact and the objective continuous, so the minimum is attained.

This defect is a physical channel-level quantity, distinct from an algebraic commutator defect.

15. Local quantum task support

Let the declared task observables be supported on region A of a many-body quantum system.

Under local dynamics, only a backward causal collar can influence them within a finite horizon.

task support at final time → backward Heisenberg support at initial time.

The relevant carrier therefore need not preserve observables outside that collar when the native dynamics prove they cannot affect the task.

16. Exact local circuit collar

In a bounded-depth local quantum circuit, Heisenberg evolution of a local observable expands support only through gates in its exact backward light cone.

Exact Quantum Collar Principle Classical circuit locality + BM placement

For a local task observable after bounded-depth local circuit evolution, degrees of freedom outside its exact backward circuit cone do not affect that task.

BM uses this to restrict the contextual operator system before sizing a carrier.

17. Approximate quantum collar

For local Hamiltonian evolution, exact support spreads immediately in many formulations, but Lieb–Robinson-type bounds can suppress influence outside an effective cone.

||[A(t),B]|| ≲ C e−μ(d−vt).

Under the native theorem's assumptions, one may choose a tolerance-dependent collar:

d ≳ vt + μ−1log(C/ε).

The resulting quantum carrier is approximate and horizon-dependent.

18. Locality can reduce carrier size dramatically

If the native system has N qubits but the task-active collar contains only m qubits, then the relevant task operator system lives inside:

B((C²)⊗m)

rather than the full 2N-dimensional Hilbert space.

The task-relative carrier burden can therefore be controlled by the active collar rather than total system size.

19. Correlation closure can enlarge the collar

A receiver may ask not only for local observables on A but also correlations with a reference, environment, or neighboring subsystem.

Then the contextual operator system can grow beyond the naively local span.

local task support + required correlations = larger contextual quantum Passport.

This is why the allowed context must be part of the carrier specification.

20. Access structures

In a distributed quantum system, let a subset S of physical subsystems be authorized if it can recover the declared task algebra or operator system.

Auth = {S : declared quantum task recoverable from S}.

This is the quantum access structure induced by the Passport.

It can be much weaker than requiring full logical-state recovery.

21. Task-relative quantum error correction

Suppose a code projector is P and error operators are Ea.

An operator algebra A is exactly correctable when:

[P Ea†EbP, X]=0   for all X∈A.

This is established operator-algebra quantum error correction.

BM uses it to define which task observables survive a given erasure or noise pattern.

22. A simple task-relative example

Preserve logical Z, not the full qubit

A two-qubit repetition encoding:

|0L⟩=|00⟩,   |1L⟩=|11⟩

can preserve the logical classical Z observable under loss of one qubit, because either remaining qubit still distinguishes the code basis states.

It does not preserve an arbitrary logical qubit under one erasure.

task-relative recovery can succeed while full quantum-state recovery fails.

23. Quantum no-broadcasting changes the distributed geometry

Classical task information can often be duplicated freely.

A noncommutative quantum algebra cannot generally be broadcast into two disjoint exact carriers.

No-Broadcast Access Constraint Classical theorem + BM placement

If two disjoint subsystems both exactly recover the same finite-dimensional C*-algebra, then that recoverable algebra must be commutative.

Thus a genuinely quantum noncommutative task induces an intersecting geometry of authorized sets.

Firewall: classical route redundancy cannot be imported unchanged into quantum carriers.

24. Threshold consequence

For a symmetric threshold access structure with m shares and threshold k, if every k-subset exactly recovers a genuinely noncommutative task algebra, disjoint authorized sets must be impossible.

Therefore:

2k>m.

This is the familiar no-cloning/no-broadcasting geometry expressed at the task-algebra level.

25. Quantum semantic width is not entanglement entropy

Entanglement entropy measures a state-dependent bipartite quantum correlation quantity.

Quantum semantic width measures task-relative carrier burden under a declared observable system, context, tolerance, and physical realization class.

Permanent firewall: entanglement entropy and quantum semantic width are different quantities.

They can correlate in special theorems, but neither should be substituted for the other generically.

26. Exact carrier size and approximate carrier size

Exact carrier

Must reproduce the declared task observables and correlations exactly.

Approximate carrier

May approximate them within a declared norm and tolerance, but must still satisfy CPTP physicality and causal compatibility.

27. Approximate algebraic versus physical defect

In approximate operator-algebra recovery, one can measure how far task observables fail to commute with the compressed error operator system.

Let:

K = span{P Ea†EbP}.

An algebraic commutator defect:

κ

can be compared with distance:

η

to the exact commutant, with finite-dimensional bounds of the form:

η≤κ≤2η.

But neither quantity is automatically the optimal physical recovery error.

Permanent firewall: approximate algebraic closeness and optimal CPTP recovery defect are distinct.

28. Perturbation stability of physical recovery defect

Let:

δA(N)

denote the optimal physical recovery defect for task algebra A under channel N.

Under the declared diamond-norm formulation:

|δA(N)-δA(M)| ≤ ||N-M||⋄.

This gives a robust continuity statement for approximate task recovery.

29. No unique greatest approximate task algebra in general

Exact recoverable algebras have a clean lattice structure.

At positive tolerance, two different approximate task algebras can each be admissible without one containing the other.

approximate capability family can be downward closed without a unique greatest element.

Approximate quantum degradation is therefore naturally multi-objective and partially ordered.

30. A reusable quantum-carrier workflow

A practical BM analysis proceeds as:

declare task observables → close under required contexts/correlations → identify task-active causal collar → bound operator-system dimension → test CPTP encoding → test dynamic closure/recovery → analyze access geometry → quantify approximation and robustness.

31. What BM contributes here

Operator systems, C*-algebras, quantum channels, OAQEC, no-broadcasting, Lieb–Robinson bounds, and semidefinite channel tests are established quantum mathematics.

The BM synthesis is:

define the quantum representation by the receiver's required observable system and contexts; separate full-state recovery from task recovery; derive carrier lower bounds from contextual operator dimension; restrict carriers using native locality; preserve CPTP physicality; and connect recovery, access geometry, and graceful degradation inside one Passport structure.

32. Open directions

Major open directions include tight carrier-dimension bounds beyond linear dimension counting, context-dependent correlation closure, dimension-aware conversion from algebraic defect to physical recovery error, approximate no-broadcasting tradeoffs, local many-body carrier scaling, and scalable synthesis of minimal physically admissible quantum carriers.
