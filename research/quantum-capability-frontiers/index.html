Quantum Capability Frontiers and Approximate Passport Geometry

Exact quantum recovery has a largest recoverable algebra. Approximate recovery generally does not. At positive tolerance, capability becomes a partially ordered frontier of tasks, carrier costs, access structures, and physical recovery errors.

Volume II development Uses established operator algebra, channel norm, and Pareto concepts
Abstract

For a quantum channel N and tolerance ε, define Capε(N)={A:δA(N)≤ε}, where δA is the optimal physical CPTP recovery defect for task algebra A. The family is downward closed under task weakening, but need not possess one greatest approximate algebra. Its inclusion-maximal elements form a capability frontier. Different frontier points can trade algebra size, carrier dimension, recovery error, access resilience, locality, and implementation complexity. Increasing ε expands the capability family; perturbing the channel changes each task defect at most by the diamond-norm perturbation. BM uses this geometry to avoid forcing approximate quantum capability into a false single-number or single-algebra summary.

1. Exact capability has a clean maximum

For a finite-dimensional code and channel N, exact operator-algebra QEC gives:

Amax(N)=𝔈N′.

Every exactly recoverable task algebra lies inside this maximal algebra.

δA(N)=0 ⇔ A⊂Amax(N).

This exact sector is ordered cleanly.

2. Positive tolerance changes the geometry

At tolerance ε>0, define the approximate capability family:

Capε(N)={A : δA(N)≤ε}.

Now two task algebras can each be recoverable within tolerance without their generated algebra also being recoverable within the same tolerance.

Permanent firewall: exact maximal recoverability does not imply a unique greatest approximate recoverable algebra.

3. Downward closure

If:

A₁⊂A₂

and A₂ is ε-recoverable, then the same recovery also preserves the weaker task A₁ within the same tolerance.

Approximate Capability Downward Closure
A₂∈Capε(N), A₁⊂A₂ ⇒ A₁∈Capε(N).

4. Inclusion-maximal approximate tasks

Although one greatest algebra may fail to exist, the family can have several inclusion-maximal elements.

Frontε(N)=Max(Capε(N),⊂).

These are task algebras that cannot be strictly enlarged while remaining within the recovery budget.

5. Why several frontier points can coexist

Suppose one approximate task protects one family of observables very well, while another protects a different incompatible family.

Neither algebra need contain the other.

A₁∉A₂, A₂∉A₁, δA₁≤ε, δA₂≤ε.

The generated algebra:

C*(A₁∪A₂)

can exceed the physical recovery budget.

6. Approximate capability is naturally an antichain frontier

The inclusion-maximal elements of a downward-closed family form an antichain: no frontier element strictly contains another.

A,B∈Frontε, A≠B ⇒ A⊄B and B⊄A.

This gives a clean combinatorial picture of nonunique approximate capability.

7. Tolerance monotonicity

If:

0≤ε₁≤ε₂,

then every ε₁-recoverable task is also ε₂-recoverable.

Capε₁(N)⊂Capε₂(N).

The capability family expands with tolerance.

8. The frontier can move discontinuously

Even though each fixed task defect can vary continuously under small channel perturbations, the identity of the inclusion-maximal task algebras can change abruptly as ε crosses a threshold.

continuous defect curves can induce discrete frontier changes.

This is a representation transition, not automatically a physical phase transition.

9. Recovery-error profile

Rather than one fidelity number, define:

A ↦ δA(N).

This function records physical recovery quality across the task-algebra poset.

The ε-capability family is simply its lower level set:

Capε(N)={A:δA(N)≤ε}.

10. Noise-parameter capability diagrams

For a channel family Nλ, one can study:

(λ,ε) ↦ Capε(Nλ).

Different task algebras can enter or leave the admissible region at different noise strengths.

This creates a task-relative quantum capability diagram.

11. Channel perturbation stability

For a fixed task algebra A:

Physical Defect Stability
|δA(N)-δA(M)|≤||N-M||⋄.

Thus each task's recovery curve is Lipschitz in channel distance.

12. Capability-family sandwich under channel perturbation

If:

||N-M||⋄≤r,

then any task recoverable under N with margin ε−r remains recoverable under M at tolerance ε.

Capε-r(N)⊂Capε(M)

when ε≥r. Symmetrically:

Capε-r(M)⊂Capε(N).

This is a direct consequence of the defect Lipschitz bound.

13. Approximate Passport reserve

For a task algebra A currently satisfying:

δA(N)=r<ε,

define its recovery reserve:

Reserve(A)=ε-r.

This is the channel perturbation budget available before the guaranteed ε-admissibility certificate is exhausted.

14. Algebra size is only one objective

A larger task algebra preserves more logical capability, but it can demand more physical resources.

ObjectivePossible preference
Task algebra richnessLarger
Recovery defectSmaller
Carrier dimensionSmaller
Authorized-set resilienceLarger/broader
Recovery circuit complexitySmaller
Locality radiusSmaller

These goals can conflict.

15. Pareto capability geometry

Associate to a design a vector such as:

J=(task richness, δ, log₂k, resilience cost, implementation cost).

A design is Pareto-dominated if another design preserves at least as much task capability while being no worse in every declared cost and strictly better in at least one.

The remaining nondominated designs form a practical capability frontier.

16. Inclusion frontier and engineering Pareto frontier are distinct

Inclusion frontier

Maximal ε-recoverable task algebras under algebra inclusion.

Engineering Pareto frontier

Nondominated combinations of task capability, error, carrier size, access resilience, locality, and implementation cost.

BM keeps these two frontiers separate.

17. Carrier-dimension pressure

If a contextual task operator system has dimension dS, any exact k-dimensional quantum carrier must satisfy:

k²≥dS.

Approximate recovery can sometimes use smaller carriers, but then carrier dimension becomes another axis of the frontier rather than disappearing from the problem.

18. Access-resilience pressure

A task algebra that is richer or more noncommutative can have a more restrictive exact authorized family.

A weaker task can enlarge authorization:

A₁⊂A₂ ⇒ Auth(A₂)⊂Auth(A₁).

Thus moving down the task lattice can increase distributed resilience.

19. Noncommutativity changes frontier geometry

For genuinely noncommutative exact tasks, authorized families must be intersecting.

For commutative tasks, disjoint exact recovery can be possible.

task algebra type → access-geometry constraint.

Approximate frontiers can therefore change qualitatively when a design downgrades from noncommutative to commutative capability.

20. Approximate no-broadcasting as a frontier problem

For disjoint regions S and T, define:

(δA,S,δA,T).

Exact no-broadcasting forbids the point (0,0) for a genuinely noncommutative algebra.

The approximate research problem is to characterize the feasible tradeoff region around that forbidden corner.

21. Capability frontiers can be context-dependent

If the receiver requires correlations with an external reference, the task operator system enlarges.

Slocal⊂Sctx.

A task that lies on the frontier under local tests may leave the frontier under the stronger contextual Passport.

Firewall: a local approximate-recovery frontier need not survive unchanged when contextual correlation requirements are added.

22. Horizon-dependent frontiers

For dynamical tasks, the Heisenberg-backpropagated task algebra can grow with horizon:

At=Φt,T*(AT).

The approximate capability frontier can therefore depend on time:

Frontε,t(N).

A carrier sufficient for a short horizon may leave the frontier at a longer horizon.

23. Static recovery and dynamic closure are different frontier axes

A design can have small static task-recovery defect but large autonomous carrier-dynamics defect:

δA small,   δQ large.

Another design can have the reverse tradeoff.

Permanent firewall: static approximate task recovery and approximate autonomous quantum closure are distinct objectives.

24. Algebraic defect adds another axis

The commutator defect κ and distance-to-commutant η can be included in the frontier analysis:

η≤κ≤2η.

But they should not replace the physical defect δ.

(κ,η,δ) = related but distinct coordinates.

25. Why BM does not collapse the frontier to one score

A weighted scalar objective can be useful once a user declares priorities, but the weights are extra assumptions.

score = α·error + β·carrier cost + γ·task loss + ···

Different weights select different frontier points.

BM preserves the nondominated set until the receiver explicitly supplies a preference or cost contract.

26. Approximate task downgrade

If the current algebra A exceeds tolerance, one can move to a weaker subalgebra:

A′⊂A

with:

δA′(N)≤ε.

The preferred downgrade need not be unique. Several incomparable subalgebras can lie on the frontier.

27. Minimal loss downgrade

A receiver can define a task-loss functional L(A′;A) measuring how much required capability is surrendered when moving from A to A′.

Then one can solve:

min L(A′;A)   subject to   δA′(N)≤ε.

The loss functional is receiver-defined; BM does not supply a universal one.

28. Robust frontier points

Two frontier tasks can have the same present recovery error but different reserves against channel perturbation.

Reserve(A)=ε-δA(N).

A slightly smaller task algebra with more reserve can be preferable in uncertain environments.

29. Frontiers under erasure patterns

For each surviving subset S, define:

Capε(S).

The distributed system then has a family of local capability frontiers indexed by damage pattern or survivor set.

A global resilient design can require acceptable frontier points across every allowed failure pattern.

30. Multi-scenario robust capability

For an allowed scenario set Ω, define the robust capability family:

Capεrob=∩ω∈ΩCapε(Nω).

This keeps only task algebras recoverable within tolerance in every declared scenario.

Adding scenarios can only shrink this robust family.

31. Portable versus deployment-specific quantum Passports

Deployment-specific

Optimized for one channel, one reference context, and one noise model.

Portable

Must remain admissible across a family of channels, contexts, or damage patterns.

Portability usually moves the feasible frontier inward because more scenarios must be satisfied simultaneously.

32. Computational representation

For finite-dimensional explicit models, candidate task algebras can be tested with algebraic criteria and CPTP optimization.

One practical pipeline is:

enumerate or parameterize task families → compute algebraic defect → solve recovery SDP → estimate δ → retain ε-admissible tasks → remove dominated points.

The difficult step is avoiding combinatorial explosion in the candidate algebra family.

33. Frontier provenance

Each retained design should carry provenance:

task algebra → channel/context → carrier → recovery map → defect bound → access structure → robustness reserve.

This prevents visually similar frontier points from being treated as interchangeable when they arise from different assumptions.

34. A reusable capability-frontier workflow

declare channel family → declare task-algebra family → compute exact envelope → evaluate δ for candidates → build Capε → find inclusion-maximal tasks → add carrier/access/locality costs → compute Pareto frontier → select only after receiver priorities are declared.

35. What BM contributes here

Operator algebras, CPTP recovery, Pareto optimization, access structures, and channel norms are established mathematics.

The BM synthesis is to treat approximate quantum capability as a receiver-relative geometric frontier rather than forcing it into a nonexistent single maximal algebra. Exact recoverability has a canonical envelope; approximate recoverability generally has a downward-closed family, multiple maximal elements, and additional tradeoffs in carrier size, access resilience, locality, robustness, and dynamics.

36. Open directions

Important next problems include efficient frontier computation, dimension-aware κ/η→δ bounds, approximate no-broadcasting regions, many-body locality constraints on frontier shape, robust frontiers over correlated-noise families, and dynamic frontiers that evolve with causal horizon.
