Approximate quantum recovery

Approximate Quantum Task Recovery and Commutator Defects

Exact quantum error correction has a clean algebraic criterion. Approximate recovery is harder: being almost in the exact commutant is not the same thing as having a small optimal physical recovery error. The missing bridge is one of the strongest next mathematical targets in the quantum BM program.

Volume II development Uses established operator-algebra QEC and quantum channel norms
Abstract

Let K=span{P E_a†E_b P} be the compressed error operator system of a finite-dimensional code and let A be a declared task algebra. Exact correction requires A⊂K′. Approximate correction can be measured algebraically by a commutator defect κ, geometrically by distance η to the exact commutant, and physically by an optimal CPTP recovery defect δ_A(N). In the finite-dimensional algebraic sector the project records η≤κ≤2η, but no universal dimension-free theorem is assumed that turns small κ or η into small physical δ. The physical defect is stable under channel perturbation: |δ_A(N)−δ_A(M)|≤||N−M||⋄. This page develops the exact/approximate separation, capability profiles, tolerance-dependent recovery, and the open dimension-aware stability problem.

1. Exact recovery gives the reference geometry

Let P project onto the code space and let Ea be Kraus operators of a noise channel N.

Define the compressed error operator system:

K=span{P Ea†EbP}.

For a declared task algebra A, exact correctability is characterized by:

[K,X]=0   for every X∈A,

equivalently:

A⊂K′.

2. Approximate recovery asks how far from exact we are

If the commutators are small rather than zero, the task is near the exact correctable sector in an algebraic sense.

But several non-equivalent approximation questions appear immediately:

QuestionQuantity
How badly do task observables fail to commute with the error system?κ
How far is the task algebra from the exact commutant?η
How well can a physical CPTP recovery actually restore the task?δA

3. A normalized commutator defect

One may define an algebraic defect schematically as:

κ(A,K)=sup ||[Y,X]||

over appropriately normalized Y∈K and X∈A.

Then:

κ=0 ⇔ A⊂K′.

So κ detects exact correctability correctly.

4. Distance to the exact commutant

Let η measure the operator-norm distance of the candidate task observables to the exact commutant K′.

η(A,K)=distance of A to K′.

The finite-dimensional project development records the comparison:

Algebraic Defect Comparison
η≤κ≤2η.

This places commutator smallness and distance-to-commutant on the same scale in the declared finite-dimensional algebraic setting.

5. Why this does not finish approximate QEC

A task can be close to the exact commutant while still requiring a nontrivial physical recovery construction.

The recovery must be completely positive and trace preserving, and it must work on states and correlations rather than only on one linear operator calculation.

Permanent firewall: algebraic near-correctability is not the same object as physical near-recoverability.

6. Physical task-recovery defect

Let:

δA(N)

denote the optimal physical recovery error for task algebra A under channel N, minimized over CPTP recovery maps in the declared norm.

Then:

δA(N)=0

exactly when the task is physically recoverable under that formulation.

7. Approximate task authorization

A physical subsystem S is ε-authorized for A when:

δA,S≤ε.

This defines a tolerance-dependent access family:

Authε(A)={S:δA,S≤ε}.

Unlike exact authorization, different subsets can enter this family at different tolerances.

8. Capability is now a curve, not a binary label

For a fixed task algebra:

λ ↦ δA(Nλ)

describes recovery quality along a noise parameter λ.

For a fixed channel:

A ↦ δA(N)

describes the channel's task-relative capability profile.

9. Downward closure under task weakening

If a stronger algebra A₂ is ε-recoverable and:

A₁⊂A₂,

then the weaker algebra A₁ is also ε-recoverable under the same recovery.

A₂ ε-recoverable ⇒ every subalgebra A₁⊂A₂ is ε-recoverable.

Thus approximate capability is naturally downward closed.

10. But a greatest approximate algebra need not exist

Two incomparable algebras can each be recoverable within tolerance ε, while their generated union fails the tolerance.

Permanent firewall: there is no general unique greatest ε-recoverable algebra.

Approximate degradation is therefore more naturally represented as a partially ordered family or Pareto frontier than by one maximal algebra.

11. Physical defect is perturbation-stable

Let N and M be two channels.

Channel Perturbation Stability
|δA(N)-δA(M)|≤||N-M||⋄.

Small diamond-norm channel perturbations change the optimal task-recovery defect by at most the perturbation size.

12. Why this bound matters

If one already knows a good recovery for a nearby channel, the defect cannot deteriorate arbitrarily under a small channel perturbation.

robust recovery quality changes at most Lipschitz-continuously with channel distance.

This is a physical stability statement, unlike the purely algebraic κ/η comparison.

13. The central missing bridge

The hard question is to connect:

small κ or η

to:

small δA.

Such a theorem must generally depend on explicit assumptions—dimension, algebraic structure, conditioning, spectral separation, or other native data.

The strongest next target is a dimension-aware stability theorem of the form “algebraically near the exact commutant ⇒ physically near recoverable,” with constants and hypotheses stated explicitly.

14. Why a dimension-free theorem is dangerous to assume

As Hilbert-space dimension grows, small operator-level defects can interact with increasingly large state and channel spaces.

Without conditioning or dimension control, one should not assume one universal constant converts κ into δ.

Firewall: BM does not currently claim a universal dimension-free κ→δ theorem.

15. Candidate shape of a future bound

A useful theorem might have the schematic form:

δA(N) ≤ C(d,A,K,conditioning) · κ

or perhaps:

δA(N) ≤ f(d)η.

The important issue is not the exact symbolic shape, but proving a valid bound with explicit dependence rather than hiding it.

16. Approximate graceful degradation

At positive tolerance, one can track a family:

Capε(N)={A : δA(N)≤ε}.

This is the set of tasks still physically recoverable within the declared error budget.

As noise grows:

Capε(N)

can lose stronger algebras while retaining weaker ones.

17. Tolerance sweeps define task phase diagrams

For a noise parameter λ and tolerance ε, one can study:

(λ,ε) ↦ Capε(Nλ).

Different task algebras can cross the recovery threshold at different locations.

This gives a task-relative recovery phase diagram without conflating it with a thermodynamic phase transition.

18. Approximate access geometry

For each subset S, define:

δA,S.

Then the family:

Authε(A)

grows as the tolerance increases.

The exact intersecting-family theorem for noncommutative tasks constrains the zero-error limit, but positive-error access geometry is richer.

19. Approximate no-broadcasting tradeoffs

Two disjoint regions may each approximately recover some information about a noncommutative task.

The relevant research object is a tradeoff region:

(δA,S,δA,T)   with   S∩T=∅.

Exact no-broadcasting forbids both errors from being zero. Sharp task-specific lower bounds away from zero remain a natural target.

20. Correlation-sensitive recovery

A task can require not only local observable expectations but also correlations with a reference system.

Approximate recovery must therefore specify whether the error norm is tested only on local observables or on extensions preserving contextual correlations.

Firewall: small local observable error does not automatically imply small error for every reference correlation.

21. Carrier dimension and approximate recovery

A smaller quantum carrier may increase approximation error even when it remains sufficient for a weaker task.

carrier size ↔ recovery defect ↔ task algebra.

The natural design problem is therefore multi-objective.

22. Pareto structure

A quantum design may trade:

QuantityGoal
Carrier dimensionSmaller representation
Recovery defect δHigher fidelity to the task
Task algebra sizeMore retained capability
Authorized-set sizeBetter distributed resilience
Implementation complexitySimpler physical recovery

There need not be one scalar optimum without declaring weights or priorities.

23. Noise-localization effects

If a task algebra is supported in one region and the noise acts outside the task's causal support, physical recovery defect can remain small or zero even when global channel distance is large.

global noise magnitude ≠ task damage magnitude.

Native locality and the declared task support determine which perturbations matter.

24. Time-dependent approximate recovery

For a dynamical quantum system, the task algebra itself can evolve in the Heisenberg picture.

At=Φt*(Afinal).

Approximate recoverability can therefore change with horizon as the backward task support grows.

This links quantum task recovery to spacetime semantic width.

25. Approximate dynamic closure

If a carrier encoding E is used through time, one can measure physical closure defect:

δQ=minΨ CPTP||EΦ-ΨE||⋄.

This is distinct from recovery of one static task algebra after a single noise channel.

Firewall: approximate static task recovery does not imply approximate autonomous carrier dynamics.

26. Recovery versus prediction

A carrier may predict the declared task accurately without admitting a physical inverse that reconstructs the protected observables on the code.

good prediction ≠ physical recovery.

BM keeps these contracts separate.

27. Numerical certification

In finite-dimensional explicit models, CPTP recovery and closure problems can often be formulated with semidefinite constraints through Choi matrices.

This provides a computational route for:

upper bounds on δ, feasibility tests, and candidate optimal recovery channels.

Algebraic κ and η can be computed separately and compared empirically to the physical optimum.

28. A useful research experiment

For a family of finite codes and channels, one can compute:

(κ,η,δ,d)

across dimensions and noise strengths, then test whether dimension-dependent inequalities emerge.

This would not prove a theorem, but it could identify the correct scaling and conditioning variables for one.

29. Provenance of approximation failure

If recovery error exceeds tolerance, the system should distinguish whether the failure came from:

large commutator defect, large distance to commutant, CPTP obstruction, insufficient carrier dimension, context/correlation requirement, or dynamic incompatibility.

Those are mathematically different failure modes.

30. A reusable approximate-QEC workflow

declare task algebra → form K → compute κ → estimate η → solve/estimate physical δ → test contextual correlations → build Authε → analyze degradation → inspect dimension and conditioning → record failure provenance.

31. What BM contributes here

Approximate QEC, operator systems, commutants, channel norms, and semidefinite recovery are established mathematics.

The BM synthesis is to keep three layers separate—algebraic defect, geometric distance to exact correctability, and physical CPTP recovery error—then place them inside one receiver-relative task structure. This prevents a common collapse where “almost commuting,” “almost correctable,” and “physically recoverable with small error” are treated as synonyms.

32. The main open theorem

The most valuable next mathematical step is a rigorous stability result that converts algebraic defect into physical recovery quality under explicit assumptions.

goal: κ or η small + stated hypotheses ⇒ δA quantitatively small.

The constants should expose their dependence on dimension, algebra structure, conditioning, and the chosen norm.

33. Open directions

Further directions include approximate no-broadcasting inequalities, efficient recovery-defect computation for large many-body systems, local-to-global approximate QEC, adaptive task downgrading, robustness under correlated noise, and semantic-width bounds for approximate quantum carriers.
