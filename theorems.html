1. How to Read This Registry

Branch Mathematics uses substantial established mathematics. The presence of BM terminology does not convert a classical result into a historically new theorem.

\[ \boxed{ \text{proof status} \neq \text{framework role} \neq \text{historical novelty} \neq \text{physical realization}. } \]
Classification Meaning
Classical infrastructure Established mathematics used directly inside BM or GURF.
BM structural result A proved statement formulated around BM structural roles. No novelty claim is implied.
GURF response result A proved specialization involving Tasks, observation, continuation, ambiguity, or response semantics.
Firewall / counterexample A result showing that a stronger inference is invalid.
Applied identity Established domain mathematics carried through BM/GURF roles without being relabeled as new domain mathematics.
Candidate structures such as Seed, Rooting, Rebranching, and unrecovered structures such as HEC/HEC-S do not supply theorem hypotheses or conclusions merely from their names.

2. Theorem Discipline

  1. Every theorem states its semantic sector.
  2. Every partial map uses its true domain.
  3. Every approximation states its error structure.
  4. Every convergence claim states its convergence semantics.
  5. Every selection claim requires a selector or uniqueness theorem.
  6. Every local-to-global result states its factorization/gluing hypotheses.
  7. Every BM role used in a proof must reduce to actual mathematical conditions.
  8. Established mathematics is credited rather than renamed.
\[ \boxed{ \text{role word} \not\Rightarrow \text{mathematical conclusion}. } \]

4. Exact Representation and Sufficiency

T01 — Exact Fibre-Factorization Theorem
Classification: Classical infrastructure
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Statement

Let \[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W. \] The following are equivalent:

  1. there exists \[ \widetilde T:r(X)\to W \] such that \[ T=\widetilde T\circ r; \]
  2. \(T\) is constant on every fibre of \(r\): \[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Proof

If \(T=\widetilde T\circ r\), equal \(r\)-values immediately give equal \(T\)-values.

Conversely, for \(y\in r(X)\), choose \(x\) with \(r(x)=y\) and define \[ \widetilde T(y)=T(x). \] Fibre constancy makes this independent of the chosen representative. Hence \[ T=\widetilde T\circ r. \] \(\square\)

The factor map is required only on the realized image \(r(X)\). This is not a universal stochastic, relational, topological, or quantum theorem.
T02 — Passport Sufficiency Corollary
Classification: GURF response result
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Statement

Let a combined deterministic Passport representation be \[ B_P:X\to Q_P. \] Task \(T:X\to W\) is exactly recoverable from the Passport iff

\[ \boxed{ B_P(x)=B_P(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Proof

Apply T01 with \(r=B_P\). \(\square\)

Passport sufficiency is relative to the declared Task and semantic sector.
T03 — Completion-Fibre Sufficiency Theorem
Classification: GURF response result
Sector: deterministic Set semantics with a valid joint observation

Statement

Let \[ r_\alpha:X\to Y_\alpha \] and \[ r_A(x)=(r_\alpha(x))_{\alpha\in A}. \] Define

\[ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). \]

Then \(T:X\to W\) factors through \(r_A\) iff \(T\) is constant on every nonempty Completion Fibre:

\[ \boxed{ \left| T(\mathfrak C_A(\ell)) \right| \le 1 \quad \text{for every }\ell. } \]

Proof

Since \[ r_A^{-1}(\ell)=\mathfrak C_A(\ell), \] apply T01. \(\square\)

T04 — Minimal Exact Task-Quotient Proposition
Classification: Classical infrastructure
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Statement

Define \[ x\sim_Tx' \iff T(x)=T(x'). \] If equivalence relation \(E\) gives a quotient sufficient for \(T\), then

\[ \boxed{ E \subseteq \sim_T. } \]

Hence \(X/{\sim_T}\) is the coarsest exact Set-level quotient that preserves \(T\).

Proof

Sufficiency of the quotient means \[ xEx' \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'), \] which is exactly \[ E\subseteq\sim_T. \] Conversely \(T\) is constant on its own equivalence classes. \(\square\)

Additional algebraic, topological, smooth, measurable, dynamic, privacy, or other structure can require a finer quotient.
T05 — Finite Exact Carrier Cardinality Bound
Classification: Classical infrastructure
Sector: finite deterministic Set semantics

Statement

If \(X\) is finite and \(r:X\to Y\) is exactly sufficient for \(T:X\to W\), then

\[ \boxed{ |r(X)| \ge |T(X)|. } \]

Proof

Exact sufficiency gives \[ T=\widetilde T\circ r. \] Therefore \[ \widetilde T:r(X)\to T(X) \] is surjective. A surjection between finite sets cannot have a smaller domain than codomain. \(\square\)

T06 — Parallel Observation Refinement Theorem
Classification: Classical infrastructure
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Statement

For \[ F:X\to Y, \qquad G:X\to Z, \] and \[ H=(F,G), \]

\[ \boxed{ \sim_H = \sim_F \cap \sim_G. } \]

Also,

\[ \boxed{ H^{-1}(y,z) = F^{-1}(y) \cap G^{-1}(z). } \]

Proof

\(H(x)=H(x')\) exactly when both coordinates agree. \(\square\)

T07 — Deterministic Serial Information-Loss Theorem
Classification: Classical infrastructure
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Statement

Let \[ F:X\to Y, \qquad G:Y\to Z. \] Then

\[ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow G(F(x))=G(F(x')), \]

hence

\[ \boxed{ \sim_F \subseteq \sim_{G\circ F}. } \]

A deterministic downstream map cannot recreate a distinction erased by \(F\) unless additional information enters through another channel.

T08 — BM-SQ-01 Structured Quotient Criterion
Classification: Classical universal-algebra theorem used by BM
Sector: finitary algebra

Statement

Let \(A=(X,\Sigma)\) be a finitary algebra and \(\sim\) an equivalence relation. Quotient operation

\[ \overline\sigma ([x_1],\ldots,[x_n]) = [\sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n)] \]

is well defined for every \(\sigma\in\Sigma\) iff \(\sim\) is a congruence:

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim x_i' \ \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(x_1',\ldots,x_n'). } \]

Proof

Congruence makes the result independent of representative choice. Conversely, well-defined quotient Operations applied to equal quotient classes give equal output classes. \(\square\)

This is classical congruence mathematics. BM uses it; BM does not claim to have discovered it.

5. Domains and Dynamics

T09 — Typed Composite-Domain Theorem
Classification: BM structural statement of classical partial composition
Sector: deterministic partial maps

Statement

Let \[ F:D_F\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad G:D_G\subseteq Y\to Z. \] Then

\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

Proof

\(G(F(x))\) is meaningful exactly when \(x\in D_F\) and \(F(x)\in D_G\). \(\square\)

T10 — Dynamic Continuation Theorem
Classification: GURF response result
Sector: deterministic dynamics

Statement

Let \[ r:X\to Y, \qquad \Phi_t:X\to X, \qquad T:X\to W. \] The future Task \(T\circ\Phi_t\) is determined by current representation \(r\) iff

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(\Phi_t(x)) = T(\Phi_t(x')). } \]

Proof

Apply T01 to the Task \(T_t=T\circ\Phi_t\). \(\square\)

Sufficiency for one time \(t\) does not imply autonomous reduced dynamics or all-time closure.
T11 — Exact Dynamic Closure Theorem
Classification: BM/GURF structural theorem
Sector: exact deterministic dynamics

Statement

Let \[ \Phi:A\to A, \qquad R:A\to Y, \qquad Y_R=R(A). \] There exists

\[ \widetilde\Phi:Y_R\to Y_R \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R } \]

iff

\[ \boxed{ R(x)=R(x') \Longrightarrow R(\Phi(x)) = R(\Phi(x')). } \]

Proof

Apply T01 to \[ r=R, \qquad T=R\circ\Phi. \] The factor map is required only on \(Y_R=R(A)\). \(\square\)

T12 — Static Task Sufficiency Does Not Imply Dynamic Closure
Classification: Counterexample / firewall
Sector: deterministic dynamics

Statement

Let \[ X=\mathbb R^2, \qquad R(x_1,x_2)=x_1, \qquad T(x_1,x_2)=x_1. \] Then \(R\) is exactly sufficient for current Task \(T\).

Define \[ \Phi(x_1,x_2)=(x_2,x_2). \] For \[ x=(0,1), \qquad x'=(0,2), \]

\[ R(x)=R(x')=0, \]

but

\[ R(\Phi(x))=1, \qquad R(\Phi(x'))=2. \]

Therefore no reduced \(\widetilde\Phi\) exists. \(\square\)

6. Branch Decomposition Results

T13 — Branch-Essentiality Monotonicity Theorem
Classification: BM structural result
Sector: fixed problem, fixed admissible coarsening class

Statement

Let \[ \mathscr Q_1 \subseteq \mathscr Q_2 \] be two obligation families for the same problem representation and same admissible Branch-removing coarsenings.

If Branch \(B\) is essential for \(\mathscr Q_1\), then \(B\) is essential for \(\mathscr Q_2\):

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Essential}_{\mathscr Q_1}(B) \Longrightarrow \operatorname{Essential}_{\mathscr Q_2}(B). } \]

Proof

Essentiality under \(\mathscr Q_1\) means every admissible coarsening removing \(B\) destroys at least one obligation \[ Q\in\mathscr Q_1. \] Since \[ \mathscr Q_1\subseteq\mathscr Q_2, \] that same \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathscr Q_2\). Hence the same coarsening fails under the larger scope. \(\square\)

The theorem assumes the admissible coarsening class itself is unchanged.
T14 — Refinement Transitivity Proposition
Classification: BM structural result
Sector: deterministic common-source refinement

Statement

Suppose

\[ R_1=A_{21}\circ R_2, \qquad R_2=A_{32}\circ R_3. \]

Then

\[ \boxed{ R_1 = (A_{21}\circ A_{32}) \circ R_3. } \]

Hence refinement is transitive whenever the comparison scope and representation semantics remain fixed.

Proof

Substitute the second factorization into the first and use associativity of composition. \(\square\)

T15 — Branch Count Is Not Generally a Problem Invariant
Classification: Counterexample / representation firewall
Sector: deterministic equivalent coordinates

Statement

The same problem may admit lawful equivalent representations with different Branch counts.

For example, phase-space state \[ (q,p)\in\mathbb R^2 \] may be treated as one phase-space Branch or as two typed \(q\)- and \(p\)-focused Branches connected by the necessary dynamical structure.

Thus, absent a theorem selecting a canonical decomposition,

\[ \boxed{ |\mathcal B(P)| \text{ is representation-dependent.} } \]
A Branch count is meaningful for a fixed declared decomposition.

7. Realization and Local-to-Global Results

T16 — Finite Tree-Gluing Theorem
Classification: Classical tree/CSP gluing theorem used by BM
Sector: finite tree-structured realization system

Assumptions

Let \[ H=(V,E) \] be a finite rooted tree.

For each \(v\in V\), let \[ F_v\neq\varnothing \] be a local realization set.

For each oriented edge \(u\to v\), let \[ C_{uv}\subseteq F_u\times F_v \] be the complete compatibility relation.

Assume global constraints factor exactly as

\[ \mathfrak F_H = \left\{ (x_v)_{v\in V}: x_v\in F_v,\; (x_u,x_v)\in C_{uv} \text{ for every }uv\in E \right\}. \]

Assume parent-surjectivity:

\[ \forall(u\to v)\in E, \quad \forall x_u\in F_u, \quad \exists x_v\in F_v: (x_u,x_v)\in C_{uv}. \]

Conclusion

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F_H \neq \varnothing. } \]

Proof

Choose any root realization. Parent-surjectivity supplies a compatible value for every child. Continue recursively through the finite tree. Every non-root node has one parent, and by the factorization assumption all global constraints have already been encoded in the local sets or edge relations. The resulting family belongs to \(\mathfrak F_H\). \(\square\)

BM must prove the factorization assumption before invoking this theorem. Hidden global constraints invalidate the reduction to edge compatibility.
T17 — Local Realizability Does Not Imply Global Realizability
Classification: Counterexample
Sector: finite binary constraints

Statement

Let \[ a,b,c\in\{0,1\} \] with constraints

\[ a=b, \qquad b=c, \qquad a\neq c. \]

Each individual constraint is satisfiable and each pair is satisfiable, but all three cannot hold simultaneously.

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F(U_i)\neq\varnothing \ \forall i \not\Rightarrow \mathfrak F(C)\neq\varnothing. } \]

Proof

The first two constraints imply \[ a=b=c, \] contradicting \(a\neq c\). \(\square\)

T18 — Finite Deterministic DAG Evaluation Theorem
Classification: Classical finite DAG induction used by BM
Sector: finite deterministic acyclic Network

Assumptions

  • The dependency graph is finite and acyclic.
  • Each source has one specified admissible value.
  • Each non-source node has one single-valued Operation on predecessor outputs.
  • The actual predecessor tuple lies in the Operation's domain.

Conclusion

Every node has one unique admissible evaluation.

Proof

A finite DAG has a topological order. Evaluate sources first, then apply the unique Operation at each node after all predecessors have been evaluated. Induction proves existence and uniqueness at every node. \(\square\)

Cycles, implicit equations, nondeterminism, or multiple fixed points require additional mathematics.

8. Witness, Coupling, and Selection Firewalls

T19 — Quantifier-Scope Non-Equivalence
Classification: Classical logical counterexample
Sector: logic / relational semantics
\[ \boxed{ \forall c\,\exists x_c\,P(c,x_c) \not\Rightarrow \exists x\,\forall c\,P(c,x). } \]

Proof by counterexample

Let \(c\in\{0,1\}\) and \[ P(c,x) \iff x=c. \] Every \(c\) has a witness \(x_c=c\), but no single \(x\) works for both. \(\square\)

T20 — Separate Feasibility Does Not Imply Joint Feasibility
Classification: Classical set-theoretic counterexample
Sector: Set semantics

Nonempty feasible sets do not imply nonempty intersection.

Let \[ X=\{0,1\}, \qquad P_1=\{0\}, \qquad P_2=\{1\}. \] Then

\[ P_1\neq\varnothing, \qquad P_2\neq\varnothing, \]

but

\[ \boxed{ P_1\cap P_2 = \varnothing. } \]
T21 — Root Receiver-Relativity Proposition
Classification: BM structural result
Sector: receiver-relative admissibility

Statement

The same exported object \(r\) may be a Root for one receiver and not another.

Proof

Let receiver Passports satisfy \[ r\in\operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P_R^{B_1}) \] but \[ r\notin\operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P_R^{B_2}). \] Roothood follows for \(B_1\) and fails for \(B_2\) by definition. \(\square\)

T22 — Roothood Is Not Transitive Across Receivers
Classification: Counterexample / BM firewall
Sector: deterministic receiver tasks

Statement

Let a Response be \[ Y=(x,y). \] Receiver \(B_1\) needs only \(x\), so export \[ E_1(Y)=x \] is sufficient for \(B_1\).

Suppose a later receiver \(B_2\) needs \[ x+y. \] The value \(x\) alone does not determine \(x+y\).

Hence

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root for }B_1 \not\Rightarrow \text{Root for }B_2. } \]
T23 — Mere Existence Does Not Define a Canonical Selection
Classification: Structural selection firewall
Sector: general set-valued existence

If a realization family contains \[ \mathfrak F(C)=\{x_1,x_2\} \] with \(x_1\neq x_2\), the existence statement \[ \mathfrak F(C)\neq\varnothing \] does not determine whether \(x_1\) or \(x_2\) should be used.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in S \not\Rightarrow \text{distinguished }x. } \]
A selector can exist for additional mathematical reasons. The point is that nonemptiness alone does not define one.

9. Coherence Results

T24 — Path-Coherence Transitivity Proposition
Classification: BM structural result
Sector: common-domain deterministic paths

Statement

Let \(F_p,F_q,F_r\) be defined on common domain \(U\), with outputs compared by a transitive relation \(\simeq_Q\).

If

\[ F_p(x)\simeq_QF_q(x), \qquad F_q(x)\simeq_QF_r(x) \]

for all \(x\in U\), then

\[ \boxed{ F_p(x)\simeq_QF_r(x) \quad \forall x\in U. } \]

Proof

Apply transitivity of \(\simeq_Q\) pointwise. \(\square\)

T25 — Pairwise Coherence Does Not Imply Global Coherence
Classification: Counterexample
Sector: finite constraint system

The system

\[ a=b, \qquad b=c, \qquad a\neq c \]

is locally/pairwise satisfiable but globally inconsistent.

Hence:

\[ \boxed{ \text{pairwise coherence} \not\Rightarrow \text{global coherence}. } \]

10. Defect and Repair Results

T26 — Serial Passport-Defect Localization Theorem
Classification: BM structural result
Sector: deterministic Boolean Passport predicates

Let \[ f:A\to B, \qquad g:B\to C, \] and Boolean predicates \(P_A,P_B,P_C\).

Define

\[ D_f = \{a: P_A(a) \land \neg P_B(f(a)) \}, \]
\[ D_g = \{b: P_B(b) \land \neg P_C(g(b)) \}, \]

and

\[ D_{g\circ f} = \{a: P_A(a) \land \neg P_C(g(f(a))) \}. \]

Conclusion

\[ \boxed{ D_{g\circ f} \subseteq D_f \cup f^{-1}(D_g). } \]

Proof

Take \(a\in D_{g\circ f}\). If \(P_B(f(a))\) fails, then \(a\in D_f\). Otherwise \(P_B(f(a))\) holds while the final predicate fails, so \(f(a)\in D_g\). These cases are exhaustive. \(\square\)

T27 — Exact Repair Refinement Proposition
Classification: GURF response result
Sector: deterministic Set semantics

Let \[ r:X\to Y \] and add repair writer \[ q:X\to Z. \] Then

\[ \boxed{ \sim_{(r,q)} \subseteq \sim_r. } \]

Proof

If \[ (r,q)(x)=(r,q)(x'), \] then in particular \[ r(x)=r(x'). \] \(\square\)

More information does not automatically mean better numerical stability, privacy, cost, or performance under a different Task.
T28 — Repair Revalidation Firewall
Classification: BM certification rule
Sector: dependency-aware Configuration repair

Suppose repair changes data in region \(S\). Let

\[ \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(S) \]

be the semantic dependency closure of the changed region.

Any certificate whose validity depends on changed data in that closure must be recomputed or separately protected by an invariance theorem.

\[ \boxed{ \text{upstream repair} \not\Rightarrow \text{downstream certification remains valid}. } \]
This is a certification discipline. It does not assert that every downstream numerical value necessarily changes.

11. Stochastic Sector Results

T29 — Coarse-State Markov Closure Requires Lumpability
Classification: Classical stochastic firewall
Sector: finite Markov chains

Statement

Let finite-state Markov kernel \(K\) be coarsened by \[ R:X\to Y. \] A Markov transition law on \(Y\) determined only by the coarse state requires that whenever \[ R(x)=R(x'), \] the total transition mass into each coarse state \(y\in Y\) agrees:

\[ \boxed{ \sum_{z:R(z)=y} K(x,z) = \sum_{z:R(z)=y} K(x',z). } \]

Without this condition, states in the same coarse class induce different next coarse-state laws.

This is classical lumpability mathematics, not a new BM theorem.
T30 — Markov Test-Function Composition Theorem
Classification: Classical Markov-kernel identity
Sector: measurable stochastic semantics

Let \[ K:X\rightsquigarrow Y, \qquad L:Y\rightsquigarrow Z \] be Markov kernels.

Define the action on bounded measurable test functions by

\[ (K\varphi)(x) = \int_Y \varphi(y) K(x,dy). \]

Then

\[ \boxed{ ((L\circ K)\phi)(x) = \big( K(L\phi) \big)(x). } \]

Proof

By kernel composition,

\[ (L\circ K)(x,A) = \int_Y L(y,A) K(x,dy). \]

Substitution and Tonelli/Fubini under the stated measurability and boundedness assumptions give

\[ \int_Y \left[ \int_Z \phi(z) L(y,dz) \right] K(x,dy), \]

which is \(K(L\phi)(x)\). \(\square\)

12. Quantitative Approximation Results

T31 — Exact Task-Diameter Criterion
Classification: GURF metric reformulation
Sector: deterministic representation with metric Task space

Let \[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to(W,d). \] For fibre \[ C_y=r^{-1}(y), \] define

\[ \operatorname{diam}_T(C_y) = \sup_{x,x'\in C_y} d(T(x),T(x')). \]

Then \(r\) is exactly sufficient for \(T\) iff

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{diam}_T(C_y)=0 \quad \forall y\in r(X). } \]

Proof

Exact sufficiency makes \(T\) constant on every fibre. Conversely, diameter zero in a metric space implies all Task values in the fibre are equal. Apply T01. \(\square\)

T32 — Approximate Fibre Reconstruction Proposition
Classification: GURF quantitative response bound
Sector: metric Task space with chosen section

Assumptions

Let \[ s:r(X)\to X \] satisfy \[ r(s(y))=y. \] Suppose every fibre has Task diameter at most \(\varepsilon\).

Conclusion

Define \[ \widehat T(y)=T(s(y)). \] Then

\[ \boxed{ d( T(x), \widehat T(r(x)) ) \le \varepsilon \quad \forall x\in X. } \]

Proof

\(x\) and \(s(r(x))\) lie in the same fibre. The fibre-diameter bound gives the result. \(\square\)

T33 — Lipschitz Serial Error-Propagation Bound
Classification: Classical quantitative bound
Sector: metric deterministic maps

Let \(G:Y\to Z\) be \(L\)-Lipschitz:

\[ d_Z(Gy,Gy') \le Ld_Y(y,y'). \]

If \[ d_Y(F(x),F(x')) \le \varepsilon, \] then

\[ \boxed{ d_Z( G(F(x)), G(F(x')) ) \le L\varepsilon. } \]

Proof

Apply the Lipschitz inequality to \(F(x)\) and \(F(x')\). \(\square\)

13. Applied Identities Carried as BM Leaves

T34 — Periodic Incompressible Navier–Stokes Energy Identity
Classification: Applied identity
Provenance: Classical PDE mathematics
Sector: sufficiently smooth periodic incompressible Navier–Stokes

Let

\[ \partial_tu + (u\cdot\nabla)u = -\nabla p + \nu\Delta u + f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0. \]

Define

\[ E(t) = \frac12 \int_\Omega |u|^2\,dx, \]
\[ D(t) = \nu \int_\Omega |\nabla u|^2\,dx, \qquad F(t) = \int_\Omega f\cdot u\,dx. \]

On a periodic domain, under sufficient smoothness,

\[ \boxed{ \frac{dE}{dt} = -D+F. } \]

Proof sketch

Take the \(L^2\) inner product with \(u\). Incompressibility and periodicity remove the convective and pressure contributions, while integration by parts gives the viscous dissipation term. \(\square\)

This classical identity does not solve the three-dimensional regularity problem.
T35 — Wilson-Loop Gauge Invariance
Classification: Applied identity
Provenance: Classical gauge theory
Sector: connection holonomy with trace in a representation

For a closed loop \(\gamma\), define

\[ W_\gamma(A) = \operatorname{Tr} \mathcal P \exp \left( i \oint_\gamma A \right). \]

Under a gauge transformation, the holonomy transforms by conjugation. Therefore the trace is invariant:

\[ \boxed{ W_\gamma(A^g) = W_\gamma(A). } \]

Proof

The transformed closed-loop holonomy has the form \[ U^g_\gamma = g(x_0) U_\gamma g(x_0)^{-1}. \] Cyclicity of trace gives \[ \operatorname{Tr}(U^g_\gamma) = \operatorname{Tr}(U_\gamma). \] \(\square\)

Wilson-loop gauge invariance is established gauge theory, not a novel BM theorem.

14. Theorem Sector Firewall

T36 — Sector Non-Portability Firewall
Classification: Governing theorem-use rule
Sector: cross-semantic theorem application

A theorem proved under one mathematical semantics may be transferred to another sector only after its hypotheses and constructions are shown to remain meaningful there.

\[ \boxed{ \text{theorem valid in sector }S_1 \not\Rightarrow \text{theorem valid in sector }S_2. } \]

In particular:

\[ \text{deterministic function} \neq \text{Markov kernel} \neq \text{relation} \neq \text{quantum channel}. \]

Exact equality, approximate equivalence, privacy indistinguishability, stochastic equality in law, and quantum operational equivalence likewise require distinct mathematics.

This is a theorem-use firewall, not a claim that translations between sectors are impossible.

16. Novelty and Prior-Art Rule

Many important results in this registry are deliberately labeled as classical.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical correctness} \neq \text{historical novelty}. } \]

Current BM research must continue comparison with existing mathematics including:

  • abstract interpretation and Galois connections;
  • contract and interface theory;
  • constraint-satisfaction and conjunctive-query theory;
  • category theory and compositional open systems;
  • institution theory and heterogeneous specifications;
  • information-flow and channel theory;
  • descent, sheaves, cohomology, and local-to-global mathematics;
  • lumpability and stochastic reduction;
  • structured quotient and universal-algebra theory.

If a known theory fully subsumes a BM subconstruction, that subconstruction is credited as an application of the known theory. If only part reduces, the unreduced residual becomes the actual research target.
