Recovery, prediction and closure are different

ObligationExact requirementWhat must be supplied
Present recovery\(T=d\circ Q\)The Seed determines the current task.
Prediction to horizon H\(T\circ F^j=d_j\circ Q,\ 0\le j\le H\)All requested outputs through the chosen horizon.
Autonomous closure\(Q\circ F=E\circ Q\)The represented state determines its own next state.

Closure together with present recovery determines every future task under the specified total deterministic law. Prediction for one finite horizon alone does not establish closure.

For an infinite binary shift, the first H+1 coordinate readings determine the next H+1 task outputs. Every nonempty finite coordinate representation fails autonomous closure: its largest observed position needs the next, unobserved bit after the shift. This is a counterexample with a specified observation catalogue, not a claim that every physical future is unpredictable.

A minimal autonomous Seed for a finite task

Let X be a finite nonempty set with n states, F a total deterministic evolution, and T a task with m distinct current outputs. Group states by equal task histories through horizon h:

\[x\sim_h y\iff (T(x),\ldots,T(F^h x))=(T(y),\ldots,T(F^h y)).\]

Each strict refinement adds at least one class. Once two consecutive partitions agree, the relation is forward-stable. Consequently the first stable horizon satisfies

\[h_*\le n-m.\]

The stable quotient Q* is the coarsest representation that recovers the task and evolves autonomously. Every other exact task-sufficient closed representation determines Q*. A chain with one distinguished terminal state attains the bound n−2 when m=2.

Observation availability remains separate. A mathematically minimal quotient need not be measurable from the receiver’s current export. In a two-bit swap system, observing the first bit answers the present task but cannot predict the next first bit.

Combine receivers on states that actually coexist

For two task-closed quotients of one shared finite deterministic source, the joint future equivalence is the intersection of the two equivalences. The lawful joint carrier is the image

\[S=\{(Q_A(x),Q_B(x)):x\in X\},\qquad \max(|Z_A|,|Z_B|)\le |S|\le\min(|X|,|Z_A||Z_B|).\]

The product update preserves S. The whole Cartesian product is realizable exactly when every A-fibre intersects every B-fibre.

For three states with outputs (0,0), (0,1), and (1,1), both local receivers have two possible values. Their independent product invents (1,0), which no source state supplies. A common source witness is therefore part of a valid joint export.

Preserve lawful branches when evolution is set-valued

Let each state have a specified nonempty successor set K(x). Refine the task partition until states in the same class have identical sets of successor classes. This stabilizes within n−m strict refinements and yields the coarsest task-respecting strong-bisimulation quotient.

\[\bar K(Q(x))=\{Q(y):y\in K(x)\}.\]

Two limits matter. Equal sets of complete output traces can conceal different intermediate branch choices. Also, intersecting two separately valid receiver equivalences need not preserve joint branching.

One root may lead to {00,11}, another to {01,10}. A receiver reading either bit alone sees {0,1} from both roots, while the paired outcomes differ. The joint receiver must retain that distinction. These are possible outcomes under a relation; no probabilities are assumed.

A Seed needs the right law information

Suppose each candidate source law is individually task-sufficient. Join two law labels in a conflict graph G when the same exported Seed value can require different task answers. A correct law-only tag is exactly a proper coloring:

\[|C|_{\min}=\chi(G),\qquad B_{\min}=\lceil\log_2\chi(G)\rceil.\]

The tag must be supplied from authorized, available information. It cannot be guessed into existence. If the same law already merges task-distinct source states, no law label alone repairs the loss.

Changing-law evolution requires the stronger code-transition test, including successor law tags. A static graph-coloring answer alone is insufficient. The complete note gives explicit switching-law failures and repairs.

When finitely many observations suffice on a compact source

If the source is compact Hausdorff, the task is continuous with finite discrete values, and continuous Hausdorff-valued probes separate every task-distinct pair, a finite probe battery determines the task with a continuous decoder.

For continuous metric-valued tasks and probes on a compact source, task separation yields a finite battery and positive measurement margin for each positive task tolerance. Guaranteed reading errors smaller than half that margin certify the tolerance.

Compactness alone does not give exact recovery of an infinite-valued task, a discontinuous threshold task, an unlimited future, an efficient search algorithm, or an instrument-error guarantee. The note proves the conditions and supplies counterexamples when they fail.
