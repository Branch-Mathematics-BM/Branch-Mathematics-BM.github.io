1. Purpose of the Encyclopedia

This encyclopedia is the public mathematical record of Branch Mathematics (BM), GURF, their formal structures, and associated mathematical research.

It is organized around mathematics rather than the conversational history through which individual ideas were developed.

Definitions, equations, proofs, assumptions, counterexamples, corrections, quantitative figures, research status, and unresolved problems are intended to be directly inspectable.

3. Branch Mathematics at a Glance

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

It studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, operations, and interfaces.

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root}. \end{cases} } \]

This is a structural schema rather than a universal sequential algorithm.

BM does not require distinct mathematics to be collapsed into one undifferentiated formalism.

4. BM and GURF Scope

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM} = \text{broader meta-structural mathematics} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{GURF} = \text{response-specialized mathematics under BM governance} } \]

BM governs lawful representation, transformation, Interaction, composition, interfaces, realization, export, re-entry, distinction preservation, and related mathematical participation.

GURF is invoked when genuine response semantics are present.

BM need not be rewritten as a response theory, and GURF need not be activated merely because BM terminology appears in a problem.

7. Leaf Mathematics

If an Interaction produces

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j, \]

then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right) \in \mathcal L_j. } \]

Therefore:

  • \(\mathcal L_j\) is the Leaf space;
  • \(\ell_j\) is the extraction map;
  • \(\lambda_j\) is the actual Leaf value.

A derived mathematical quantity is not automatically a formal BM Leaf unless its Interaction provenance and extraction map are specified.

8. Tree, Response, and Root

\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \xrightarrow{\text{combined computation}} \text{Response} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal mathematical result},\\[4pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]

A Response may be a value, state, law, relation, operator, classification, structured object, family of objects, or another mathematically typed result.

BM does not restrict Responses to laws.

Root status requires downstream compatibility and is therefore a stronger role than mere output production.

9. GURF Core at a Glance

Let

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y \]

be a retained representation and

\[ T: X \longrightarrow W \]

a deterministic task.

Exact task sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ \exists\, \widetilde T: r(X) \longrightarrow W \quad \text{such that} \quad T = \widetilde T\circ r. } \]

The realized image \(r(X)\) is the natural factorization domain.

11. SOE — Operations and Composition

SOE governs lawful operations performed on response systems.

A general Operation may have form

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

SOE tracks:

input type · output type · admissible domain · active regime · required information · preserved information · operation order · failure conditions

For serial composition,

\[ F: D_F\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad G: D_G\subseteq Y\to Z, \]

the actual domain is

\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

Codomain matching alone does not prove admissibility.

12. ACE — Admissibility and Constraint Structure

ACE governs constraint-sensitive response.

If constraints define

\[ K_1,\ldots,K_m \subseteq X, \]

the simultaneously admissible region may be

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A = \bigcap_{j=1}^{m} K_j. } \]

A theorem proved on \(\mathcal A\) does not automatically extend to all of \(X\).

ACE also maintains the distinction between static admissibility conditions and genuinely dynamical stability claims.

13. IRE — Inference and Recovery

IRE handles inverse recovery, feasible fibres, ambiguity, identifiability, uncertainty, reconstruction, and recovery certificates.

For

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y \]

and observation \(y\), an admissible inverse fibre is

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C(y) = \{x\in\mathcal A:F(x)=y\}. } \]

Full-state identifiability requires

\[ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow x=x', \]

while task-relative identifiability requires only

\[ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]

Earlier GURE material has been consolidated into IRE and should not be counted as an additional active extension.

14. UKE — Unified Kernel Extension

UKE contains advanced kernel-specific response mathematics.

It maintains a strict typing distinction among mathematically different meanings of the word kernel.

Nullspace Kernel

\[ \ker F = \{x:F(x)=0\}. \]

Integral Kernel

\[ (Tf)(x) = \int K(x,y)f(y)\,dy. \]

Green Kernel

\[ L_xG(x,x') = \delta(x-x'). \]

Resolvent

\[ R(z) = (L-zI)^{-1} \]

where defined.

Memory Response

\[ R(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} K(t,s)x(s)\,ds. \]
A mathematically convenient kernel is not automatically a physically derived mechanism.

15. GRDE — Diagrammatic Response Extension

GRDE is an optional response-structure representation calculus.

Its current status is

\[ \boxed{ \text{GRDE frozen at GURF-GRDE-008}. } \]

GRDE may represent response fibres, refinements, state/carrier distinctions, provenance, response cuts, response fusion, and higher-order response information.

GRDE does not replace the mathematics being represented.

The original GRDE structural diagrams are not currently reproduced publicly and substitute diagrams are not invented.

16. HDE — Halo Domain Extension

HDE studies response problems in which relevant response structure extends beyond a narrowly localized source or primary domain.

A useful working decomposition may be

\[ D \subseteq D_{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad H = D_{\mathrm{ext}}\setminus D. \]

An extended response state may require

\[ x_{\mathrm{ext}} = (x_D,x_H). \]

A task may depend on both components even when a local observation retains only \(x_D\).

HDE therefore studies extended support, propagation, retained halo-domain information, boundary transfer, observation, memory, and persistence.

HDE is not restricted to one specific physical halo model.

17. GHSE — Glue-Hold State Extension

GHSE studies response states whose behavior depends on lawful joining, attachment, holding, persistence, release, or transition among coupled components.

If components have state spaces

\[ X_A, \qquad X_B, \]

a joined state may occupy an admissible subset

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal H \subseteq X_A\times X_B. } \]

A working glue Operation may be

\[ J: D_J \subseteq X_A\times X_B \longrightarrow X_{AB}. \]

Arbitrary joining is not automatically admissible.

Domain-independent mathematical machinery is promoted or retained under BM when its identity does not depend essentially on response semantics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{portable domain-independent kernel} \longrightarrow \text{BM} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{essential response semantics} \longrightarrow \text{GURF}. } \]

20. Representation and Fibres

For

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y, \]

the fibre over \(y\in r(X)\) is

\[ r^{-1}(y) = \{x\in X:r(x)=y\}. \]

States in one fibre are indistinguishable under the retained representation.

Whether those lost distinctions matter depends on downstream mathematics.

21. Completion Fibres

For a family of observations

\[ r_\alpha: X \longrightarrow Y_\alpha, \]

a compatible state set may be

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

Completion fibres are central to recovery, sufficiency, ambiguity, continuation, and response analysis.

22. Structured Quotients

BM distinguishes ordinary set-level equivalence from equivalence compatible with mathematical operations.

For

\[ \sigma: X^n \longrightarrow X, \]

quotient compatibility requires

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_Py_i \quad \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

In the finitary algebraic sector this is the relevant congruence requirement.

The project theorem is recorded as BM-SQ-01.

23. Interfaces and Coherence

One exact deterministic interface condition is

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

The broader BM coherence program includes:

typed paths · parallel-path compatibility · equalizers · pullbacks · coequalizers · gluing · path closure · naturality · approximate coherence · higher coherence

The comparison relation must be appropriate to the native mathematical sector.

24. Obstruction and Repair

A deterministic task obstruction occurs when

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \quad \text{but} \quad T(x)\neq T(x'). } \]

A repair may add information

\[ q: X \longrightarrow Z \]

to produce a refined representation

\[ (r,q): X \longrightarrow Y\times Z. \]

Repair is successful only if the resulting representation satisfies the declared mathematical requirement.

25. Dynamic Continuation

Let

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X \]

be an evolution.

A present representation remains sufficient for a future task when

\[ \boxed{ T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t\circ r } \]

on the relevant domain.

A distinction irrelevant to the current task may become essential to a future one.

26. Dynamic Closure

For a reduced representation

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Z \]

and exact deterministic evolution

\[ \Phi: X \longrightarrow X, \]

dynamic closure requires a reduced evolution

\[ \widetilde\Phi: R(X) \longrightarrow R(X) \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

Static task sufficiency does not automatically imply dynamic closure.

28. Nondeterministic Quantifier Firewall

For a relation

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y \]

and target set \(U\subseteq Y\),

\[ \Diamond_R(U) = \{x:\exists y\in U\text{ with }xRy\}, \]

while

\[ \Box_R(U) = \{x:\forall y,\ xRy\Rightarrow y\in U\}. \]

Existential reachability and universal safety are different statements and must remain different.

29. Joint Feasibility and Coupling

If condition \(c\) has feasible set

\[ P_c \subseteq X, \]

common feasibility requires

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} P_c \neq \varnothing. } \]

Separate witnesses do not prove that one common witness satisfies all requirements.

Coupling laws and witness reuse must therefore be stated explicitly where they matter.

30. Root Mathematics

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.

If a Root transformation closes on an appropriate state space,

\[ F: X_R \longrightarrow X_R, \]

and the required iterates remain admissible, then

\[ R_{n+1} = F(R_n) \]

defines an ordinary iterated system.

31. Seed Mathematics

Seed is a candidate BM primitive describing information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes lawful generative or self-seeding development until a specified export condition is met.

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \]

followed conditionally by

\[ S_n \longrightarrow R. \]

Seed is not merely initial data.

Seed, Seed State, and Seed Export Condition remain candidate BM mathematics under active development.

Status: active derived mathematics and theorem-kernel research.

Current derived objects and relations include work involving:

derivative covariance · strain-gradient covariance · co-location structure · helical decomposition · triad variables · amplitude/coherence relations · interaction loading · spectral redistribution · continuation-oriented quantities

Status: active conditional research.

Current mathematics includes:

boundary susceptibility · first-exit leakage · tail floors · boundary Green matrices · shell-depth recursion · coherent return · local thresholds · Gauss-law shell support · global-return obstruction · variational Ritz bounds

The four-dimensional Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap problem remains open.

35. Gravity and Spacetime Status

Status: active mathematical-physics investigation.

General Relativity remains an essential comparison theory.

Physical investigation is focused on forward-causal geometric effects such as proper-time gradients, geodesic behavior, tidal response, frame dragging, focusing, defocusing, and observation.

Closed-timelike-curve or retrocausal constructions may be studied mathematically without being treated as physically established mechanisms.

36. Quantum Status

Status: active mathematical and interpretive investigation.

Entanglement is treated through joint state structure, distinguishability, measurement statistics, correlations, admissible operations, and information availability.

Correlation structure is not equated with controllable superluminal signalling.

41. Worked Iterative Root Example

Consider the specific affine recurrence

\[ R_{n+1} = \frac12R_n+1. \]

Its fixed point satisfies

\[ R_* = \frac12R_*+1, \]

so

\[ R_*=2. \]

For \(R_0=0\),

\[ R_n = 2(1-2^{-n}). \]

Because the affine map closes on the scalar Root state space and \(|1/2|<1\), this example converges globally to \(R_*=2\).

It is a worked repeated-Root model, not a universal law governing all Roots.

45. Relationship Between Proof and Framework

BM/GURF terminology does not turn a statement into a theorem.

\[ \boxed{ \text{framework placement} \neq \text{mathematical proof}. } \]

Mathematical validity comes from definitions, assumptions, derivation, proof, computation where appropriate, counterexample, and sector-correct validation.

Framework placement determines how mathematical results are organized and used.

46. Internal Status and External Status

Question Meaning
Internal formal status Whether a structure is accepted inside the current project architecture.
External novelty or priority Whether equivalent mathematical structure already exists in the literature.
Independent validation Whether external mathematicians, researchers, experiments, or independent computations have reproduced or validated the claim.

These questions are separate.
