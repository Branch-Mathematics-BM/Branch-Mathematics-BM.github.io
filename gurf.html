GURF

GURF — General Unified Response Framework — is the response-specialized framework of Branch Mathematics. It studies what a declared receiver can determine from a representation, which distinctions are lost, what is required for continuation, and what additional state or observations repair a specific response failure.

Canonical extensions in this edition: Core, SOE, ACE, IRE, UKE, HDE, and GHSE. GRDE remains frozen at GURF-GRDE-008. The names of the extensions do not replace their native mathematical hypotheses.

1 · Core

Response problem specification

A GURF response problem declares the mathematical objects needed to determine whether a retained representation supports a requested response. At minimum it states:

State carrier

The native state space or carrier \(X\).

Response law

A function, relation, kernel, operator, or other native response object.

Observation

The retained representation or observation \(R\).

Tasks

The receiver task family \(\mathscr Q\) whose distinctions matter.

Acceptance

Applicability and acceptance predicates or Passport obligations.

Continuation

Context, interventions, and any requested evolution or future response.

Semantics are declared, not guessed. A deterministic response is a function; a nondeterministic response is a relation; a stochastic response is a kernel. These are different mathematical objects and activate different theorems.
native state→ observation / representation→ receiver task→ continuation audit→ defect or accepted response

2 · What remains possible after observation

Completion fibre

Let \(r_\alpha\) be declared observations and let \(\ell_\alpha\) be their observed values. For an observation index set \(A\), the compatible underlying states are

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha) }. \]
Fibre conditionMeaning
\(\mathfrak C_A(\ell)=\varnothing\) The proposed observation is unrealizable in the declared model.
Nonempty fibre At least one native state is compatible with the observations.
Multiple states The observation has not identified the native state uniquely.
Task image singleton The task can still be exactly recoverable even when the full state is not.
A unique optimizer selected by regularization is not the same as an identified state. Regularization can choose one member of an ambiguous fibre without proving that the fibre itself is a singleton.

Supporting results: T01 Exact fibre factorization, T02 Passport sufficiency, and T03 Completion fibre sufficiency.

3 · Exact receiver sufficiency

Task recovery from a representation

For a deterministic representation \(R:X\to Z\) and task \(T:X\to W\), an exact decoder exists on the realized image of \(R\) precisely when \(T\) is constant on every \(R\)-fibre:

\[ T=\bar T\,R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R(x)=R(x')\Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x') \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ker R\subseteq\ker T. \]

GURF therefore separates full-state recovery from task recovery. A many-to-one observation may fail to reconstruct \(x\) and still be perfectly sufficient for a receiver task that is constant on each observation fibre.

Full-state task

If \(T=\mathrm{id}_X\), exact recovery requires \(R\) to be injective on the relevant domain.

Receiver-relative task

If the receiver only needs \(T\), distinctions inside a fibre may be irrelevant when all states in that fibre have the same task value.

4 · Quantitative ambiguity

Task diameter and unrestricted reconstruction error

Let \(T:X\to(W,d)\) be metric-valued and let \(F_z=R^{-1}(z)\) be a nonempty observation fibre. Its task ambiguity is measured by

\[ \operatorname{diam}T(F_z) = \sup_{x,x'\in F_z} d\!\left(T(x),T(x')\right). \]

The best unrestricted decoder is governed by an enclosing-radius problem for each fibre. Globally, if

\[ \Delta= \sup_{z\in R(X)} \operatorname{diam}T(R^{-1}z), \]

then unrestricted worst-case decoding obeys the two-sided bound

\[ \frac{\Delta}{2}\le e^*\le\Delta \]

when fibre representatives exist. For bounded real-valued tasks, the midpoint of the fibre infimum and supremum attains the lower value \(\Delta/2\).

Ambiguity and stability are different. Fibre diameter measures variation inside one observation fibre. Stability against perturbing the observed value requires estimates comparing different fibres.

Supporting results: T31 Exact task diameter criterion, T32 Two-sided reconstruction bound, and T47 Receiver sufficiency modulus.

5 · Do not merge distinct failure modes

Three independent response defects

Let \(\mathcal E\subseteq X\times X\) be a relation and define the observed successor set

\[ Q(x)=R(\mathcal E[x]). \]

5.1 Task collision after evolution

Distinct source task values collide when their possible observed successors overlap:

\[ \operatorname{Col}_{\mathcal E,R,T} = \left\{ (x,x'): T(x)\ne T(x'),\ Q(x)\cap Q(x')\ne\varnothing \right\}. \]

5.2 Closure defect

Current states that are observationally identical fail exact relational closure when they have different observed successor sets:

\[ \operatorname{ClDef}_{\mathcal E,R} = \left\{ (x,x'): R(x)=R(x'),\ Q(x)\ne Q(x') \right\}. \]

5.3 Interaction defect

Interaction failure concerns input tuples with the same retained interfaces but different task-relevant outputs or different applicability/definedness.

Collision

Can two different source tasks lead to a shared observed successor?

Closure

Does one present reduced state determine one reduced successor set?

Interaction

Can equivalent branch interfaces be substituted without changing definedness or receiver output?

Collision overlap is not generally transitive: \(\{a\}\), \(\{a,b\}\), and \(\{b\}\) overlap successively while the first and last do not. Likewise, thresholding a metric by \(d\le\varepsilon\) need not define an equivalence relation.
SOE

Structured Operations Extension active

SOE tracks operation type, domain, order, provenance, and handoff. Its purpose is to prevent a response chain from silently treating partial, ordered, adaptive, or feedback operations as if they were ordinary total maps with interchangeable order.

Partial composition

If \(f:D_f\subseteq X\to Y\) and \(g:D_g\subseteq Y\to Z\), then

\[ D_{g\circ f} = D_f\cap f^{-1}(D_g). \]

Composition requires both stages to be defined. Output comparison without domain comparison is insufficient for partial operations.

Order

Order can change the result. With \(f(x)=x+1\) and \(g(x)=2x\),

\[ (g\circ f)(x)=2x+2, \qquad (f\circ g)(x)=2x+1. \]

An order defect is meaningful only where both expressions are defined and their outputs are placed in the same declared comparison space.

Adaptive operations and REBIND

If the operation selected at a step depends on state, mode, history, or context, the state that selects the operation belongs in the declared system. REBIND is a change of binding or context followed by an audit; it has no universal algebra independent of the native operation being rebound.

Feedback

A feedback diagram must state whether it denotes a fixed point, delayed recursion, relation, dynamical system, or another construction. For

\[ x_{n+1}=ax_n+b, \qquad |a|<1, \] the fixed point is \[ x_*=\frac{b}{1-a}, \qquad x_n-x_*=a^n(x_0-x_*). \]

For \(a=1,b=1\), there is no fixed point. Existence, uniqueness, stability, convergence rate, and selection are separate mathematical claims.

Supporting result: T09 Typed composite domain. See also the dedicated SOE page.

ACE

Admissibility and Constraints Extension active

ACE coordinates declared constraints while keeping static feasibility distinct from dynamical invariance.

Constraint intersection

For

\[ A=\bigcap_j A_j, \]

nonempty individual sets \(A_j\) do not imply \(A\neq\varnothing\). Separate feasibility witnesses do not automatically combine into one joint witness.

A scalar violation function, feasibility margin, penalty, convexity assumption, or “soft constraint” interpretation must be explicitly supplied. ACE does not infer those structures from vocabulary.

Trajectory invariance

In the regular scalar case \(A=\{g\le0\}\), with \(g\in C^1\) and \(\nabla g\neq0\) at every relevant boundary point, the tangent condition takes the form

\[ \nabla g(x)\cdot f(x)\le0 \qquad\text{for }x\in\partial A. \]

With the required native existence and regularity hypotheses, an appropriate tangent-cone theorem can establish forward invariance on the solution’s interval of existence.

Why regularity matters

Let \(g(x)=x^3\), \(A=(-\infty,0]\), and \(f(x)=1\). At the boundary, \(g'(0)f(0)=0\), yet the trajectory starting at zero immediately moves into positive \(x\) and leaves \(A\). The naive scalar boundary test is therefore insufficient without the regularity assumptions.

Relational admissibility

Weak viability

At least one successor remains in the admissible set.

Strong invariance

Every successor remains in the admissible set.

Nonblocking

A separate condition requires a nonempty successor set.

Static feasibility

Current membership in \(A\) says nothing by itself about future invariance.

Related result: T20 Separate feasibility does not imply joint feasibility.

IRE

Inverse Recovery Extension active

IRE separates three questions that are often conflated: whether a task is identifiable, whether an inverse is stable, and whether a chosen reconstruction procedure has selected the true underlying state.

Linear task identifiability

For linear \(H:V\to Y\) and linear task \(L:V\to W\),

\[ \boxed{ L\text{ factors through }H \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ker H\subseteq\ker L }. \]

The nullspace determines exact ambiguity. If two states differ by a vector in \(\ker H\), the data \(H x\) cannot distinguish them. A task remains identifiable only if it is also constant along that nullspace.

Conditioning

On Euclidean spaces, when \(H\neq0\), the minimum-norm inverse on \(\operatorname{ran}H\) has operator norm

\[ \|H^\dagger\| = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}^{+}(H)}. \]

Small positive singular values create instability even when the nullspace relevant to a task has been removed.

Two-coordinate example

Let \(H(x_1,x_2)=x_1\). The task \(L_1(x)=x_1\) is identifiable from the observation. The task \(L_2(x)=x_2\) is not. A regularizer that selects \(x_2=0\) changes the chosen completion; it does not prove that the unknown native coordinate was actually zero.

Small residual, unique numerical minimizer, exact identifiability, and stable inversion are four different properties.
UKE

Unified Kernel Extension active

UKE exists partly to prevent the word kernel from erasing established mathematical distinctions.

Nullspace

Kernel of a linear map or operator.

Integral kernel

A function defining an integral operator.

Green function

Depends on an operator plus boundary or initial conditions and a solution class.

Resolvent

\((\lambda I-A)^{-1}\), defined only on the resolvent set.

Reproducing / spectral kernel

Native objects of their respective functional or spectral settings.

Memory kernel

Encodes history dependence in an integral response law.

For an integral response \(y(t)=\int K(t,s)x(s)\,ds\), the integration domain, function spaces, and convergence or boundedness assumptions are part of the mathematical statement.

Exact augmentation for exponential memory

Let \(\beta>0\), let \(x\) be continuous, and define

\[ m(t) = e^{-\beta t}m_0 + \int_0^t e^{-\beta(t-s)}x(s)\,ds. \]

Differentiation gives

\[ \dot m=-\beta m+x, \qquad m(0)=m_0. \]

Conversely, solving this ODE by an integrating factor reproduces the integral formula. Therefore a system whose response depends on this exponential memory can use the augmented state \((x,m)\) exactly.

This example does not imply that every memory kernel admits a finite-dimensional exact state augmentation.

HDE

Halo Domain Extension active

HDE represents response-relevant state outside a selected primary domain. The additional state can be spatial, hidden-state, environmental, or historical. “Halo” names the mathematical role; it does not denote a new substance or force.

Extended state and local observation

\[ (x,h), \qquad R(x,h)=x, \qquad F(x,h)=\bigl(f(x,h),g(x,h)\bigr). \]

An autonomous reduced evolution for \(x\) exists exactly when the first component \(f(x,h)\) is constant in \(h\) on each admissible \(R\)-fibre, together with domain saturation when \(F\) is partial.

\[ R(x,h)=R(x,h') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x,h)=f(x,h') \] on the applicable domain.

This is an instance of exact Dynamic Closure, not a new physical force law.

History defect

For history space \(\mathcal H\), present-state map \(\pi\), and declared future-response object \(\mathcal F\), define

\[ \operatorname{MemDef} = \left\{ (h,h'): \pi(h)=\pi(h'), \ \mathcal F(h)\ne\mathcal F(h') \right\}.

A nonempty memory defect means that the present retained state is insufficient for the declared future response. A lawful repair retains additional history-derived state that separates the conflicting histories.

Memory-state repair

In the exponential-memory model above, two histories can have the same present \(x(t)\) but different \(m(t)\). If future response depends on \(m\), retaining \((x,m)\) repairs that particular defect exactly.

Finite interface synthesis can compute a coarsest exact history partition only after a finite history-state model and transitions are supplied. HDE does not discover a finite Markov state for every continuum history process.

Related theorem: T11 Exact Dynamic Closure.

GHSE

Glue Hold State Extension active

GHSE handles joining, holding, and release under native rules. A held representation is sufficient only relative to the release task and release availability that the receiver actually asks for.

Held spring example

Let a joined spring-like state be \((q,v,k)\), with a hold observation retaining only

\[ h(q,v,k)=q. \]

If the release task asks for

\[ E(q,v,k) = \frac{v^2+kq^2}{2}, \]

then \(q\) alone is generally insufficient. The retained state must also provide \(v\), \(k\), or some other sufficient combination that determines the declared release task.

No additional energy is inferred from calling the system “joined,” “held,” or “glued.” Energy comes only from the native physical model and its state variables.

13 · Cross-extension lawfulness

Composition and sector boundaries

Cross-extension use is legal only when the interface between extensions is itself specified. At minimum, state:

Shared carrier

Which objects are actually shared between the two constructions?

Interface map

How are outputs translated into the receiving extension’s type?

Witness reuse

Does one witness have to satisfy several obligations, or may each obligation choose its own?

Composition law

Function composition, relational composition, kernel composition, coupling, or another native law?

Relational quantifiers

For a relation \(R\) and set \(U\),

\[ \Diamond_R(U) \quad\text{means some successor lies in }U, \] \[ \Box_R(U) \quad\text{means every successor lies in }U. \]

A state with no successors satisfies the ordinary box statement vacuously. If the receiver also requires continuation to exist, the nonblocking requirement must be stated separately.

\[ \operatorname{Dom}(R)\cap \Box_R(U) \]

therefore has different composition behavior from \(\Box_R(U)\) by itself.

Imported stochastic models

For a finite Markov kernel, exact closure through an observation means equality of aggregated transition probabilities for states in the same observation block. Equality of reachable supports or equality of means is not enough.

\[ R(x)=R(x') \Longrightarrow \sum_{R(y)=z}K(x,y) = \sum_{R(y)=z}K(x',y) \quad \text{for every reduced state }z.

If joint stochastic behavior matters, a coupling or joint law must be supplied. Marginal descriptions alone do not determine one.

Related results: T29 Finite Markov closure, T30 Markov test-function composition, T41 One-step approximate lumpability, T42 Multi-step approximate lumpability, and T53 Mixed deterministic and stochastic continuation.

Privacy

Differential privacy guarantees use their declared adjacency relation and divergence or likelihood bounds. They are not exact quotient equivalences and should not be imported into the exact fibre calculus as if they were.

Quantum recovery

Algebraically inverting a known output density matrix is different from realizing a physical recovery channel. A physical quantum channel must meet the applicable positivity and trace-preservation requirements; algebraic matrix inversion alone does not establish that.

The edition’s dephasing example makes the distinction explicit: for \(0<\lambda<1\), dividing the off-diagonal entry by \(\lambda\) algebraically recovers a known input matrix but amplifies coherence error by \(1/\lambda\), and the corresponding inverse is not a positive map on all density matrices. At \(\lambda=0\), exact matrix recovery fails because different coherences have identical outputs.

14 · One response problem, several extensions

Worked GURF response logic

The extensions are not independent labels to be stacked arbitrarily. They answer different mathematical questions about one supplied system.

1. Core

Declare \(X\), \(R\), the receiver task, acceptance conditions, and the requested future response. Compute the completion fibre and test task constancy.

2. SOE

If response requires several operations, audit domains, order, adaptive state, and handoffs.

3. ACE

Check whether all declared constraints are jointly feasible and whether trajectories remain admissible.

4. IRE

If the task is inverse recovery, distinguish nullspace ambiguity from conditioning and regularization.

5. UKE

If a kernel or memory object appears, use its native definition and test whether exact state augmentation exists.

6. HDE / GHSE

If hidden, historical, environmental, or held-state distinctions control future response or release, add exactly the distinctions required by the named defect.

Repair rule. A repair is successful only when the added observation, history state, mode, or other lawful source actually separates the fibre pairs responsible for the failed obligation and the repaired system is re-audited under its applicable constraints and continuation rules.

15 · Mathematical status

What GURF currently establishes

Core status

Core, SOE, ACE, IRE, UKE, HDE, and GHSE retain their declared mathematical roles in the 2026-09-10 edition. GRDE is frozen at 008.

Finite exact sector

The finite simultaneous-refinement theorem gives a uniquely coarsest exact interface family for the supplied finite local relations and partial Interactions under its product-context hypotheses.

Approximate sector

GURF uses explicit metrics such as task diameter, Hausdorff error, Lipschitz propagation, or total variation rather than pretending approximate equality is an exact quotient.

Physical status

HDE is not a halo substance or hidden energy source. GHSE does not add binding energy by terminology. GURF itself is not a new force law.

Not claimed. This page does not establish Navier–Stokes global regularity, a Yang–Mills mass gap, a universal physical information-conservation theorem, automatic finite memory closure for continuum systems, or physical quantum reversibility from algebraic matrix inversion.

Audit a GURF claim

  1. Declare the native state, response law, domain, context, and sector.
  2. Declare the retained observation and every receiver task or acceptance obligation.
  3. Compute or characterize the relevant completion fibres.
  4. Separate present task sufficiency from continuation and Interaction compatibility.
  5. Use the native successor semantics: deterministic, relational, stochastic, or quantum.
  6. If there is a defect, identify the exact fibre pair, domain mismatch, successor mismatch, or Interaction witness.
  7. Add only a lawful source of the missing distinction and revalidate the affected obligations.
  8. Label proof, numerical evidence, model interpretation, and external novelty separately.
