GURF

GURF asks what a receiver must still be able to distinguish, recover, continue, intervene on, or repair after observation, compression, interaction, or evolution.

Core

Receiver/task declaration, response objects, law passports, completion fibres, exact and approximate recovery.

SOE

Structured observation and equivalence: which states become indistinguishable to the declared receiver.

ACE

Admissibility and constraint enforcement: whether a candidate response lies inside lawful receiver requirements.

IRE

Intervention/response structure: how lawful interventions alter recoverable responses.

UKE

Unified kernel discipline: keeps nullspaces, integral kernels, Green functions, resolvents, spectral kernels, and memory kernels distinct.

HDE / GHSE

Higher-dimensional and global/hierarchical response extensions. GRDE remains frozen at GURF-GRDE-008.

Completion fibre

\[\mathfrak C_A(\ell)=\bigcap_\alpha r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha).\]

A task is exactly recoverable from the declared response family when it is constant on every nonempty completion fibre. A small fibre may still be unstable; approximate recovery therefore uses task-space diameter and receiver-specific error criteria.

Obstruction and repair

GURF does not force a missing distinction to vanish. It records an obstruction when the receiver lacks distinctions required by the task, then asks what refinement, memory, side channel, intervention, or Passport change repairs the failure.

Interpretation boundary. Representation loss is not automatically physical destruction. BM/GURF separate what a chosen receiver can no longer access from what remains represented elsewhere in the complete modeled system.
