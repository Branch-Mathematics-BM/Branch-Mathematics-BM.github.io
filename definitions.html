Definitions

Canonical and working vocabulary for Branch Mathematics and GURF, including the mathematical meaning, role, typing, status, and limitations of the principal objects used throughout the research program.

1. Purpose of This Registry

Definitions are separated from theorems, models, examples, and applications.

A definition establishes how a term is being used inside the BM/GURF research program.

A definition by itself does not prove that an object exists, that an operation is admissible, that a physical realization is possible, or that the terminology is historically novel.

3. Branch Mathematics

Branch Mathematics — BM
Status: canonical framework definition

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures can participate in a common problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, and interfaces.

Its scope includes:

representation · distinction preservation · transformation · reduction · quotienting · composition · interaction · interfaces · realization · export · re-entry · coherence · Root formation

4. GURF

GURF
Status: canonical response-framework definition

GURF is the mathematical framework used when a problem has explicit response semantics.

Its central concern is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which distinctions in the underlying state are necessary for the required response or future task?} } \]

GURF studies how observation, compression, constraints, evolution, regimes, and interventions affect that ability.

5. Branch

Branch
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A useful working form is

\[ B = \left( X_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, P_B \right), \]

where \(X_B\) is a carrier or state space, \(\mathcal L_B\) contains laws or constraints, \(\mathcal O_B\) contains admissible operations, and \(P_B\) contains Passport/interface information.

This tuple is a working formalization rather than a requirement that all Branches possess exactly these four fields.

6. Branch Information

Branch Information
Status: canonical interpretation

Branch information is the mathematical information carried by a Branch, including distinctions that the native mathematics recognizes as relevant.

BM does not treat information as detached from mathematical structure.

What counts as distinguishable depends on the Branch and its laws.

7. Interaction

Interaction
Status: canonical BM structural role

An Interaction is a lawful joint mathematical action involving one or more Branches.

For Branches \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), a working form is

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1} \times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

The domain \(D_I\) contains only admissible combinations.

8. Leaf

Leaf
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Leaf is extracted interacting information.

If

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \]

is an interaction result and

\[ \ell: Z_I \longrightarrow L, \]

then the extracted Leaf is

\[ \boxed{ L_{\mathrm{leaf}} = \ell(z). } \]

A Leaf may preserve only part of the information contained in the full interaction result.

9. Tree

Tree
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches and Interactions capable of generating a Response.

The term does not require every formal realization to be a literal graph-theoretic tree.

A sequential special case has

\[ \mathcal R_T = I_m \circ\cdots\circ I_1. \]

10. Response

Response
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Response is the law-valued output produced by a Tree or other organized BM structure.

\[ \mathcal R_T: D_T \longrightarrow Y_T. \]

For \(x\in D_T\),

\[ r = \mathcal R_T(x). \]

11. Root

Root
Status: canonical role; extended Rooting machinery remains active research

A Root is a Response that remains available as lawful input to further mathematical response.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Response} + \text{future admissibility} = \text{Root role}. } \]

A Root is therefore not merely any output.

It must remain type-compatible and mathematically admissible for the structure that receives it.

12. Rooting

Rooting
Status: active candidate structural definition

Rooting is the lawful attachment of a Root, Branch, or compatible law family to an interior point of another mathematical structure, after which further lawful development may occur from the attachment site.

A candidate attachment operation may have form

\[ \mathcal A: D_{\mathcal A} \subseteq X\times X_R \longrightarrow X'. \]

Rooting is not arbitrary insertion; the attachment pair must lie in the admissible domain.

13. Rebranching

Rebranching
Status: active candidate BM structure

Rebranching is the generation of new lawful Branch development from an attachment point created or activated by Rooting.

14. Passport

Passport
Status: canonical active BM/GURF structure

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, law context, admissibility requirements, or interface conditions that must remain available for later mathematical use.

A working form is

\[ P = \left( X, \mathcal C, \{b_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C}, \mathcal A \right). \]

15. Passport Observation

Passport Observation
Status: formal definition

A Passport observation is a map

\[ b_c: X \longrightarrow Y_c \]

that records one distinction or mathematical quantity required by the Passport.

16. Combined Passport Map

Combined Passport Map
Status: exact construction
\[ B_P: X \longrightarrow \prod_{c\in\mathcal C}Y_c \] \[ \boxed{ B_P(x) = (b_c(x))_{c\in\mathcal C}. } \]

It collects all active Passport observations simultaneously.

17. Passport Equivalence

Passport Equivalence
Status: exact equivalence relation
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_P x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_P(x)=B_P(x'). } \]

Equivalently,

\[ x\sim_P x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_c(x)=b_c(x') \quad \forall c\in\mathcal C. \]

18. Fibre

Fibre
Status: standard mathematical definition

For a map

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y \]

and \(y\in Y\), the fibre over \(y\) is

\[ \boxed{ F^{-1}(y) = \{x\in X:F(x)=y\}. } \]

It contains every state represented by the same observed value.

19. Completion Fibre

Completion Fibre
Status: canonical GURF construction

For observations

\[ r_\alpha: X \longrightarrow Y_\alpha, \]

and observed values

\[ \ell = (\ell_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}, \]

the completion fibre is

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

It represents every state compatible with the entire active observation family.

20. Task

Task
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Task specifies the mathematical quantity, classification, decision, prediction, control action, continuation, or response that must be determined.

In the deterministic case,

\[ T: X \longrightarrow W. \]

21. Exact Sufficiency

Exact Information Sufficiency
Status: exact factorization concept

An observation

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y \]

is sufficient for task \(T:X\to W\) when there exists

\[ \widetilde T: r(X) \longrightarrow W \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ T = \widetilde T \circ r. } \]

22. Task Equivalence

Task Equivalence
Status: exact relation
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_T x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(x)=T(x'). } \]

States are task-equivalent when the required task gives the same result for both.

23. Sufficiency by Equivalence Inclusion

Sufficiency Relation
Status: exact equivalent characterization
\[ \boxed{ \sim_r \subseteq \sim_T. } \]

Every distinction erased by the observation must also be irrelevant to the task.

24. Approximate Sufficiency

Approximate Information Sufficiency
Status: domain-dependent quantitative definition

Let \(d_W\) be a metric or justified discrepancy on task space.

A representation may be \(\varepsilon\)-sufficient when

\[ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow d_W(T(x),T(x')) \le \varepsilon. \]

The discrepancy and tolerance must be explicitly specified.

25. Observation

Observation
Status: canonical GURF role

An Observation is a mathematical representation of accessible system information.

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Observation may represent measurement, projection, compression, coarse-graining, filtering, discretization, or another transformation of state information.

26. Representation

Representation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Representation is a mathematical encoding of an object, state, law, or structure in another mathematical space.

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Representation may preserve all distinctions or intentionally identify some of them.

27. Transformation

Transformation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Transformation is a lawful map or relation changing one mathematical state or representation into another.

\[ F: D_F \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y. \]

BM requires the transformation's type, domain, laws, and interface consequences to be explicit.

28. Operation

Operation
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Operation is a typed admissible mathematical transformation used inside a BM construction.

An \(n\)-ary operation may have the form

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

29. Admissibility

Admissibility
Status: canonical BM/GURF requirement

Admissibility means that a mathematical object or operation satisfies the conditions required for it to participate lawfully in the current construction.

If

\[ A \subseteq X \]

is the admissible set, then a valid state requires

\[ x\in A. \]

30. Interface

Interface
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Interface is a typed mathematical structure governing how information passes between Branches, representations, or operations.

Exact compatibility may be expressed as

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

Other mathematical domains may require an explicitly justified weaker notion of compatibility.

31. Composition

Composition
Status: canonical BM operation

For maps

\[ F: X\longrightarrow Y \]

and

\[ G: Y\longrightarrow Z, \]

their composition is

\[ \boxed{ G\circ F: X\longrightarrow Z. } \]

BM additionally audits whether the intermediate state remains admissible and information-sufficient for the downstream operation.

32. Configuration

Configuration
Status: active BM structural object

A Configuration records the active mathematical state of a BM construction.

A working form is

\[ \mathcal X = \left( \mathcal B, \mathcal I, \mathcal L, \mathcal R, \mathcal P, \mathcal O, \mathcal A \right). \]

Components may represent active Branches, Interactions, Leaves, Roots, Passports, Operations, and admissibility data.

33. Network

Network
Status: active BM structural concept

A Network is a connected collection of Branches, Interactions, Roots, Passports, and Operations.

Depending on the mathematics, a Network may be modeled using graphs, directed graphs, hypergraphs, categories, or other structures.

34. Typed Path

Typed Path
Status: active BM concept

A Typed Path is a sequence of composable mathematical transformations whose domain, codomain, and admissibility requirements match at every stage.

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{F_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{F_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{F_n} X_n. \]

35. Path Coherence

Path Coherence
Status: active BM concept

Path coherence requires distinct lawful paths intended to represent the same result to agree under an explicitly stated mathematical equivalence.

\[ F_{\mathrm{path\,1}}(x) \approx F_{\mathrm{path\,2}}(x). \]

36. Higher Coherence

Higher Coherence
Status: active formal development

Higher coherence concerns compatibility among families of interacting paths, transformations, interfaces, and compositions beyond pairwise agreement.

37. Quotient

Quotient
Status: standard mathematical construction

Given an equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(X\), the quotient

\[ X/{\sim} \]

is the set of equivalence classes

\[ [x] = \{x'\in X:x'\sim x\}. \]

38. Structured Quotient

Structured Quotient
Status: canonical BM distinction

A Structured Quotient is a quotient on which specified mathematical operations or structure remain well-defined.

For operation

\[ \sigma: X^n \longrightarrow X, \]

representative independence requires

\[ x_i\sim y_i \quad\forall i \] \[ \Longrightarrow \] \[ \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). \]

39. Congruence

Congruence
Status: standard algebraic concept

A congruence is an equivalence relation compatible with the operations of an algebraic structure.

In BM-SQ-01, congruence is the condition that allows Passport equivalence to support a lawful finitary algebraic quotient.

40. Refinement

Information Refinement
Status: exact relational concept

Representation \(P\) is at least as informative as \(Q\) when

\[ \boxed{ \sim_P \subseteq \sim_Q. } \]

The finer representation merges fewer states.

41. Realization

Realization
Status: canonical active BM concept

Realization maps an abstract mathematical specification into a concrete mathematical or physical object satisfying the required native laws.

\[ \rho: S \longrightarrow X. \]

Formal specification does not imply existence of a realization.

42. Export

Export
Status: canonical active BM concept

Export is the lawful transfer of a mathematical object or selected information into another representation.

\[ E: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

43. Re-entry

Re-entry
Status: canonical active BM concept

Re-entry is the lawful introduction of exported, transformed, or Root-derived information into a receiving Branch or mathematical structure.

\[ R: Y \longrightarrow X'. \]

The receiving mathematics determines whether re-entry is admissible.

44. Gluing

Gluing
Status: standard mathematical idea used within BM

Gluing is the construction of a larger mathematical object from compatible local objects.

If local objects \(s_i\) are defined on regions \(U_i\), compatibility may require

\[ s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} = s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}. \]

Existence of a global glued object depends on the native mathematical setting.

45. Continuation

Continuation
Status: canonical GURF concept

Continuation asks whether the currently retained representation contains enough information for a required future response or task.

If evolution is

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X, \]

then future-task sufficiency requires factorization of

\[ T\circ\Phi_t \]

through the currently retained representation.

46. Obstruction

Obstruction
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Obstruction is a mathematical failure of the retained information to determine the response required by a task.

For observation \(r\) and task \(T\), an explicit obstruction is a pair

\[ x,x'\in X \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \quad\text{but}\quad T(x)\neq T(x'). } \]

47. Repair

Repair
Status: canonical GURF concept

Repair adds or restores enough information to eliminate a specified obstruction.

If \(r\) is insufficient and

\[ q: X \longrightarrow Z \]

is additional information, the repaired representation is

\[ (r,q): X \longrightarrow Y\times Z. \]

Repair succeeds when the joint representation becomes task-sufficient.

48. Minimal Repair

Minimal Repair
Status: optimization concept

Minimal repair seeks the least costly additional information that restores task sufficiency.

\[ \min_q \operatorname{Cost}(q) \] \[ \text{subject to task sufficiency of }(r,q). \]

49. Regime

Regime
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Regime identifies the active law context under which a system evolves or responds.

Let

\[ \lambda \in \Lambda. \]

A regime-dependent evolution may be

\[ \Phi_t^{(\lambda)}: X \longrightarrow X. \]

50. Law Passport

Law Passport
Status: active GURF/BM structure

A Law Passport records distinctions about the governing law or regime that are necessary for correct interpretation, response, or continuation.

The effective state may therefore require

\[ (x,\lambda) \in X\times\Lambda. \]

51. Intervention

Intervention
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Intervention is an external action, control, modification, or operation capable of changing system state, evolution, admissibility, or required response.

For control space \(U\), a controlled evolution may be

\[ \Phi_t: X\times U \longrightarrow X. \]

52. Constraint

Constraint
Status: standard concept used canonically

A Constraint restricts the admissible states, operations, parameters, or trajectories of a mathematical system.

If

\[ K \subseteq X, \]

then admissible states may require

\[ x\in K. \]

53. Deterministic Semantics

Deterministic Semantics
Status: standard mathematical semantics

Deterministic behavior assigns one output to each admissible input.

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

54. Stochastic Semantics

Stochastic Semantics
Status: standard probability-theoretic semantics

Stochastic behavior assigns a probability law rather than a single deterministic output.

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

For a test function \(\varphi\),

\[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E[ \varphi(Y)\mid X=x ]. \]

55. Nondeterministic Semantics

Nondeterministic Semantics
Status: standard relational semantics

Nondeterministic behavior permits a set of possible outputs.

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

Existential and universal response conditions must remain distinct.

56. Existential Reachability

Existential Reachability
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Diamond_R(U) = \{x:\exists y\in U,\ xRy\}. } \]

It asks whether at least one admissible outcome reaches \(U\).

57. Universal Safety

Universal Safety
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Box_R(U) = \{x:\forall y,\ xRy\Rightarrow y\in U\}. } \]

It asks whether every admissible outcome remains inside \(U\).

58. Joint Feasibility

Joint Feasibility
Status: exact feasibility concept

A family of conditions with feasible sets \(P_c\subseteq X\) is jointly feasible when

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} P_c \neq \varnothing. } \]

This requires one common witness satisfying all conditions.

59. Coupling

Coupling
Status: standard probability concept used in GURF

A Coupling is a joint probability law having specified marginal laws.

For marginals \(\mu\) and \(\nu\),

\[ \gamma \in \Gamma(\mu,\nu) \]

denotes a compatible coupling.

Separate marginal feasibility does not determine a unique or even task-appropriate joint coupling.

60. Conservative Passport

Conservative Passport
Status: active candidate research structure

A Conservative Passport carries information about a quantity that the native mathematics requires to persist across a transformation even when it is not retained directly in the active representation.

A Conservative Passport records an independently established invariant. It does not create conservation.

61. Root Passport Transfer

Root Passport Transfer
Status: active candidate BM structure

Root Passport Transfer is the proposed typed persistence of required Passport information from a source structure into a Root and onward to a compatible receiving structure.

\[ P_{\mathrm{source}} \longrightarrow P_R \longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{receiver}}. \]

The transfer is lawful only when the associated maps and compatibility conditions are mathematically defined.

62. Seed

Seed
Status: candidate BM primitive under active development

A Seed is information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes a lawful generative or self-seeding process, producing Seed-state information until an export condition is satisfied.

Only after that condition is met does the process produce a Root.

Schematically,

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow R. \]

This notation represents a generative sequence, not a recreated BM structural diagram.

63. Seed State

Seed State
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed State is an intermediate state in a lawful Seed process before Root export has occurred.

A simple iterative realization may have

\[ S_{n+1} = G(S_n). \]

This is one possible mathematical realization, not the universal definition of Seed behavior.

64. Export Condition

Seed Export Condition
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed export condition is the mathematical criterion determining when a Seed-state process is permitted to generate a Root.

For a condition

\[ E(S_n)=1, \]

Root export occurs only when the specified criterion is satisfied.

65. Dynamic Closure

Dynamic Closure
Status: exact factorization concept

A reduced representation

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Z \]

is dynamically closed under evolution \(\Phi\) when there exists

\[ \widetilde\Phi: R(X) \longrightarrow R(X) \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

66. Response Ambiguity

Response Ambiguity Set
Status: useful GURF construction

For observation \(r:X\to Y\) and task \(T:X\to W\), define

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A_T(y) = \{T(x):x\in r^{-1}(y)\}. } \]

It records all task outputs still possible after observing \(y\).

Exact sufficiency corresponds to

\[ |\mathcal A_T(y)| \le 1. \]

67. Task Diameter

Task Diameter
Status: quantitative GURF diagnostic

If task space \(W\) carries metric \(d_W\), define

\[ \boxed{ D_{r,T}(y) = \sup_{x,x'\in r^{-1}(y)} d_W(T(x),T(x')). } \]

Exact task sufficiency gives

\[ D_{r,T}(y)=0. \]

68. Interface Defect

Interface Defect
Status: quantitative diagnostic

Given an intended compatibility relation

\[ P_2\circ F \approx G\circ P_1, \]

and metric \(d\), one may define

\[ \boxed{ \Delta(x) = d \left( P_2(F(x)), G(P_1(x)) \right). } \]

Exact compatibility corresponds to \(\Delta(x)=0\).

69. Law Space

Law Space
Status: active BM organizational concept

A Law Space is a mathematical space or indexed family used to represent the possible laws, rule sets, operators, regimes, or constraints relevant to a BM construction.

Schematically,

\[ \lambda \in \mathcal L. \]

The exact mathematical structure of \(\mathcal L\) is domain-dependent.

70. Conservation

Conserved Quantity
Status: native-domain mathematical concept

For evolution

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X, \]

a quantity

\[ C: X \longrightarrow V \]

is conserved when

\[ \boxed{ C(\Phi_t(x)) = C(x) } \]

throughout the relevant evolution.

BM may track conservation but does not determine conservation independently of the native theory.

71. Mathematical Realizability

Mathematical Realizability
Status: canonical BM distinction

A formal specification is mathematically realizable when at least one object exists satisfying all stated mathematical requirements.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in X \text{ satisfying every required condition.} } \]

72. Physical Realizability

Physical Realizability
Status: application-level requirement

A mathematical structure is physically realizable only when a physical system can satisfy the relevant physical laws, constraints, parameters, initial conditions, and implementation conditions.

Mathematical consistency alone does not establish physical realizability.

73. Internal Framework Status

Internally Established Architecture
Status: project governance term

This indicates that a definition or architecture has been accepted for use inside the current research program.

It does not mean that the structure has received independent external validation or that its integrated formulation has established external novelty.

74. External Novelty

External Novelty
Status: separate research-history question

External novelty concerns whether a mathematical definition, theorem, framework, or integrated construction was previously known in equivalent form.

Novelty is separate from mathematical correctness.

75. Independent Validation

Independent External Validation
Status: separate from internal derivation

Independent validation means review, reproduction, verification, or acceptance by mathematically competent parties outside the internal development process.

76. Definition Versus Theorem

Definitions establish terminology.

Theorems establish consequences.

A definition cannot prove its own usefulness, consistency with another structure, realizability, or physical truth.

77. Definition Versus Model

A model assigns specific mathematical laws or parameters to the defined objects.

For example,

\[ R_{n+1} = \frac12R_n+1 \]

is a particular repeated-Root worked model.

It is not the definition of Root.

78. Definition Versus Metaphor

BM vocabulary such as Branch, Leaf, Tree, Root, and Seed has structural meaning inside the framework.

The terms should not be personified or treated as biological mechanisms.

The mathematical definitions govern their use.

80. Structural Diagram Status

The definitions in this registry are currently expressed through text and equations.

Formal BM/GURF structural diagrams have not yet been recreated for the public encyclopedia.

No substitute diagrams are being invented.

81. Definition Principle

\[ \boxed{ \text{A BM/GURF term specifies a mathematical role; the native mathematics determines whether that role can actually be realized.} } \]

This separation prevents terminology from being mistaken for proof.
