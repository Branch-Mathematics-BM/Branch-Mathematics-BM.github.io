Definitions

Extended canonical and working vocabulary for Branch Mathematics and GURF. Major definitions include their formal structure, characteristics, abilities, limits, admissibility requirements, failure modes, sector dependence, and relationships to neighboring mathematical concepts.

1. Purpose of This Registry

The Definitions Registry specifies how technical terms are used throughout the current BM/GURF research corpus.

Definitions are intentionally separated from proofs, models, numerical experiments, applications, conjectures, and novelty claims.

A definition does not prove existence.

Declaring a mathematical object by definition does not establish that an object satisfying the definition exists, is unique, is computationally accessible, is physically realizable, or is historically novel.

2. Definition Specification Standard

Major BM/GURF definitions are treated as mathematical specifications, not merely glossary entries.

Where applicable, an extended definition should identify:

Identity What the object means in BM/GURF.
Formal Data Carrier spaces, maps, relations, laws, indices, and types.
Characteristics Properties distinguishing the object from neighboring concepts.
Abilities Operations and mathematical roles the object may perform.
Limits Statements that do not follow merely from the definition.
Admissibility Conditions required before the object may participate lawfully.
Failure Modes Explicit ways a proposed instance can fail.
Sector Scope Dependence on Set, algebra, topology, probability, quantum theory, dynamics, geometry, computation, or other native mathematics.

5. Branch Mathematics — BM

Branch Mathematics
Status: canonical framework definition

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, operations, and interfaces.

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\ \text{Root} \end{cases} } \]
Primary concern Mathematical participation across distinct structures without unlawful identification of their native mathematics.
Native validity The correctness of mathematics inside a Branch is determined by the mathematics of that Branch, not by BM.
Meta-structural role BM governs representation, transformation, interaction, composition, interfaces, realization, export, re-entry, distinction preservation, and Root participation.
Non-universality BM does not require every mathematical problem to use BM machinery. Structures may remain entirely native when BM adds no leverage.
Abilities
  • Keep mathematically distinct structures explicitly distinct.
  • Specify lawful interfaces among those structures.
  • Audit composition and transformation.
  • Track information required by downstream mathematics.
  • Represent export, realization, re-entry, and Root participation.
  • Organize multi-Branch mathematical computation.
Limits
  • BM does not make an invalid native theorem valid.
  • BM does not prove physical realizability.
  • BM does not automatically authorize every cross-domain interaction.
  • BM does not replace algebra, analysis, topology, geometry, probability, category theory, or physics.
  • BM does not establish novelty merely by reorganizing known mathematics.

6. GURF

GURF
Status: canonical response-specialized framework under BM governance

GURF is invoked when a mathematical problem has genuine response semantics.

Its central question is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which distinctions in the underlying state are necessary for the required response or future task?} } \]

Typical GURF objects include system states, observations, tasks, interventions, constraints, regimes, continuation requirements, obstructions, repairs, and responses.

Abilities
  • Audit task sufficiency.
  • Detect information loss relevant to future response.
  • Represent observation and intervention order.
  • Track regime dependence.
  • Identify obstruction witnesses.
  • Construct and compare repairs.
  • Separate static task sufficiency from dynamic closure.
Limits
  • GURF is not required when response semantics are absent.
  • Task sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • Observational equivalence does not imply physical identity.
  • Information loss does not automatically imply causal influence.
  • Deterministic formulas do not automatically extend to stochastic, relational, or quantum sectors.

7. Branch

Branch
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A useful organizational form is

\[ B = \left( X_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, P_B \right), \]

where:

  • \(X_B\) is the carrier or state space;
  • \(\mathcal L_B\) records laws, constraints, invariants, or structural requirements;
  • \(\mathcal O_B\) records admissible operations;
  • \(P_B\) records Passport or interface information when needed.
The four-component record is a working organizational formalization. It is not a theorem that every mathematical Branch intrinsically consists of exactly four fields.

Characteristics

  • A Branch has a mathematical identity independent of its later interactions.
  • A Branch retains the native laws required by its mathematical sector.
  • A Branch carries distinctions recognized by its native mathematics.
  • A Branch may be finite, infinite, discrete, continuous, deterministic, stochastic, algebraic, geometric, computational, or otherwise typed.
\[ \operatorname{type}(B), \qquad \operatorname{carrier}(B), \qquad \operatorname{Law}(B), \qquad \operatorname{Op}(B) \]
Abilities
  • Participate in an admissible Interaction.
  • Supply information to a Leaf extraction.
  • Participate in a Tree.
  • Receive compatible exported or Root-derived information.
  • Support native operations and native transformations.
Limits

The existence of two Branches does not imply the existence of a lawful Interaction between them:

\[ B_1,B_2 \not\Rightarrow \exists I(B_1,B_2). \]

A proposed interaction domain may be empty:

\[ D_I=\varnothing. \]

Declaring an object to be a Branch does not establish existence, consistency, realizability, physical truth, or novelty.

Sector dependence. A Branch may naturally be a set, algebra, manifold, measurable space, Hilbert space, graph, category, operator system, PDE state space, function space, computational state space, or another mathematical structure. Native mathematical validity remains controlled by that sector.

8. Branch Information

Branch Information
Status: canonical interpretation

Branch information is mathematical information carried by a Branch, including distinctions its native mathematics treats as meaningful.

BM does not treat information as detached from structure.

\[ x\neq_B x' \] indicates that the Branch distinguishes \(x\) and \(x'\) in a manner relevant to its mathematical structure.
Two underlying states can be different while a particular representation fails to distinguish them. Conversely, not every microscopic distinction must remain relevant to every downstream task.

9. Interaction

Interaction
Status: canonical BM structural role

An Interaction is a lawful typed mathematical action involving one or more Branches, with multi-Branch interaction being the central case.

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1} \times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

The admissible domain

\[ D_I \]

contains only combinations for which the Interaction is mathematically defined.

Characteristics

  • Typed input Branches.
  • An explicit admissible domain.
  • A mathematically defined output type.
  • Compatibility with required native laws.
  • Possible dependence on order, regime, context, or interface data.
Abilities
  • Combine information from multiple Branches.
  • Generate an intermediate result.
  • Expose Leaves.
  • Participate in sequential or networked Trees.
  • Transform distinctions when explicitly lawful.
Limits
  • Formal writability does not imply admissibility.
  • An Interaction need not be commutative.
  • An Interaction need not be associative.
  • An Interaction need not be invertible.
  • An Interaction may lose information.
  • An Interaction may fail to exist for some or all candidate inputs.
In deterministic Set-like sectors an Interaction may be represented by a function. In stochastic sectors it may instead require a Markov kernel. In nondeterministic sectors a relation may be appropriate. Quantum sectors generally require completely positive maps, instruments, or other sector-correct morphisms.

10. Leaf

Leaf
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Leaf is extracted information from an Interaction result.

If

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j \]

is a Leaf-extraction map, then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right) \in \mathcal L_j. } \]

Characteristics

  • The Leaf is derived from an Interaction.
  • The Leaf need not contain the full Interaction result.
  • Several distinct Leaves may be extracted from one Interaction.
  • A Leaf may itself become relevant to later Branches or Passports.
A Leaf is not automatically a Root. Extraction alone does not establish downstream admissibility.

11. Tree

Tree
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches, Interactions, interfaces, and intermediate information through which combined computation is performed and a Response is generated.

The word Tree is a BM structural term. A formal realization need not be a literal graph-theoretic tree.

A sequential special case may admit

\[ \mathcal R_T = I_m \circ I_{m-1} \circ \cdots \circ I_1. \]

More general Trees may require parallel composition, products, pullbacks, equalizers, limits, hypergraphs, DAGs, recursive structures, coupled systems, or other mathematical constructions.

Abilities
  • Coordinate multiple Branches.
  • Support sequential and parallel computation.
  • Carry intermediate Leaves and Passports.
  • Generate a Tree-level Response.
  • Support downstream Root formation when admissibility survives.
A Tree is not the same object as its Response. The Tree organizes the computation; the Response is the resulting mathematical output.

12. Response

Response
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Response is the typed mathematical output produced by a Tree or another organized BM computation.

\[ \mathcal R_T: D_T \longrightarrow Y_T. \] \[ r = \mathcal R_T(x). \]

A Response may be a value, state, law, relation, operator, classification, structured object, family of objects, or other mathematically typed result.

A Response may terminate the computation or, when downstream admissibility exists, assume the role of a Root.
\[ \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal mathematical result},\\[4pt] \text{Root} \end{cases} \]
Producing a Response does not imply that the Response is admissible as input to any later structure.

13. Root

Root
Status: canonical role; extended Rooting machinery remains active research

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Required characteristics

  • The originating result exists.
  • A receiving mathematical structure exists.
  • The receiving structure accepts the Root's type or a lawful transformation of it.
  • Required Passport information remains available.
  • Native laws and constraints are satisfied.
Abilities
  • Serve as input to another Interaction.
  • Re-enter a compatible Branch.
  • Carry Passport information.
  • Participate in repeated Rooting when an iterative structure actually exists.
  • Not every Response is a Root.
  • Root status does not imply invertibility.
  • Root status does not imply conservation.
  • Root status does not imply recursive behavior.
  • A Root cannot be inserted into an incompatible receiving structure merely by declaration.

14. Rooting

Rooting
Status: active candidate structural definition

Rooting is the proposed lawful attachment of a Root, compatible Branch, or compatible law-bearing structure to a mathematical attachment site from which further lawful development may occur.

\[ \mathcal A: D_{\mathcal A} \subseteq X \times X_R \longrightarrow X'. \]

An attachment is lawful only for

\[ (x,r)\in D_{\mathcal A}. \]
Rooting may create a new continuation point, activate a new Branch, transfer compatible Passport information, or initiate another structured computation.
  • Rooting is not arbitrary insertion.
  • Repeated Rooting is not automatically an RG flow.
  • Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.
  • A Root attachment may fail through type, law, interface, or Passport incompatibility.

15. Rebranching

Rebranching
Status: active candidate BM structure

Rebranching is the generation of new lawful Branch development from a site created or activated by Rooting.

Rooting does not guarantee Rebranching. A valid attachment may still terminate if no admissible downstream development exists.

16. Passport

Passport
Status: canonical active BM/GURF structure

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, law context, admissibility requirements, interface requirements, or task-relevant information that must remain available for later mathematical use.

A useful working form is

\[ P = \left( X, \mathcal C, \{b_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C}, \mathcal A \right), \]

where:

  • \(X\) is the underlying state space;
  • \(\mathcal C\) indexes required Passport components;
  • \(b_c:X\to Y_c\) records a required observation or distinction;
  • \(\mathcal A\) records admissibility or interface requirements.

Characteristics

  • A Passport is requirement-relative.
  • Different tasks may induce different Passports on the same state space.
  • A Passport may preserve only selected distinctions.
  • A Passport can be refined or coarsened.
Abilities
  • Track distinctions needed downstream.
  • Define an observational equivalence.
  • Support sufficiency audits.
  • Support interface compatibility tests.
  • Track regime or conservation information when independently justified.
  • A Passport does not replace the native mathematics of a Branch.
  • Passport equivalence does not automatically define a lawful algebraic quotient.
  • Passport equality does not imply physical identity.
  • A Passport does not create conservation laws.
  • An exact equivalence Passport does not automatically represent approximate or privacy semantics.

17. Passport Observation

Passport Observation
Status: formal construction

A Passport observation is a typed map

\[ b_c: X \longrightarrow Y_c \]

recording one required observable, distinction, invariant, diagnostic, law index, or interface quantity.

The term observation does not require a physical measurement. It may be an abstract mathematical map.

18. Combined Passport Map

Combined Passport Map
Status: exact construction when the required product exists
\[ B_P: X \longrightarrow \prod_{c\in\mathcal C}Y_c, \] \[ \boxed{ B_P(x) = (b_c(x))_{c\in\mathcal C}. } \]

The map collects all active Passport observations into one joint representation.

For infinite families, existence and usefulness of the product must be interpreted in the relevant mathematical category. A set-level Cartesian product cannot be silently substituted for a categorical, measurable, topological, or analytic product when additional structure matters.

19. Passport Equivalence

Passport Equivalence
Status: exact equivalence relation in deterministic equality-based sectors
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_P x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_P(x)=B_P(x'). } \]

Equivalently,

\[ x\sim_P x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_c(x)=b_c(x') \quad \forall c\in\mathcal C. \]

Characteristics

  • Reflexive.
  • Symmetric.
  • Transitive.
  • Relative to the active Passport observations.
Passport equivalence is observational equality relative to the Passport. It need not be equality in \(X\), physical identity, gauge identity, causal identity, algebraic congruence, or approximate indistinguishability.

20. Fibre

Fibre
Status: standard imported mathematical definition

For a map

\[ F:X\to Y \]

and \(y\in Y\), the fibre over \(y\) is

\[ \boxed{ F^{-1}(y) = \{x\in X:F(x)=y\}. } \]

A fibre contains all states represented by the same output value.

21. Completion Fibre

Completion Fibre
Status: canonical GURF construction

For observations

\[ r_\alpha:X\to Y_\alpha \]

and observed values

\[ \ell=(\ell_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}, \]

the completion fibre is

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

It represents all underlying states simultaneously compatible with the active observations.

Completion fibres support compatibility, ambiguity, task sufficiency, reconstruction, and obstruction analysis.
A nonempty completion fibre need not contain a unique state. An empty fibre indicates inconsistency between the asserted observation values and the model.

22. Task

Task
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Task specifies the quantity, classification, decision, prediction, control action, continuation, or response that must be determined.

In the deterministic case:

\[ T: X \longrightarrow W. \]

Characteristics

  • A Task determines which state distinctions matter.
  • The same observation may be sufficient for one Task and insufficient for another.
  • A Task can be static or future-dependent.
Task dependence is essential. There is generally no requirement that one representation preserve every distinction in the original state space.

23. Exact Sufficiency

Exact Information Sufficiency
Status: exact factorization concept

For

\[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W, \]

the observation \(r\) is exactly sufficient for \(T\) when there exists

\[ \widetilde T:r(X)\to W \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ T = \widetilde T \circ r. } \]

Equivalently in Set:

\[ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]
Exact sufficiency guarantees that the Task can be evaluated using the retained representation without recovering the full original state.
  • Exact sufficiency for \(T\) does not imply sufficiency for another task \(S\).
  • Static sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • The deterministic Set criterion does not automatically extend unchanged to stochastic or quantum sectors.

24. Task Equivalence

Task Equivalence
Status: exact relation
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_T x' \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Task-equivalent states may differ in many other respects while remaining indistinguishable to the selected Task.

25. Sufficiency by Equivalence Inclusion

Sufficiency Relation
Status: exact equivalent characterization in deterministic Set-like semantics
\[ \boxed{ \sim_r \subseteq \sim_T. } \]

Every distinction merged by the representation must also be irrelevant to the Task.

26. Approximate Sufficiency

Approximate Information Sufficiency
Status: metric-dependent working concept

Suppose \(W\) carries a metric \(d_W\). A representation is \(\varepsilon\)-sufficient for task \(T\) when task variation inside every representation fibre is bounded by \(\varepsilon\).

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow d_W(T(x),T(x')) \le \varepsilon. } \]
Approximate sufficiency is meaningless until the error structure, metric, divergence, norm, probability criterion, or operational tolerance has been specified.

27. Observation

Observation
Status: canonical GURF role

An Observation is a mathematical representation of accessible system information.

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Observation may represent measurement, projection, compression, filtering, discretization, coarse-graining, feature extraction, or another information-producing transformation.

An Observation need not be injective, invertible, lossless, or physically measured.

28. Representation

Representation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Representation is a mathematically typed encoding of an object, state, law, or structure in another mathematical space.

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Characteristics

  • May be exact or lossy.
  • May be injective or many-to-one.
  • May preserve some structures while discarding others.
  • May be task-specific.
A map between sets does not automatically preserve algebraic, topological, measurable, geometric, symplectic, probabilistic, or quantum structure. Such preservation requires sector-specific conditions.

29. Transformation

Transformation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Transformation is a lawful map, operator, kernel, relation, or sector-correct morphism that changes one mathematical state or representation into another.

\[ F: D_F \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y. \]
Transformations may change representation, reduce information, transport structure, perform evolution, implement interfaces, or support export and re-entry.
A Transformation is lawful only relative to explicit domain, codomain, structural, and admissibility requirements.

30. Operation

Operation
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Operation is a typed admissible mathematical transformation used inside a BM construction.

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

Distinction from Interaction

Operation is the broader transformation concept. Interaction emphasizes lawful joint participation of Branches.

An Operation need not be total, associative, commutative, invertible, structure-preserving, or composable with every other Operation.

31. Admissibility

Admissibility
Status: canonical BM/GURF requirement

Admissibility means that a proposed state, object, operation, interaction, attachment, path, interface, or realization satisfies every condition required for lawful participation in the current construction.

\[ A \subseteq X, \qquad x\text{ admissible} \Longleftrightarrow x\in A. \]

More generally an admissibility predicate may be written

\[ \operatorname{Adm} (o;x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in \{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}\}. \]
Admissibility is contextual. An object may be admissible in one Branch, regime, or interface and inadmissible in another.

32. Interface

Interface
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Interface is a typed mathematical structure governing how information, state, laws, or distinctions pass between Branches, representations, or operations.

One exact compatibility form is

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

Characteristics

  • Source typing.
  • Target typing.
  • Compatibility requirements.
  • Required Passport preservation.
  • Possible transformation or conversion law.
Approximate compatibility requires an explicitly defined metric, norm, divergence, topology, probability law, or operational criterion. The symbol \(\approx\) has no universal meaning.

33. Composition

Composition
Status: canonical BM operation

For

\[ F:X\to Y, \qquad G:Y\to Z, \]

ordinary map composition is

\[ \boxed{ G\circ F: X\to Z. } \]

BM additionally audits whether intermediate information and structure remain admissible for downstream mathematics.

  • Matching set-level codomains is not always sufficient for structured composition.
  • Partial maps require domain compatibility.
  • Stochastic composition requires kernel composition.
  • Relational composition has different semantics.
  • Infinite composition requires appropriate limits, convergence, or other structure.

34. Configuration

Configuration
Status: active BM structural object

A Configuration records the active mathematical state of a BM construction.

\[ \mathcal X = \left( \mathcal B, \mathcal I, \mathcal L, \mathcal R, \mathcal P, \mathcal O, \mathcal A \right). \]

Components may record active Branches, Interactions, Leaves, Responses or Roots, Passports, Operations, and admissibility data.

This record is organizational. A concrete sector may require additional structure or omit fields that are irrelevant.

35. Network

BM Network
Status: active BM organizational structure

A Network is a typed collection of Branches, Interactions, interfaces, paths, and dependencies whose lawful connectivity need not be representable by one linear sequence.

Depending on the problem, a Network may be represented by a graph, DAG, hypergraph, category, dependency system, or another structured object.

The term Network does not require a particular graph-theoretic formalism.

36. Typed Path

Typed Path
Status: active BM concept

A Typed Path is a sequence of composable mathematical transformations whose domain, codomain, and admissibility requirements match at every stage.

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{F_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{F_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{F_n} X_n. \]
One invalid interface breaks the legality of the entire path.

37. Path Coherence

Path Coherence
Status: active BM concept

Path coherence requires two lawful paths intended to represent the same mathematical construction to agree according to an explicitly specified comparison relation.

\[ F_{\mathrm{path\,1}}(x) \sim F_{\mathrm{path\,2}}(x). \]
Equality, isomorphism, equivalence, gauge equivalence, approximate equality, and observational equivalence are not interchangeable. The intended comparison must be stated.

38. Higher Coherence

Higher Coherence
Status: active formal development

Higher coherence concerns compatibility among families of paths, transformations, interfaces, associators, rebracketings, or other compositions beyond pairwise agreement.

Pairwise commuting diagrams do not automatically establish all higher-order compatibility conditions.

39. Quotient

Quotient
Status: standard imported mathematical construction

Given an equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(X\), the set quotient is

\[ X/{\sim} = \{[x]:x\in X\}, \] where \[ [x] = \{x'\in X:x'\sim x\}. \]
A set quotient exists at the level of equivalence classes, but additional algebraic, topological, smooth, measurable, geometric, or quantum structure does not automatically descend.

40. Structured Quotient

Structured Quotient
Status: canonical BM concept; sector-specific realization

A Structured Quotient is a quotient in which the operations or structure intended to survive the quotient are well-defined on equivalence classes.

In a finitary algebraic sector, for an \(n\)-ary operation

\[ \sigma:X^n\to X, \]

compatibility requires

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim y_i \quad\forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]
Observational equivalence alone is insufficient. The equivalence must be compatible with the structure being passed to the quotient.

41. Congruence

Congruence
Status: standard imported algebraic concept

A congruence is an equivalence relation compatible with the operations of an algebra.

In BM-SQ-01, congruence compatibility is what allows the relevant finitary operations to descend to Passport equivalence classes.

Congruence is an algebraic notion. Other sectors require their own compatibility structures.

42. Information Refinement

Refinement
Status: exact relational concept

Representation \(P\) is at least as informative as representation \(Q\) when \(P\) merges no pair of states that \(Q\) distinguishes.

\[ \boxed{ \sim_P \subseteq \sim_Q. } \]

Finer representations correspond to smaller equivalence classes.

This exact equivalence ordering does not directly represent differential privacy, noisy observations, or approximate distinguishability. Those require divergences, metrics, orders, or probabilistic structures.

43. Realization

Realization
Status: canonical active BM concept

Realization is the construction or mapping of an abstract specification into a mathematical or physical object satisfying the requirements of the specification and the receiving native theory.

A simplified form is

\[ \rho: S \longrightarrow X. \]

Required conditions

  • The specification \(S\) is mathematically meaningful.
  • The receiving space \(X\) is defined.
  • The required laws and constraints are stated.
  • The realized object satisfies those requirements.
Realization connects abstract BM structure to concrete mathematical objects and, when additional empirical requirements are met, physical implementations.
  • Specification does not imply realization.
  • Mathematical realization does not imply physical realization.
  • Existence does not imply uniqueness.
  • Existence does not imply constructive or computational accessibility.

44. Export

Export
Status: canonical active BM concept

Export is the lawful transfer of a mathematical object or selected information from one structure into another representation or interface.

\[ E: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Characteristics

  • Export may be lossless or lossy.
  • Export may change representation type.
  • Export may preserve only Passport-required information.
  • Export may prepare a Response for downstream re-entry.
Export into \(Y\) does not imply that the exported result may be legally inserted into every structure using \(Y\).

45. Re-entry

Re-entry
Status: canonical active BM concept

Re-entry is the lawful introduction of exported, transformed, or Root-derived information into a receiving Branch or other mathematical structure.

\[ R_{\mathrm{in}}: D_R \subseteq Y \longrightarrow X'. \]

The receiving mathematics determines the admissible domain \(D_R\).

Successful export does not guarantee successful re-entry.

46. Gluing

Gluing
Status: standard mathematical idea used within BM; sector-dependent

Gluing constructs a larger mathematical object from compatible local objects.

A familiar exact compatibility condition is

\[ s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} = s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}. \]
  • Pairwise agreement does not always guarantee global existence.
  • Uniqueness is sector-dependent.
  • Partition-of-unity methods require appropriate topological hypotheses.
  • Nonabelian or nonlinear obstruction theories require their own machinery.
  • BM does not assert one universal obstruction tower for every gluing problem.

47. Continuation

Continuation
Status: canonical GURF concept

Continuation asks whether currently retained information is sufficient for a required future Task.

For evolution

\[ \Phi_t:X\to X \]

and future Task \(T\), continuation through observation \(r\) requires factorization of

\[ T\circ\Phi_t \]

through \(r\).

\[ \exists \widetilde T_t \quad \text{such that} \quad T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t\circ r. \]
Present-time sufficiency does not imply future sufficiency.

48. Obstruction

Obstruction
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Obstruction is a mathematical failure of retained information to determine a required Task or Response.

In the exact deterministic setting, an explicit obstruction witness is a pair \(x,x'\in X\) satisfying

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \quad\text{but}\quad T(x)\neq T(x'). } \]
An obstruction witness proves that no deterministic function of the retained value \(r(x)\) alone can reproduce \(T(x)\) exactly.
Different sectors require different obstruction notions. The pair witness above is not a universal stochastic, quantum, approximate, or nondeterministic obstruction definition.

49. Repair

Repair
Status: canonical GURF concept

Repair adds, restores, or exposes enough information to eliminate a specified obstruction.

If

\[ r:X\to Y \]

is insufficient and

\[ q:X\to Z \]

supplies additional information, the repaired representation is

\[ (r,q): X \longrightarrow Y\times Z. \]

Repair succeeds for \(T\) when

\[ T = \widehat T \circ (r,q) \]

for some lawful \(\widehat T\).

  • A repair is Task-relative.
  • A repair may be insufficient for another Task.
  • Adding more data is not automatically minimal or useful.
  • Further deterministic forgetting cannot restore distinctions already absent from the current representation.

50. Minimal Repair

Minimal Repair
Status: optimization concept

Minimal Repair seeks a least-cost augmentation that restores a specified sufficiency condition.

\[ \inf_q \operatorname{Cost}(q) \] subject to \[ T = \widehat T \circ (r,q). \]
A minimum need not exist; only an infimum may exist. Minimality also depends entirely on the chosen cost functional and admissible repair class.

51. Regime

Regime
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Regime identifies the active law context under which a system evolves or responds.

\[ \lambda \in \Lambda. \]

A regime-dependent evolution may be

\[ \Phi_t^{(\lambda)}: X \longrightarrow X. \]
Two states with the same state coordinates but different active regimes need not have the same future behavior.

52. Law Passport

Law Passport
Status: active GURF/BM structure

A Law Passport records distinctions concerning the active law, regime, parameter family, or rule context required for correct interpretation or continuation.

\[ (x,\lambda) \in X\times\Lambda. \]
Omitting \(\lambda\) is lawful only when the downstream Task does not depend on the regime or when the regime can be recovered from retained information.

53. Intervention

Intervention
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Intervention is an external control, action, modification, or operation capable of changing state, evolution, admissibility, regime, or required response.

For control space \(U\), a controlled system may have

\[ \Phi_t: X\times U \longrightarrow X. \]
Observation and intervention must not be silently identified. Observing a system and changing a system are mathematically distinct operations unless the native model explicitly couples them.

54. Constraint

Constraint
Status: standard imported concept used canonically

A Constraint restricts admissible states, parameters, operations, trajectories, laws, or interactions.

\[ K \subseteq X, \qquad x\text{ admissible} \Rightarrow x\in K. \]

Constraints may also be expressed through equations, inequalities, logical predicates, conservation laws, boundary conditions, gauge conditions, feasibility conditions, or variational restrictions.

55. Deterministic Semantics

Deterministic Semantics
Status: standard mathematical semantics

Deterministic behavior assigns one output to every admissible input.

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y. \]
Deterministic factorization theorems cannot automatically be transported into stochastic or nondeterministic semantics by replacing functions with probability distributions or relations.

56. Stochastic Semantics

Stochastic Semantics
Status: standard mathematical semantics with BM/GURF firewall

Stochastic behavior is represented by a probability kernel rather than a deterministic function.

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

For an appropriate test function

\[ \varphi:Y\to\mathbb R, \]

the induced expectation operator is

\[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E[ \varphi(Y)\mid X=x ]. \]

Characteristics

  • Outputs are probability laws.
  • Measurability is part of the lawful structure.
  • Expectation-level equality need not imply equality of kernels.
  • Marginal laws do not determine joint laws.
Pointwise choices of couplings are not automatically measurable parameterized kernels. Any stochastic Interaction requiring a coupling must define a lawful measurable coupling family.

57. Nondeterministic Semantics

Nondeterministic Semantics
Status: standard relational semantics

Nondeterministic behavior permits multiple admissible outputs.

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

We write

\[ xRy \]

when \(y\) is an admissible outcome from \(x\).

Existential and universal quantifiers must remain distinct. The existence of one successful continuation does not establish that every continuation succeeds.

58. Existential Reachability

Existential Reachability
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Diamond_R(U) = \{ x: \exists y\in U \text{ such that }xRy \}. } \]

It asks whether at least one admissible outcome reaches \(U\).

59. Universal Safety

Universal Safety
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Box_R(U) = \{ x: \forall y, \ xRy \Rightarrow y\in U \}. } \]

It asks whether every admissible outcome remains inside \(U\).

\[ \Diamond_R(U) \neq \Box_R(U) \] in general.

60. Joint Feasibility

Joint Feasibility
Status: exact feasibility concept

A family of conditions with feasible sets \(P_c\subseteq X\) is jointly feasible when there exists one common witness satisfying all conditions.

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} P_c \neq \varnothing. } \]
Separate statements \[ P_c\neq\varnothing \] for every \(c\) do not imply joint feasibility.

61. Coupling

Coupling
Status: standard imported probability concept used in GURF

A Coupling is a joint probability law having specified marginals.

For probability measures \(\mu\) and \(\nu\),

\[ \gamma \in \Gamma(\mu,\nu) \]

denotes a coupling with marginals \(\mu\) and \(\nu\).

  • Marginals do not uniquely determine a coupling.
  • Separate feasibility does not determine a task-appropriate coupling.
  • A parameterized coupling must satisfy required measurability or regularity conditions.

62. Conservative Passport

Conservative Passport
Status: active candidate research structure

A Conservative Passport tracks a quantity that the native mathematics independently requires to persist across a transformation even when the active representation does not retain that quantity explicitly.

A Conservative Passport records conservation. It does not create or prove the conservation law.

63. Root Passport Transfer

Root Passport Transfer
Status: active candidate BM structure

Root Passport Transfer is the proposed typed persistence of required Passport information from a source structure into a Root and onward into a compatible receiving structure.

\[ P_{\mathrm{source}} \longrightarrow P_R \longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{receiver}}. \]
Passport transfer requires actual maps, compatibility conditions, and preservation claims. The arrow notation alone does not establish a lawful transfer.

64. Seed

Seed
Status: candidate BM primitive under active development

A Seed is information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes a lawful generative or self-seeding process until a specified export condition is reached.

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \] followed, when an export condition is satisfied, by \[ S_n \longrightarrow R. \]

Characteristics

  • Not merely initial data.
  • Generative behavior is part of the candidate concept.
  • Root export is conditional rather than immediate.
  • Generation may be iterative, recursive, branching, stochastic, or otherwise structured if explicitly defined.
The Seed vocabulary does not imply biological behavior or personification.

65. Seed State

Seed State
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed State is an intermediate state produced during a lawful Seed process before Root export.

\[ S_n \in \mathcal S. \]
A Seed State is not automatically a Branch, Leaf, Response, or Root. Those roles require their own conditions.

66. Seed Export Condition

Seed Export Condition
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed Export Condition is the criterion determining when a Seed process is permitted to generate or export a Root.

\[ E: \mathcal S \longrightarrow \{0,1\}, \] with export allowed when \[ E(S_n)=1. \]
The criterion must be specified by the model or mathematics. BM does not supply one universal Seed export condition.

67. Dynamic Closure

Dynamic Closure
Status: exact deterministic factorization concept

Let

\[ R:X\to Z \]

be a reduced representation and

\[ \Phi:X\to X \]

an exact deterministic evolution.

The representation is dynamically closed when there exists

\[ \widetilde\Phi:R(X)\to R(X) \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

Equivalently,

\[ R(x)=R(x') \Longrightarrow R(\Phi(x)) = R(\Phi(x')). \]
Exact closure permits autonomous deterministic evolution on the reduced representation.
  • Task sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • Failure of closure may produce memory or hidden-state dependence.
  • Stochastic closure requires a stochastic/lumpability formulation.
  • Quantum closure requires channel-correct semantics.
  • Approximate closure requires a defined approximation criterion.

68. Response Ambiguity Set

Response Ambiguity Set
Status: useful GURF construction

For

\[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W, \]

define

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A_T(y) = \{ T(x): x\in r^{-1}(y) \}. } \]

It records every Task output still compatible with the observation \(y\).

Exact sufficiency corresponds to

\[ |\mathcal A_T(y)| \le 1 \]

for every realized observation value.

69. Task Diameter

Task Diameter
Status: quantitative GURF diagnostic

If \(W\) carries metric \(d_W\), define

\[ \boxed{ D_{r,T}(y) = \sup_{x,x'\in r^{-1}(y)} d_W(T(x),T(x')). } \]

Exact sufficiency implies

\[ D_{r,T}(y)=0. \]
The diagnostic depends on the chosen metric and may be infinite. If the fibre is empty, a convention must be stated rather than inferred silently.

70. Interface Defect

Interface Defect
Status: quantitative diagnostic

Suppose exact interface compatibility would require

\[ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. \]

If the common target carries metric \(d\), a pointwise defect may be defined by

\[ \boxed{ \Delta(x) = d \left( P_2(F(x)), G(P_1(x)) \right). } \]

Exact compatibility corresponds to

\[ \Delta(x)=0. \]
A scalar defect is meaningful only after a mathematically justified comparison structure has been chosen.

71. Law Space

Law Space
Status: active BM organizational concept

A Law Space is a mathematical space or indexed family representing laws, operators, parameterized rule sets, regimes, constraints, or other governing structures relevant to a BM construction.

\[ \lambda \in \mathcal L. \]
The structure of \(\mathcal L\) is native to the problem. It may be discrete, topological, geometric, algebraic, functional, probabilistic, operator-valued, or otherwise structured.
Calling something a Law Space does not imply that interpolation, differentiation, probability, topology, or a metric exists on that space.

72. Conserved Quantity

Conservation
Status: native-domain mathematical concept

For evolution

\[ \Phi_t:X\to X, \]

a quantity

\[ C:X\to V \]

is conserved when

\[ \boxed{ C(\Phi_t(x)) = C(x) } \]

throughout the stated evolution domain.

BM may preserve, transport, or audit an independently established conservation law. BM does not independently create energy, momentum, charge, probability, or another conserved quantity.

73. Mathematical Realizability

Mathematical Realizability
Status: canonical BM distinction

A formal specification is mathematically realizable when at least one mathematical object exists satisfying all stated requirements.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in X \text{ satisfying every required condition.} } \]

Possible failure modes

  • Contradictory constraints.
  • Empty admissible domain.
  • Unsatisfied boundary or regularity conditions.
  • Incompatible structural requirements.
  • Nonexistence established by theorem.
Mathematical existence does not imply efficient construction, uniqueness, stability, computability, or physical realizability.

74. Physical Realizability

Physical Realizability
Status: application-level requirement

A mathematical structure is physically realizable only when a physical system can satisfy the relevant physical laws, parameters, constraints, initial conditions, boundary conditions, material limitations, causal requirements, and implementation conditions.

Mathematical consistency alone does not establish physical realizability.
Physical realizability is empirical and theory-dependent. BM can organize the realization question but cannot replace experiment, engineering constraints, or validated physical law.

75. Internal Framework Status

Internally Established Architecture
Status: project governance term

This status indicates that a definition or architecture has been accepted for current use inside the BM/GURF research program.

Internal establishment does not imply independent validation, publication acceptance, priority, novelty, or universal mathematical usefulness.

76. External Novelty

External Novelty
Status: separate research-history question

External novelty concerns whether a definition, theorem, method, framework, or integrated construction was previously known in an equivalent or materially equivalent form.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical correctness} \neq \text{novelty}. } \]
Direct derivation does not by itself establish historical priority.

77. Independent External Validation

Independent External Validation
Status: separate from internal derivation

Independent validation means review, reproduction, verification, criticism, or acceptance by mathematically competent parties outside the internal development process.

Independent validation is evidence concerning reliability or acceptance. It does not replace proof where proof is required.

78. Definition Versus Theorem

Definitions establish terminology.

Theorems establish mathematical consequences.

A definition cannot prove its own existence, usefulness, consistency with another structure, realizability, or physical truth.

79. Definition Versus Model

A model assigns specific laws, maps, parameters, equations, or numerical values to defined mathematical roles.

For example,

\[ R_{n+1} = \frac12R_n+1 \]

is one possible iterative model involving repeated Root-valued states.

It is not the definition of Root.

80. Definition Versus Metaphor

BM vocabulary such as Branch, Leaf, Tree, Root, and Seed has formal structural meaning inside the framework.

These words should not be interpreted as biological agents, intentions, consciousness, growth drives, or other personified mechanisms.

\[ \boxed{ \text{formal definition governs usage}. } \]

81. Sector-Semantics Firewall

Sector-Semantics Firewall
Status: canonical rigor rule

A theorem or definition stated in one mathematical semantic sector may be transported into another sector only after the required mathematical structures and hypotheses have been re-established.

Set / deterministic Functions One output per admissible input

Similar notation across sectors does not imply identical mathematics.

82. Tree, Response, and Root Distinction

Structural Role Separation
Status: canonical clarification
\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \xrightarrow{\text{combined computation}} \text{Response} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\ \text{Root if downstream admissible} \end{cases} } \]
Role Meaning
Tree Organized mathematical structure performing or coordinating the computation.
Response Mathematical output produced by that computation.
Root A Response or resulting structure that remains admissible for further mathematical participation.

83. BM and GURF Distinction

Framework Scope Separation
Status: canonical governance clarification
\[ \boxed{ \text{BM} = \text{broader meta-structural mathematics} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{GURF} = \text{response-specialized use when response semantics are essential} } \]

BM governs lawful representation, transformation, interaction, composition, realization, interfaces, export, re-entry, and related structural mathematics.

GURF is invoked when system response, observation, Task sufficiency, intervention, constraint, regime, continuation, or response ambiguity becomes mathematically essential.

BM need not be rewritten as a response theory. GURF need not be invoked merely because BM terminology appears.
