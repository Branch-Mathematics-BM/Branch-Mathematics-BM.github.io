Definitions

Extended canonical and working vocabulary for Branch Mathematics and GURF. Definitions specify formal structure, characteristics, abilities, limits, admissibility requirements, failure modes, sector dependence, framework ownership, and relationships to neighboring mathematical concepts.

1. Purpose of This Registry

The Definitions Registry specifies how technical terms are used throughout the current BM/GURF research corpus.

Definitions are intentionally separated from proofs, models, numerical experiments, applications, conjectures, and novelty claims.

A definition does not prove existence.

Declaring a mathematical object by definition does not establish that an object satisfying the definition exists, is unique, computationally accessible, physically realizable, stable, useful, or historically novel.

2. Definition Specification Standard

Major BM/GURF definitions are mathematical specifications rather than short glossary labels.

Where applicable, a definition should identify:

Identity What the object means inside BM/GURF.
Formal Data Carrier spaces, maps, relations, laws, indices, domains, and types.
Characteristics Properties distinguishing the object from nearby concepts.
Abilities Mathematical roles the object may perform when its hypotheses hold.
Limits Statements that do not follow merely from the definition.
Admissibility Conditions required before lawful participation is established.
Failure Modes Explicit ways a proposed instance can fail.
Sector Scope Dependence on Set, algebra, topology, probability, quantum theory, dynamics, geometry, computation, analysis, or another native field.

5. Branch Mathematics — BM

Branch Mathematics
Status: canonical framework definition

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, operations, interfaces, and native mathematical identities.

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root} \end{cases} } \]

Characteristics

  • Meta-structural rather than domain-replacing.
  • Typed rather than symbolically unrestricted.
  • Admissibility-sensitive.
  • Distinction-preserving where downstream mathematics requires it.
  • Compatible with multiple mathematical sectors.
  • Allows different Branches to retain different native mathematics.
Abilities
  • Organize multiple mathematical structures in one problem.
  • Specify lawful Interfaces.
  • Audit transformations and composition.
  • Track information needed downstream.
  • Represent Trees, Responses, Roots, Networks, and Configurations.
  • Audit realization, export, and re-entry.
  • Support structured quotient and coherence analysis.
Limits
  • BM does not replace native proof.
  • BM does not make an invalid theorem valid.
  • BM does not authorize arbitrary cross-domain interaction.
  • BM does not prove physical realizability.
  • BM does not establish novelty merely by organizing mathematics.
  • BM need not be invoked when it provides no genuine leverage.

6. GURF

GURF
Status: canonical response-specialized framework under BM governance

GURF is the response-specialized framework used when a mathematical problem contains genuine response semantics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which distinctions in the underlying state are necessary for the required response or future task?} } \]

Typical GURF objects include system states, response laws, observations, tasks, interventions, constraints, regimes, continuation requirements, ambiguity sets, obstructions, repairs, and responses.

Abilities
  • Audit task sufficiency.
  • Detect task-relevant information loss.
  • Separate response from observation.
  • Track intervention order.
  • Track regime dependence.
  • Analyze ambiguity and recovery.
  • Identify obstruction witnesses.
  • Construct and compare repairs.
  • Separate static sufficiency from dynamic closure.
Limits
  • GURF is not required when response semantics are absent.
  • GURF does not replace native mathematics.
  • Observation equivalence does not imply physical identity.
  • Task sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • Information loss does not by itself imply causal influence.
  • Deterministic formulas do not automatically extend to other sectors.

7. Branch

Branch
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A useful organizational form is

\[ B= \left( X_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, P_B \right), \]

where:

  • \(X_B\) is the carrier or state space;
  • \(\mathcal L_B\) records laws, constraints, invariants, or native structural requirements;
  • \(\mathcal O_B\) records admissible operations;
  • \(P_B\) records Passport or Interface information when required.

This tuple is an organizational representation rather than a universal theorem that every mathematical object possesses exactly four intrinsic fields.

Characteristics

  • A Branch has a mathematical identity independent of later Interactions.
  • It retains its native laws.
  • It retains distinctions recognized by its native mathematics.
  • It may belong to any mathematically specified sector.
Abilities
  • Participate in admissible Interactions.
  • Supply information to Leaf extraction.
  • Participate in a Tree or Network.
  • Receive compatible exported or Root-derived information.
  • Support native Operations.
Limits

The existence of two Branches does not imply a lawful Interaction:

\[ B_1,B_2 \not\Rightarrow \exists I(B_1,B_2). \]

Declaring an object a Branch does not establish existence, consistency, physical truth, realizability, or novelty.

8. Branch Information

Branch Information
Status: canonical interpretation

Branch Information is mathematical information carried by a Branch, including distinctions that the Branch's native mathematics treats as meaningful.

BM does not treat information as detached from structure.

\[ x\neq_Bx' \]

indicates that Branch \(B\) distinguishes \(x\) and \(x'\) in a mathematically relevant manner.

Limits

Not every distinction in an underlying model must be preserved by every representation or for every downstream task.

9. Interaction

Interaction
Status: canonical BM structural role

An Interaction is a lawful typed mathematical action in which one or more Branches participate, with multi-Branch joint action being the central case.

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

\(D_I\) is the admissible Interaction domain.

Characteristics

  • Typed Branch participation.
  • Explicit admissible domain.
  • Mathematically defined output type.
  • Compatibility with required native laws.
  • Possible dependence on order, regime, context, or Interface data.
Abilities
  • Combine Branch information.
  • Generate intermediate mathematical results.
  • Expose Leaves.
  • Participate in Trees and Networks.
  • Transform distinctions when explicitly lawful.
Limits
  • Formal writability does not imply admissibility.
  • An Interaction need not be commutative.
  • An Interaction need not be associative.
  • An Interaction need not be invertible.
  • An Interaction may lose information.
  • Its admissible domain may be empty.
Sector scope: deterministic Set-like Interactions may be functions; stochastic Interactions may require kernels; nondeterministic Interactions may require relations; quantum Interactions require sector-correct quantum maps or instruments.

10. Leaf

Leaf
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Leaf is information extracted from an Interaction result.

If

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j \]

is a Leaf-extraction map, then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right) \in \mathcal L_j. } \]

Therefore:

  • \(\mathcal L_j\) is the Leaf space;
  • \(\ell_j\) is the extraction map;
  • \(\lambda_j\) is the extracted Leaf value.
Limits
  • A Leaf need not contain the full Interaction result.
  • A Leaf is not automatically sufficient for a later task.
  • A Leaf is not automatically a Root.
  • A quantity should not be called a formal BM Leaf unless its originating Interaction and extraction map are specified.

11. Tree

Tree
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches, Interactions, Interfaces, Leaves, Passports, and intermediate information through which combined computation is performed and a Response is generated.

A sequential special case may admit

\[ \mathcal R_T = I_m\circ I_{m-1}\circ\cdots\circ I_1. \]

More general Trees may require parallel composition, products, pullbacks, equalizers, limits, coupled systems, recursive structures, DAGs, hypergraphs, stochastic kernels, relations, or other native mathematical constructions.

Limits
  • A BM Tree need not be a literal graph-theoretic tree.
  • A Tree is not identical to its Response.
  • A Tree structure does not by itself establish coherence.

12. Response

Response
Status: canonical BM structural role

A Response is the typed mathematical output produced by a Tree or another organized BM computation.

\[ \mathcal R_T: D_T \longrightarrow Y_T, \qquad r=\mathcal R_T(x). \]

A Response may be a value, state, function, law, relation, operator, classification, structured object, family of objects, or another mathematically typed output.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal mathematical result},\\[4pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]
Limits
  • A Response is not restricted to being law-valued.
  • Producing a Response does not establish downstream admissibility.
  • Not every Response becomes a Root.

13. Root

Root
Status: canonical BM role; extended Rooting remains active research

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Required characteristics

  • The originating result exists.
  • A receiving structure exists.
  • The receiving structure accepts its type or a lawful transformation.
  • Required Passport information remains available.
  • Native laws and constraints are satisfied.
Abilities
  • Serve as input to another Interaction.
  • Re-enter a compatible Branch.
  • Carry compatible Passport information.
  • Participate in repeated Rooting when iteration is actually defined.
Limits
  • Root status does not imply invertibility.
  • Root status does not imply conservation.
  • Root status does not imply recursion.
  • Root status does not imply physical realization.
  • Root status is not universal compatibility.

14. Rooting

Rooting
Status: active candidate BM structural definition

Rooting is the proposed lawful attachment of a Root, compatible Branch, compatible law-bearing structure, or other admissible mathematical object to a mathematical attachment site from which further lawful development may occur.

\[ \mathcal A: D_{\mathcal A} \subseteq X\times X_R \longrightarrow X'. \] \[ (x,r)\in D_{\mathcal A} \] is required for lawful attachment.

Characteristics

  • Attachment is typed.
  • The attachment site need not be terminal.
  • Interior attachment is permitted when the native mathematics permits it.
  • Rooting can establish a new continuation site.
Limits
  • Rooting is not arbitrary insertion.
  • Rooting does not guarantee Rebranching.
  • Repeated Rooting is not automatically an RG flow.
  • Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.
  • Attachment may fail through type, law, Interface, or Passport incompatibility.

15. Rebranching

Rebranching
Status: active candidate BM structure

Rebranching is the generation of new lawful Branch development from a site created or activated by Rooting.

Limit: Rooting does not guarantee Rebranching. A lawful attachment may terminate if no admissible downstream construction exists.

16. Passport

Passport
Status: canonical active BM/GURF structure

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, law context, admissibility requirements, Interface requirements, or task-relevant information that must remain available for later mathematical use.

A useful working form is

\[ P= \left( X, \mathcal C, \{b_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C}, \mathcal A \right). \]

Here:

  • \(X\) is the underlying state space;
  • \(\mathcal C\) indexes required components;
  • \(b_c:X\to Y_c\) records a required distinction or observation;
  • \(\mathcal A\) records admissibility or Interface requirements.

Characteristics

  • Requirement-relative.
  • Task-relative where tasks are present.
  • May preserve selected rather than all distinctions.
  • May be refined or coarsened.
Limits
  • A Passport does not replace native mathematics.
  • Passport equivalence does not automatically define a structured quotient.
  • Passport equality does not imply physical identity.
  • A Passport does not create conservation laws.
  • Exact equivalence does not represent every approximate or privacy notion.

17. Passport Observation

Passport Observation
Status: formal construction

A Passport Observation is a typed map

\[ b_c: X \longrightarrow Y_c \]

recording one required observable, distinction, invariant, diagnostic, law index, or Interface quantity.

The term Observation here does not require a physical measurement.

18. Combined Passport Map

Combined Passport Map
Status: exact construction when the required product exists
\[ B_P: X \longrightarrow \prod_{c\in\mathcal C}Y_c, \] \[ \boxed{ B_P(x) = (b_c(x))_{c\in\mathcal C}. } \]

The map collects the active Passport components into a joint representation.

For infinite families, the relevant product must exist in the appropriate mathematical category. A Set-level Cartesian product cannot automatically substitute for measurable, topological, analytic, categorical, or quantum products.

19. Passport Equivalence

Passport Equivalence
Status: exact equivalence relation in deterministic equality-based sectors
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_Px' \Longleftrightarrow B_P(x)=B_P(x'). } \] \[ x\sim_Px' \Longleftrightarrow b_c(x)=b_c(x') \quad \forall c\in\mathcal C. \]

Passport Equivalence is observational equality relative to the active Passport.

It need not be equality in \(X\), physical identity, gauge identity, causal identity, algebraic congruence, or approximate indistinguishability.

20. Fibre

Fibre
Status: standard imported mathematical definition

For

\[ F:X\to Y \]

and \(y\in Y\), the fibre over \(y\) is

\[ \boxed{ F^{-1}(y) = \{x\in X:F(x)=y\}. } \]

The fibre records states represented by the same output value.

21. Completion Fibre

Completion Fibre
Status: canonical GURF construction

For observations

\[ r_\alpha: X \longrightarrow Y_\alpha \]

and observed values

\[ \ell = (\ell_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}, \]

the Completion Fibre is

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

It is the set of underlying states simultaneously compatible with the active observations.

A nonempty Completion Fibre need not contain a unique state. An empty fibre indicates incompatibility between the asserted observations and the model.

22. Task

Task
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Task specifies the quantity, classification, decision, prediction, control action, continuation, inference target, or response that must be determined.

\[ T: X \longrightarrow W. \]

Characteristics

  • A Task determines which distinctions matter.
  • The same observation may support one Task but not another.
  • A Task may be static or future-dependent.
There is generally no requirement that a representation retain every distinction in \(X\), only those required by the declared Task and continuation semantics.

23. Exact Information Sufficiency

Exact Sufficiency
Status: exact deterministic factorization concept

For

\[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W, \]

\(r\) is exactly sufficient for \(T\) when there exists

\[ \widetilde T: r(X) \longrightarrow W \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ T = \widetilde T\circ r. } \]

Equivalently, in deterministic Set semantics,

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]
  • Sufficiency is Task-relative.
  • Static sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • The deterministic criterion does not automatically extend unchanged to stochastic, quantum, or nondeterministic sectors.

24. Task Equivalence

Task Equivalence
Status: exact deterministic relation
\[ \boxed{ x\sim_Tx' \Longleftrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Task-equivalent states may differ in many other respects while remaining indistinguishable for the declared Task.

25. Sufficiency by Equivalence Inclusion

Sufficiency Relation
Status: exact equivalent characterization in deterministic Set semantics
\[ \boxed{ \sim_r \subseteq \sim_T. } \]

Every distinction merged by the representation must also be irrelevant to the Task.

26. Approximate Information Sufficiency

Approximate Sufficiency
Status: metric-dependent working concept

Suppose \(W\) carries a justified metric \(d_W\). A representation is \(\varepsilon\)-sufficient for \(T\) when Task variation inside every representation fibre is bounded.

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow d_W(T(x),T(x')) \le \varepsilon. } \]
Approximate sufficiency is undefined until the error structure, metric, divergence, norm, probability criterion, or operational tolerance has been specified.

27. Observation

Observation
Status: canonical GURF role

An Observation is a mathematical representation of accessible information about a system or response.

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Observation may represent measurement, projection, compression, filtering, discretization, coarse-graining, feature extraction, or another information-producing transformation.

An Observation need not be injective, invertible, lossless, or a literal physical measurement.

28. Representation

Representation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Representation is a mathematically typed encoding of an object, state, law, or structure in another mathematical space.

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Characteristics

  • May be exact or lossy.
  • May be injective or many-to-one.
  • May preserve some structures and discard others.
  • May be task-specific.
A Set map does not automatically preserve algebraic, topological, measurable, geometric, symplectic, probabilistic, or quantum structure.

29. Transformation

Transformation
Status: canonical BM concept

A Transformation is a lawful map, operator, kernel, relation, process, or sector-correct morphism that changes one mathematical state or representation into another.

\[ F: D_F \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y. \]
A Transformation is lawful only relative to explicit domain, codomain, structural, and admissibility requirements.

30. Operation

Operation
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Operation is a typed admissible mathematical transformation used inside a BM construction.

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

Distinction from Interaction

Operation is the broader transformation concept. Interaction emphasizes participation of Branches in a lawful mathematical action.

An Operation need not be total, associative, commutative, invertible, structure-preserving, or composable with every other Operation.

31. Admissibility

Admissibility
Status: canonical BM/GURF requirement

Admissibility means that a proposed state, object, Operation, Interaction, attachment, path, Interface, realization, or continuation satisfies every condition required for lawful participation in the current construction.

\[ A\subseteq X, \qquad x\text{ admissible} \Longleftrightarrow x\in A. \] \[ \operatorname{Adm} (o;x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in \{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}\}. \]
Admissibility is contextual. An object may be admissible in one Branch, regime, or Interface and inadmissible in another.

32. Interface

Interface
Status: canonical active BM structure

An Interface is a typed mathematical structure governing how information, state, laws, or distinctions pass between Branches, representations, Operations, or other structures.

One exact deterministic compatibility condition is

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

Characteristics

  • Source typing.
  • Target typing.
  • Compatibility requirements.
  • Required Passport preservation.
  • Possible conversion or transformation law.
Approximate compatibility requires a declared metric, norm, divergence, topology, probability criterion, or other comparison structure. The symbol \(\approx\) has no universal BM meaning.

33. Composition

Composition
Status: canonical BM operation

For partial deterministic maps

\[ F: D_F\subseteq X \longrightarrow Y, \] \[ G: D_G\subseteq Y \longrightarrow Z, \]

the actual composite domain is

\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

Hence

\[ G\circ F: D_{G\circ F} \longrightarrow Z. \]
  • Matching written codomains is not always sufficient.
  • Structured composition may require additional Interface laws.
  • Stochastic composition uses kernel composition.
  • Relational composition has different semantics.
  • Infinite composition may require limits or convergence.

34. Configuration

Configuration
Status: active BM structural object

A Configuration records the active mathematical state of a BM construction.

One organizational record is

\[ \mathcal X = \left( \mathcal B, \mathcal I, \mathcal L, \mathcal R, \mathcal P, \mathcal O, \mathcal A \right). \]

Components may record active Branches, Interactions, Leaves, Responses or Roots, Passports, Operations, and admissibility data.

This tuple is organizational rather than a universal intrinsic structure for every BM Configuration.

35. Network

BM Network
Status: active BM organizational structure

A Network is a typed collection of Branches, Interactions, Interfaces, Trees, Roots, paths, Operations, and dependencies whose lawful connectivity need not be representable by one Tree or linear sequence.

A Network may be represented by a graph, DAG, hypergraph, category, dependency structure, operator network, or another mathematically appropriate object.

36. Typed Path

Typed Path
Status: active BM concept

A Typed Path is a sequence of mathematically compatible transformations whose domain, codomain, type, and admissibility requirements match at every stage.

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{F_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{F_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{F_n} X_n. \]
One unlawful intermediate Interface invalidates the path as a lawful BM path.

37. Path Coherence

Path Coherence
Status: active BM concept

Path Coherence requires lawful paths intended to represent the same mathematical construction to agree according to an explicitly specified comparison relation.

\[ F_{\mathrm{path\,1}}(x) \approx F_{\mathrm{path\,2}}(x). \]
Equality, isomorphism, natural isomorphism, homotopy, gauge equivalence, equality in distribution, approximate equality, and observational equivalence are not interchangeable.

38. Higher Coherence

Higher Coherence
Status: active formal development

Higher Coherence concerns compatibility among families of paths, transformations, Interfaces, associators, rebracketings, or other compositions beyond pairwise path agreement.

Pairwise commuting diagrams do not automatically establish every higher-order compatibility condition.

39. Quotient

Quotient
Status: standard imported mathematical construction

Given an equivalence relation \(\sim\) on \(X\), the Set-level quotient is

\[ X/{\sim} = \{[x]:x\in X\}, \] \[ [x] = \{x'\in X:x'\sim x\}. \]
Additional algebraic, topological, smooth, measurable, geometric, probabilistic, or quantum structure does not automatically descend to the quotient.

40. Structured Quotient

Structured Quotient
Status: canonical BM concept; sector-specific realization

A Structured Quotient is a quotient in which the operations or structure intended to survive are well-defined on equivalence classes.

In a finitary algebraic sector, for

\[ \sigma:X^n\to X, \]

compatibility requires

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim y_i \quad \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]
Observational equivalence alone is insufficient. The equivalence must be compatible with the structure intended to descend.

41. Congruence

Congruence
Status: standard imported algebraic concept

A Congruence is an equivalence relation compatible with the operations of an algebra.

In BM-SQ-01, congruence compatibility allows relevant finitary algebraic operations to descend to Passport equivalence classes.

Congruence is specifically algebraic; other sectors require their own quotient compatibility structures.

42. Information Refinement

Refinement
Status: exact deterministic equivalence concept

Representation \(P\) is at least as informative as \(Q\) when \(P\) merges no pair of states that \(Q\) distinguishes.

\[ \boxed{ \sim_P \subseteq \sim_Q. } \]

Finer representations therefore have smaller equivalence classes.

This exact ordering does not directly model differential privacy, noisy measurements, approximate distinguishability, or every information order.

43. Realization

Realization
Status: canonical active BM concept

Realization is the construction or mapping of an abstract specification into a mathematical object satisfying the required conditions of the specification and receiving theory.

\[ \rho: S \longrightarrow X. \]

Requirements

  • The specification is mathematically meaningful.
  • The receiving structure is defined.
  • Required laws and constraints are stated.
  • The realized object satisfies those requirements.
  • Specification does not imply existence.
  • Existence does not imply uniqueness.
  • Existence does not imply computability.
  • Mathematical realization does not imply physical realization.

44. Export

Export
Status: canonical active BM concept

Export is the lawful transfer of a mathematical object or selected information from one structure into another representation or Interface.

\[ E: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Characteristics

  • May be lossless or lossy.
  • May change representation type.
  • May preserve only Passport-required information.
  • May prepare a result for downstream re-entry.
Successful export into \(Y\) does not authorize insertion into every mathematical structure that uses \(Y\).

45. Re-entry

Re-entry
Status: canonical active BM concept

Re-entry is the lawful introduction of exported, transformed, Response-derived, or Root-derived information into a receiving Branch or mathematical structure.

\[ R_{\mathrm{in}}: D_R \subseteq Y \longrightarrow X'. \]
The receiving mathematics determines \(D_R\). Successful export does not guarantee successful re-entry.

46. Gluing

Gluing
Status: standard mathematical idea used within BM; sector-dependent

Gluing constructs a larger mathematical object from compatible local objects.

A familiar exact local compatibility condition is

\[ s_i|_{U_i\cap U_j} = s_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}. \]
  • Pairwise agreement need not guarantee global existence.
  • Uniqueness is sector-dependent.
  • Partition-of-unity arguments require appropriate hypotheses.
  • BM does not impose one universal obstruction tower.

47. Continuation

Continuation
Status: canonical GURF concept

Continuation asks whether currently retained information is sufficient for a required future Task.

For evolution

\[ \Phi_t:X\to X \]

and future Task \(T\), continuation through \(r\) requires factorization of

\[ T\circ\Phi_t \]

through the retained representation:

\[ T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t\circ r. \]
Present-time sufficiency does not imply future sufficiency.

48. Obstruction

Obstruction
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Obstruction is a mathematical failure of retained information to determine a required Task or Response.

In exact deterministic semantics, an explicit obstruction witness is a pair \(x,x'\) satisfying

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \quad \text{but} \quad T(x)\neq T(x'). } \]
The pair-witness form is not a universal stochastic, quantum, approximate, or nondeterministic obstruction definition.

49. Repair

Repair
Status: canonical GURF concept

Repair adds, restores, exposes, or changes enough information to eliminate a specified obstruction.

If

\[ r:X\to Y \]

is insufficient and

\[ q:X\to Z \]

adds information, the refined representation is

\[ (r,q): X \longrightarrow Y\times Z. \]

Repair succeeds for \(T\) when

\[ T = \widehat T\circ(r,q) \]

for a lawful \(\widehat T\).

  • Repair is Task-relative.
  • A repair need not be minimal.
  • More information is not automatically useful.
  • Further deterministic forgetting cannot reconstruct a distinction already absent without new information.

50. Minimal Repair

Minimal Repair
Status: optimization concept

Minimal Repair seeks a least-cost augmentation restoring a specified sufficiency condition.

\[ \inf_q \operatorname{Cost}(q) \] subject to \[ T = \widehat T\circ(r,q). \]
A minimum need not exist. Minimality depends entirely on the declared cost functional and admissible repair class.

51. Regime

Regime
Status: canonical GURF concept

A Regime identifies the active law context under which a system evolves or responds.

\[ \lambda \in \Lambda. \] \[ \Phi_t^{(\lambda)}: X \longrightarrow X. \]
Equal state coordinates under different active regimes need not imply equal future behavior.

52. Law Passport

Law Passport
Status: active GURF/BM structure

A Law Passport records distinctions concerning the active law, Regime, parameter family, or rule context required for correct interpretation or continuation.

\[ (x,\lambda) \in X\times\Lambda. \]
Omitting \(\lambda\) is lawful only when the downstream mathematics does not depend on it or when it is recoverable from retained information.

53. Intervention

Intervention
Status: canonical GURF concept

An Intervention is an external control, action, modification, or Operation capable of changing state, evolution, admissibility, Regime, or required response.

\[ \Phi_t: X\times U \longrightarrow X. \]
Observation and Intervention are distinct unless the native model explicitly couples them.

54. Constraint

Constraint
Status: standard imported concept used canonically

A Constraint restricts admissible states, parameters, Operations, trajectories, laws, or Interactions.

\[ K\subseteq X. \]

Constraints may be equations, inequalities, predicates, conservation laws, boundary conditions, gauge conditions, feasibility conditions, variational conditions, or other mathematical restrictions.

55. Deterministic Semantics

Deterministic Semantics
Status: standard mathematical semantics

Deterministic behavior assigns one specified output to every admissible input.

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y. \]
Deterministic factorization theorems cannot automatically be transported to stochastic, nondeterministic, or quantum sectors.

56. Stochastic Semantics

Stochastic Semantics
Status: standard mathematical semantics with BM/GURF firewall

Stochastic behavior is represented by probability kernels or other sector-correct stochastic objects rather than deterministic functions.

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \] \[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E [ \varphi(Y) \mid X=x ]. \]

Characteristics

  • Outputs are probability laws.
  • Measurability is part of lawful structure.
  • Expectation equality need not imply kernel equality.
  • Marginals do not determine a joint law.
Pointwise selections of couplings do not automatically define a measurable parameterized coupling kernel.

57. Nondeterministic Semantics

Nondeterministic Semantics
Status: standard relational semantics

Nondeterministic behavior permits multiple admissible outputs.

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \] \[ xRy \] means \(y\) is an admissible outcome from \(x\).
Existential and universal quantifiers must remain distinct. One successful continuation does not imply that every continuation succeeds.

58. Existential Reachability

Existential Reachability
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Diamond_R(U) = \{ x: \exists y\in U \text{ such that } xRy \}. } \]

It asks whether at least one admissible outcome reaches \(U\).

59. Universal Safety

Universal Safety
Status: exact relational definition
\[ \boxed{ \Box_R(U) = \{ x: \forall y,\, xRy \Rightarrow y\in U \}. } \]

It asks whether every admissible outcome remains inside \(U\).

\[ \Diamond_R(U) \neq \Box_R(U) \]

in general.

60. Joint Feasibility

Joint Feasibility
Status: exact feasibility concept

A family of conditions with feasible sets \(P_c\subseteq X\) is jointly feasible when one common witness satisfies all conditions.

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} P_c \neq \varnothing. } \]
Separate nonemptiness \(P_c\neq\varnothing\) for every \(c\) does not imply joint feasibility.

61. Coupling

Coupling
Status: standard imported probability concept used in GURF

A Coupling is a joint probability law having specified marginals.

\[ \gamma \in \Gamma(\mu,\nu). \]
  • Marginals need not uniquely determine a coupling.
  • Separate feasibility does not determine a task-appropriate coupling.
  • Parameterized couplings require appropriate measurability or regularity.

62. Conservative Passport

Conservative Passport
Status: active candidate BM research structure

A Conservative Passport tracks a quantity that native mathematics independently requires to persist across a transformation even when the active representation does not retain that quantity explicitly.

A Conservative Passport records a conservation requirement. It does not create or prove the conservation law.

63. Root Passport Transfer

Root Passport Transfer
Status: active candidate BM structure

Root Passport Transfer is the proposed typed persistence of required Passport information from a source structure into a Root and onward into a compatible receiving structure.

\[ P_{\mathrm{source}} \longrightarrow P_R \longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{receiver}}. \]
The arrows are schematic. Actual transfer requires defined maps, compatibility conditions, and preservation claims.

64. Seed

Seed
Status: candidate BM primitive under active development

A Seed is information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes a lawful generative or self-seeding process until a specified export condition is reached.

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \] followed conditionally by \[ S_n \longrightarrow R. \]

Characteristics

  • Not merely initial data.
  • Generative continuation is part of the candidate concept.
  • Root export is conditional rather than immediate.
  • Generation may be iterative, recursive, branching, stochastic, or otherwise structured when explicitly defined.
Seed terminology does not imply biological agency, intention, purpose, or personified mathematical behavior.

65. Seed State

Seed State
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed State is an intermediate state produced during a lawful Seed process before Root export.

\[ S_n \in \mathcal S. \]
A Seed State is not automatically a Branch, Leaf, Response, or Root. Each role has separate requirements.

66. Seed Export Condition

Seed Export Condition
Status: candidate BM concept

A Seed Export Condition is the criterion determining when a Seed process may produce or export a Root.

\[ E: \mathcal S \longrightarrow \{0,1\}, \] with export permitted when \[ E(S_n)=1. \]
The model or native mathematics must supply the criterion. BM has no universal Seed export condition.

67. Dynamic Closure

Dynamic Closure
Status: exact deterministic factorization concept

Let

\[ R:X\to Z \]

be a reduced representation and

\[ \Phi:X\to X \]

an exact deterministic evolution.

The representation is dynamically closed when there exists

\[ \widetilde\Phi: R(X) \longrightarrow R(X) \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

Equivalently,

\[ R(x)=R(x') \Longrightarrow R(\Phi(x)) = R(\Phi(x')). \]
  • Task sufficiency does not imply dynamic closure.
  • Failure of closure may expose hidden state or memory dependence.
  • Stochastic closure requires stochastic/lumpability semantics.
  • Quantum closure requires channel-correct semantics.
  • Approximate closure requires a declared approximation criterion.

68. Response Ambiguity Set

Response Ambiguity Set
Status: useful canonical GURF construction

For

\[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W, \]

define

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A_T(y) = \{ T(x): x\in r^{-1}(y) \}. } \]

It records every Task output still compatible with the observation \(y\).

Exact sufficiency corresponds to

\[ |\mathcal A_T(y)| \le 1 \]

for every realized observation value.

69. Task Diameter

Task Diameter
Status: quantitative GURF diagnostic

If \(W\) carries a metric \(d_W\), define

\[ \boxed{ D_{r,T}(y) = \sup_{x,x'\in r^{-1}(y)} d_W(T(x),T(x')). } \]

Exact sufficiency implies

\[ D_{r,T}(y)=0. \]
The diagnostic depends on the metric and may be infinite. Empty fibres require an explicit convention.

70. Interface Defect

Interface Defect
Status: quantitative BM diagnostic

Suppose exact compatibility requires

\[ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. \]

If the common target carries metric \(d\), define

\[ \boxed{ \Delta(x) = d \left( P_2(F(x)), G(P_1(x)) \right). } \]

Exact compatibility corresponds to

\[ \Delta(x)=0. \]
A scalar defect is meaningful only after a mathematically justified comparison structure has been chosen.

71. Law Space

Law Space
Status: active BM organizational concept

A Law Space is a mathematical space or indexed family representing laws, operators, parameterized rule sets, Regimes, constraints, or other governing structures relevant to a BM construction.

\[ \lambda \in \mathcal L. \]

The structure of \(\mathcal L\) is native to the problem.

Calling something a Law Space does not imply that a topology, metric, probability law, derivative, interpolation rule, or algebraic Operation exists on it.

72. Conserved Quantity

Conservation
Status: native-domain mathematical concept

For evolution

\[ \Phi_t:X\to X \]

a quantity

\[ C:X\to V \]

is conserved when

\[ \boxed{ C(\Phi_t(x)) = C(x) } \]

throughout the stated evolution domain.

BM may preserve, transport, or audit an independently established conservation law. BM does not independently create energy, momentum, charge, probability, or another conserved quantity.

73. Mathematical Realizability

Mathematical Realizability
Status: canonical BM distinction

A formal specification is mathematically realizable when at least one mathematical object exists satisfying all stated requirements.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in X \text{ satisfying every required condition.} } \]

Failure modes

  • Contradictory constraints.
  • Empty admissible domain.
  • Unsatisfied boundary conditions.
  • Regularity failure.
  • Incompatible structural requirements.
  • Nonexistence established by theorem.
Existence does not imply uniqueness, stability, efficient construction, computability, or physical realizability.

74. Physical Realizability

Physical Realizability
Status: application-level requirement

A mathematical structure is physically realizable only when a physical system can satisfy the relevant physical laws, parameters, causal requirements, constraints, initial conditions, boundary conditions, material limitations, stability conditions, and implementation requirements.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical consistency} \not\Rightarrow \text{physical realizability}. } \]
Physical realizability is empirical and theory-dependent. BM can organize the question but cannot replace validated physical law, experiment, or engineering constraints.

75. GURF Response System

GURF Response System
Status: canonical working Core representation

A GURF Response System is a typed response-semantic structure containing a source or system state, a response law or relation, accessible observations, an information/degradation relation when required, and an admissibility structure.

A useful deterministic Core record is

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak G = \left( X, Y, \mathfrak R, \mathcal O, \preceq, \mathcal A \right). } \]
Object Meaning
\(X\) Source or system state space.
\(Y\) Physical or mathematical response space.
\(\mathfrak R\) Response law or response relation.
\(\mathcal O\) Observation map or observation family.
\(\preceq\) Declared information or degradation relation/order.
\(\mathcal A\) Admissibility structure.

Response–Observation Distinction

\[ x \xrightarrow{\mathfrak R} y \xrightarrow{\mathcal O} z. \] \[ F_{\mathfrak G} = \mathcal O\circ\mathfrak R. \]
The response \(y\) and retained observation \(z\) are not automatically the same object.

76. GURF Core

GURF Core
Status: stable / canonical

The GURF Core is the minimal response-specialized architecture governing source state, response, observation, Task, fibres, sufficiency, continuation, ambiguity, obstruction, and repair.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Core asks what response information exists and what that information supports.} } \]
Abilities
  • Define observable equivalence.
  • Construct response fibres.
  • Test exact and approximate Task sufficiency.
  • Audit continuation.
  • Expose response ambiguity.
  • Identify obstructions.
  • Represent repair.
Specialized operation, constraint, inverse, kernel, diagrammatic, extended-domain, and glue/hold semantics belong to extensions rather than being repeatedly inserted into the Core.

77. SOE — Typed Operations and Composition

SOE
Status: stable canonical GURF extension

SOE is the GURF extension governing lawful Operations performed on response systems.

Its scope includes:

typing · operation order · composition · localization · filtering · differentiation · reconstruction · intervention · handoff · rebinding · feedback discipline

A general Operation may have form

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

SOE Operation Contract

  • Input type.
  • Output type.
  • Admissible domain.
  • Active Regime.
  • Required information.
  • Preserved information.
  • Operation order.
  • Failure conditions.
\[ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. \]
SOE does not assume commutativity, unrestricted distributivity, or that matching codomains establish lawful composition.

78. ACE — Admissibility and Constraint Structure

ACE
Status: stable canonical GURF extension

ACE governs mathematical admissibility and constraint-sensitive response.

Its scope includes:

admissible state sets · hard constraints · soft constraints · convexity · coercivity · viability · positivity · constraint activation · stability gates · boundary conditions

\[ \mathcal A \subseteq X. \] If constraints define \(K_1,\ldots,K_m\), \[ \boxed{ \mathcal A = \bigcap_{j=1}^{m}K_j. } \]

Characteristics

  • Separates ambient state space from admissible state space.
  • Tracks active and inactive constraints.
  • Can support viability and coercivity analysis.
  • Distinguishes different mathematical meanings of stability.
Static positivity, convexity, or coercivity does not automatically imply hyperbolicity, ghost freedom, gradient stability, spectral stability, or nonlinear global stability.

79. IRE — Inference and Recovery

IRE
Status: stable canonical GURF extension

IRE is the current GURF extension for inference, inverse recovery, feasible fibres, ambiguity, task-relative identifiability, uncertainty, reconstruction, inverse certificates, stability, and inference complexity.

For observation

\[ F:X\to Y \]

and observation value \(y\), the admissible inverse set may be

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C(y) = \{x\in\mathcal A:F(x)=y\}. } \]

Full-state identifiability

\[ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow x=x'. \]

Task-relative identifiability

\[ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]
  • A unique optimizer does not prove physical identifiability.
  • Exact uniqueness does not imply stable inversion.
  • Regularization choices must remain explicit.
  • Earlier GURE material is historical lineage consolidated into IRE.

80. UKE — Unified Kernel Extension

UKE
Status: stable canonical GURF extension

UKE contains advanced kernel-specific response mathematics.

Its central rigor requirement is that distinct meanings of the word kernel remain mathematically typed.

Nullspace kernel

\[ \ker F = \{x:F(x)=0\}. \]

Integral or operator kernel

\[ (Tf)(x) = \int K(x,y)f(y)\,dy. \]

Green kernel

\[ L_xG(x,x') = \delta(x-x'). \]

Resolvent

\[ R(z) = (L-zI)^{-1} \]

where defined.

Memory kernel

\[ R(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} K(t,s)x(s)\,ds. \]
Current UKE sectors
  • UKE-N — nullspace structure.
  • UKE-I — integral and convolution kernels.
  • UKE-G — Green and causal kernels.
  • UKE-S — spectral and resolvent kernels.
  • UKE-RK — reproducing kernels.
  • UKE-M — memory and nonlocal response.
A mathematically chosen kernel is not automatically a physically derived response mechanism. Origin, domain, causality, boundary conditions, and admissibility remain separate requirements.

81. GRDE — Diagrammatic Response Extension

GRDE
Status: frozen at GURF-GRDE-008

GRDE is an optional diagrammatic response-structure representation calculus.

Its recorded scope includes:

response fibres and refinement · state versus carrier rails · writers · response cuts · provenance · response fusion · higher-order or jet response information

\[ \boxed{ \text{formal mathematics} \longrightarrow \text{GRDE representation}. } \]
  • GRDE is not a replacement for underlying mathematics.
  • GRDE is not a separate native mathematical field.
  • Its current architecture is frozen at GURF-GRDE-008.
  • Public diagrams are withheld until the canonical diagrams are formally reconstructed and approved.

82. HDE — Halo Domain Extension

HDE
Status: active canonical GURF extension

HDE handles response problems in which the relevant response domain extends beyond a narrowly localized source or primary region.

Its role includes extended response support, domain propagation, halo-flow behavior, boundary transfer, retained response structure, persistence, and observation across an enlarged domain.

A useful working decomposition is

\[ D \subseteq D_{\mathrm{ext}}, \] \[ H = D_{\mathrm{ext}}\setminus D \] when a Set-like decomposition is appropriate.

An extended response state may require

\[ x_{\mathrm{ext}} = (x_D,x_H). \]

Characteristic ambiguity

\[ x_D=x_D' \]

may coexist with

\[ T(x_D,x_H) \neq T(x_D',x_H'). \]
  • HDE is not restricted to one physical halo theory.
  • Literal nested spatial domains are a working formalization, not a universal requirement.
  • The actual propagation law is domain-specific.
  • Physical realization must be established separately.

83. GHSE — Glue-Hold State Extension

GHSE
Status: active canonical GURF extension

GHSE studies response states whose behavior depends on lawful joining, attachment, holding, persistence, release, or transition among coupled components.

It gives glue/hold behavior a response-semantic home without moving all attachment mathematics into GURF.

For component spaces

\[ X_A, \qquad X_B, \]

a joined state may occupy

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal H \subseteq X_A\times X_B. } \]

A working hold predicate may be

\[ h: X_A\times X_B \longrightarrow \{0,1\}. \]

A working glue Operation may be

\[ J: D_J \subseteq X_A\times X_B \longrightarrow X_{AB}. \]
  • Arbitrary joining is not admissible.
  • Hold criteria may be relational, geometric, probabilistic, or dynamical.
  • Discrete state labels are not universally required.
  • The native theory determines actual persistence and release laws.

84. HEC

HEC
Status: BM-owned calculus; not a GURF extension

HEC is a project-level mathematical calculus owned by Branch Mathematics because its surviving mathematical role is treated as domain-independent rather than essentially response-semantic.

\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{HEC} \subset \text{BM-owned mathematics}. } \]

Ownership characteristic

GURF may use HEC where appropriate, but use by GURF does not make HEC a GURF extension.

The public Definitions Registry does not infer additional HEC operations, axioms, or theorem claims beyond those that have been separately formalized in the BM corpus.

85. HEC-S

HEC-S

HEC-S is not independently counted as a GURF extension.

86. Defect Calculus

Defect Calculus
Status: domain-independent mathematics under BM

Defect Calculus is BM-owned mathematical machinery for representing and studying explicitly defined failures of compatibility, preservation, closure, commutation, realization, or another declared mathematical condition.

A generic defect may take form

\[ \Delta(x) = d(F(x),G(x)) \]

when a justified comparison metric \(d\) exists, but Defect Calculus is not restricted to scalar metric defects.

  • A defect must be defined relative to a specific mathematical requirement.
  • No universal scalar defect exists for all sectors.
  • Defect terminology does not itself prove instability or physical failure.
  • Defect Calculus is not a GURF extension merely because GURF can use it.

87. Attachment Complex

Attachment Complex
Status: bridge object

Attachment Complex is the project-level bridge role for mathematical structures used to relate compatible attachment mathematics across BM-owned structure and response-specialized GURF contexts.

It belongs to the bridge layer rather than being treated as an additional GURF extension.

Calling a structure an Attachment Complex does not establish that an attachment exists or is admissible. The relevant maps, domains, laws, and compatibility conditions must still be defined.

88. Extension Interface Contract

Extension Interface Contract

\[ \iota_E: \mathfrak G \longrightarrow \mathfrak G_E. \] \[ \boxed{ \text{extension} \neq \text{unannounced Core mutation}. } \]
An extension does not automatically modify every Core definition, theorem, Task, Observation, or Response.

90. Portability Firewall

Portability Firewall
Status: canonical BM/GURF ownership rule

The Portability Firewall determines whether mathematical machinery belongs under BM or remains response-specialized under GURF.

\[ \boxed{ \text{domain-independent mathematical kernel} \longrightarrow \text{BM}. } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{identity essentially dependent on response semantics} \longrightarrow \text{GURF}. } \]

A mathematical construction is a strong candidate for BM ownership when it can be stated without essential response semantics and remains meaningful across multiple mathematical sectors.

A construction remains GURF-specialized when its identity fundamentally depends on system response, observation, Task, intervention, Regime, continuation, response ambiguity, or response-state semantics.

92. Internal Framework Status

Internally Established Architecture
Status: project governance term

Internal establishment means that a definition or architecture has been accepted for current use inside the BM/GURF research program.

Internal establishment does not imply independent validation, publication acceptance, historical priority, novelty, or universal mathematical usefulness.

93. External Novelty

External Novelty
Status: separate research-history question

External novelty concerns whether a definition, theorem, method, framework, or integrated construction was previously known in an equivalent or materially equivalent form.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical correctness} \neq \text{novelty}. } \]

Direct derivation does not by itself establish historical priority.

94. Independent External Validation

Independent External Validation
Status: separate from internal derivation

Independent validation means review, reproduction, verification, criticism, or acceptance by mathematically competent parties outside the internal development process.

Independent validation does not replace proof where proof is required, and internal proof does not automatically imply external acceptance.

95. Definition Versus Theorem

Definitions establish terminology and formal object requirements.

Theorems establish mathematical consequences.

\[ \boxed{ \text{definition} \neq \text{proof of existence or consequence}. } \]

96. Definition Versus Model

A model assigns specific laws, maps, parameters, equations, or numerical values to defined mathematical roles.

For example,

\[ R_{n+1} = \frac12R_n+1 \]

is one possible iterative model using Root-valued states. It is not the definition of Root.

97. Definition Versus Metaphor

BM vocabulary such as Branch, Leaf, Tree, Root, and Seed has formal structural meaning.

These terms do not imply biological agency, intention, consciousness, purpose, instinct, or autonomous growth.

\[ \boxed{ \text{formal definition governs usage}. } \]

100. BM and GURF Distinction

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM} = \text{broader meta-structural mathematics} } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{GURF} = \text{response-specialized mathematics under BM governance} } \]

BM governs lawful representation, transformation, Interaction, composition, Interfaces, realization, export, re-entry, Roots, Networks, Configurations, coherence, and related structure.

BM need not be rewritten as a response theory. GURF need not be invoked merely because BM terminology appears.
