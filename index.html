What This Site Contains

This website is the public mathematical corpus for Branch Mathematics (BM), GURF, their formal structures, and the research programs developed through them.

The focus is the mathematics itself: definitions, equations, proofs, formal structures, quantitative examples, corrections, research status, applications, and open problems.

Mathematical development is preserved through formal revisions, versioned definitions, theorem status, research logs, and changelogs. Development conversations are not treated as part of the public mathematical record.

Branch Mathematics

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, operations, interfaces, and native notions of validity.

A Branch is therefore not stripped of its native mathematics merely because it participates in a larger BM construction.

Governing Structural Direction

The broad mathematical direction of BM may be written schematically as

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root} \end{cases} } \]

This is a structural schema rather than a universal numerical equation. It does not imply that every BM computation must be linear, sequential, deterministic, or graph-theoretically tree-shaped.

Every transformation, Interaction, composition, quotient, realization, export, attachment, or re-entry must remain lawful relative to the mathematical structures participating in it.

BM Structural Roles

BM is not defined by one Passport theory, one quotient construction, one information-loss theorem, or one recurrence law. Those are mathematical components within a broader meta-structural framework.

Branch

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A Branch may be represented by a set, algebra, manifold, measurable space, Hilbert space, graph, category, operator system, PDE state space, function space, computational state space, or another mathematically specified structure.

Its native mathematical validity remains controlled by the mathematical sector to which it belongs.

Interaction

An Interaction is a lawful typed mathematical action involving one or more Branches, with multi-Branch interaction being the central case.

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

The admissible domain \(D_I\) contains only combinations for which the Interaction is mathematically defined.

Formal writability does not imply admissibility.

Leaf

A Leaf is extracted information from an Interaction result.

If

\[ z=I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j:Z_I\longrightarrow\mathcal L_j, \]

then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j\!\left(I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\right) \in\mathcal L_j. } \]

Several distinct Leaves may be extracted from one Interaction. A Leaf is not automatically a Root.

Tree

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches, Interactions, interfaces, and intermediate information through which combined computation is performed and a Response is generated.

A Tree may carry intermediate Leaves and Passports and may use sequential, parallel, coupled, recursive, networked, categorical, or other mathematically appropriate organization.

A BM Tree is not required to be a literal graph-theoretic tree.

Response

A Response is the typed mathematical output produced by a Tree or another organized BM computation.

\[ \mathcal R_T:D_T\longrightarrow Y_T, \qquad r=\mathcal R_T(x). \]

A Response may be a value, state, law, relation, operator, classification, structured object, family of objects, or another mathematically typed result.

Producing a Response does not imply that the Response may lawfully participate in later mathematics.

Root

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

A terminal result need not become a Root. Root status does not imply recursion, invertibility, conservation, or physical realizability.

Passport

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, law context, admissibility requirements, interface requirements, or task-relevant information that must remain available for later mathematical use.

Passports support BM and GURF. They do not replace the native mathematics of a Branch.

Operation

An Operation is a lawful mathematical transformation with declared input type, output type, domain, assumptions, and compatibility conditions.

Operations may be unary, multi-input, partial, deterministic, stochastic, relational, operator-valued, or otherwise typed according to the mathematical sector.

Interface

An Interface governs how information or structure may pass lawfully between mathematical objects.

An Interface must preserve whatever typing, laws, distinctions, Passport information, or admissibility conditions are required by the receiving structure.

Realization

Mathematical Realization asks whether an abstract specification can actually be constructed in the mathematical structure in which it is claimed to exist.

Mathematical realization and physical realization are separate questions.

Export and Re-entry

BM distinguishes producing a mathematical result from exporting that result and lawfully inserting it into another mathematical context.

Export into a representation does not automatically authorize re-entry into every structure using that representation type.

Seed

A Seed is a candidate BM primitive for information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes a lawful generative process until a specified export condition is reached.

\[ S_0\longrightarrow S_1\longrightarrow S_2\longrightarrow\cdots \]

followed, when a declared export condition is satisfied, by

\[ S_n\longrightarrow R. \]

Seed structure remains candidate mathematics under active development.

Tree, Response, and Root Are Different Roles

A central BM distinction is that the structure performing the computation, the result of that computation, and the downstream reusable role of that result are not identical.

Role Meaning
Tree Organized mathematical structure coordinating Branches, Interactions, interfaces, Leaves, Passports, and intermediate computation.
Response Typed mathematical output produced by the organized computation.
Root A Response or resulting structure whose downstream admissibility has been established.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \xrightarrow{\text{combined computation}} \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal mathematical result},\\[4pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]

GURF

GURF is the response-specialized framework under Branch Mathematics governance.

BM governs the broader mathematics of lawful representation, distinction, transformation, interaction, composition, realization, export, and re-entry.

GURF is invoked when a mathematical problem genuinely contains response semantics: systems, states, observations, tasks, interventions, constraints, regimes, continuation, response ambiguity, or future responses.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch Mathematics} \longrightarrow \text{GURF when response semantics are present}. } \]

Central Sufficiency Question

For an observation

\[ r:X\longrightarrow Y \]

and a task

\[ T:X\longrightarrow W, \]

exact deterministic task sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Equivalently, the task factors through the realized observation image:

\[ \boxed{ \exists\, \widetilde T:r(X)\longrightarrow W \quad \text{such that} \quad T=\widetilde T\circ r. } \]

This asks whether the representation preserves every distinction needed to determine the selected task.

The deterministic criterion is not silently reused for stochastic, nondeterministic, or quantum sectors; those require their sector-correct semantics.

BM-SQ-01 — Structured Quotient

In the finitary algebraic sector, a Passport-induced equivalence supports corresponding quotient operations only when that equivalence is compatible with the relevant operations.

Let

\[ A=(X,\Sigma) \]

be a finitary algebra and let

\[ \sigma:X^n\longrightarrow X \]

be an operation. The required compatibility condition is

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad\forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

In ordinary algebraic language, the equivalence must be a congruence for the operations being passed to the quotient.

BM therefore does not treat an observational equivalence relation as sufficient by itself to guarantee a lawful structured quotient.

Distinction-Preservation Principle

One important principle used throughout BM, and especially within GURF, is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Keep mathematical distinctions when later mathematics requires them; remove them only when their loss is proven safe.} } \]

This is a major preservation principle, but it is not by itself the complete definition of Branch Mathematics.

Passports, fibres, recoverability, obstruction, repair, quotienting, interfaces, continuation, and dynamic closure provide mathematical tools for studying the principle when it is relevant.

Roots and Further Mathematical Participation

A Root is a Response or resulting structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

When a Root process genuinely has iterative form on an appropriate closed or invariant domain, one may have

\[ F:X_R\longrightarrow X_R, \qquad R_{n+1}=F(R_n). \]

Ordinary fixed-point, stability, spectral, dynamical-systems, asymptotic, and related mathematical tools may then be used when their assumptions are satisfied.

BM does not assume that every Root process is iterative, that every lawful attachment produces another Root, or that one recurrence law describes all Rooting behavior.

Proof and Novelty

Mathematical validity and novelty are separate questions.

A directly derived theorem may be mathematically correct while also having prior art or an equivalent formulation elsewhere in mathematics.

Conversely, novelty does not rescue an invalid derivation.

Project rule: mathematical admissibility is determined by definitions, assumptions, derivation, proof, counterexample, computation, and appropriate validation. Claims of novelty or priority require a separate external prior-art investigation.

BM and GURF Do Not Replace Native Mathematics

BM and GURF organize mathematical participation, representation, response, interfaces, and preservation requirements. They do not override the mathematical truth conditions of the participating fields.

Algebra, analysis, geometry, topology, probability theory, category theory, differential equations, quantum theory, general relativity, numerical analysis, control theory, and other native disciplines continue to determine the validity of their own mathematical claims.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM/GURF organization} \neq \text{replacement of native mathematical proof}. } \]

Scientific Visualization

Quantitative figures are used when the mathematics produces meaningful numerical or geometric behavior.

Appropriate figures include: response curves, spectra, phase portraits, vector fields, parameter sweeps, surfaces, heatmaps, PDE fields, stability maps, convergence curves, residuals, uncertainty distributions, and related scientific plots.

Figures are intended to expose mathematical behavior rather than serve as decoration.

Governing BM Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Each Branch retains the mathematics that makes it lawful;}\\[4pt] \text{Branches participate together only through admissible mathematical structure;}\\[4pt] \text{combined mathematics must preserve the distinctions and laws}\\ \text{required by later mathematics.} \end{gathered} } \]

Branch Mathematics governs the broader problem of lawful mathematical representation, transformation, interaction, composition, computation, realization, export, and re-entry.

GURF specializes this architecture to problems in which system response, observation, intervention, task sufficiency, constraints, regimes, ambiguity, or future continuation are mathematically essential.

BM need not be invoked when it offers no genuine mathematical leverage, and GURF need not be invoked when response semantics are absent.
