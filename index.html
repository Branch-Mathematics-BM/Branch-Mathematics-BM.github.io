What This Site Contains

This website is the public mathematical corpus for Branch Mathematics (BM), GURF, their formal structures, and the research programs developed through them.

The focus is the mathematics itself: definitions, equations, proofs, formal structures, quantitative examples, corrections, research status, applications, and open problems.

Mathematical development is preserved through formal revisions, versioned definitions, theorem status, research logs, and changelogs. Development conversations are not treated as part of the public mathematical record.

Branch Mathematics

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM provides a mathematical framework in which one problem may involve several distinct mathematical structures without forcing those structures into one undifferentiated formalism.

Each participating Branch retains the mathematics that makes it lawful: its types, equations, operations, constraints, equivalences, invariants, admissibility conditions, and other native structure.

Governing Structural Direction

The broad BM mechanism can be written schematically as

\[ \boxed{ \text{many lawful mathematical Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interaction} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Root or law result} } \]

This is a structural schema rather than a numerical equation. BM does not authorize an Interaction merely because mathematical objects can be written beside one another.

Every transformation, Interaction, composition, quotient, realization, export, attachment, or re-entry must remain lawful relative to the mathematical structures participating in it.

BM Structural Backbone

BM is not defined by a single Passport theory, quotient theory, or information-loss theory. Those are important parts of the framework, but they operate inside a broader structural mathematics.

Branch

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A Branch may contain a manifold, algebra, probability space, differential equation, graph, field, operator system, computational representation, or another mathematically specified object.

Interaction

An Interaction is a lawful joint mathematical action involving two or more Branches.

For Branches \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), a schematic Interaction may be written

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

The admissible domain \(D_I\) matters: not every formally writable combination is lawful.

Leaf

A Leaf is mathematical information extracted from an Interaction.

\[ L_j = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right). \]

Different Leaves may expose different mathematical consequences of the same Interaction.

Tree

A Tree is an organized mathematical structure of Branches and Interactions through which combined computation is carried out.

Trees may be sequential, branching, parallel, coupled, recursive, or require more general constructions such as products, limits, pullbacks, equalizers, or other lawful mathematical structures.

Root

A Root is a mathematical result or resulting structure that remains available for lawful further mathematical participation.

A terminal result need not become a Root. Root status depends on whether the output can lawfully enter additional mathematical structure.

Passport

A Passport records distinctions, requirements, constraints, observations, compatibility conditions, or other information that must survive a mathematical interface or later use.

Passports support BM. They do not replace the native mathematics of a Branch.

Operations

Operations specify lawful mathematical transformations. Their domains, codomains, structural assumptions, and compatibility requirements remain explicit.

Interfaces

Interfaces govern how information and mathematical structure pass between Branches, representations, Roots, or other BM objects.

Realization

Realization asks whether an abstract mathematical specification can actually be constructed in the mathematical structure in which it is claimed to exist.

Export and Re-entry

BM distinguishes producing a mathematical result from lawfully exporting that result and inserting it into another mathematical context.

GURF

GURF is the response-specialized framework within the Branch Mathematics research architecture.

BM governs the broader mathematics of lawful representation, distinction, transformation, interaction, composition, realization, export, and re-entry.

GURF is invoked when a problem genuinely has response semantics: systems, states, observations, tasks, interventions, constraints, regimes, continuation, or future responses.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch Mathematics} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{GURF when response semantics are present} } \]

For an observation

\[ r:X\longrightarrow Y \]

and a task

\[ T:X\longrightarrow W, \]

exact task sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x') }. \]

Equivalently, when such a factorization exists,

\[ T=\widetilde T\circ r. \]

This asks whether the representation \(r\) preserves every distinction required to determine the task \(T\).

BM-SQ-01 — Structured Quotient

In the finitary algebraic sector, a Passport-induced equivalence supports the corresponding quotient operations only when that equivalence is compatible with the relevant operations.

Let

\[ A=(X,\Sigma) \]

be a finitary algebra, and let

\[ \sigma:X^n\longrightarrow X \]

be an operation. The required compatibility condition is

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad\forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n) }. \]

In ordinary algebraic language, the equivalence must be a congruence for the operations being passed to the quotient.

BM therefore does not treat an observational equivalence relation as sufficient by itself to guarantee a lawful structured quotient.

Distinction-Preservation Principle

One important principle used throughout BM and especially within GURF is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Keep mathematical distinctions when later mathematics requires them; remove them only when their loss is proven safe.} } \]

This is a major preservation principle, but it is not by itself the complete definition of Branch Mathematics.

Passports, fibres, recoverability, obstruction, repair, quotienting, and continuation provide mathematical tools for studying this principle when it is relevant.

Roots and Further Mathematical Participation

A Root is a mathematical result or structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

When a Root process genuinely has iterative form, an example abstraction is

\[ R_{n+1}=F(R_n). \]

Ordinary fixed-point, stability, spectral, dynamical-systems, asymptotic, and other mathematical tools may then be used when their assumptions are satisfied.

BM does not assume that every Root process is iterative or that one recurrence law describes all Rooting behavior.

Proof and Novelty

Mathematical validity and novelty are separate questions.

A directly derived theorem may be mathematically correct while also having prior art or an equivalent formulation elsewhere in mathematics.

Conversely, novelty does not rescue an invalid derivation.

Project rule: mathematical admissibility is determined by definitions, assumptions, derivation, proof, counterexample, computation, and appropriate validation. Claims of novelty or priority require a separate external prior-art investigation.

Scientific Visualization

Quantitative figures are used when the mathematics produces meaningful numerical or geometric behavior.

Appropriate figures include: response curves, spectra, phase portraits, vector fields, parameter sweeps, surfaces, heatmaps, PDE fields, stability maps, convergence curves, residuals, uncertainty distributions, and related scientific plots.

Figures are intended to expose mathematical behavior rather than serve as decoration.
