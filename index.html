What This Site Contains

This website is the public mathematical corpus for Branch Mathematics (BM), GURF, their formal structures, and the research programs developed through them.

The corpus contains definitions, equations, proofs, formal structures, quantitative examples, counterexamples, corrections, theorem dependencies, research status, applications, and open problems.

Mathematical development is preserved through formal revisions, versioned definitions, theorem status, research logs, and changelogs. Development conversations are not treated as part of the public mathematical record.

Reading rule. A displayed formula may be a definition, structural schema, established identity, classical imported result, BM structural result, conditional result, or open research object. These classes are deliberately distinguished rather than being presented as equivalent claims.

Branch Mathematics

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own types, laws, distinctions, admissible states, operations, morphisms, interfaces, invariants, and native notions of validity.

The object of study is therefore not merely a collection of mathematical structures. BM studies the conditions under which those structures may be connected, transformed, composed, reduced, exported, and reused without silently changing the mathematics that made them lawful.

Governing Structural Direction

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root}. \end{cases} } \]

This is a structural schema rather than a universal numerical equation. It does not assert that all BM computations are linear, deterministic, sequential, reversible, or graph-theoretically tree-shaped.

Every transformation, Interaction, quotient, composition, realization, export, attachment, or re-entry must instead satisfy the mathematical obligations belonging to the participating sector.

Compact Formal Core

The following record gives a common BM-level organization for a Branch. Concrete mathematical sectors supply the actual meaning of each field.

\[ \boxed{ B= \left( \mathsf S_B, \tau_B, X_B, A_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, \mathcal P_B \right) } \]

where:

  • \(\mathsf S_B\) is the mathematical sector in which the Branch is interpreted;
  • \(\tau_B\) is its declared mathematical type;
  • \(X_B\) is its carrier object or state object;
  • \(A_B\subseteq X_B\), or the sector-correct analogue, is the admissible state domain;
  • \(\mathcal L_B\) is the family of laws, identities, constraints, invariants, or structural requirements;
  • \(\mathcal O_B\) is the family of admissible native operations or morphisms;
  • \(\mathcal P_B\) records Passport and interface obligations required for later mathematical participation.

Thus a Branch is not defined merely by saying that it could be a manifold, algebra, Hilbert space, graph, or category. Those are possible realizations of \(X_B\) and \(\mathsf S_B\). What makes the object a BM Branch is the complete typed structural record together with its admissibility data.

Admissible Branch Transformation

A candidate transformation

\[ f:B\longrightarrow C \]

is admitted by BM only after its mathematical obligations are checked. In a deterministic set-like realization this includes at least

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &x\in D_f\cap A_B,\\ &f(x)\in A_C,\\ &f\text{ is type-compatible},\\ &f\text{ satisfies the required native laws},\\ &f\models \mathcal P_{B\to C}. \end{aligned} } \]

We may summarize successful verification by writing

\[ f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{BM}}(B,C), \]

with the understanding that the exact meaning of \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{BM}}\) is sector-dependent.

Important: formal type compatibility is necessary but not sufficient. A map may be writable while still violating an invariant, admissibility condition, domain restriction, Passport obligation, conservation rule, positivity condition, regularity requirement, or receiving-interface condition.

Interaction Domain

For Branches \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), a deterministic Interaction may be represented as

\[ I: D_I \longrightarrow Z_I, \]

where

\[ \boxed{ D_I \subseteq A_{B_1}\times\cdots\times A_{B_n}. } \]

The domain is part of the mathematical object. BM therefore does not replace a partial law by an implicitly total one.

If

\[ z=I(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \]

a Leaf extraction map

\[ \ell_j:Z_I\longrightarrow\mathcal L_j \]

produces

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j\!\left(I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\right). } \]

Composition Is a Checked Operation

Let

\[ f:B\longrightarrow C, \qquad g:C\longrightarrow D \]

be candidate transformations. Even before Passport or law checks, the natural domain of the deterministic composite is

\[ \boxed{ D_{g\circ f} = \left\{ x\in D_f: f(x)\in D_g \right\}. } \]

BM then asks separately whether

\[ (g\circ f)(x)\in A_D \]

and whether all law, interface, and Passport requirements survive the composite.

Consequently,

\[ \boxed{ f\text{ lawful} \ \wedge\ g\text{ lawful} \not\Rightarrow g\circ f\text{ globally lawful} } \]

unless the intermediate domain, interface, and preservation conditions required for composition have also been established.

Predicate Passport Defect

A simple Passport realization can be expressed through predicates

\[ P_B:A_B\to\{0,1\}, \qquad P_C:A_C\to\{0,1\}. \]

For a transformation \(f:B\to C\), define its Passport-violation set by

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak D_{f,P} = \left\{ x\in A_B: P_B(x)=1 \text{ and } P_C(f(x))=0 \right\}. } \]

In this realization, exact Passport preservation requires

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak D_{f,P}=\varnothing. } \]

More general Passports may be relational, quantitative, measure-theoretic, operator-valued, stochastic, or task-relative; they are not reduced universally to Boolean predicates.

Parallel-Path Coherence

A BM network may contain different lawful routes from one Branch to another. Let \(p\) and \(q\) be two paths with the same source and target and let their induced composites be

\[ F_p, \qquad F_q. \]

Let \(\simeq_D\) denote the equality or sector-correct equivalence required at the destination. Define the parallel-path defect by

\[ \boxed{ \Delta_{p,q} = \left\{ x\in D_p\cap D_q: F_p(x)\not\simeq_D F_q(x) \right\}. } \]

Exact path coherence on the shared domain requires

\[ \boxed{ \Delta_{p,q}=\varnothing. } \]

Thus global BM composition is not merely the existence of local arrows. Parallel routes, interface identifications, and higher compatibility conditions may themselves become mathematical obligations.

Configuration-Level Admissibility

For a finite BM configuration

\[ \mathcal X = \left( \{B_i\}_{i\in V}, \{F_e\}_{e\in E}, \mathcal P, \mathcal C \right), \]

where \(\mathcal P\) is its Passport data and \(\mathcal C\) its coherence requirements, configuration admissibility requires simultaneous satisfaction of local and global constraints:

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Adm}(\mathcal X) = \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{type}} \cap \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{domain}} \cap \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{law}} \cap \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{passport}} \cap \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{interface}} \cap \operatorname{Adm}_{\mathrm{coherence}}. } \]

This is an organizational decomposition of admissibility, not a claim that every mathematical sector expresses these sets in identical syntax.

Sector-Correct Semantics

BM does not silently identify deterministic maps, stochastic kernels, nondeterministic relations, and quantum channels. Their composition and information semantics are different.

Deterministic

\[ f:X\to Y \]

Ordinary function composition applies:

\[ (g\circ f)(x)=g(f(x)). \]

Fibres and equality-induced equivalence relations may be used directly.

Stochastic

A stochastic transformation is represented by a Markov kernel

\[ K:X\rightsquigarrow Y. \]

For a measurable test \(\phi\),

\[ \boxed{ K^\dagger\phi(x) = \int_Y \phi(y)\, K(x,dy). } \]

Composition uses kernel integration, not deterministic substitution.

Nondeterministic / Relational

A relation may be written

\[ R:X\rightrightarrows Y. \]

For \(U\subseteq Y\), the may and must pullbacks are distinct:

\[ \Diamond_R(U) = \{x:\exists y\in U,\ xRy\}, \] \[ \Box_R(U) = \{x:\forall y,\ xRy\Rightarrow y\in U\}. \]

Existential and universal continuation therefore cannot be interchanged.

Quantum / Operator

Quantum realizations require their native linear, operator-theoretic, positivity, normalization, and measurement structure.

Depending on the problem, transformations may be unitary operators, isometries, completely positive maps, channels, instruments, or other sector-correct objects.

Deterministic Set-level fibre formulas are not automatically promoted to this sector.

BM Structural Roles

BM is not defined by one Passport theorem, quotient construction, information-loss statement, recurrence, or abstraction method. Those are components inside a broader structural system.

Branch

Typed mathematical carrier together with admissibility, laws, operations, distinctions, and interface information.

Interaction

Lawful joint mathematical action on a declared admissible domain.

Leaf

Extracted information produced from an Interaction result. Multiple Leaves may be obtained from the same Interaction.

Tree

Organized structure of Branches, Interactions, interfaces, Passports, Leaves, and intermediate computation.

Response

Typed mathematical output generated by a Tree or other organized BM computation.

Root

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Root status does not automatically imply recurrence, conservation, invertibility, or physical realization.

Passport

Declared information, distinctions, invariants, observations, law context, coupling requirements, or task requirements that must remain available at an interface or later stage.

Operation

Typed lawful transformation with explicit domain, codomain, assumptions, and compatibility requirements.

Interface

Mathematical conditions governing lawful transfer between Branches or representations.

Realization

Determination of whether an abstract specification can be instantiated within the mathematical structure in which its existence is claimed.

Export and Re-entry

BM separates producing a result, representing that result elsewhere, and proving that it may lawfully participate in the receiving mathematics.

Seed

Candidate primitive for information undergoing lawful generative evolution before a declared Root-export condition is reached.

Tree, Response, and Root Are Different Roles

The structure performing a computation, the result of that computation, and the downstream reusable status of that result are not identified.

Role Meaning
Tree Organized mathematical structure coordinating Branches, Interactions, interfaces, Leaves, Passports, and intermediate computation.

Root Response or resulting mathematical structure whose required downstream admissibility has been established.

GURF

GURF is the response-specialized framework under Branch Mathematics governance.

BM governs the broader mathematics of lawful representation, distinction, transformation, interaction, composition, realization, export, and re-entry.

GURF is invoked when a problem genuinely contains response semantics: systems, states, observations, tasks, interventions, constraints, regimes, continuation, ambiguity, or future response.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch Mathematics} \longrightarrow \text{GURF when response semantics are present}. } \]

Exact Deterministic Task Sufficiency

For

\[ r:X\to Y, \qquad T:X\to W, \]

exact deterministic task sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ \exists\, \widetilde T:r(X)\to W \quad \text{such that} \quad T=\widetilde T\circ r. } \]

This deterministic result is classical mathematical infrastructure. GURF uses it as one exact sufficiency test; it is not presented as a novel theorem merely because it receives a role inside the framework.

Structured Quotient Gate

In the finitary algebraic sector, a Passport-induced equivalence can carry algebraic operations to a quotient only when the equivalence is compatible with the relevant operations.

Let

\[ A=(X,\Sigma) \]

be a finitary algebra and

\[ \sigma:X^n\to X \]

an operation. Required compatibility is

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad\forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

In standard algebraic language, \(\sim_P\) must be a congruence for the operations being descended to the quotient.

BM uses this classical requirement as a general warning: observational equivalence alone does not guarantee that every native mathematical operation survives quotienting.

Distinction-Preservation Principle

\[ \boxed{ \text{Preserve distinctions required by later mathematics; remove them only when the proposed loss is shown to be safe.} } \]

Passports, fibres, task equivalence, quotienting, interface compatibility, obstruction, repair, continuation, and coherence are separate mathematical mechanisms used to make that principle precise in different sectors.

Roots and Further Mathematical Participation

A Root is a Response or resulting structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

When Root evolution genuinely possesses an invariant iterative domain,

\[ F:X_R\to X_R, \qquad R_{n+1}=F(R_n), \]

ordinary fixed-point, stability, spectral, dynamical-systems, and asymptotic machinery may be invoked when their assumptions hold.

BM does not assume that every Root process is iterative, that every lawful attachment produces another Root, or that a universal recurrence governs all Root behavior.

Proof, Provenance, and Novelty

Mathematical validity and historical novelty are separate questions.

A result may be correctly derived inside this corpus while being equivalent to previously known mathematics. When that relationship is known, the result should be marked as classical infrastructure, a specialization, a reformulation, or a BM/GURF use of established mathematics.

Conversely, unfamiliar terminology or a novel structural organization does not establish a new theorem.

Project rule: mathematical admissibility is determined by definitions, assumptions, proof, counterexample, computation, structural verification, and appropriate domain-specific validation. Claims of novelty or priority require a separate prior-art investigation.

BM and GURF Do Not Replace Native Mathematics

BM and GURF organize mathematical participation, representation, response, interfaces, admissibility, and preservation requirements.

Algebra, analysis, geometry, topology, probability theory, category theory, differential equations, operator theory, quantum theory, general relativity, numerical analysis, control theory, and other native disciplines continue to determine the truth conditions of their own mathematical claims.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM/GURF organization} \neq \text{replacement of native mathematical proof}. } \]

Governing BM Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Each Branch retains the mathematics that makes it lawful;}\\[4pt] \text{Branches participate together only through admissible mathematical structure;}\\[4pt] \text{composition requires type, domain, law, Passport, interface, and coherence checks;}\\[4pt] \text{combined mathematics must preserve every distinction and law required downstream.} \end{gathered} } \]

Branch Mathematics governs the broader problem of lawful mathematical representation, transformation, interaction, composition, computation, realization, export, and re-entry.

GURF specializes this architecture to problems in which system response, observation, intervention, task sufficiency, constraints, regimes, ambiguity, or future continuation are mathematically essential.

BM need not be invoked when it offers no genuine mathematical leverage, and GURF need not be invoked when response semantics are absent.
