Structural Registry

Formal mathematical objects and architectural structures used by Branch Mathematics to organize lawful representation, transformation, interaction, composition, realization, export, re-entry, response, and continued mathematical participation.

1. Purpose of the Structural Registry

Branch Mathematics uses a typed structural architecture so that distinct mathematical objects can participate in one problem without losing the mathematical laws, distinctions, domains, constraints, or identities that make those objects lawful.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM structure} = \text{objects} + \text{types} + \text{laws} + \text{interfaces} + \text{admissible operations}. } \]

The structural vocabulary describes mathematical roles. It does not replace the native mathematics of the objects occupying those roles.

2. Core Structural Direction

The broad structural direction of BM is

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root}. \end{cases} } \]

This is a structural schema, not a universal linear sequence. Leaves, Passports, Interfaces, Configurations, and intermediate structures may appear throughout the computation.

A Tree organizes computation. A Response is the output of that computation. A Root is a Response or resulting structure that has verified downstream admissibility.

3. Branch

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

A working representation is

\[ B = \left( X_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, P_B \right). \]
Component Role
\(X_B\) Carrier, state space, or object space.
\(\mathcal L_B\) Laws, identities, equations, constraints, invariants, or native mathematical structure.
\(\mathcal O_B\) Admissible operations or transformations.
\(P_B\) Passport or interface information when required.

This tuple is organizational. BM does not claim every mathematical object intrinsically consists of exactly four components.

4. Branch Type

Let

\[ \tau(B) \]

denote the mathematical type of Branch \(B\).

Examples may include:

vector space · manifold · algebra · probability space · measurable space · graph · Hilbert space · Banach space · PDE state space · operator family · finite-state system · computational representation

Type information constrains which Interactions, Operations, Interfaces, exports, and re-entry maps are meaningful.

5. Branch State

A state carried by Branch \(B\) may be written

\[ x_B\in X_B. \]

If the admissible state set is

\[ A_B\subseteq X_B, \]

then lawful Branch states satisfy

\[ \boxed{ x_B\in A_B. } \]

The carrier \(X_B\) and the admissible subset \(A_B\) should not be confused.

6. Interaction

An Interaction is a lawful typed mathematical action involving one or more Branches, with multi-Branch interaction being the central case.

For Branches

\[ B_1,\ldots,B_n, \]

a deterministic Set-like representation may be

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

The exact mathematical form of an Interaction is sector-dependent. Stochastic, nondeterministic, quantum, operator-theoretic, geometric, or other sectors may require different morphisms.

7. Interaction Domain

The Interaction domain

\[ D_I \]

records the combinations of Branch states for which the Interaction is actually mathematically defined.

In general,

\[ D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n}, \]

and the inclusion may be strict.

Candidate inputs may fail through:

  • type incompatibility,
  • domain failure,
  • constraint violation,
  • boundary incompatibility,
  • regularity failure,
  • operator-domain failure,
  • Passport failure,
  • or violation of another native law.

8. Interaction Output

For

\[ (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in D_I, \]

the Interaction produces

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I. \]

The Interaction result may later:

  • remain an intermediate state,
  • be examined by Leaf extraction maps,
  • contribute to a Tree-level Response,
  • be exported,
  • enter another lawful Interaction,
  • or terminate without further use.

The raw Interaction output is not automatically a Leaf, Response, or Root merely because it exists.

9. Leaf

A Leaf is extracted information from an Interaction result.

If

\[ z = I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j \]

is a Leaf-extraction map, then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right) \in \mathcal L_j. } \]

The symbols have distinct roles:

  • \(\mathcal L_j\) is the Leaf space or codomain;
  • \(\ell_j\) is the extraction map;
  • \(\lambda_j\) is the extracted Leaf value.

10. Multiple Leaves

One Interaction result may support several extraction maps:

\[ \ell_1, \ell_2, \ldots, \ell_m. \]

These produce

\[ \lambda_1=\ell_1(z), \quad \lambda_2=\ell_2(z), \quad \ldots, \quad \lambda_m=\ell_m(z). \]

Different Leaves may expose different mathematical information.

A Leaf need not contain the full Interaction result and is not automatically sufficient for any later task.

11. Leaf Sufficiency

Suppose a later deterministic task is

\[ T: Z_I \longrightarrow W \]

and a Leaf extraction is

\[ \ell: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L. \]

The Leaf representation is sufficient for \(T\) when there exists

\[ \widetilde T: \ell(Z_I) \longrightarrow W \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ T = \widetilde T\circ\ell. } \]

Equivalently,

\[ \ell(z)=\ell(z') \Longrightarrow T(z)=T(z'). \]

12. Tree

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches, Interactions, Interfaces, and intermediate information through which combined computation is performed and a Response is generated.

A Tree may carry:

  • Branches,
  • Interactions,
  • Leaves,
  • Passports,
  • Interfaces,
  • intermediate representations,
  • constraints,
  • and other required mathematical information.

A Tree is not the same object as its Response.

13. Sequential Tree as a Special Case

A simple sequential Tree may have

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{I_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{I_2} X_2 \xrightarrow{} \cdots \xrightarrow{I_m} X_m. \]

Where all compositions are defined, its combined map may be

\[ \mathcal R_T = I_m \circ I_{m-1} \circ \cdots \circ I_1. \]

This is one special realization of a Tree, not the universal definition.

14. General Tree

More general Trees may involve:

parallel composition · products · pullbacks · equalizers · shared constraints · feedback · conditional Operations · recursive structure · stochastic transitions · coupled systems · hyperinteractions · multiple outputs · Roots · re-entry

Such Trees may require mathematical structures other than ordinary sequential function composition.

15. Response

A Response is the typed mathematical output produced by a Tree or another organized BM computation.

\[ \mathcal R_T: D_T \longrightarrow Y_T. \]

For admissible input \(x\in D_T\),

\[ r = \mathcal R_T(x) \in Y_T. \]

A Response may be:

  • a value,
  • a state,
  • a function,
  • a law,
  • a relation,
  • an operator,
  • a classification,
  • a structured object,
  • a family of objects,
  • or another mathematically typed result.

A Response is therefore not restricted to being law-valued.

16. Response Versus Root

A Response may terminate the computation.

It assumes the role of a Root only when downstream admissibility has been established.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal mathematical result},\\[4pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]

Producing a Response does not prove that any later mathematical structure accepts it.

17. Root

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Root status may require:

  • a receiving structure,
  • type compatibility,
  • law compatibility,
  • interface compatibility,
  • Passport sufficiency,
  • and satisfaction of the receiving domain.

Root status does not imply recursion, conservation, invertibility, physical realization, or indefinite continuation.

18. Rooting

Rooting is an active candidate BM structure describing lawful attachment of a Root, compatible Branch, or compatible law-bearing structure to a mathematical attachment site from which further lawful development may occur.

\[ \mathcal A: D_{\mathcal A} \subseteq X\times X_R \longrightarrow X'. \]

Attachment is lawful only when

\[ (x,r)\in D_{\mathcal A}. \]

An attachment site may be terminal or interior when the underlying mathematics permits that attachment.

Rooting remains active candidate machinery. It is not assumed to occur in every BM construction.

19. Rebranching

Rebranching is the candidate generation of new lawful Branch development from a site created or activated by Rooting.

Rooting does not guarantee Rebranching. A valid attachment may still terminate if no admissible downstream structure exists.

20. Repeated Rooting

Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.

If an iterative Root transformation is actually defined on a closed or invariant Root state space,

\[ F: X_R \longrightarrow X_R, \]

and all required iterates remain admissible, then

\[ R_{n+1} = F(R_n) \]

defines an ordinary iterated system.

Fixed points, stability, cycles, contraction theory, Lyapunov analysis, or other dynamical tools may then be applied when their assumptions are satisfied.

21. Passport

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, law context, admissibility requirements, interface requirements, or task-relevant information that must remain available for later mathematical use.

Passports are active structures in both BM and GURF.

Calling information a Passport does not prove that it is sufficient.

22. Root Passport Transfer

Root Passport Transfer is an active candidate BM structure describing typed persistence of required Passport information from a source structure into a Root and onward into a compatible receiver.

\[ P_{\mathrm{source}} \longrightarrow P_R \longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{receiver}}. \]

These arrows are schematic. Actual transfer requires defined maps and explicit preservation conditions.

23. Seed

A Seed is a candidate BM primitive representing information that does not directly export a Root but undergoes a lawful generative or self-seeding process until a specified export condition is reached.

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \]

followed, when a declared export condition is satisfied, by

\[ S_n \longrightarrow R. \]

A Seed is not merely initial data.

A Seed State is not automatically a Branch, Leaf, Response, or Root. Each of those BM roles has separate requirements.

24. Operation

An Operation is a typed lawful mathematical transformation.

A unary Operation may have the form

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y. \]

An \(n\)-input Operation may have the form

\[ O: D_O \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

Operations are broader than multi-Branch Interactions.

25. Operation Contract

A BM Operation should identify at least:

  • input type,
  • output type,
  • admissible domain,
  • required laws,
  • required constraints,
  • Passport requirements when relevant,
  • preservation claims,
  • and failure conditions.

A written symbolic rule without these requirements may fail to define a lawful BM Operation.

26. Admissibility

Admissibility determines whether a proposed mathematical object, input, transformation, attachment, interface, or continuation is actually permitted by the participating mathematics.

For

\[ F: D_F \longrightarrow Y, \]

inputs outside \(D_F\) are not authorized merely because the written formula can be evaluated formally.

27. Composition

Suppose

\[ F: D_F \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y \]

and

\[ G: D_G \subseteq Y \longrightarrow Z. \]

The actual domain of the composite is

\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \left\{ x\in D_F: F(x)\in D_G \right\}. } \]

Hence

\[ G\circ F: D_{G\circ F} \longrightarrow Z. \]

28. Typed Composability

A basic requirement for direct composition is that the output of \(F\) be accepted by the mathematical type required by \(G\).

This may occur through:

  • literal matching types,
  • a lawful inclusion,
  • a coercion whose mathematics is defined,
  • an interface map,
  • or another explicitly justified conversion.

Matching notation is not proof of type compatibility.

29. Interface

An Interface is a typed compatibility structure through which mathematical information passes between Branches, representations, or other BM structures.

Consider

\[ F: X_1 \longrightarrow X_2, \] \[ P_1: X_1 \longrightarrow Q_1, \] \[ P_2: X_2 \longrightarrow Q_2. \]

An induced represented transformation may be

\[ G: Q_1 \longrightarrow Q_2. \]

30. Exact Interface Compatibility

In a deterministic setting where literal equality is appropriate, exact compatibility may require

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

This says that transformation before representation and transformation after representation agree.

31. Approximate Interface Compatibility

If the comparison space carries a justified metric or discrepancy

\[ d: Q_2\times Q_2 \longrightarrow [0,\infty), \]

an approximate interface requirement may be

\[ d \left( P_2(F(x)), G(P_1(x)) \right) \le \varepsilon. \]

The metric, norm, divergence, tolerance, and domain over which the comparison is required must be declared.

32. Interface Defect

When a common comparison metric \(d\) exists, define

\[ \boxed{ \Delta(x) = d \left( P_2(F(x)), G(P_1(x)) \right). } \]

Exact compatibility corresponds to

\[ \Delta(x)=0. \]

A scalar interface defect is meaningful only after a justified comparison structure has been chosen.

33. Configuration

A Configuration records the active mathematical state of a BM construction.

A general organizational record may be written

\[ \mathcal X = \left( \mathcal B, \mathcal I, \mathcal L, \mathcal R, \mathcal P, \mathcal O, \mathcal A \right), \]

where components may record:

  • active Branches,
  • Interactions,
  • Leaves,
  • Responses or Roots,
  • Passports,
  • Operations,
  • and admissibility data.

The exact Configuration structure is problem-dependent.

34. Configuration State

A concrete state may contain

\[ x_{\mathcal X} = \left( x_{B_1}, \ldots, x_{B_n}, r_1, \ldots, p_1, \ldots \right). \]

This notation is schematic. A specific BM model must supply the actual product, fibre, category, manifold, state space, or other structure used.

35. Configuration Update

A BM update may be represented by

\[ U: \mathcal X \longrightarrow \mathcal X'. \]

An update may:

activate a Branch · produce a Leaf · generate a Response · establish a Root · modify a Passport · apply an Operation · rebind an Interface · update admissibility · change the active law or regime

36. Transactional Operation

Some BM Operations may be modeled transactionally.

If \(O\) acts on Configuration \(\mathcal X\), then

\[ O(\mathcal X) = \mathcal X' \]

only when all required checks succeed.

Otherwise the Operation should fail explicitly rather than silently produce an invalid Configuration.

37. Network

A BM Network is a typed collection of Branches, Interactions, Interfaces, paths, Roots, Passports, Operations, and dependencies whose lawful connectivity need not be represented by one linear sequence.

Depending on the mathematics, a Network may be modeled by:

  • a graph,
  • a directed acyclic graph,
  • a hypergraph,
  • a category,
  • a dependency network,
  • an operator network,
  • or another structured object.

The term Network does not require one particular graph-theoretic representation.

38. Hyperinteraction

When several Branches participate simultaneously, a graph-like model may require a hyperedge

\[ e = \{B_1,\ldots,B_k\}. \]

An associated Interaction may be

\[ I_e: D_e \subseteq \prod_{j=1}^{k} X_{B_j} \longrightarrow Z_e. \]

Hypergraph notation is an available representation, not a universal ontology for all BM Interactions.

39. Typed Path

A Typed Path is a sequence of mathematically compatible transitions through a BM structure.

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{F_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{F_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{F_n} X_n. \]

The path is lawful only when every transition is typed and every intermediate state satisfies the next Operation's admissibility conditions.

40. Path Composite

A lawful deterministic path may induce

\[ F_{\mathrm{path}} = F_n \circ \cdots \circ F_1. \]

The actual domain of \(F_{\mathrm{path}}\) is determined by every intermediate admissibility condition.

41. Parallel Paths

Suppose two lawful paths connect corresponding source and target structures:

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y, \qquad G: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

BM asks whether the paths agree under the comparison relation appropriate to the mathematical sector.

Literal equality is only one possibility.

42. Path Coherence

Relative to a declared equivalence or comparison relation \(\approx\), relevant parallel paths may be required to satisfy

\[ \boxed{ F_{\mathrm{path\,1}}(x) \approx F_{\mathrm{path\,2}}(x). } \]

Depending on the sector, \(\approx\) may mean:

  • literal equality,
  • isomorphism,
  • natural isomorphism,
  • homotopy,
  • gauge equivalence,
  • equality almost surely,
  • equality in distribution,
  • or controlled approximation.

43. Commutativity

Two Operations commute when

\[ \boxed{ F\circ G = G\circ F } \]

wherever both sides are defined.

BM does not assume commutativity.

44. Noncommuting Operations

If

\[ F\circ G \neq G\circ F, \]

then Operation order carries mathematical information.

This is especially relevant to:

differentiation · filtering · localization · projection · reconstruction · coarse-graining · intervention · evolution · quotienting

45. Equalizer-Type Compatibility

For

\[ F,G: X \longrightarrow Y, \]

the equalizer set is

\[ \boxed{ E(F,G) = \left\{ x\in X: F(x)=G(x) \right\}. } \]

This identifies states on which the two maps agree exactly.

46. Pullback-Type Compatibility

Suppose

\[ f: X \longrightarrow Z \]

and

\[ g: Y \longrightarrow Z. \]

The Set-level pullback is

\[ \boxed{ X\times_ZY = \left\{ (x,y)\in X\times Y: f(x)=g(y) \right\}. } \]

In richer categories, the appropriate categorical pullback must be used rather than assuming the Set-level formula is sufficient.

47. Coequalizer-Type Identification

Given

\[ f,g: X \longrightarrow Y, \]

a coequalizing map \(q\) satisfies

\[ q\circ f = q\circ g. \]

Such constructions can encode lawful identification of outputs when the native mathematical category admits the required coequalizer.

48. Quotient

An equivalence relation

\[ \sim \]

on \(X\) produces a Set-level quotient

\[ X/{\sim}. \]

Set-level quotienting alone does not prove that all original mathematical structure descends to the quotient.

49. Structured Quotient

For an \(n\)-ary algebraic Operation

\[ \sigma: X^n \longrightarrow X, \]

compatibility requires

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim y_i \quad \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

This is the usual congruence requirement in the relevant algebraic sector.

Other mathematical sectors require their own quotient-existence conditions.

50. Mathematical Realization

A formal BM specification may be described before a concrete realization is known.

If \(S\) is an abstract specification, a realization may be represented schematically by

\[ \rho: S \longrightarrow X. \]

The resulting object must actually satisfy the native requirements of \(X\).

\[ \boxed{ \text{formal specification} \not\Rightarrow \text{mathematical existence}. } \]

51. Realization Failure

Mathematical realization may fail because:

  • constraints conflict,
  • the admissible set is empty,
  • required limits do not exist,
  • regularity fails,
  • boundary conditions are incompatible,
  • positivity fails,
  • topology is incompatible,
  • algebraic conditions cannot be satisfied,
  • or nonexistence is proved.

52. Physical Realization

Mathematical realization and physical realization are distinct.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematically realizable} \not\Rightarrow \text{physically realizable}. } \]

Physical realization additionally depends on validated physical law, causal conditions, materials, parameter ranges, stability, initial and boundary conditions, implementation limits, and empirical evidence.

53. Export

Export transfers mathematical information from one compatible context into another representation.

\[ E: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Export may preserve all information or only selected information.

The preservation claim must be declared.

54. Re-entry

Re-entry inserts exported or transformed information into a receiving mathematical structure.

\[ X \xrightarrow{E} Y \xrightarrow{R} Z. \]

Successful export does not automatically establish lawful re-entry.

55. Re-entry Coherence

Suppose a direct transformation is

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Z. \]

A strong deterministic coherence condition is

\[ \boxed{ R\circ E = F. } \]

Other sectors may require a weaker but explicitly defined notion of equivalence.

56. Re-entry Loss

If deterministic export identifies distinct source states,

\[ E(x) = E(x') \qquad \text{for some } x\neq x', \]

then deterministic re-entry cannot reconstruct that distinction using \(E(x)\) alone.

Additional information may be required.

57. Local Versus Global Structure

A BM structure may be lawful locally without extending globally.

Let

\[ U_i \subseteq X \]

be local domains and let

\[ s_i \in \mathcal S(U_i) \]

be local structures.

Global realization may require compatibility on overlaps

\[ U_i\cap U_j. \]

58. Gluing

Where restriction maps are available, a standard compatibility requirement may be

\[ s_i \big|_{U_i\cap U_j} = s_j \big|_{U_i\cap U_j}. \]

Under appropriate mathematical hypotheses, compatible local pieces may admit a global object.

BM does not assume that pairwise agreement automatically guarantees global gluing in every mathematical category.

59. Coherence

Coherence asks whether different lawful constructions intended to represent the same mathematical process agree under the relevant comparison relation.

For example,

\[ F_2\circ F_1 \approx G_2\circ G_1. \]

The meaning of \(\approx\) must come from the mathematical sector.

60. Higher Coherence

Large BM Networks may contain many overlapping paths, transformations, interfaces, equivalences, and compositions.

Pairwise coherence may not guarantee global compatibility.

Higher coherence studies compatibility among the compatibility relations themselves.

Status: active formal development.

61. Law Space

A Law Space is a mathematical space or indexed family representing laws, operators, parameterized rule sets, regimes, constraints, or other governing structures relevant to a BM construction.

\[ \lambda \in \mathcal L. \]

The mathematical structure of \(\mathcal L\) is native to the problem.

It may be discrete, topological, geometric, algebraic, functional, probabilistic, operator-valued, or otherwise structured.

Calling something a Law Space does not imply that a metric, topology, probability law, interpolation rule, derivative, or algebraic Operation automatically exists on it.

62. Conservation

Suppose

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X \]

is an evolution and

\[ C: X \longrightarrow V. \]

\(C\) is conserved when

\[ \boxed{ C(\Phi_t(x)) = C(x) } \]

throughout the stated evolution domain.

BM may preserve, transport, or audit a conservation law established by the native mathematics.

BM does not independently create energy, momentum, charge, probability, or another conserved quantity.

63. Conservative Passport

A Conservative Passport is an active candidate research structure for tracking a quantity that the native mathematics independently requires to persist across a transformation even when the active representation does not retain that quantity explicitly.

A Conservative Passport records conservation requirements. It does not prove or create the conservation law.

64. Deterministic Structural Semantics

A deterministic transformation may use a function

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Each admissible input has one specified output.

Deterministic fibre and factorization arguments should not be silently transferred to other semantic sectors.

65. Stochastic Structural Semantics

A stochastic Branch or Operation may instead use a Markov kernel

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

For a measurable test function \(\varphi\),

\[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E \left[ \varphi(Y) \mid X=x \right]. \]

Stochastic composition, sufficiency, equivalence, and preservation must respect stochastic semantics.

66. Nondeterministic Structural Semantics

A nondeterministic relation may be represented by

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

Its reachable set is

\[ R(x) = \left\{ y\in Y: xRy \right\}. \]

Existential and universal conditions must remain distinct:

\[ \Diamond_R(U) = \left\{ x: \exists y\in U \text{ with } xRy \right\}, \] \[ \Box_R(U) = \left\{ x: \forall y,\, xRy \Rightarrow y\in U \right\}. \]

67. Finite-Arity Structures

A finite family of participating Branches may admit

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_1\times\cdots\times X_n \longrightarrow Y. \]

Finite arity does not remove the need to specify the actual admissible domain.

68. Indexed or Infinite Structures

For an indexed family

\[ \{X_i\}_{i\in I}, \]

one may formally encounter

\[ \prod_{i\in I}X_i. \]

Infinite constructions require whatever mathematical conditions the native sector demands:

  • existence of products or limits,
  • topology,
  • convergence,
  • completeness,
  • measurability,
  • compactness,
  • continuity,
  • or other required structure.

69. Structure Preservation

A map

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y \]

is structure-preserving only relative to a specified structure.

For a binary Operation \(\star\), one possible requirement is

\[ \boxed{ F(x\star y) = F(x)\star'F(y). } \]

Other structures require different preservation equations.

70. Invariant Preservation

Let

\[ C_X: X \longrightarrow V \]

and

\[ C_Y: Y \longrightarrow V. \]

Transformation \(F:X\to Y\) preserves the declared invariant when

\[ \boxed{ C_Y(F(x)) = C_X(x). } \]

71. Structural Failure Modes

Failure Meaning
Type failure Output is not accepted by the receiving mathematical type.
Domain failure Input lies outside an admissible Operation or Interaction domain.
Passport failure Required information, distinctions, laws, or conditions are unavailable.
Law failure Native equations, constraints, or structural laws are violated.
Path incoherence Paths disagree where declared coherence requires agreement.
Quotient failure The proposed equivalence does not support required quotient structure.
Realization failure The formal specification has no admissible mathematical realization.
Export failure Required information or structure cannot be lawfully exported.
Re-entry failure The receiving structure cannot lawfully interpret the exported object.
Root failure A Response lacks the conditions needed for downstream participation.
Rooting failure A proposed attachment violates type, law, interface, Passport, or attachment-site requirements.
Gluing failure Local structures cannot be consistently assembled into the proposed global structure.

72. Worked Example — Typed Composition

Let

\[ F: \mathbb R \longrightarrow \mathbb R^2 \]

be

\[ F(x) = (x,x^2), \]

and let

\[ G: \mathbb R^2 \longrightarrow \mathbb R \]

be

\[ G(u,v) = u+v. \]

Then

\[ (G\circ F)(x) = x+x^2. \]

The composition is well typed because \(F(x)\in\mathbb R^2\), which is accepted by \(G\).

73. Worked Example — Domain Restriction

Let

\[ G(u,v) = \sqrt v \]

over the real numbers.

Its admissible domain is

\[ D_G = \left\{ (u,v)\in\mathbb R^2: v\ge0 \right\}. \]

If

\[ F(x) = (x,x^2), \]

then

\[ F(x)\in D_G \qquad \forall x\in\mathbb R. \]

Hence \(G\circ F\) is defined for every real \(x\).

74. Worked Example — Operation Order

Let

\[ F(x) = x+1 \]

and

\[ G(x) = 2x. \]

Then

\[ (F\circ G)(x) = 2x+1, \]

while

\[ (G\circ F)(x) = 2x+2. \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{ F\circ G \neq G\circ F. } \]

75. Quantitative Structural Diagnostics

When a justified metric or discrepancy exists, structural compatibility may be quantified.

For parallel paths \(F\) and \(G\), define

\[ \Delta(x) = d(F(x),G(x)). \]

Exact coherence gives

\[ \Delta(x)=0. \]

Approximate coherence may require

\[ \Delta(x) \le \varepsilon. \]

Such diagnostics support genuine quantitative figures over state and parameter spaces.

76. Scientific Visualization

Structural mathematics may generate quantitative figures such as:

interface defect versus state · path error versus parameter · invariant drift · convergence of valid iterations · admissibility regions · stability regions · network response magnitude · condition numbers · parameter sweeps

These are equation-driven scientific figures.

They are distinct from formal BM structural diagrams, which remain unpublished until their canonical relationships are formally reconstructed and approved.

79. Structural Role Separation

Structure What It Does What It Does Not Automatically Mean
Branch Carries typed mathematical information and structure. Does not replace its native mathematics.
Interaction Performs a lawful typed mathematical action. Does not guarantee commutativity or invertibility.
Leaf Extracts information from an Interaction result. Does not imply sufficiency or Root status.
Tree Organizes combined computation. Is not the same object as its Response.
Response Is a typed output of organized computation. Need not be a law and need not be a Root.
Root Supports verified downstream mathematical participation. Does not imply recursion, conservation, or universal compatibility.
Passport Tracks required mathematical information. Does not by itself prove sufficiency.
Seed Candidate generative structure preceding conditional Root export. Is not merely initial data or automatically a Root.

80. Central Structural Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Every mathematical object keeps its type;}\\[4pt] \text{every Operation keeps its law and domain;}\\[4pt] \text{every Interface must justify passage between structures;}\\[4pt] \text{and every downstream reuse must establish admissibility.} \end{gathered} } \]

BM does not authorize mathematical participation by terminology alone.

Calling something a Branch does not make invalid mathematics lawful. Calling something a Leaf does not prove provenance unless an Interaction and extraction are specified. Calling an output a Root does not establish downstream compatibility. Calling several Operations a Tree does not establish coherence.
