Structural Registry

The architecture of Branch Mathematics: what structures participate in a problem, how native mathematics remains intact, how dependencies and realization are represented, where lawfulness is checked, how failure is localized and repaired, and how mathematical results are exported and reused.

1. How to Read the Structural Registry

The Structural Registry describes how Branch Mathematics is assembled. It does not replace algebra, analysis, geometry, topology, probability, computation, PDE theory, operator theory, category theory, or any other native mathematics appearing inside the problem.

\[ \boxed{ \text{native mathematics} + \text{BM structural role} = \text{lawful participation in the larger problem} } \]

BM role names identify how mathematical objects participate. They do not create new mathematical properties by themselves.

Role-erasure audit. If a claimed theorem loses all mathematical content after deleting words such as Branch, Tree, Leaf, Root, Passport, or Seed, then the theorem has not yet been mathematically justified.

2. Full BM Architecture

BM is better represented as a structured system than as one linear chain.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{Problem Scope and Obligations}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Branches carrying native mathematics}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Operations + Interactions + Interfaces + Passports}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Leaves + provenance + law/regime context}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Configuration + semantic dependency structure}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{local/global realization + coherence + feedback}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Tree = selected lawful realization}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Response}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{Export}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{receiver-relative Root}\\[4pt] \downarrow\\[4pt] \text{terminal use or lawful re-entry} \end{array} } \]

Orthogonal specialized directions include Law Space, refinement, abstraction, structured quotients, Defect Calculus, repair, information persistence, and higher coherence.

This diagram is organizational. Real BM problems may contain several parallel paths, multiple Responses, cyclic dependency, feedback, heterogeneous native sectors, no Root, several Roots, or multiple lawful realizations.

3. Structural Layers

Problem Layer

Declares the mathematical scope, terminal obligations, and dependency closure of what the problem claims to preserve or solve.

Native Carrier Layer

Branches carry mathematical types, states, laws, admissibility, Operations, and relevant obligations.

Transformation Layer

Operations perform lawful transformations. Interactions are Operations or native relations carrying a joint-participation role.

Interface Layer

Interfaces mediate transport, comparison, discretization, boundary matching, coupling, or cross-sector communication.

Obligation Layer

Contracts specify reusable obligation schemas. Passports carry instantiated conditions for actual roles and transitions.

Derivation Layer

Leaves record selected derived results with sufficient provenance and continuation context.

Configuration Layer

Configurations coordinate all active structures before or during realization analysis.

Realization Layer

Networks represent dependency; realization families determine whether the full mathematical system can exist coherently.

Output Layer

Trees record selected lawful realizations. Responses record the typed outputs of those computations.

Continuation Layer

Exports produce receiver-directed data. Roothood determines whether the export is admissible for the declared receiver.

Failure Layer

Defects and obstructions localize failure. Repair acts only through an admissible repair class.

Recursive Layer

Roots may re-enter later mathematics. Rooting, Rebranching, and Seed remain candidate specialized structures.

5. Problem Scope

BM architecture begins with a declared mathematical problem scope:

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak S_P } \]

This identifies what the analysis actually claims to preserve, solve, compare, certify, or continue.

Scope may differ between:

  • present-state evaluation;
  • one-step continuation;
  • finite-horizon evolution;
  • infinite-horizon evolution;
  • receiver-specific export;
  • reconstruction;
  • obstruction detection.
A result proved for one scope is not automatically valid for a larger scope.

6. Obligation Family

Terminal obligations are collected in \[ \mathscr T_P. \]

The active family is obtained by semantic dependency closure:

\[ \boxed{ \mathscr Q_P = \operatorname{ReqCl}_{\mathfrak D_C} (\mathscr T_P). } \]

The obligation family may include:

  • Tasks;
  • future continuation;
  • laws or regimes;
  • comparison requirements;
  • receiver conditions;
  • convergence obligations;
  • witness conditions;
  • reconstruction or inverse conditions;
  • information distinctions;
  • certification targets.
Scope and obligation families cannot be intentionally weakened solely to make a chosen representation appear sufficient.

7. Branch

A Branch is a mathematically distinct focus participating in the declared problem.

\[ \boxed{ B= \left( \mathsf S_B, \tau_B, X_B, A_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, \mathcal P_B \right). } \]
Component Structural role
\(\mathsf S_B\) Native mathematical sector.
\(\tau_B\) Type information required for lawful use.
\(X_B\) Ambient carrier or sector-correct state object.
\(A_B\) Admissible region or admissibility structure.
\(\mathcal L_B\) Native laws, invariants, equations, or constraints.
\(\mathcal O_B\) Native Operations.
\(\mathcal P_B\) Passport / Interface obligations associated with the Branch.
The underlying Branch object may be completely standard mathematics. BM records enough structure to prevent later mathematics from silently changing what that object means.

8. Branch Essentiality

A mathematical quantity does not earn separate Branch status merely by being named.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch separation is justified when collapsing it changes required mathematics.} } \]

In deterministic factorization form, if coarsening \(q\) removes the proposed Branch distinction, the separation is witnessed as necessary when some required obligation \(T\) cannot satisfy

\[ T = \widetilde T \circ q \circ R_{\mathcal B}. \]

This creates two important structural firewalls:

\[ \boxed{ \text{no arbitrary Branch explosion} } \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{no destructive under-branching} } \]

9. Operation

An Operation is the general BM role of a typed lawful transformation or native relation.

\[ \boxed{ O: D_O \subseteq X \to Y. } \]

is the deterministic form.

Other sectors may use:

  • relations;
  • set-valued maps;
  • Markov kernels;
  • quantum channels;
  • differential operators;
  • optimization operators;
  • rewrite systems;
  • sector-specific transformations.

True serial domain

\[ F:D_F\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad G:D_G\subseteq Y\to Z, \]
\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

10. Interaction

An Interaction is an Operation or native relation carrying a declared joint-participation role.

\[ \boxed{ I: D_I \subseteq A_{B_1}\times\cdots\times A_{B_n} \to Z_I. } \]

is the deterministic multi-input form.

The joint domain \(D_I\) may enforce:

  • shared-state identities;
  • boundary compatibility;
  • coupling relations;
  • common regime;
  • common or coupled witnesses;
  • regularity conditions;
  • Interface obligations.
The ambient Cartesian product does not imply that every tuple is a lawful interacting state.

11. Interface

Interfaces connect mathematical structures without requiring those structures to share one native semantics.

Examples include:

  • maps;
  • relations;
  • spans;
  • correspondences;
  • embeddings;
  • projections;
  • discretization maps;
  • boundary maps;
  • comparison maps;
  • transport laws.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Interface} \not\Rightarrow \text{automatic preservation}. } \]

Preservation properties require explicit hypotheses or specialized Interface subclasses.

12. Passport

A Passport is an instantiated typed obligation family associated with an actual role, transition, or receiver.

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal P^\rho = \{\varphi_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in A}. } \]

Passports can carry:

  • admissibility requirements;
  • law/regime context;
  • coupling conditions;
  • witness scope;
  • continuation requirements;
  • reconstruction conditions;
  • error bounds;
  • receiver obligations.

Applicability

\[ \operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P) \subseteq D_{\mathcal P}. \]
\[ \boxed{ x\notin D_{\mathcal P} \neq x\in D_{\mathcal P} \setminus \operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P). } \]

The first is a type/applicability failure. The second is an actual Passport rejection.

13. Leaf

A Leaf is a selected derived result retained for later use.

\[ z=I(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \qquad \boxed{ L=\ell(z). } \]

A Leaf may carry an equation, invariant, residual, classification, coefficient, local solution, spectral quantity, or other derived structure.

A Leaf is a structural/provenance role. It is not automatically a new Branch, Root, or terminal result.

14. Provenance and History

BM separates derivational provenance from state/history context.

\[ \boxed{ \Pi^{\mathrm{der}} = \text{how the result was derived or certified} } \]
\[ \boxed{ H = \text{path-dependent state/history required for continuation} } \]

The distinction matters because a future calculation may require actual path-dependent state while not requiring the complete proof history of every earlier result.

Retain exactly the provenance and history that future mathematics needs.

15. Configuration

A Configuration is the coordinated BM specification containing the active mathematical objects, roles, Branches, Operations, Interactions, Interfaces, Passports, laws, dependencies, provenance, and other declared structural information.

\[ \boxed{ C= \text{coordinated BM specification of the current problem state}. } \]

A Configuration may exist before global realization has been established.

Configuration is a specification structure. It is not automatically a solution.

16. Configuration Status Ladder

Status Structural meaning
Candidate Proposed Configuration. No mathematical validity is implied.
Well formed Types, applicability, semantic syntax, and Interface signatures are coherent.
Locally admissible Every declared local region admits at least one realization.
Globally realizable The complete global realization family is nonempty.
Realized Tree A particular lawful realization has been selected and recorded.
\[ \boxed{ \text{local admissibility} \not\Rightarrow \text{global realizability}. } \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{global realizability} \not\Rightarrow \text{unique realization}. } \]

17. Semantic Dependency Structure

The semantic dependency structure records which mathematical objects, conditions, and results actually depend on which others.

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak D_C } \]

may be represented as:

  • a graph;
  • a directed acyclic graph;
  • a hypergraph;
  • a factor graph;
  • a variable–constraint incidence structure;
  • a category;
  • an operator network;
  • another suitable representation.

Dependency closure

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(S) } \]

denotes the affected region generated by a set \(S\).

In a feedback component, closure may include an entire strongly connected component rather than only naive descendants.

18. Network

A Network is a chosen mathematical representation of the semantic dependency/incidence structure.

A Network may contain:

  • parallel paths;
  • branching;
  • merging;
  • shared variables;
  • hyperedges;
  • feedback;
  • cycles;
  • global constraints;
  • several local Trees;
  • multiple Responses.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Network} \neq \text{realization}. } \]

19. Realization Family

The realization family contains every lawful global realization of the declared Configuration under context \(c\).

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F(C;c). } \]

Global realizability is:

\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{GR}(C;c) \iff \mathfrak F(C;c)\neq\varnothing. } \]

Realizations must satisfy all applicable:

  • Branch admissibility;
  • Operation domains;
  • Interaction domains;
  • Interfaces;
  • Passports;
  • law/regime conditions;
  • witness and coupling requirements;
  • coherence conditions;
  • global constraints;
  • feedback closure.
Failure to find an element of \(\mathfrak F(C;c)\) does not prove that the family is empty.

20. Tree

A Tree is a Configuration together with one selected lawful realization and the context required to interpret it.

\[ \boxed{ T= (C,\xi,\Pi,\omega,\Lambda), \qquad \xi\in\mathfrak F(C;c). } \]

The Tree may retain:

  • the active Configuration \(C\);
  • selected realization \(\xi\);
  • relevant provenance \(\Pi\);
  • order/dependency information \(\omega\);
  • law/regime context \(\Lambda\).
  • A BM Tree need not be a graph-theoretic tree.
  • A Network is not a Tree until a lawful realization is selected.
  • A Tree is not identical to its Response.

21. Response

A Response is the typed output of a declared BM computation.

\[ \boxed{ T \xrightarrow{\mathcal E} Y. } \]

Depending on the native sector, \(Y\) may be:

  • a value;
  • a set;
  • a relation;
  • a distribution;
  • an operator;
  • a function;
  • a law family;
  • a geometric object;
  • a certificate;
  • an obstruction.
A Response need not be reusable downstream.

22. Export

Export is a receiver-directed transformation of a Response.

\[ \boxed{ E_r: D_{E_r} \subseteq \mathcal Y_T \to \mathcal R_r. } \]
\[ \boxed{ r=E_r(Y). } \]

In exact deterministic semantics, receiver sufficiency may be expressed by

\[ \boxed{ T_r = \widetilde T_r \circ E_r. } \]
Export may discard information only relative to the declared receiver obligations. A valid export need not be minimal.

23. Root

A Root is an exported result admitted by a specified receiver.

\[ \boxed{ r\text{ is a Root for }B_r \iff r \in \operatorname{Acc} (\mathcal P_R^{B_r}). } \]

Roothood therefore depends on:

  • receiver type;
  • Root Passport applicability;
  • receiver admissibility;
  • required law/regime context;
  • required continuation information.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Root for }B_1 \not\Rightarrow \text{Root for }B_2. } \]
Root does not imply recurrence, convergence, conservation, uniqueness, physical realization, or automatic creation of another Branch.

24. Re-entry

Re-entry is lawful downstream participation of an accepted export.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \to \text{Response} \to \text{Export} \to \text{Root} \to \text{receiver} \to \text{later Configuration}. } \]

Re-entry may require:

  • re-Passporting;
  • law/regime update;
  • revalidation;
  • downstream recomputation;
  • repair;
  • new Branch separation if mathematically justified.
Re-entry is general BM architecture. Rooting and Rebranching are more specialized candidate structures.

25. Law Space

Law Space is a specialized BM structure used when the active law or regime itself is a mathematically relevant distinction.

\[ \lambda\in\mathscr L, \qquad \mathcal L_\lambda = \text{active law at }\lambda. \]

Law assignment may be multivalued:

\[ \boxed{ \Lambda: \Omega_{\mathscr L} \rightrightarrows \mathscr L. } \]

If

\[ |\Lambda(C)|>1, \]

the architecture must preserve that multiplicity or use an independently justified selector.

Law Space is not mandatory when the law is fixed and carries no separate mathematical role.

26. Coherence

Coherence compares lawful parallel mathematical paths only after their domains and comparison semantics have been declared.

Domain coherence

\[ x \in D_{P_1} \cap D_{P_2}. \]

Comparison target

\[ q_1:Y_1\to Q, \qquad q_2:Y_2\to Q. \]

Exact coherence

\[ \boxed{ q_1P_1(x) = q_2P_2(x). } \]

Approximate coherence

\[ d_Q \big( q_1P_1(x), q_2P_2(x) \big) \le \varepsilon. \]
Comparison maps cannot be selected after the fact merely to manufacture agreement.

27. Higher Coherence

\[ \boxed{ \text{pairwise coherence} \not\Rightarrow \text{global coherence}. } \]

Higher-order consistency may require:

  • CSP consistency;
  • cycle-space conditions;
  • descent or sheaf conditions;
  • cohomological obstructions;
  • categorical coherence;
  • analytic compatibility;
  • sector-specific gluing mathematics.
Use the simplest established mathematics that exactly matches the active sector.

28. Feedback

A cycle represents dependency. It does not by itself define a solution.

\[ \boxed{ \text{cycle} \neq \text{realization}. } \]

For a feedback component \(C_f\), declare its native realization family:

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F(C_f). } \]

A fixed point

\[ x=\Theta(x) \]

is one possible sector.

Other feedback semantics may involve:

  • differential equations;
  • delay systems;
  • periodic trajectories;
  • stochastic recursions;
  • relational closure;
  • optimization conditions.
Multiple feedback realizations require preserved multiplicity or an explicit selection rule.

29. Defect

A Defect is tied to an actual mathematical requirement \(\Psi\).

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Def}_\Psi. } \]

Depending on the sector, a defect may be:

  • a failure set;
  • a residual;
  • a norm;
  • a rank defect;
  • an operator;
  • a relation;
  • a geometric obstruction;
  • a cohomology class;
  • another native object.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Defect Calculus} \neq \text{one universal defect number}. } \]

30. Repair

Repair is a lawful modification chosen from a declared admissible repair relation.

\[ \boxed{ \mathscr R_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \mathsf{Conf} \times \mathsf{Conf}. } \]

A successful repair must:

  1. belong to the admissible repair class;
  2. restore the failed condition;
  3. preserve every invariant the repair is forbidden to destroy.

Repair invalidation

If region \(S\) is changed, certification throughout

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(S) } \]

must be revalidated unless an invariance theorem proves the result unaffected.

Repair may not be reverse-engineered solely to force a preferred answer.

31. Refinement

Refinement compares two representations or Branch decompositions only after the comparison semantics are fixed.

Deterministic common-source special case:

\[ R_1:X_P\to Y_1, \qquad R_2:X_P\to Y_2, \]
\[ \boxed{ R_1 = A_{21} \circ R_2. } \]

may witness

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal B_1 \preceq_{P,\mathcal T} \mathcal B_2. } \]

With fixed comparison data, this yields a preorder.

  • Different lawful decompositions may be incomparable.
  • Mutual refinement may define an equivalence class.
  • A lattice is used only when meets and joins actually exist.

32. Information Structure

BM treats information structurally through distinctions represented by the active mathematics.

A noninjective representation

\[ F:X\to Y \]

may satisfy

\[ x_1\neq x_2, \qquad F(x_1)=F(x_2). \]

This means the \(Y\)-representation no longer distinguishes those source states.

\[ \boxed{ \text{representation loss} \neq \text{automatic total-system destruction}. } \]

Exact deterministic persistence form

\[ \widehat F:X\to Y\times H \]
\[ \boxed{ \widehat F(x_1) = \widehat F(x_2) \Longrightarrow x_1 \sim_{\mathcal I} x_2. } \]
Stochastic, relational, quantum, and approximate sectors require their own native preservation semantics.

33. Selection Structure

Existence and selection are distinct.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in S \not\Rightarrow \text{canonical }x. } \]

This applies to:

  • multiple realizations;
  • multiple feedback solutions;
  • multiple admissible laws;
  • multiple repairs;
  • multiple witnesses;
  • multiple Roots;
  • multiple inverse reconstructions.

BM preserves multiplicity unless an independently lawful selector is specified.

34. Semantic Status vs Certification

Semantic truth is separate from current mathematical knowledge.

\[ \mathfrak F(C;c) \neq \varnothing \]

may be true even when the current certification state is unresolved.

Allowed certification labels include:

Certified realizable Certified obstructed Unresolved

\[ \boxed{ \text{failure to certify} \neq \text{certification of failure}. } \]

35. Dynamic Closure Structure

Let

\[ \Phi:A\to A, \qquad R:A\to Y, \qquad Y_R=R(A). \]

Exact Dynamic Closure requires

\[ \boxed{ \widetilde\Phi: Y_R \to Y_R } \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi \circ R. } \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{static sufficiency} \not\Rightarrow \text{Dynamic Closure}. } \]

36. Convergence and Approximation Structures

\[ \boxed{ x_n\to x } \]

is not a complete BM statement until the native convergence semantics are declared.

Possible meanings include:

  • metric convergence;
  • norm convergence;
  • weak convergence;
  • weak-* convergence;
  • operator convergence;
  • order convergence;
  • convergence in probability;
  • other native structures.
\[ \boxed{ x\approx y } \]

likewise requires a declared comparison space, error functional or distance/divergence, and tolerance.

38. Formal-Recovery Structures

HEC

HEC is retained as a project-history name for a BM mathematical direction whose complete canonical calculus has not yet been recovered in the current corpus.

HEC-S

HEC-S is retained as a historical internal HEC sector.

Neither HEC nor HEC-S may supply theorem hypotheses, conclusions, Operations, axioms, or deductions merely from their names until the underlying mathematics has been formally recovered.

42. One Object Can Occupy Several Roles

BM structures are roles in a mathematical Configuration, not mutually exclusive ontological species.

Suppose

\[ z=I(x,y). \]

If the whole output is retained as a derived result:

\[ z\models_C\mathrm{Leaf}. \]

If the Tree exports exactly that value:

\[ z\models_C\mathrm{Response}. \]

If receiver \(B_r\) accepts it:

\[ z\models_C\mathrm{Root}_{B_r}. \]

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{ z \models_C \mathrm{Leaf} \land \mathrm{Response} \land \mathrm{Root}_{B_r}. } \]
The mathematics did not multiply. Only the object's structural role changed relative to different parts of the Configuration.

43. Compact Structural Example

Consider a regime-dependent constitutive problem.

\[ \lambda\in\{0,1\}, \qquad k_0=1, \qquad k_1=2. \]

Let

\[ y=k_\lambda x. \]

and feedback

\[ x_{n+1}=3-y_n. \]

The mathematical structure can be represented with:

  • a continuous state Branch carrying \(x\);
  • a Law Space / regime Branch carrying \(\lambda\);
  • a constitutive Interaction producing \(y\);
  • a feedback Operation producing \(x_{n+1}\);
  • a Passport requiring a lawful active regime;
  • a Configuration coordinating the whole system;
  • a realization family solving the coupled equations.

If the proposed export keeps only \(x\), then future evolution may fail to be determined because \(k_\lambda\) is missing.

\[ \boxed{ \text{same }x + \text{different }\lambda \Rightarrow \text{different future continuation}. } \]

Therefore regime information is structurally essential for the continuation scope.

BM did not invent the constitutive equation or feedback algebra. It coordinated why the law distinction had to remain represented in the same problem.

45. Central Structural Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Native mathematical objects retain their own mathematics.}\\[4pt] \text{BM assigns them explicit roles inside one declared problem.}\\[4pt] \text{Operations and Interfaces must have true mathematical domains.}\\[4pt] \text{Passports carry actual obligations rather than narrative metadata.}\\[4pt] \text{Configurations coordinate the active structure.}\\[4pt] \text{Realization establishes whether the combined mathematics can exist.}\\[4pt] \text{Trees record selected lawful realizations.}\\[4pt] \text{Responses become Roots only relative to specific receivers.}\\[4pt] \text{Failure is localized through defects and obstructions.}\\[4pt] \text{Repair must be admissible and forces downstream revalidation.}\\[4pt] \text{Candidate terminology remains candidate until its mathematics is earned.} \end{gathered} } \]
