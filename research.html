1. Purpose of the Research Registry

This page records what is being investigated, what has been formally derived, what remains conditional, and what has not been established.

It is not a list of claims of discovery.

A research program may contain mathematically proved intermediate results without having solved the larger problem that motivated them.

\[ \boxed{ \text{proved intermediate mathematics} \neq \text{solution of the entire research problem} } \]

2. Research Status System

Status Meaning

Conditional valid only when explicitly stated hypotheses hold

Active current research direction

3. Governing Research Rule

Branch Mathematics and GURF organize mathematical reasoning.

They do not override proof requirements in algebra, analysis, probability, geometry, PDE theory, physics, computation, or any other native mathematical domain.

The governing research sequence is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{formalize} \longrightarrow \text{derive} \longrightarrow \text{audit} \longrightarrow \text{test} \longrightarrow \text{compare} } \]

4. Branch Mathematics Foundations

Status: active formal development

The foundational BM program studies lawful representation and transformation across distinct mathematical structures.

Core research areas include:

typed Branches · Interactions · Leaves · Trees · Responses · Passports · Roots · operations · structured quotients · interfaces · realization · export · re-entry · composition · coherence

5. Structured Quotients

Status: active, with proved finitary algebraic core

A central BM question is when observational or Passport equivalence can support a genuine mathematical quotient.

For

\[ A=(X,\Sigma), \]

operation compatibility requires

\[ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad\forall i \] \[ \Longrightarrow \] \[ \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). \]

This finitary algebraic result is recorded as BM-SQ-01.

6. Quotient Generalization Program

Research extends beyond finitary algebra toward other categories of mathematical structure.

Questions include:

topology · measure · probability kernels · operator spaces · dynamical systems · PDE solution spaces · graphs · geometric structures · nondeterministic relations · infinite products and limits

No universal quotient theorem is assumed merely from success in the finitary algebraic sector.

7. Interface and Coherence Program

Status: active

BM studies whether multiple lawful mathematical paths remain compatible.

\[ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1 \]

is one exact interface condition.

Broader research includes:

path closure · parallel-path compatibility · equalizers · pullbacks · coequalizers · gluing · naturality · higher coherence · approximate coherence

8. Root Mathematics

Status: core Root role established internally; extended Rooting mechanisms remain active/candidate research

Root research studies when a mathematical response can become lawful input to additional mathematical structure.

\[ R_{n+1} = F(R_n) \]

is one possible repeated-Root model.

Current questions include:

repeated Rooting · fixed Roots · stability · attachment · rebranching · Root Passport transfer · conservation · rate limits · accumulation · realization

9. Seed Mathematics

Status: candidate primitive under development

A Seed is intended to model information that undergoes lawful generative evolution before meeting an export condition and producing a Root.

\[ S_{n+1} = G(S_n) \]

may represent one iterative realization.

Current questions include growth, termination, recursive behavior, branching, stability, and Root-export conditions.

10. GURF Foundations

Status: active response framework

GURF studies whether the distinctions retained by a system are sufficient for specified responses, tasks, or future continuation.

A central exact condition is

\[ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]

Equivalently,

\[ T = \widetilde T \circ r. \]

11. Completion and Recovery

For observations

\[ r_\alpha: X \longrightarrow Y_\alpha, \]

GURF uses the completion fibre

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

Exact recovery of task \(T\) requires \(T\) to be constant across this fibre.

12. Obstruction and Repair

Status: active

GURF identifies an obstruction when

\[ r(x)=r(x') \quad\text{but}\quad T(x)\neq T(x'). \]

Repair augments the representation using additional information

\[ q: X\longrightarrow Z \]

so that

\[ (r,q)(x) = (r,q)(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]

13. Dynamic Continuation

Status: active

If system evolution is

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X, \]

then future sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t \circ r. } \]

The research question is not merely whether a representation works now, but whether it preserves distinctions needed later.

14. Stochastic and Nondeterministic Semantics

GURF explicitly separates deterministic, stochastic, and nondeterministic response.

Stochastic response:

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

Nondeterministic response:

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

Existential and universal semantics remain distinct:

\[ \Diamond_R(U) \neq \Box_R(U) \]

in general.

15. Navier–Stokes Research

Status: active derived Leaves and theorem-kernel results; not a Clay Millennium solution

The project investigates Navier–Stokes through continuation, localization, spectral interaction, derivative structure, helicity, and information required to control nonlinear transfer.

Current derived objects include:

dual palinstrophy representation · normalized strain-gradient covariance tensor · co-location numerator · derivative-covariance hierarchy · triad amplitude/coherence variables · quartic neighboring-triad forcing · helical loading identities · spectral redistribution variables

16. Navier–Stokes Continuation Question

The research seeks quantities capable of distinguishing states that may appear similar under coarse observables but possess different future nonlinear behavior.

GURF expresses this abstractly as:

\[ r(u)=r(v) \] \[ \text{but} \] \[ T(\Phi_t(u)) \neq T(\Phi_t(v)). \]

Such a pair demonstrates that the chosen reduced representation is insufficient for that future task.

17. Navier–Stokes Operation Order

The research also tracks order-sensitive operations such as

differentiation · filtering · localization · reconstruction · spectral projection · coarse-graining

because in general

\[ F\circ G \neq G\circ F. \]

This matters whenever operation order changes task-relevant information.

18. Yang–Mills Research

Status: active conditional mathematical research; the four-dimensional Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap problem remains unsolved

The project investigates spectral leakage, boundary susceptibility, coherent return, shell structure, variational bounds, and Gauss-law constraints.

Derived or retained objects include:

environment block structure · boundary susceptibility · tail floor · coherent-return quantity · first-exit leakage · Green-matrix representation · shell-depth recursion · local coherent-return thresholds · Gauss-law shell support · global-return obstruction

19. Yang–Mills Variational Firewall

For nested trial spaces

\[ V_1 \subseteq V_2 \subseteq \cdots, \]

ordered Ritz eigenvalue approximations have standard variational monotonicity properties under the appropriate hypotheses.

However,

differences between approximate eigenvalues do not automatically inherit monotonicity.

Any mass-gap argument therefore requires separate error control rather than assuming that spectral-gap differences are variationally monotone.

20. Quarantined Yang–Mills Arguments

Arguments or external sources that fail mathematical audit are retained only as case studies.

They are not used as proof foundations.

\[ \boxed{ \text{interesting argument} \neq \text{accepted proof} } \]

21. Gravity and Spacetime Research

Status: active mathematical-physics research

The project investigates gravity through geometry, tidal response, geodesic structure, observational information, and possible emergent or response-based descriptions.

General Relativity remains a primary comparison theory.

BM/GURF analysis does not make an alternative description physically correct merely because it can be represented mathematically.

22. Forward-Causal Geometry

Physical investigation emphasizes forward-causal geometric phenomena such as:

proper-time gradients · geodesic deflection · tidal stretching and squeezing · frame dragging · focusing and defocusing · lensing · redshift · propagation delay

Closed timelike curves and backward-time constructions may be examined as mathematical geometry without being assumed physically realizable.

23. Observation and Motion

Astrophysical observation is treated as an observer-dependent information problem.

Measured frequency, direction, intensity, timing, polarization, and inferred distance depend on the physical state of source, observer, and intervening geometry.

A BM/GURF analysis therefore requires the observation map itself to be modeled:

\[ O: X_{\mathrm{physical}} \longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{observed}}. \]

24. Quantum Theory

Status: active

Quantum research focuses on distinguishability, measurement, entanglement, response, control, information preservation, and the relationship between quantum state descriptions and accessible observations.

The project does not assume that entanglement permits controllable superluminal information transfer.

25. Quantum Passport Problem

A quantum Passport must distinguish between the full quantum state and the information available under a specified measurement family.

For density operator

\[ \rho, \]

and measurement operators \(E_i\), accessible probabilities are

\[ p_i = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_i). \]

The research question becomes which distinctions in \(\rho\) remain necessary for the intended future quantum task.

26. Entanglement Research

Entanglement is treated as a joint-state and joint-observation problem, not as evidence that one subsystem locally transmits an arbitrary usable message to another.

Relevant mathematical questions include:

reduced states · joint measurement statistics · correlations · Bell-type constraints · conditional states · measurement compatibility · joint witness structure · information availability

27. Electromagnetism and Radiation

Status: active multi-scale research program

The project studies electromagnetic radiation across:

HF · VHF · UHF · radio · microwave · infrared · visible light · ultraviolet · X-ray · gamma-ray regimes

Topics include propagation, emission, absorption, reflection, scattering, attenuation, thermal radiation, vacuum-to-medium transmission, and observation limits.

28. Light Propagation and Observation

Research distinguishes:

propagation of the electromagnetic field · probability of detection · finite detector sensitivity · geometric spreading · absorption · scattering · redshift · source evolution · observational horizon

Failure to detect a photon or source at large distance does not by itself establish that electromagnetic propagation has a universal finite termination distance.

29. Thermal Physics

The thermal program includes:

black-body radiation · grey-body radiation · heat transfer · thermal expansion · emission and absorption · frictional heating · radiative cooling · material response

BM/GURF analysis tracks which macroscopic representations preserve enough microscopic information for the desired thermal task.

30. Computation and Information

Status: active

Computational research uses BM/GURF to study lawful information loss across transformations such as:

source code → intermediate representation → machine code · compression · optimization · abstraction · privacy transformation · secure compilation · state reduction · inference

31. Secure Compilation

A compiler transformation

\[ C: S \longrightarrow M \]

may preserve some source-level distinctions, erase others, and expose distinctions absent at the source level.

BM/GURF asks which observational equivalences and security properties survive compilation.

32. Property Versus Hyperproperty

Some security statements concern one execution.

Others concern relations among multiple executions.

A transformation that preserves ordinary state properties does not automatically preserve hyperproperties.

This distinction is part of the active secure-transformation research program.

33. Differential Privacy

Differential privacy is not represented faithfully by ordinary exact equivalence relations alone.

It requires probability-law comparison through a divergence or multiplicative/additive bound.

Thus approximate or stochastic Passport semantics are required.

34. Control and Model Reduction

Reduced models are studied through the closure condition

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi \circ R. } \]

When this fails, hidden information in the original state influences the reduced future.

Related areas include lumpability, observability, controllability, state estimation, and closure modeling.

35. Engineering Research

Status: application and feasibility research

Engineering applications are treated as realization problems.

A mathematical concept must survive:

materials limits · thermal limits · structural loads · power balance · control stability · manufacturability · reliability · maintainability · cost · environmental constraints · physical law

36. Energy Systems

Energy-system research includes:

nuclear energy · heat transfer · closed and open cooling loops · desalination · seawater systems · thermal utilization · material degradation · resource recovery · waste minimization

Speculative transmutation or material-conversion concepts are not treated as viable engineering merely because a nuclear reaction can be written.

Reaction cross-sections, energy balance, isotope production, radiation, separation cost, and safety remain decisive.

37. Propulsion

Propulsion research is treated through conservation of momentum, energy, field interaction, thermodynamics, and realizability.

BM can organize interacting mathematical branches, but it cannot waive conservation laws or experimental constraints.

38. Mathematical Physics Validation

Candidate physical results should be tested against established limits.

Examples include:

dimensional consistency · known analytic limits · conservation laws · symmetries · perturbative limits · numerical benchmarks · laboratory data · astrophysical observations

39. Numerical Validation

Numerical agreement is evidence about a model, not automatic proof of its underlying mathematical claims.

\[ \boxed{ \text{numerical agreement} \not\Rightarrow \text{mathematical theorem} } \]

Numerical tests are nevertheless essential for:

detecting algebraic mistakes · testing scaling · identifying unstable regimes · comparing approximations · checking limiting behavior · evaluating physical relevance

40. Scientific Figures

Research pages will use equation-driven figures when they reveal actual mathematical behavior.

Appropriate figures include:

spectra · phase portraits · PDE fields · vector fields · response surfaces · parameter sweeps · convergence plots · stability maps · heatmaps · residual plots · error bounds · probability distributions

Structural BM/GURF diagrams remain separate and will not be invented as substitutes for the unrecreated originals.

41. Falsifiability

Physical research must eventually produce observations capable of distinguishing a proposed model from its competitors.

A useful test has the form:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Model A prediction} \neq \text{Model B prediction} } \]

within an experimentally accessible regime.

42. Free Parameters

A model with unconstrained adjustable parameters may fit data without making a strong prediction.

Therefore active research aims to determine parameters from:

mathematical consistency · symmetry · conservation · known constants · independent experiments · dimensional relations · boundary conditions

43. Units and Dimensional Analysis

Physical equations must be dimensionally consistent.

If a proposed relation is

\[ A=B+C, \]

then \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) must have compatible dimensions.

Dimensionless constants must be distinguished from quantities carrying physical units.

44. Limits

New models should recover known behavior in regimes where established theories have already been tested.

A proposed correction

\[ F_\varepsilon \]

may be expected to satisfy a limit such as

\[ \lim_{\varepsilon\to0} F_\varepsilon = F_0 \]

when \(F_0\) is the established limiting theory.

45. Failure Preservation

Failed derivations and counterexamples are part of the research record.

They should not be silently deleted merely because they do not support the desired result.

\[ \boxed{ \text{failed route} \Longrightarrow \text{constraint on future routes} } \]

46. External Prior Art

Once a mathematical result has been derived internally, external literature is used to determine:

whether the result is already known · whether stronger results exist · whether terminology conflicts · which standard theorem contains it · whether the integrated construction may still be distinctive

Prior-art review does not determine whether an internal derivation is mathematically correct.

47. Research Re-entry

A result derived in one domain may return to BM as a reusable mathematical structure only after its domain assumptions are separated from its portable mathematical core.

Schematically:

\[ \text{application result} \longrightarrow \text{portable mathematical kernel} \longrightarrow \text{BM re-entry}. \]

48. Portability Test

A mathematical construction is a candidate for promotion into BM when it can be stated without essential response semantics and remains meaningful across several mathematical domains.

Structures whose identity fundamentally depends on tasks, interventions, observations, regimes, or responses remain primarily GURF structures.

49. Research Page Architecture

As the encyclopedia grows, each major research program should receive its own page containing:

problem statement · established background · notation · equations · derivations · theorem kernels · counterexamples · numerical tests · quantitative figures · assumptions · failure modes · open questions · external literature · revision history

51. Public Research Standard

The public encyclopedia will not present an active hypothesis as an established physical law.

It will not present an intermediate theorem as a solution to a larger unsolved problem.

It will not erase counterexamples or failed routes.

It will not claim external novelty before prior-art review.

52. Research Principle

\[ \boxed{ \text{derive aggressively} \quad \text{but classify conservatively} } \]

The research program is intended to push mathematical structures as far as valid derivation permits while keeping proof, hypothesis, numerical evidence, physical interpretation, and novelty as separate questions.
