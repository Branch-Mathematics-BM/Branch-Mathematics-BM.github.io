1. What Belongs on the Research Page

The formal registries record definitions, equations, structures, Passports, Roots, and proved results.

This page begins where those registries stop.

\[ \boxed{ \text{research} = \text{questions} + \text{derivations} + \text{tests} + \text{counterexamples} + \text{open gates}. } \]

Research entries may contain:

  • unproved structural conjectures;
  • prior-art comparison;
  • formalization targets;
  • derived diagnostics;
  • open mathematical problems;
  • physical working models;
  • numerical experiments;
  • empirical tests;
  • negative results;
  • quarantined arguments.

2. Research Status Is Multi-Dimensional

One label such as “active” or “established” is not enough.

Axis Question
Mathematical validity Does the stated result follow from the stated assumptions?
Provenance Is the mathematics classical, imported, specialized, project-derived, or unresolved?
Framework maturity Is the object core, active specialized, candidate, frozen, recovery-required, or rejected?
Novelty Has historical prior art been ruled out?
Mathematical realizability Does the proposed mathematical structure actually possess a realization?
Computability Can the relevant object be constructed or computed?
Complexity Can it be computed within meaningful resources?
Physical interpretation Has a physical meaning been independently justified?
Empirical support Has the physical or applied model survived data or experiment?
External validation Has independent expert review confirmed the claim?
\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematically valid} \neq \text{historically new} \neq \text{physically true} \neq \text{experimentally confirmed}. } \]

6. Branch Mathematics Foundations

Status: Current canonical project baseline
Research class: metaframework foundations

Branch Mathematics carries one mathematical problem through multiple lawful mathematical Branches while preserving their native mathematics and governing their lawful interaction, realization, comparison, transformation, export, and re-entry.

\[ \boxed{ \text{one problem} \to \text{multiple lawful mathematical focuses} \to \text{lawful combined mathematics} \to \text{mathematical consequences}. } \]

Current core architecture

\[ \boxed{ \text{problem} \to \text{Branches} \to \text{Operations / Interactions} \to \text{Leaves} \to \text{Configuration} \to \text{realization} \to \text{Tree} \to \text{Response} \to \text{Export} \to \text{Root gate}. } \]

Currently supported

  • typed Branch specification;
  • role-carrier separation;
  • Operation / Interaction distinction;
  • Interaction Non-Inheritance;
  • Contract / Passport distinction;
  • Configuration vs Tree distinction;
  • local vs global realization;
  • semantic dependency structure \(\mathfrak D_C\);
  • Dynamic Closure on the realized image;
  • receiver-relative Root semantics;
  • sector and computability firewalls.

Current foundations questions

  • Which BM distinctions are indispensable rather than organizational convenience?
  • Which substructures reduce exactly to existing theories?
  • Which residual structures remain after those reductions?
  • Can the useful Configuration class be represented faithfully in one existing formalism?

7. Role-Erasure Program

Every proposed BM theorem must survive removal of BM vocabulary.

Suppose:

\[ x \models_C \rho \]

records that mathematical carrier \(x\) occupies role \(\rho\).

The theorem must remain expressible using the underlying:

  • sets or spaces;
  • maps;
  • relations;
  • domains;
  • constraints;
  • operators;
  • comparison structures;
  • realization conditions.
\[ \boxed{ \text{erase Branch/Leaf/Tree/Root words} \Longrightarrow \text{mathematical content should remain}. } \]
If a claimed theorem disappears entirely when the role vocabulary is removed, it has not yet earned theorem status.

Status: Corpus A complete and frozen for structural comparison
Corpus size: 200 equations
Supplement: equations 111–200 tagged FRONTIER-SUPPLEMENT

A cross-domain equation scrub was used to test whether BM's structural vocabulary encounters mathematical roles that the architecture cannot represent.

The scrub identified nine recurring structural roles:

  1. representation / transport;
  2. composition / decomposition / provenance;
  3. admissibility / closure / obstruction;
  4. comparison / coherence / context;
  5. sufficiency / reconstruction / certification;
  6. local-to-global aggregation;
  7. dynamics / convergence / scaling / regimes;
  8. extremality / rigidity / inverse classification;
  9. computability / decidability limits.

Corrections forced by the scrub

  • typed coherence rather than generic equality;
  • separate domain and output coherence;
  • Interaction Non-Inheritance;
  • explicit Tree provenance/order/context;
  • declared convergence semantics;
  • law/regime context;
  • context-carrying and multidimensional Roots;
  • feedback semantics;
  • computability firewall.
\[ \boxed{ \text{200-equation coverage} \Rightarrow \text{structural stress evidence} } \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{200-equation coverage} \not\Rightarrow \text{historical novelty}. } \]
The surviving project files do not provide a complete verified equation-by-equation source manifest for all 200 entries. The public site must not fabricate missing arXiv identifiers or bibliographic provenance.

9. Prior-Art Reduction Program

Before promoting a specialized BM construction, test whether existing mathematics already solves the same formal problem.

\[ \boxed{ \text{proposed BM structure} \xrightarrow{\text{prior-art audit}} \begin{cases} \text{known theory},\\ \text{known theory + residual},\\ \text{unresolved comparison}. \end{cases} } \]

Major comparison families

  • abstract interpretation and Galois connections;
  • contract and interface theories;
  • constraint-satisfaction problems and conjunctive queries;
  • category theory;
  • structured/decorated cospans;
  • wiring-diagram operads;
  • institutions and heterogeneous specification;
  • information-flow theories;
  • sheaves and descent;
  • bisimulation and lumpability;
  • Blackwell-style sufficiency;
  • privacy semantics;
  • lenses and bidirectional Interfaces where appropriate.

No-duplicate-theory rule

\[ \boxed{ \text{if established theory }M \text{ completely realizes }S, \quad S = \text{BM use of }M. } \]

Partial reduction

\[ \boxed{ S = S_{\mathrm{known}} + S_{\mathrm{residual}}. } \]

The residual is the genuine research question.

10. Faithful Representation Problem

Status: Major open foundations target

The current strongest foundations question is not whether any individual BM component resembles existing mathematics. Many do.

The harder question is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Can the useful BM Configuration class be represented faithfully inside an existing formalism?} } \]

Let:

\[ \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{BM}} = \text{a declared class of BM Configurations}. \]

Seek a translation:

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal F: \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{BM}} \to \mathfrak M } \]

into an established or independently formalized structure \(\mathfrak M\).

The translation must preserve

  • Branch distinctions that matter;
  • native sector mathematics;
  • Operations and Interactions;
  • Interfaces;
  • Passports;
  • law/regime context;
  • provenance and history;
  • realization semantics;
  • scope obligations;
  • export and receiver-relative Root semantics.

Possible outcomes

Outcome Meaning
Fully faithful known representation BM architecture is primarily a coordinated use of existing mathematics.
Faithful only on restricted subclasses The failure boundary identifies the actual residual research.
No adequate known representation found This creates a sharper target for new mathematics, but still does not by itself establish historical novelty.

12. Local-to-Global Research Program

The finite tree theorem is only a controlled special case.

General BM Configurations may contain:

  • cycles;
  • hyperedges;
  • shared variables;
  • global constraints;
  • parallel paths;
  • mixed semantic sectors;
  • law/regime switching;
  • coupled witnesses.

The research target is to identify conditions under which:

\[ \boxed{ \text{local realizations} + \text{compatibility data} \Longrightarrow \text{global realization}. } \]

without hiding global constraints inside an assumed graph factorization.

\[ \boxed{ \text{local admissibility} \not\Rightarrow \text{global realizability}. } \]

\[ \boxed{ \text{framework value} = \text{better organization, diagnosis, certification, inference, or experimental design} } \]

not merely:

\[ \boxed{ \text{known equation rewritten with BM labels}. } \]

Benchmark families

  • finite deterministic systems;
  • finite Markov systems;
  • linear inverse problems;
  • hidden-state state-space models;
  • thermal-control systems;
  • quantum dephasing;
  • PDE observation/reduction;
  • blackbody/greybody inversion;

Required protocol

  1. predeclare Task;

  4. report failures;
  5. do not redefine the Task after observing the outcome.

16. Navier–Stokes Research

Status: Active derived mathematics
Open-problem status: Millennium Prize problem not solved

Native incompressible Navier–Stokes:

\[ \partial_tu + (u\cdot\nabla)u = -\nabla p + \nu\Delta u + f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0. \]

BM/GURF research studies information required for:

  • continuation;
  • vorticity growth;
  • nonlinear transfer;
  • spectral interaction;
  • phase-sensitive Tasks;
  • task-aware reduction.

Active derived quantities

  • derivative-covariance hierarchy \(F^{(n)}\);
  • normalized strain-gradient covariance;
  • triad amplitude/coherence variables;
  • helical-sector loading diagnostics;
  • heterochiral scale-phase covariance;
  • neighbor-network nonlinear forcing structure;
  • co-location and continuation diagnostics.
These are active derived Leaves and diagnostics. They are not currently a proof of global regularity.

17. Navier–Stokes Proof-Gap Map

\[ \boxed{ \text{local theory} \longrightarrow \text{continuation criterion} \longrightarrow \text{critical control} \longrightarrow \text{global regularity or breakdown result}. } \]

Any proposed BM result must identify which arrow it actually strengthens.

Meaningful promotion

  • a genuinely stronger a priori estimate;
  • a new compactness mechanism;
  • a stronger continuation criterion;
  • a blow-up exclusion result;
  • a rigorous breakdown construction satisfying the problem statement.
\[ \boxed{ \text{new diagnostic} \not\Rightarrow \text{new regularity theorem}. } \]

18. Navier–Stokes Derived Leaves

Classical exact identities include:

\[ \frac{dE}{dt} = -D + F \]

under the appropriate periodic/smooth assumptions.

Vorticity/enstrophy evolution supplies a distinct exact identity involving stretching, viscous dissipation, and forcing.

A magnitude-only spectral representation may preserve:

\[ \sum_k |\widehat u(k)|^2 \]

while losing phase information entering nonlinear products:

\[ \widehat u(p) \widehat u(q), \qquad p+q=k. \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{energy sufficiency} \not\Rightarrow \text{nonlinear-transfer sufficiency}. } \]

19. Yang–Mills Research

Status: Active conditional research
Open-problem status: four-dimensional existence and mass gap unresolved

Research currently uses controlled finite/truncated sectors to study:

  • boundary susceptibility;
  • boundary Green matrices;
  • tail floors;
  • shell-depth recursions;
  • coherent return;
  • Gauss-law shell support;
  • global-return obstruction;
  • variational trial-space enrichment.

Established correction

Nested trial-space enrichment gives nonincreasing Ritz upper bounds for ordered eigenvalues:

\[ \lambda_k^{(n+1)} \le \lambda_k^{(n)}. \]

But differences between successive Ritz eigenvalues are not thereby monotone.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Ritz-value monotonicity} \not\Rightarrow \text{gap monotonicity}. } \]

20. Yang–Mills Mass-Gap Gates

A valid solution must establish the actual target theory, not merely a finite-model analogue.

\[ \boxed{ \text{candidate mass-gap argument} \Longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{four-dimensional theory existence},\\ \text{correct gauge-invariant state space},\\ \text{required positivity / axioms},\\ \text{controlled continuum/infinite-volume limit},\\ \text{uniform positive spectral gap}. \end{cases} } \]
A finite truncation gap, effective mass parameter, infrared suppression, or large boundary diagonal is not by itself the Millennium Prize mass gap.

22. Extended / Halo Response Research

HDE provides a formal place to test whether a narrowly local state omits information required for the observed response.

\[ D \subseteq D_{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad x_{\mathrm{ext}} = (x_D,x_H). \]

Local insufficiency is witnessed when:

\[ x_D=x_D' \quad\text{but}\quad \boxed{ T(x_D,x_H) \neq T(x_D',x_H'). } \]

Research questions

  • Can joint timing/lensing/dynamical observations constrain \(x_H\)?
  • Does the extended description produce distinct observable consequences?
  • Can it satisfy all known constraints with one parameter set?
  • Can a standard model explain the same data with fewer assumptions?
An extended mathematical state does not automatically imply a physical halo or new physical substance.

23. Quantum Information and Control

Status: Active structural and applied research

Native quantum mathematics remains authoritative.

BM/GURF studies which operator-level distinctions survive:

  • channels;
  • measurements;
  • dephasing;
  • coarse observations;
  • control;
  • quantum-classical Interfaces.

Task dependence

A channel may preserve one Task while destroying another.

For example, dephasing may preserve computational-basis populations while eliminating phase-sensitive observables.

\[ \boxed{ \text{channel sufficiency} \text{ is Task-relative}. } \]

Active program

  • joint-state vs local-marginal information;
  • measurement-context Passports;
  • quantum control Interfaces;
  • no-signalling-compatible joint statistics;
  • classical post-measurement information loss.

24. Quantum Interpretation Research

Status: Candidate interpretive research

BM does not require probability to be treated as a primitive ontological property of the underlying system.

Research may instead test whether apparently probabilistic behavior can be represented through:

  • incomplete accessible information;
  • unresolved Branches;
  • hidden or inaccessible state;
  • lawful dynamics;
  • measurement context;
  • environmental coupling.
\[ \boxed{ \text{probability mathematics} \neq \text{mandatory probabilistic ontology}. } \]

This interpretive program does not remove standard probability mathematics from quantum calculations.

Promotion gate

Any deterministic/geometric replacement model must reproduce all relevant operational predictions and produce a distinguishing empirical or mathematical consequence before it can be treated as more than an interpretation or working model.

29. Open-Problem Stress Tests

Major unsolved problems are used as adversarial tests only when BM/GURF is judged against the actual proof gap.

\[ \boxed{ \text{framework organization} \not\Rightarrow \text{progress on an open theorem}. } \]

A framework step counts as mathematical progress on an open problem only when it supplies something such as:

  • a new bound;
  • a new compactness mechanism;
  • a new construction;
  • a new obstruction;
  • a stronger continuation criterion;
  • a valid reduction of the proof target;
  • a theorem closing an actual missing step.

30. Falsifiability and Prediction Discipline

\[ \boxed{ \text{assumptions} \to \text{predeclared prediction} \to \text{observation} \to \{ \text{pass}, \text{fail}, \text{inconclusive} \}. } \]

For physical models

  • state parameter ranges before testing;
  • state nuisance parameters;
  • state the distinguishing observable;
  • state what outcome rejects the model;
  • avoid post-outcome parameter retuning.

For mathematical framework claims

A failed application does not falsify all of BM unless the tested claim was genuinely universal.

\[ \boxed{ \text{numerical failure} \neq \text{proof of nonexistence}. } \]

32. Negative Results and No-Go Statements

Negative results are retained as part of the research corpus.

Current important no-go results

  • observational equivalence need not define a structured quotient;
  • separate witnesses do not imply a shared witness;
  • pairwise/local compatibility does not imply global realization;
  • static Task sufficiency does not imply Dynamic Closure;
  • Roothood is not transitive across receivers;
  • Branch count is not generally invariant;
  • finite-model Yang–Mills gaps do not prove the continuum mass gap;
  • diagnostic Navier–Stokes identities do not prove global regularity;
  • probability marginals do not determine a unique coupling;
  • existence does not define a canonical selection.
\[ \boxed{ \text{failed stronger claim} \to \text{preserved counterexample} \to \text{corrected theorem}. } \]

33. Quarantine

An argument is quarantined when a known failure prevents it from supporting the intended claim.

\[ \boxed{ \text{quarantined} \neq \text{silently reusable}. } \]

Reuse requires:

  1. state the failed gate;
  2. repair the exact failure;
  3. rerun the relevant counterexample audit;
  4. re-establish all dependent claims.

Structures with missing defining mathematics are not repaired by inventing replacement formulas.

\[ \boxed{ \text{candidate} \to \text{derived} \to \text{proved / validated} \to \text{broader claim} } \]

Promotion occurs only when the relevant evidence has actually been supplied.

35. Current High-Value Open Questions

  1. Faithful-representation question: what class of BM Configurations admits a faithful representation in existing categorical, institutional, constraint, compositional, or related mathematics?
  2. Residual-structure question: after maximal prior-art reduction, what genuinely unreduced structure remains?
  3. Local-to-global question: what useful realization theorems survive beyond tree-factorized systems?
  4. Mixed-sector question: how should heterogeneous native mathematical sectors be coordinated without flattening their semantics?
  5. Dependency question: what general properties can be proved for \(\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}\) under repair and scope change?
  6. Passport question: which Passport constructions are genuinely stronger than established contract/interface/sufficiency theory?
  7. Root question: which useful re-entry theorems can be proved beyond deterministic receiver predicates?
  8. Navier–Stokes question: can any active derived Leaf produce a new a priori estimate, continuation mechanism, or rigorous obstruction?
  9. Yang–Mills question: can controlled finite/truncated results be connected to a valid continuum/infinite-volume statement without losing the required gauge and positivity structure?
  10. Physical-model question: can any current gravity or quantum interpretation program produce a precise distinguishing prediction while preserving all established native constraints?

37. Central Research Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Native mathematics first.}\\[4pt] \text{Framework role second.}\\[4pt] \text{Scope every claim.}\\[4pt] \text{Separate carrier mathematics from BM role names.}\\[4pt] \text{Reduce to existing mathematics wherever the reduction is exact.}\\[4pt] \text{Treat the unreduced residual as the real research question.}\\[4pt] \text{Preserve negative results and counterexamples.}\\[4pt] \text{Do not promote numerical evidence into proof.}\\[4pt] \text{Do not promote mathematical realizability into physical reality.}\\[4pt] \text{Do not promote internal validity into historical novelty.}\\[4pt] \text{For open problems, close an actual proof gap or make no stronger claim.}\\[4pt] \text{For physical models, expose a prediction that can fail.}\\[4pt] \text{Let the strongest surviving mathematics determine the public status.} \end{gathered} } \]
A corrected theorem, precise obstruction, failed model, no-go result, or demonstrated boundary of applicability is a valid research result. The research program is not measured by how many new names can be added.
