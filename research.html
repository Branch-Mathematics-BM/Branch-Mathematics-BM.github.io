1. Purpose of the Research Registry

This page records what is being investigated, what has been formally derived, what has been proved under stated assumptions, what has been numerically tested, what remains conditional, and what has not been established.

It is not a list of claims of discovery.

\[ \boxed{ \text{proved intermediate mathematics} \neq \text{solution of the entire research problem}. } \]

A research program may produce valid new intermediate mathematics without resolving the larger problem that motivated the work.

3. Governing Research Rule

Branch Mathematics and GURF organize mathematical reasoning.

They do not override proof requirements in algebra, analysis, probability, geometry, PDE theory, physics, computation, control, numerical analysis, or any other native mathematical domain.

\[ \boxed{ \text{formalize} \longrightarrow \text{derive} \longrightarrow \text{audit} \longrightarrow \text{test} \longrightarrow \text{compare}. } \]

4. Current Research Architecture

\[ \boxed{ \text{native mathematics} + \text{BM meta-structure} + \text{GURF when response semantics are present} } \]

BM is the broader meta-structural mathematics.

GURF is the response-specialized framework used when observation, Task, Intervention, Regime, response ambiguity, continuation, admissibility, inference, or related response semantics become mathematically essential.

6. Derived Quantity Versus Formal BM Leaf

Research results should not be labeled BM Leaves solely because they were derived during a BM analysis.

Formal Leaf provenance requires an Interaction

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I \]

and a declared extraction map

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j. \]

The Leaf is then

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right). } \]

Until that provenance is specified, the safer classification is derived mathematical quantity, derived relation, or theorem-kernel result.

7. Structured Quotients

Status: active, with a proved finitary algebraic core.

A central BM question is when observational or Passport equivalence can support a genuine mathematical quotient.

For finitary algebra

\[ A=(X,\Sigma), \]

operation compatibility requires

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

The current proved finitary algebraic result is recorded as BM-SQ-01.

12. Repeated Rooting and Closure

Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.

A general Root chain may have

\[ R_n\in X_n, \qquad F_n: D_n\subseteq X_n \longrightarrow X_{n+1}, \]

with

\[ R_{n+1}=F_n(R_n). \]

Ordinary repeated iteration by one map requires closure:

\[ \boxed{ F: X_R \longrightarrow X_R } \]

or an invariant admissible subset

\[ A_R\subseteq X_R \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ F(A_R)\subseteq A_R. } \]

Only then does

\[ R_{n+1}=F(R_n) \]

define the standard discrete iteration used by fixed-point and dynamical-systems theory.

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \longrightarrow S_n \] followed conditionally by \[ \boxed{ S_n \longrightarrow R. } \]

Current questions include:

generative rules · termination · recursive behavior · branching · stability · admissibility · Seed-state information · Root-export criteria

14. GURF Foundations

Status: canonical response-specialized framework under BM governance.

GURF studies which distinctions in an underlying system must remain available for specified responses, Tasks, Interventions, inference, or future continuation.

A central deterministic condition is

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

Equivalently, there exists

\[ \widetilde T: r(X) \longrightarrow W \]

satisfying

\[ \boxed{ T = \widetilde T\circ r. } \]

17. Completion and Recovery

For observations

\[ r_\alpha: X \longrightarrow Y_\alpha, \]

GURF uses the Completion Fibre

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_A(\ell) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in A} r_\alpha^{-1}(\ell_\alpha). } \]

Exact recovery of deterministic Task \(T\) requires \(T\) to remain constant across each nonempty relevant Completion Fibre.

18. SOE Research

Status: stable canonical GURF extension.

SOE studies the effect of lawful ordered Operations on response information.

Research questions include:

filtering before differentiation · differentiation before filtering · localization · reconstruction · intervention order · handoffs · projection · coarse-graining · feedback · rebinding

For Operations

\[ F: D_F\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad G: D_G\subseteq Y\to Z, \]

the actual composite domain is

\[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

SOE does not assume

\[ F\circ G = G\circ F. \]

19. ACE Research

Status: stable canonical GURF extension.

ACE studies response systems whose mathematical behavior is restricted by admissibility or constraints.

If

\[ K_1,\ldots,K_m \subseteq X, \]

then a simultaneous admissible region may be

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A = \bigcap_{j=1}^{m}K_j. } \]

Active topics include:

feasibility · positivity · coercivity · convexity · viability · boundary conditions · constraint activation · continuation gates · stability classifications

Static positivity is not automatically hyperbolicity, spectral stability, ghost freedom, gradient stability, or nonlinear global stability.

20. IRE Research

Status: stable canonical GURF extension.

IRE studies inference and inverse recovery.

For observation

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y \]

and observation value \(y\), the admissible inverse fibre is

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C(y) = \{x\in\mathcal A:F(x)=y\}. } \]

Active research includes:

existence · uniqueness · Task-relative identifiability · stable recovery · uncertainty fibres · regularization · certificates · reconstruction · inverse complexity

A unique regularized optimizer does not prove that the observation uniquely identified the underlying source.

21. UKE Research

Status: stable canonical GURF extension.

UKE organizes advanced kernel-specific response mathematics while preserving the distinct mathematical meanings of the word kernel.

Nullspace

\[ \ker F = \{x:F(x)=0\}. \]

Integral Kernel

\[ (Tf)(x) = \int K(x,y)f(y)\,dy. \]

Green Kernel

\[ L_xG(x,x') = \delta(x-x'). \]

Resolvent

\[ R(z) = (L-zI)^{-1} \]

where defined.

Memory Response

\[ y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{t} K(t,s)x(s)\,ds. \]
A mathematically selected kernel is not automatically a physically derived mechanism.

Its recorded scope includes:

23. HDE Research

Status: active canonical GURF extension.

HDE studies response systems whose relevant information extends beyond a narrowly localized primary domain.

A working decomposition may be

\[ D \subseteq D_{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad H = D_{\mathrm{ext}}\setminus D. \]

An extended response state may require

\[ \boxed{ x_{\mathrm{ext}} = (x_D,x_H). } \]

Research questions include:

extended support · boundary transfer · domain propagation · persistence · memory · observation outside the primary region · local/halo information loss · extended-domain continuation

HDE is not itself a universal physical halo theory.

24. GHSE Research

If component spaces are

\[ X_A, \qquad X_B, \]

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal H \subseteq X_A\times X_B. } \]

Active questions include:

attachment criteria · hold predicates · persistence · release · hysteresis · state transitions · admissible joined states · response memory

26. Obstruction and Repair

Status: active GURF Core research.

In deterministic semantics, an obstruction exists when

\[ \boxed{ r(x)=r(x') \quad \text{but} \quad T(x)\neq T(x'). } \]

Repair may augment retained information through

\[ q: X \longrightarrow Z \]

so that

\[ (r,q)(x) = (r,q)(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). \]

27. Dynamic Continuation

If system evolution is

\[ \Phi_t: X \longrightarrow X, \]

future Task sufficiency requires

\[ \boxed{ T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t\circ r } \]

for some

\[ \widetilde T_t: r(X) \longrightarrow W. \]

The question is not merely whether the present representation works now, but whether it preserves distinctions needed later.

28. Dynamic Closure Research

Let

\[ R: X \longrightarrow Z \]

be a reduced representation and

\[ \Phi: X \longrightarrow X. \]

Exact reduced closure requires

\[ \widetilde\Phi: R(X) \longrightarrow R(X) \]

such that

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

Static Task sufficiency and dynamic closure remain different requirements.

Failure of closure may expose:

hidden state · unresolved memory · missing boundary information · omitted controls · missing Regime variables · extended-domain state

29. Stochastic and Nondeterministic Semantics

GURF separates deterministic, stochastic, and nondeterministic response.

Stochastic Response

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

For a suitable test function,

\[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E [ \varphi(Y) \mid X=x ]. \]

Nondeterministic Response

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

Existential and universal semantics remain distinct:

\[ \Diamond_R(U) = \{x:\exists y\in U,\ xRy\}, \] \[ \Box_R(U) = \{x:\forall y,\ xRy\Rightarrow y\in U\}. \]

In general,

\[ \boxed{ \Diamond_R(U) \neq \Box_R(U). } \]

30. Differential Privacy Research

Differential privacy is not faithfully represented by an ordinary exact equivalence relation.

For neighboring datasets

\[ x\sim_{\mathrm{adj}}x' \]

and randomized mechanism

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y), \]

an \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-privacy requirement has the form

\[ \boxed{ \Pr[M(x)\in S] \le e^\varepsilon \Pr[M(x')\in S] + \delta. } \]

Privacy therefore requires probability-law comparison, divergence-like control, or related stochastic semantics.

33. Navier–Stokes Continuation Question

The research seeks quantities capable of distinguishing states that may appear similar under coarse observables but possess different future nonlinear behavior.

Abstractly,

\[ r(u)=r(v) \]

while

\[ \boxed{ T(\Phi_t(u)) \neq T(\Phi_t(v)). } \]

Such a pair demonstrates that the selected reduced representation is insufficient for that future Task.

34. Navier–Stokes Operation Order

The research tracks order-sensitive Operations such as:

differentiation · filtering · localization · reconstruction · spectral projection · coarse-graining

because in general

\[ \boxed{ F\circ G \neq G\circ F. } \]

This is a natural SOE research interface.

35. Yang–Mills Research

Status: active conditional mathematical research.

The four-dimensional Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap problem remains unsolved.

Current research includes:

spectral leakage · boundary susceptibility · coherent return · shell structure · Green-matrix response · tail floors · Gauss-law support · local thresholds · global-return obstructions · variational bounds

36. Yang–Mills Variational Firewall

For nested trial spaces

\[ V_1 \subseteq V_2 \subseteq \cdots, \]

individual ordered Ritz eigenvalue approximations possess the standard variational monotonicity properties under the relevant min–max hypotheses.

Differences of approximate eigenvalues do not automatically inherit monotonicity.

Therefore a spectral-gap argument requires separate approximation or error control rather than assuming that

\[ \lambda_2^{(n)}-\lambda_1^{(n)} \]

is variationally monotone merely because the individual Ritz values are.

37. Quarantined Yang–Mills Arguments

Arguments or external sources that fail mathematical audit are retained as research-history or failure-analysis material.

They are not used as proof foundations.

\[ \boxed{ \text{interesting argument} \neq \text{accepted proof}. } \]

38. Gravity and Spacetime Research

Status: active mathematical-physics research.

The project investigates gravity through:

geometry · proper time · geodesic structure · tidal response · lensing · observation · extended-domain response · realization questions · comparison with established gravitational theory

General Relativity remains a primary comparison theory.

A mathematically consistent alternative description is not thereby established as a physically correct theory of gravity.

39. Forward-Causal Geometry

Physical investigation emphasizes forward-causal geometric phenomena such as:

proper-time gradients · geodesic deflection · tidal stretching · tidal squeezing · frame dragging · focusing · defocusing · lensing · redshift · propagation delay

Closed-timelike-curve and backward-time constructions may be studied mathematically without being treated as physically established mechanisms.

40. Observation, Motion, and Reference State

Astrophysical observation is treated as an observer-dependent information problem.

Measured frequency, direction, intensity, timing, polarization, inferred distance, and angular structure can depend on the state of the source, observer, and intervening geometry.

A BM/GURF treatment therefore requires an explicit Observation map:

\[ \boxed{ O: X_{\mathrm{physical}} \longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{observed}}. } \]

41. Quantum Theory

Status: active.

Quantum research focuses on:

distinguishability · measurement · entanglement · response · control · information preservation · joint state structure · accessible observations · continuation

The project does not assume that entanglement permits controllable superluminal information transfer.

42. Quantum Passport Problem

A quantum Passport must distinguish the full quantum state from the information accessible through a declared measurement family.

For density operator

\[ \rho \]

and POVM elements

\[ E_i, \]

the accessible probabilities are

\[ \boxed{ p_i = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_i). } \]

The research question is which distinctions in \(\rho\) remain necessary for the intended future quantum Task.

43. Entanglement Research

Entanglement is treated through joint state structure and joint observation statistics.

Active mathematical questions include:

reduced states · joint measurements · correlations · Bell-type constraints · conditional states · measurement compatibility · witness structure · information availability · local versus joint Tasks

Correlation does not by itself constitute a controllable local signalling channel.

44. Electromagnetism and Radiation

Status: active multi-scale research program.

The project studies electromagnetic radiation across:

HF · VHF · UHF · radio · microwave · infrared · visible · ultraviolet · X-ray · gamma-ray regimes

Topics include:

propagation · emission · absorption · reflection · scattering · attenuation · thermal radiation · vacuum-to-medium transmission · detector response · observation limits

45. Light Propagation and Observation

The research distinguishes:

  • propagation of the electromagnetic field;
  • photon detection probability;
  • finite detector sensitivity;
  • geometric spreading;
  • absorption;
  • scattering;
  • redshift;
  • source evolution;
  • intervening geometry;
  • observational horizons.
Failure to detect a photon or distant source does not by itself establish a universal finite termination distance for electromagnetic propagation.

46. Thermal Physics

The thermal research program includes:

black-body radiation · grey-body radiation · heat transfer · thermal expansion · emission · absorption · frictional heating · radiative cooling · material response

BM/GURF analysis asks which macroscopic representations preserve enough microscopic or constitutive information for the required thermal Task.

47. Computation and Information

Status: active.

Computational research uses BM/GURF to study lawful information loss across transformations such as:

source code → intermediate representation → machine code · compression · optimization · abstraction · privacy transformation · secure compilation · state reduction · inference

48. Secure Compilation

A compiler Transformation

\[ C: S \longrightarrow M \]

may preserve some source-level distinctions, erase others, and expose distinctions absent at the source representation.

Research questions include:

contextual equivalence · source/target observation maps · hyperproperty preservation · side channels · target-level distinguishability · security contracts

49. Property Versus Hyperproperty

Some security properties concern one execution.

Hyperproperties concern sets or relations among multiple executions.

\[ \boxed{ \text{single-trace property preservation} \not\Rightarrow \text{hyperproperty preservation}. } \]

This distinction remains part of the active secure-transformation research program.

50. Control and Model Reduction

Reduced models are studied through closure:

\[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

When this fails, hidden information in the original state influences the reduced future.

Related mathematical areas include:

observability · controllability · state estimation · lumpability · closure modeling · reduced-order dynamics · memory kernels

51. Engineering Research

Status: application and feasibility research.

Engineering applications are treated as realization problems.

A mathematical concept must survive:

physical law · material limits · thermal limits · structural loads · power balance · control stability · manufacturability · reliability · maintainability · cost · environmental constraints · safety

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematical realizability} \not\Rightarrow \text{engineering realizability}. } \]

Energy-system research includes:

nuclear energy · heat transfer · closed cooling loops · open cooling loops · desalination · seawater systems · thermal utilization · material degradation · resource recovery · waste minimization

Propulsion research is evaluated through:

conservation of momentum · energy balance · field interaction · thermodynamics · reaction mass where applicable · boundary conditions · system efficiency · realizability

54. Mathematical-Physics Validation

Candidate physical results should be tested against established mathematical and physical limits.

Examples include:

55. Numerical Validation

\[ \boxed{ \text{numerical agreement} \not\Rightarrow \text{mathematical theorem}. } \]

Numerical tests are nevertheless essential for:

  • testing scaling;
  • identifying unstable regimes;
  • comparing approximations;
  • checking limiting behavior;
  • evaluating sensitivity;

Research pages should use equation-driven scientific figures when they expose actual mathematical behavior.

Appropriate figures include:

57. Falsifiability

Physical research must eventually produce observations capable of distinguishing a proposed model from competing models.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Model A prediction} \neq \text{Model B prediction} } \]

must occur in a regime that is experimentally or observationally accessible if the proposed difference is to be testable.

58. Free Parameters

A model with many unconstrained adjustable parameters may reproduce data without making a strong prediction.

Active research therefore seeks to determine parameters through:

mathematical consistency · symmetry · conservation · known constants · independent experiments · dimensional relations · boundary conditions · microscopic derivation · normalization requirements

A fitted parameter is not automatically a derived constant.

59. Units and Dimensional Analysis

Physical equations must be dimensionally consistent.

If

\[ A=B+C, \]

then \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) must have compatible dimensions.

Dimensionless constants must remain distinct from quantities carrying physical units.

60. Established-Theory Limits

New models should recover known behavior in regimes where established theories have already been successfully tested.

A proposed correction

\[ F_\varepsilon \]

may be required to satisfy

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{\varepsilon\to0} F_\varepsilon = F_0 } \]

when \(F_0\) is the established limiting theory and the limit is mathematically meaningful.

61. Failure Preservation

Failed derivations, counterexamples, no-go results, and broken assumptions remain part of the research record.

\[ \boxed{ \text{failed route} \Longrightarrow \text{constraint on future routes}. } \]

Failure preservation prevents the project from silently returning to already-refuted assumptions.

62. External Prior Art

After mathematical derivation, literature review is used to determine:

  • whether the result is already known;
  • whether a stronger theorem already exists;
  • whether standard terminology already covers the object;
  • whether a known theorem contains the result as a special case;
  • whether the integrated architecture remains distinctive.
\[ \boxed{ \text{truth} \neq \text{novelty}. } \]

Prior-art review addresses history and context. It does not determine whether an internally derived proof is mathematically correct.

63. Research Re-entry

A result derived in one application may return to BM as reusable mathematics only after its application-specific assumptions are separated from its portable mathematical kernel.

\[ \boxed{ \text{application result} \longrightarrow \text{portable mathematical kernel} \longrightarrow \text{BM re-entry}. } \]

64. Portability Firewall

A mathematical construction is a candidate for BM ownership when it can be stated without essential response semantics and remains meaningful across multiple mathematical sectors.

\[ \boxed{ \text{domain-independent mathematical kernel} \longrightarrow \text{BM}. } \] \[ \boxed{ \text{essential response semantics} \longrightarrow \text{GURF}. } \]

Observation, Task, Intervention, Regime, continuation, response ambiguity, and response-state semantics are indicators that a construction may remain GURF-specialized.

67. Public Research Standard

The public encyclopedia will not:

  • present an active hypothesis as an established physical law;
  • present an intermediate theorem as a solution to a larger unsolved problem;
  • call every derived quantity a formal BM Leaf;
  • turn candidate Rooting machinery into a universal theorem;
  • silently erase counterexamples or failed routes;
  • collapse deterministic, stochastic, quantum, or nondeterministic semantics;
  • claim physical realizability from mathematical consistency alone;
  • claim external novelty before prior-art review.

68. Research Principle

\[ \boxed{ \text{derive aggressively} \qquad \text{but classify conservatively}. } \]

The research program is intended to push mathematical structures as far as valid derivation permits while keeping:

proof · hypothesis · numerical evidence · physical interpretation · realization · novelty · independent validation

as separate questions.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Native mathematics determines truth;}\\[4pt] \text{BM determines lawful structural participation;}\\[4pt] \text{GURF specializes that structure when response semantics matter;}\\[4pt] \text{experiment and observation determine physical adequacy.} \end{gathered} } \]
