1. What Belongs on the Research Page

Research begins where the formal registries stop. Definitions tell us what an object means. Theorems tell us what has been proved. The research program records what is being tested, generalized, derived, falsified, repaired, or left open.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Research claim} = \text{mathematical object} + \text{status} + \text{evidence} + \text{failure conditions} + \text{promotion gate}. } \]

2. Research Status Is Multi-Dimensional

One status word is usually not enough. A research object can be mathematically correct but already classical, structurally useful but not theorem-producing, physically suggestive but unrealized, numerically supported but unproved, or internally coherent but externally unaudited.

Mathematical validityDoes the stated derivation or theorem follow from its assumptions?
ProvenanceIs the result classical infrastructure, a specialization, a BM/GURF formulation, or not yet prior-art audited?
Framework maturityCanonical, active, candidate, frozen, conditional, or experimental.
Physical interpretationDoes the mathematical object have a justified physical meaning?
RealizabilityDoes the mathematical or physical construction actually exist?
Empirical supportHas the model been confronted with data or experiment?
FalsifiabilityWhich observation, computation, or theorem would reject the claim?
External validationHas independent mathematical or scientific review been established?

6. Branch Mathematics Foundations

Status: Active formal development
Research class: framework foundations

The BM foundations program studies lawful representation and transformation across typed mathematical structures. The current core architecture is already explicit enough to support theorem, equation, Passport, Root, and example registries; research now asks which additional structural results follow from that architecture.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch} \to \text{Interaction} \to \text{Leaves} \to \text{Tree} \to \text{Response} \to \text{Root gate}. } \]

Established / currently supported

  • Current seven-field Branch specification with explicit sector, type, admissibility, laws, Operations, and Passport obligations.
  • Tree/Response/Root separation.
  • Typed partial composition and true composite domains.
  • Passport/fibre exact-sufficiency machinery in deterministic Set semantics.
  • Structured quotient gate under the appropriate congruence conditions.
  • Sector firewalls separating deterministic, stochastic, relational, privacy, and quantum semantics.

Active work

  • Stronger finite-Network coherence and local-to-global admissibility criteria.
  • Passport-defect propagation through larger Networks.
  • Root re-entry and transfer theorems beyond the deterministic predicate case.
  • Higher-coherence conditions for multi-path and mixed-sector Networks.
  • Exact ownership boundary between portable BM kernels and response-specialized GURF machinery.
Promotion gates
  • State every proposed structural theorem with explicit semantic sector and hypotheses.
  • Separate classical infrastructure from any genuinely BM-native statement.
  • Provide nontrivial examples where the theorem changes what can be proved, computed, or ruled out.

Research outputs to expose publicly

  • Formal theorem statements and proofs.
  • Counterexamples to overgeneralized transport.
  • Worked multi-Branch configurations.
  • Dependency diagrams generated only from formally defined objects.
Definitions: BM · Branch · Tree · Root
Theorems: Leaf propagation · Finite Network evaluation · Defect localization
Structures: Core architecture · Network
Examples: Integrated BM example

7. GURF Foundations

Status: Active canonical response-specialized framework
Research class: response/task/continuation mathematics

GURF is used when response semantics are essential: observation, Task sufficiency, Intervention, admissibility, ambiguity, inference, continuation, repair, law/Regime state, or future response.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Which distinctions in the underlying state are required for the response or future Task?} } \]

Established / currently supported

  • Exact deterministic recoverability by fibre constancy / factorization.
  • Completion-Fibre Task sufficiency.
  • Static Task sufficiency separated from Dynamic Closure.
  • Stochastic test-pullback semantics.
  • Nondeterministic may/must and witness-scope firewalls.
  • Repair as refinement of a Task-insufficient representation.

Active work

  • Constraint-aware recoverability.
  • Approximate and robust continuation under model error.
  • Online Passport refinement under changing Tasks or Regimes.
  • Resource-aware recovery and computational constraints.
  • Experimental certification in tractable inverse problems.
Promotion gates
  • A new GURF theorem must do more than rename a classical factorization fact.
  • For claimed improvement, demonstrate a new bound, obstruction, certification result, or experimentally testable advantage.
Definitions: GURF · Task · Continuation
Theorems: Exact Recoverability · Dynamic Closure
Passports: Passport semantics

9. Benchmark and Validation Program

Status: Active experimental / mathematical validation
Research class: controlled benchmark research

BM and GURF should be tested first on systems whose native mathematics is already well understood. A useful framework must improve organization, certification, experimental design, inference, or failure detection before it is trusted on harder open problems.

Established / currently supported

  • Controlled deterministic and stochastic examples.
  • Linear-algebra residual correction.
  • Markov-kernel composition.
  • Quantum dephasing and measurement examples.
  • Thermal-control and navigation configurations.
  • Structured quotient and witness-scope failure examples.

Active work

  • Blackbody/greybody inverse-recovery benchmark.
  • Controlled deconvolution and reconstruction tasks.
  • State-space systems with hidden-state continuation failures.
  • PDE observation/reduction benchmarks.
  • Secure compilation and privacy preservation examples.
Promotion gates
  • Predeclare the Task and success metric.
  • Compare against an ordinary baseline method.
  • Do not retune the framework after seeing the held-out outcome.
  • Report both successful and failed predictions.
Examples: Full stochastic electro-thermal run

10. Navier–Stokes Research

Status: Active derived mathematics; Clay problem remains open
Research class: PDE research / adversarial framework benchmark

The Navier–Stokes branch studies what information is required for continuation, nonlinear transfer, vorticity growth, spectral interaction, and task-aware reduction. The native PDE remains authoritative.

\[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u = -\nabla p+\nu\Delta u+f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0. \]

Established / currently supported

  • Classical periodic-domain energy identity exposed as an energy/dissipation/forcing Leaf chain.
  • Classical three-dimensional vorticity/enstrophy identity exposed as a distinct Leaf family.
  • Energy-only and magnitude-only spectral representations can be insufficient for phase-sensitive nonlinear-transfer Tasks.
  • Active project-derived diagnostics include derivative-covariance, helical, triadic, and spectral quantities.

Active work

  • Derivative-covariance hierarchy \(F^{(n)}\) and associated invariant diagnostics.
  • Triad amplitude/coherence variables and helical interaction classes.
  • Continuation Passports that retain local-gradient, phase, or vorticity information lost by coarse observables.
  • Search for a genuinely new a priori estimate or continuation obstruction.
Promotion gates
  • A new a priori bound strong enough to advance continuation/control of a critical quantity.
  • A new compactness mechanism.
  • A rigorously proved blow-up exclusion mechanism.
  • Or a genuinely stronger continuation criterion.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Rewriting the PDE in BM notation is organizational, not progress toward global regularity.
  • A diagnostic correlation is not a regularity proof.
  • Finite numerical evidence cannot establish global smoothness.
Reading note

No claim on this page should be read as a solution of the Clay Navier–Stokes problem.

Theorems: Energy identity · Enstrophy identity
Equations: Energy equation · Enstrophy equation
Examples: Navier–Stokes configuration · Energy derivation · Vorticity derivation

11. Navier–Stokes Proof-Gap Map

Status: Open
Research class: research-gate map

The research branch is judged by whether it closes a real arrow in the proof chain, not by how many new diagnostics are introduced.

\[ \boxed{ \text{local existence} \longrightarrow \text{continuation criterion} \longrightarrow \text{critical norm / regularity control} \longrightarrow \text{global regularity}. } \]
Promotion gates
  • Every proposed BM/GURF Transformation must identify which arrow it strengthens.
  • If it produces no new bound, compactness, continuation, or exclusion result, classify it as structural organization or diagnostic research.

12. Navier–Stokes Derived Leaves

Status: Active derived diagnostics
Research class: BM-native organization of PDE-derived quantities

The research value of a Leaf is not the label; it is whether the extracted quantity feeds a later derivation or exposes information that another representation loses.

Established / currently supported

  • Energy \(E(t)\), dissipation \(D(t)\), and forcing \(F(t)\) form a closed exact balance identity under the stated assumptions.
  • Enstrophy, vortex stretching, viscous vorticity dissipation, and vorticity forcing form a different exact identity.
  • Magnitude-only spectral reduction preserves energy-type diagnostics while generally losing phase-sensitive nonlinear-transfer information.

Active work

  • Derivative covariance invariants.
  • Helical sector loading.
  • Triad coherence and amplitude variables.
  • Neighbor-network structure of nonlinear forcing.
  • Co-location and continuation diagnostics.
Examples: Spectral Passport loss

13. Yang–Mills Research

Status: Active conditional research; mass-gap problem open
Research class: gauge/spectral/open-problem research

The Yang–Mills branch studies gauge-compatible representation, infrared structure, spectral behavior, boundary response, leakage, shell depth, coherent return, and conditional finite/truncated models.

Established / currently supported

  • Finite lattice gauge transformations and Wilson-loop invariance are used as classical benchmark mathematics.
  • Project work has derived conditional finite/truncated statements involving boundary susceptibility, tail floors, leakage, shell-depth recursions, coherent-return thresholds, and Gauss-law support.
  • Variational enrichment correctly gives monotone upper bounds for ordered Ritz eigenvalues; differences of successive Ritz values are not automatically monotone.

Active work

  • Gauge-invariant observable sectors.
  • Boundary Green-matrix and susceptibility analysis.
  • Shell-depth localization and coherent return.
  • Global-return obstructions.
  • Controlled truncated Hilbert-space benchmarks.
Promotion gates
  • Construction/existence of the relevant four-dimensional quantum theory.
  • Correct gauge-invariant Hilbert-space structure.
  • Positivity and all required axioms.
  • A uniform positive spectral gap in the required continuum/infinite-volume sense.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Infrared suppression is not a proof of a mass gap.
  • A finite truncation gap is not the continuum Yang–Mills mass gap.
  • Large boundary diagonal terms alone are insufficient without the required off-diagonal/global-return control.
  • A proposed proof failing one of the existence/positivity/gauge/spectral gates is not a Clay solution.
Reading note

The four-dimensional Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap problem remains unsolved.

Theorems: Wilson-loop invariance
Equations: Wilson loop
Examples: Lattice gauge example

14. Yang–Mills Mass-Gap Gates

Status: Open
Research class: claim-certification gates

Any mass-gap claim must pass all required mathematical gates; an effective or approximate infrared scale is insufficient.

\[ \boxed{ \text{candidate gap argument} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} \text{theory existence},\\ \text{gauge-invariant state space},\\ \text{positivity / required axioms},\\ \text{controlled continuum/infinite-volume limit},\\ \text{uniform positive spectral gap}. \end{cases} } \]
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Skipping a gate and interpreting a finite-model spectral feature as the full theorem.
  • Treating an effective mass parameter as the rigorous spectral gap.
  • Using a variational statement about eigenvalue upper bounds to infer unproved monotonicity of the gap.

15. Gravity and Spacetime Research

Status: Active mathematical / physical-model research
Research class: geometry, inference, and physical realization

Gravity research is organized around spacetime geometry, proper time, geodesics, tidal response, frame dragging, lensing, observational reconstruction, and the distinction between mathematical geometry and speculative physical mechanism.

Established / currently supported

  • General Relativity remains the native geometric authority in GR applications.
  • Proper time, geodesics, curvature, tidal response, lensing, and frame dragging are treated as native geometric quantities rather than new BM laws.
  • Observation and inverse reconstruction can be organized through GURF Passports and completion fibres.

Active work

  • Joint-observation reconstruction of metric/source information.
  • Tidal-response organization.
  • Extended/halo response hypotheses under HDE.
  • Forward-causal proper-time gradients and observational consequences.
  • Lensing, memory, and boundary-response tests.
Promotion gates
  • Specify an actual metric or metric family.
  • Show the source/stress-energy or other governing field equations consistently.
  • Derive geodesic, lensing, redshift, timing, and tidal consequences from the same model.
  • Compare against observations without post-outcome parameter retuning.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • A time component alone is not a complete spacetime metric.
  • A qualitative proper-time analogy is not a physical model.
  • A compact-center or halo interpretation must satisfy the full field equations and observational constraints.

16. Forward-Causal Geometry Rule

Status: Governing research constraint
Research class: physical-realizability constraint

Physical research does not use backward travel through an observer's experienced past as a realizable mechanism. Closed-timelike-curve or retrocausal constructions may be studied as mathematical geometries, but physical proposals focus on forward-causal effects.

Established / currently supported

  • Forward-causal observables include proper-time gradients, geodesic deflection/acceleration, frame dragging, tidal stretch/squeeze, focusing/defocusing, redshift, lensing, and memory effects.
Promotion gates
  • Preserve causal consistency of the actual physical model.
  • Separate mathematical spacetime constructions from physically realizable mechanisms.

17. Extended / Halo Response Research

Status: Active experimental / conditional
Research class: HDE-linked gravitational response hypothesis

Extended-response research studies whether observationally relevant gravitational information may require a state extending beyond a narrow local source description. The HDE framework organizes the distinction between a primary domain and additional response-support information.

\[ D\subseteq D_{\rm ext}, \qquad H=D_{\rm ext}\setminus D, \qquad x_{\rm ext}=(x_D,x_H). \]

Active work

  • Metric inversion from joint timing/lensing/dynamical observations.
  • Boundary and persistence conditions.
  • Comparison of compact-source and extended-response descriptions.
  • Observable signatures capable of distinguishing the proposed model from standard alternatives.
Promotion gates
  • Full metric/source closure.
  • Distinct falsifiable observational prediction.
  • No violation of established causal and conservation requirements.
  • Fit existing constraints before extrapolating to new regimes.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Matching one scaling relation does not establish the ontology.
  • An extended mathematical carrier is not automatically a physical halo.
  • A model with free parameters that can absorb every outcome is not sufficiently falsifiable.

18. Quantum Information and Control

Status: Active
Research class: operator / measurement / response research

Quantum research uses ordinary quantum mechanics as the native authority and BM/GURF to track which operator-level distinctions survive channels, measurements, coarse observations, and control.

Established / currently supported

  • Density operators, quantum channels, measurements, and expectation values retain their native operator semantics.
  • Dephasing can preserve computational-basis populations while destroying phase-sensitive information.
  • Classical output statistics can be sufficient for one Task and insufficient for another operator-sensitive Task.

Active work

  • Task-relative quantum distinguishability.
  • Entanglement Passports.
  • Measurement/control interface ordering.
  • Quantum-classical handoff and re-entry.
  • Joint statistics and coupling constraints.
Promotion gates
  • State complete positivity/trace conditions where required.
  • Do not replace a quantum channel by a deterministic Set map unless the declared Task justifies the reduction.
  • Separate no-signalling constraints from interpretive claims about entanglement.

Equations: Dephasing
Examples: Quantum example

19. Entanglement Passport Program

Status: Active research
Research class: quantum joint-information program

The Entanglement Passport program asks which joint quantum distinctions must remain available for a later Task when local marginal information is insufficient.

Active work

  • Local marginals versus joint state information.
  • Correlation-sensitive Tasks.
  • Measurement-basis dependence.
  • No-signalling-compatible joint statistics.
  • Classical post-measurement representations and what they erase.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Treating entanglement as a classical hidden communication channel.
  • Inferring a unique joint quantum state from insufficient local marginals.
  • Collapsing measurement context when the Task depends on it.

21. Blackbody / Greybody Benchmark

Status: Active benchmark candidate
Research class: tractable empirical validation program

The blackbody/greybody inverse problem is a useful GURF proving ground because the forward physics is comparatively well understood while the inverse problem still contains real degeneracy, calibration, uncertainty, and multi-channel observation issues.

Active work

  • Target-relative identifiability.
  • Calibration and bandpass effects.
  • Temperature/emissivity degeneracy.
  • Multi-channel observation design.
  • Preregistered model discrimination.
Promotion gates
  • Predeclare the forward model and Task.
  • Use held-out or independently generated observations.
  • Compare with ordinary inverse-method baselines.
  • Report uncertainty and model rejection, not only best fits.

Research outputs to expose publicly

  • Synthetic-data benchmark.
  • Experimental protocol.
  • Prediction ledger.
  • Calibration and uncertainty analysis.
  • Baseline comparison.

22. Computation, Privacy, and Compilation

Status: Active
Research class: formal/computational systems research

Computation research studies lawful abstraction, compression, compilation, secure transformation, privacy, hyperproperties, and preservation of distinctions required by later Tasks.

Established / currently supported

  • Exact Set-level equivalence is not a valid substitute for differential privacy.
  • Compilation or abstraction can preserve one property while destroying another.
  • Property and hyperproperty requirements must be distinguished.

Active work

  • Secure-compiler Passports.
  • Contextual/hyperproperty preservation.
  • Source-to-target distinction tracking.
  • Resource-aware recoverability.
  • Privacy Passports based on divergences/inequalities rather than exact equivalence.
Known failure / no-go conditions
  • Treating source equivalence as automatically preserved at machine level.
  • Using exact quotient equivalence to represent differential privacy.
  • Ignoring computational cost when the Task itself is complexity-sensitive.

24. Open-Problem Stress Tests

Status: Active as adversarial benchmarks
Research class: open-problem audit

Major unsolved problems are useful stress tests only when the framework is judged by whether it produces a genuine mathematical advance at the correct proof gate.

Established / currently supported

  • Navier–Stokes and Yang–Mills are not solved by the current work.
  • Framework organization, fibre language, or response reformulation is not itself progress on an open theorem unless it produces a new mathematical ingredient.

Active work

  • Navier–Stokes as a continuation/a-priori-estimate stress test.
  • Yang–Mills as a gauge/spectral/existence stress test.
  • Other hard problems only where BM/GURF exposes a concrete missing mathematical structure.
Promotion gates
  • State the exact theorem target.
  • Map the proposed framework step to a missing proof step.
  • Reject the claim if the framework produces no new bound, compactness, obstruction, construction, or theorem.

25. Falsifiability and Prediction Discipline

Status: Governing research rule
Research class: claim certification

A physical or computational research proposal should expose a prediction that can fail. Flexible parameter fitting after the outcome does not count as a strong test.

\[ \boxed{ \text{assumptions} \longrightarrow \text{predeclared prediction} \longrightarrow \text{observation} \longrightarrow \{\text{pass},\text{fail},\text{inconclusive}\}. } \]
Promotion gates
  • Freeze the evaluated model before the held-out test where practical.
  • Specify parameter ranges and nuisance parameters.
  • State what result would falsify the model.
  • Separate model failure from failure of the broader BM/GURF architecture unless the tested claim is genuinely framework-universal.

26. Numerical and Experimental Evidence

Status: Active
Research class: validation methodology

Numerical work supports a research claim only to the degree warranted by convergence, uncertainty, resolution, model assumptions, and independent checks.

Active work

  • Parameter sweeps.
  • Convergence and grid/refinement studies.
  • Residual and conservation audits.
  • Uncertainty propagation.
  • Synthetic-data recovery.
  • Cross-method or cross-code comparison.
  • Experimental calibration where physical data are used.
Promotion gates
  • Expose numerical method and parameters.
  • Report resolution/convergence behavior.
  • Distinguish numerical evidence from proof.
  • Keep model uncertainty separate from numerical error where possible.

27. Negative Results, No-Go Statements, and Quarantine

Status: Canonical research practice
Research class: failure preservation

Failed derivations and counterexamples remain part of the mathematical record when they identify a real obstruction. A research branch is stronger when it can explain why a tempting shortcut fails.

Established / currently supported

  • Raw observational equivalence need not define a lawful structured quotient.
  • Separate witnesses need not give one joint witness.
  • A stochastic mean can be insufficient for a nonlinear Task.
  • Static Task sufficiency need not imply Dynamic Closure.
  • A finite/truncated spectral feature does not establish the Yang–Mills mass gap.
  • A diagnostic identity does not solve Navier–Stokes regularity.
Promotion gates
  • Preserve the exact failed claim.
  • State the counterexample or missing hypothesis.
  • Do not recycle a quarantined argument without explicitly repairing the failed gate.
Examples: Failure laboratory

28. Research Promotion Gates

Status: Governing rule
Research class: status-control system

Research objects are promoted only when the evidence required by the stronger status has been supplied.

\[ \boxed{ \text{candidate} \to \text{derived} \to \text{proved / validated} \to \text{broader claim} } \] only when every intermediate gate is satisfied.

Established / currently supported

  • Definition does not imply existence.
  • Numerical agreement does not imply theorem.
  • Theorem in a toy/truncated model does not imply theorem in the target continuum theory.
  • Mathematical realization does not imply physical realization.
  • Framework usefulness does not establish historical novelty.

Research outputs to expose publicly

  • Proof artifact.
  • Counterexample audit.
  • Numerical/experimental artifact.
  • Provenance status.
  • Falsification criterion.

29. Central Research Principle

Status: Canonical governing principle
Research class: research discipline

BM and GURF should make difficult mathematics easier to audit, not easier to overclaim.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{native mathematics first;}\\ \text{framework role second;}\\ \text{derive before promoting;}\\ \text{preserve failures;}\\ \text{separate proof, provenance, realization, and experiment;}\\ \text{let the strongest surviving mathematics determine the public claim.} \end{gathered} } \]
Reading note

The best research result is not always a new theorem. A precise obstruction, corrected claim, falsified model, or verified boundary of applicability is also genuine mathematical progress.
