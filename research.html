Research Program

This section contains mathematical investigations, derived results, conjectures, applications, computational studies, and open problems.

Each research area is evaluated according to its own mathematics. Branch Mathematics provides the governing meta-structural framework, while GURF is used where genuine response semantics are present.

Pure Mathematics

Branch Mathematics Foundations

Representation, transformation, interaction, composition, Roots, Passports, Leaves, Trees, interfaces, realization, and re-entry.

Structural Quotients and Equivalence

Study of lawful quotienting, preserved distinctions, congruence, abstraction, realization, and compatibility.

Coherence and Composition

Typed interfaces, commuting diagrams, path compatibility, gluing, re-entry, and higher structural coherence.

Mathematical Physics

Gravity and Spacetime

Geometry, tidal response, observation, propagation, gravitational structure, and candidate extensions.

Quantum Theory

Distinguishability, observation, entanglement, information, response structure, and lawful continuation.

Yang–Mills Theory

Spectral structure, mass-gap related mathematics, boundary response, shell structure, variational methods, and obstruction analysis.

Navier–Stokes and Fluid Dynamics

Vorticity, strain, scale interaction, spectral structure, continuation variables, and nonlinear fluid behavior.

Electromagnetism and Radiation

Light, photons, radio waves, microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays, transmission, absorption, reflection, thermal radiation, and electromagnetic propagation.

Computation and Information

Information preservation, computation, compression, control, secure transformation, stochastic systems, nondeterministic systems, and mathematical response structures.

Engineering and Physical Systems

Applications may include energy systems, propulsion, nuclear systems, radiation transport, thermal systems, fluids, communications, control systems, and other engineering domains.

Research Status

Research entries distinguish among established mathematics, direct derivations, conditional results, numerical or experimental evidence, conjectures, proposed physical interpretations, and open questions.

A mathematical derivation does not by itself establish external novelty, physical realization, or independent validation.
