1. How to Read an Equation in BM

An equation is useful only when its mathematical objects and the conditions under which it is valid are visible. The registry therefore separates an equation's symbolic form from its role in a larger BM or GURF structure.

DefinitionIntroduces a mathematical object or quantity.
Exact identityFollows exactly from the stated mathematics.
Factorization / compatibility conditionStates when one structure lawfully descends, composes, or closes.
Quantitative diagnosticMeasures a defect, spread, sensitivity, error, or ambiguity.
Structural schemaShows mathematical organization rather than a numerical law.
Applied identityNative mathematics used inside BM/GURF without being relabeled as newly discovered domain mathematics.
Student rule

Never read a displayed equation by symbols alone. Ask: what are the objects, what is the domain, what is the semantic sector, what assumptions are active, and what does the equation actually allow you to conclude?

3. Structural BM Schema

Classification: structural schema
\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[3pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]

Meaning

This is the broad structural direction of BM. It is not a numerical law and does not require every Tree to be a linear sequence.

Limits
  • A Branch may participate in several Interactions.
  • A Tree may be parallel, branching, coupled, recursive, or otherwise organized.
Definitions: BM · Tree/Response/Root distinction
Examples: Integrated example

4. Current Branch Specification

Classification: formal BM organizational definition

Objects

  • \(\mathsf S_B\): mathematical sector.
  • \(\tau_B\): type.
  • \(X_B\): carrier or state object.
  • \(A_B\): admissible domain or sector-correct analogue.
  • \(\mathcal L_B\): laws, constraints, identities, invariants.
  • \(\mathcal O_B\): admissible native Operations.
  • \(\mathcal P_B\): Passport and Interface obligations.
\[ \boxed{ B= \left( \mathsf S_B, \tau_B, X_B, A_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, \mathcal P_B \right) } \]

Meaning

The current Branch record explicitly separates mathematical sector, type, carrier, admissible domain, native laws, native Operations, and Passport/Interface obligations.

Limits
  • The tuple is an organizational record; a native mathematical sector may require richer internal structure.
Definitions: Branch
Examples: Vector Branch · Function-space Branch

5. Interaction Equation

Classification: canonical BM typed structure
\[ \boxed{ I: D_I \subseteq A_{B_1}\times\cdots\times A_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. } \]

Meaning

The governing domain is built from the participating Branches' admissible domains, not merely their ambient carriers. A point in the ambient product is not automatically an admissible Interaction input.

Assumptions

  • The deterministic function form is being used. Other sectors use their native Interaction object.
Limits
  • Interaction lawfulness may also require unit, regularity, positivity, conservation, coupling, boundary, or Passport conditions.
Definitions: Interaction · Admissibility
Examples: Power Interaction · Matrix Interaction

6. Leaf Extraction Equation

Classification: canonical BM structural equation
\[ z=I(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \qquad \boxed{ L_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j(I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)). } \]

Meaning

The Interaction produces \(z\). A Leaf-extraction map \(\ell_j\) selects typed information \(L_j\) from that result. One Interaction can expose several different Leaves.

Limits
  • Not every intermediate symbol is a Leaf.
  • A Leaf is not automatically a Root or an independent Branch.
Definitions: Leaf
Theorems: Leaf propagation theorem
Examples: Multiple vector Leaves · Spectral Leaves

7. Leaf-to-Leaf Propagation

Classification: typed composite equation
\[ I:D_I\to Z, \qquad L=\ell(I(x)), \] \[ J:D_J\subseteq X_L\times X_C\to W, \] \[ \boxed{ L' = \ell'\!\left(J(L,c)\right), \qquad (L,c)\in D_J. } \]

Meaning

A Leaf can feed later mathematics only when the receiving Interaction admits it. The structural chain is

\[ x\xrightarrow{I}z\xrightarrow{\ell}L \xrightarrow{J(\,\cdot\,,c)}w \xrightarrow{\ell'}L'. \]
Limits
  • Leaf provenance does not waive the next domain, type, unit, law, or Passport checks.

Theorems: Leaf propagation

51. Continuous Root-State Rate

Classification: model-dependent differential quantity
\[ \boxed{ \dot R(t)=\frac{dR}{dt}. } \] In a normed vector space, \[ \|\dot R(t)\| \] measures instantaneous state-change magnitude.

Meaning

Limits

55. Sensitivity / Jacobian

Classification: standard differential diagnostic

Meaning

Limits

\[ d_Z(G(y),G(y')) \le L\,d_Y(y,y'), \] so if \[ d_Y(F(x),F(x'))\le\varepsilon, \] then \[ \boxed{ d_Z(G(F(x)),G(F(x'))) \le L\varepsilon. } \]

Meaning

The downstream Lipschitz constant converts upstream approximation error into a guaranteed downstream bound.

Examples: Quantization error

57. Gaussian Fisher Carrier

Classification: classical local Gaussian information equation
\[ g = V^{-1/2}\frac{d\mu}{d\theta}, \qquad I_{\rm full} = \|g\|^2, \] \[ \boxed{ I(P)=\|Pg\|^2\le\|g\|^2. } \]

Meaning

For a scalar parameter with locally fixed covariance, a whitened orthogonal projection preserves all local Fisher information exactly when the Task direction \(g\) lies in the projection range.

Assumptions

  • Gaussian observation model.
  • Parameter-independent covariance locally.
  • Orthogonal projection in whitened coordinates.
Limits
  • This is local and linear/Gaussian; it is not a universal compression law.
Theorems: Gaussian Fisher theorem

58. Finite-Hypothesis Mahalanobis Carrier

Classification: classical whitened-geometry equation
\[ D= [\,z_2-z_1,\ldots,z_M-z_1\,], \] \[ \boxed{ \dim(\text{smallest exact linear carrier}) = \operatorname{rank}(D). } \]

Meaning

The span of the pairwise model differences is the smallest linear subspace that can preserve every whitened pairwise distance exactly.

Limits
  • The result is finite-hypothesis and linear in whitened Euclidean geometry.
Theorems: Mahalanobis carrier theorem

59. Periodic Incompressible Navier–Stokes Energy Identity

Classification: classical applied PDE identity
\[ \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u = -\nabla p+\nu\Delta u+f, \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0, \] \[ E(t)=\frac12\int_\Omega|u|^2dx, \qquad D(t)=\nu\int_\Omega|\nabla u|^2dx, \qquad F(t)=\int_\Omega f\cdot u\,dx, \] \[ \boxed{ \frac{dE}{dt} = -D+F. } \]

Meaning

On a smooth periodic incompressible flow, convection and pressure make no net contribution to the total energy balance; viscosity dissipates energy and forcing injects it.

Limits
  • This identity does not solve the three-dimensional regularity problem.
Theorems: Energy identity proof
Examples: Full derivation

60. Three-Dimensional Vorticity / Enstrophy Identity

Classification: classical applied PDE identity
\[ \omega=\nabla\times u, \] \[ \partial_t\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega = (\omega\cdot\nabla)u +\nu\Delta\omega +\nabla\times f, \] \[ \boxed{ \frac12\frac{d}{dt}\int_\Omega|\omega|^2dx = \int_\Omega \omega\cdot(\omega\cdot\nabla)u\,dx - \nu\int_\Omega|\nabla\omega|^2dx + \int_\Omega \omega\cdot(\nabla\times f)\,dx. } \]

Meaning

The stretching term is a distinct vorticity Leaf family that is not determined merely by total kinetic energy.

Limits
  • The identity is not a global-regularity theorem.

61. Wilson-Loop Gauge Invariance

Classification: classical lattice-gauge identity
\[ U_{xy}\mapsto \Omega_xU_{xy}\Omega_y^{-1}, \] \[ U(C)= U_{x_0x_1}\cdots U_{x_{m-1}x_0}, \] \[ \boxed{ W(C)=\operatorname{tr}U(C) } \] with \[ \boxed{ W(C)\mapsto W(C). } \]

Adjacent gauge transformations cancel around a closed loop; the remaining conjugation disappears under the trace.
