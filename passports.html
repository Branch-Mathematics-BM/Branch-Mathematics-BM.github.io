Passports

Mathematical specifications of what information must remain available for later lawful use: distinctions, Tasks, laws, invariants, uncertainty, quantifiers, couplings, Interface data, continuation information, and receiving conditions.

1. What a Passport Is

A Passport is declared mathematical information that must remain available, verifiable, or compatible for a later mathematical purpose.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Passport} = \text{downstream mathematical obligations attached to retained information}. } \]

A Passport can govern representation, reduction, Transformation, Interaction, composition, quotienting, export, Root reuse, re-entry, continuation, inference, control, safety, or another declared downstream use. It is not a permission label and does not replace native proof.

2. Structural Role

\[ \boxed{ \text{source mathematics} \longrightarrow \text{representation / Transformation} \longrightarrow \text{Passport audit} \longrightarrow \text{later lawful mathematics}. } \]

The Passport says what must survive. The native mathematics decides whether it actually survives and whether the receiving object can use it.

3. Passport Semantics Must Be Declared

There is no single universal Passport comparison relation. The mathematical sector determines the correct semantics.

Exact deterministicEquality of retained values; fibres and equivalence classes.
Approximate / metricTolerance, norm, metric, divergence, risk, or error bounds.
StochasticProbability-law equality, test expectations, divergences, couplings, and measurable structure.
NondeterministicRelations, may/must reachability, strategies, and quantifier scope.
PrivacyProbabilistic inequalities between neighboring inputs; not exact equivalence.
Law / RegimeThe active law or model context itself is Passport information.
ConservativeNative conserved or invariant quantities required to persist.
DynamicInformation needed for future Tasks or reduced-state closure.
Student rule

Before asking whether two states have “the same Passport,” first ask what mathematical comparison is actually intended. Equality, closeness, equality in distribution, common feasibility, and privacy are different mathematical statements.

5. General Passport Specification

Role: organizational specification

A Passport record identifies the source, the information to retain, the semantic comparison used for that information, and the later mathematical obligations that make retention necessary.

\[ \boxed{ P= \left( \mathsf S_P, X, \mathcal C, \{b_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C}, \mathcal A_P, \mathcal T_P, \mathcal I_P \right) } \] as a useful organizational form, where \[ \begin{aligned} \mathsf S_P&=\text{semantic sector},\\ X&=\text{source object or state space},\\ \mathcal C&=\text{Passport-component index set},\\ b_c&=\text{component observation / requirement map},\\ \mathcal A_P&=\text{admissibility and comparison rules},\\ \mathcal T_P&=\text{Tasks or future uses},\\ \mathcal I_P&=\text{receiving Interface obligations}. \end{aligned} \]
Limits
  • The record is organizational. Different sectors may use kernels, relations, operator maps, couplings, divergences, or other native objects instead of ordinary functions.

6. Passport Observation Maps

A Passport component can be represented by a map recording one declared observable, invariant, law index, boundary value, Task-relevant statistic, Interface variable, or other required quantity.

\[ \boxed{ b_c:X\to Y_c, \qquad c\in\mathcal C. } \]
Limits
  • The deterministic-map form is one semantic sector, not the universal Passport model.

7. Combined Passport Map

Role: joint exact deterministic representation

When the required product exists in the native sector, the active Passport observations can be assembled into a joint map.

\[ \boxed{ B_P(x)=\bigl(b_c(x)\bigr)_{c\in\mathcal C}, \qquad B_P:X\to\prod_{c\in\mathcal C}Y_c. } \]
Limits

8. Exact Passport Equivalence

Role: exact deterministic semantics

Two states are exactly Passport-equivalent when every active deterministic Passport observation agrees.

\[ \boxed{ x\sim_Px' \iff B_P(x)=B_P(x') \iff b_c(x)=b_c(x') \quad\forall c\in\mathcal C. } \]
Limits
  • Approximate closeness, equality in distribution, privacy, simulation, or gauge equivalence are not automatically represented by this relation.

Theorems: Passport equivalence theorem

9. Equivalence-Relation Audit

Role: exact deterministic theorem use

For ordinary well-defined component functions and ordinary equality, the exact Passport relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

\[ x\sim_Px, \] \[ x\sim_Px'\Rightarrow x'\sim_Px, \] \[ x\sim_Px',\;x'\sim_Px'' \Rightarrow \boxed{x\sim_Px''}. \]
Limits
  • Tolerance relations often fail transitivity and should not be promoted to equivalence relations without proof.
Theorems: Passport equivalence theorem

10. Passport Classes

Role: exact deterministic equivalence classes

The class of \(x\) contains exactly the states that the active exact Passport cannot distinguish from \(x\).

\[ \boxed{ [x]_P=\{x'\in X:x'\sim_Px\}. } \]
Limits
  • A class is representation-relative, not a claim that its states are identical in the source mathematics.

11. Passport Fibres

Role: exact compatible-state set

For a realized Passport value \(\ell\), the fibre contains the source states consistent with that exact retained information.

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak C_P(\ell) = B_P^{-1}(\ell) = \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} b_c^{-1}(\ell_c). } \]
Definitions: Completion fibre

12. Empty, Singleton, and Nontrivial Fibres

Role: exact-set diagnostic

The fibre size tells us how much exact source ambiguity remains under the declared Passport.

\[ \mathfrak C_P(\ell)=\varnothing \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{inconsistent exact Passport data}, \] \[ |\mathfrak C_P(\ell)|=1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{unique compatible source state}, \] \[ |\mathfrak C_P(\ell)|>1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{source ambiguity remains}. \]
Limits
  • A nontrivial fibre is not automatically a failure. It matters only when the later Task or law distinguishes states inside that fibre.
Examples: Finite fibre audit

14. Exact Passport Sufficiency

Role: exact deterministic factorization

A Passport is exactly sufficient for Task \(T\) when the Task can be computed from the realized Passport value alone.

\[ \boxed{ T=\widetilde T\circ B_P, \qquad \widetilde T:B_P(X)\to W. } \]
Limits
  • The natural factor domain is \(B_P(X)\), not an arbitrary ambient codomain.
  • Sufficiency for one Task does not imply sufficiency for another or Dynamic Closure.

Role: equivalent exact deterministic condition

Exact Task sufficiency holds precisely when the Task is constant on every Passport fibre.

\[ \boxed{ B_P(x)=B_P(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

16. Task Equivalence

Role: exact Task-generated equivalence

The Task itself induces an equivalence relation according to the distinctions it cares about.

\[ x\sim_Tx' \iff T(x)=T(x'), \] \[ \boxed{ \text{exact Passport sufficiency} \iff \sim_P\subseteq\sim_T. } \]

17. Sufficiency Is Task-Dependent

Role: response/task distinction

The same Passport can be sufficient for one Task and insufficient for another.

\[ \sim_P\subseteq\sim_{T_1}, \qquad \boxed{ \sim_P\not\subseteq\sim_{T_2}. } \]
Limits
  • There is no meaningful unqualified claim that a Passport is simply 'sufficient' without naming the required use.
Examples: Temperature/humidity/pressure Tasks

18. Task-Generated Exact Quotient

Role: maximal exact Set-level compression

The Task equivalence produces the coarsest Set-level quotient that still determines the Task exactly.

\[ \boxed{ X/{\sim_T}. } \]
What it allows
  • Merge every source distinction invisible to the Task while preserving exact Task determination at the Set level.
Limits
  • Additional algebraic, topological, smooth, measurable, dynamic, privacy, or Interface structure can require more information.

19. Finite Exact Carrier Bound

Role: finite information lower bound

For finite exact deterministic Tasks, the number of realized Passport states cannot be smaller than the number of distinct Task outputs.

\[ \boxed{ |B_P(X)|\ge|T(X)|. } \]
Limits
  • This is a finite cardinality bound, not a bit-rate, statistical efficiency, or computational-complexity theorem.

20. Passport Refinement

Role: exact information order

Passport \(P\) is at least as informative as Passport \(Q\) when \(P\) distinguishes every pair distinguished by \(Q\).

\[ \boxed{ \sim_P\subseteq\sim_Q. } \]
What it allows
  • Add exact information without enlarging ambiguity classes.
  • Transfer exact Task sufficiency from a coarser sufficient Passport to a finer one.

21. Combined Passports

Role: parallel exact refinement

Two exact deterministic Passports can be combined through a joint representation when the native product exists.

\[ (B_P,B_Q):X\to Y\times Z, \] \[ \boxed{ \sim_{P\wedge Q} = \sim_P\cap\sim_Q. } \]
What it allows
  • Refine ambiguity by retaining both exact observation families.
Limits
  • For stochastic objects, a pair of marginals is not a joint law.

22. Joint Feasibility

Role: shared-witness Passport condition

When several Passport requirements must hold simultaneously, one common admissible witness is required unless the specification explicitly allows separate witnesses.

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{j=1}^m P_j(x)\neq\varnothing. } \]
Failure conditions
  • Every individual feasible set is nonempty, but their intersection is empty.
  • Separate witnesses are incorrectly reused as one common witness.

27. Approximate Passport Conditions

Role: metric/divergence/tolerance semantics

Approximate Passport comparison requires an explicit discrepancy and tolerance.

\[ \boxed{ d_c(b_c(x),b_c(x')) \le\varepsilon_c. } \]
Limits
  • The discrepancy, tolerance, and domain are part of the mathematical specification.
  • A tolerance relation need not be transitive and must not automatically be treated as an equivalence or quotient relation.

Role: quantitative ambiguity diagnostic

When the Task space carries a metric, Task diameter measures how much Task ambiguity remains inside one exact Passport fibre.

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{diam}_T(\mathfrak C_P(\ell)) = \sup_{x,x'\in\mathfrak C_P(\ell)} d_W(T(x),T(x')). } \]
What it allows
  • Distinguish harmless source ambiguity from Task-relevant ambiguity.
  • Provide an exact criterion when the diameter is zero.

29. Approximate Reconstruction from Small Fibres

Role: quantitative Task guarantee

If every fibre has Task diameter at most \(\varepsilon\), choosing one representative from each fibre gives an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate Task reconstruction.

\[ \operatorname{diam}_T(r^{-1}(y))\le\varepsilon \] and \[ \widehat T(y)=T(s(y)) \] give \[ \boxed{ d_W(T(x),\widehat T(r(x)))\le\varepsilon. } \]
Limits
  • The metric and representative selection are part of the construction; the bound need not be optimal.

30. Stochastic Passports

Role: probability-law semantics

A stochastic Passport may map a source state to a probability law rather than to one deterministic point.

\[ \boxed{ M:X\rightsquigarrow Y, \qquad x\mapsto M(x,\cdot). } \] For a bounded or otherwise admissible test function, \[ \boxed{ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \int_Y\varphi(y)M(x,dy). } \]
Limits
  • Measurability, integrability, law equality, and the chosen probability space remain native mathematical requirements.
Definitions: Stochastic semantics
Equations: Stochastic kernel · Test pullback
Examples: Noisy sensor

31. Exact Stochastic Passport Equivalence

Role: exact equality of output laws

Two source states are exactly stochastically Passport-equivalent when their output probability laws are identical.

\[ \boxed{ x\sim_Mx' \iff M(x,\cdot)=M(x',\cdot). } \]
Limits
  • Equality of one moment or one test statistic is weaker than equality of laws unless the chosen test family is measure-determining.

32. Approximate Stochastic Passports

Role: probability discrepancy semantics

Approximate stochastic comparison uses a declared distance or divergence between output laws.

\[ \boxed{ D\!\left(M(x,\cdot),M(x',\cdot)\right) \le\varepsilon. } \]

Carries

  • Possible discrepancies include total variation, Wasserstein distance, relative entropy, Hellinger distance, or another justified law comparison.
Limits
  • Different discrepancies answer different mathematical questions and are not interchangeable.

33. Stochastic Passport Composition

Role: Markov-kernel composition

When a stochastic Passport is followed by another stochastic Transformation, composition integrates over the intermediate law.

\[ K:X\rightsquigarrow Y, \qquad L:Y\rightsquigarrow Z, \] \[ \boxed{ (L\circ K)(x,A) = \int_YL(y,A)\,K(x,dy). } \] For tests, \[ \boxed{ (L\circ K)^\dagger\phi = K^\dagger(L^\dagger\phi). } \]
Theorems: Markov composition theorem
Examples: Finite Markov chain

34. Differential-Privacy Passport Firewall

Role: privacy semantics firewall

Differential privacy is not an exact-equivalence Passport. It constrains the relative output laws produced by neighboring inputs.

\[ x\sim_{\rm adj}x' \] and \[ \boxed{ \Pr[M(x)\in S] \le e^\varepsilon \Pr[M(x')\in S] +\delta. } \]
Limits
  • Exact equivalence classes, approximate metric balls, and differential privacy are different mathematical structures.
  • Privacy requires adjacency, probability-law semantics, and quantified output events.
Theorems: Privacy firewall

35. Nondeterministic Passports

Role: relation/set-valued semantics

A nondeterministic Passport must record not only reachable outcomes but also the quantifier governing downstream requirements.

\[ R\subseteq X\times Y, \] \[ \Diamond_R(U)=\{x:\exists y\in U,\ xRy\}, \] \[ \Box_R(U)=\{x:\forall y,\ xRy\Rightarrow y\in U\}. \]
Limits
  • May and must semantics differ even when the same relation is used.

Theorems: Existential/universal distinction

36. Quantifier Firewall

Role: logical scope Passport

Quantifier order, witness reuse, and strategy reuse are part of the Passport whenever the downstream statement depends on them.

\[ \boxed{ \exists \pi\,\forall\omega \neq \forall\omega\,\exists\pi_\omega } \] and \[ \boxed{ \forall c\,\exists x_c\,P(c,x_c) \not\Rightarrow \exists x\,\forall c\,P(c,x). } \]
Failure conditions
  • A witness selected separately for each Task is incorrectly treated as one shared witness.
  • An adaptive strategy is treated as if it were fixed in advance, or vice versa.
Theorems: Quantifier-scope non-equivalence
Examples: Witness reuse failure

37. Joint Witness Scope

Role: coupled feasibility structure

When one object must satisfy several requirements simultaneously, Passport scope must say whether the same witness is reused.

\[ \boxed{ \exists x\in\bigcap_jP_j } \] is stronger than \[ \forall j\,\exists x_j\in P_j. \]
Limits
  • Separate feasibility does not imply one common feasible state or action.
Theorems: Joint feasibility theorem/counterexample

38. Coupling Passports

Role: joint-law dependence structure

When stochastic marginals must coexist in one joint realization, coupling information is Passport information.

\[ \mu,\nu \quad\leadsto\quad \boxed{ \gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu). } \]

Carries

  • Whether one shared coupling is required.
  • Whether different Tasks may use different couplings.
  • Whether a shared witness or strategy must be reused.
  • Which joint constraints the coupling must satisfy.
Limits
  • Marginals alone do not determine the joint law.
  • Existence of some coupling does not establish existence of a coupling satisfying additional joint constraints.

Examples: Coupling example

39. Law Passports

Role: active-law / Regime information

The active law or Regime can itself be information that must survive a representation or handoff.

\[ \boxed{ (x,\lambda)\in X\times\Lambda, \qquad \lambda=\text{active law / Regime index}. } \]
Limits
  • Equal state coordinates under different laws need not imply equal continuation.
  • Law indices are useful only when the law family is actually defined.

40. Conservative Passports

Role: candidate invariant-preservation Passport
Status: active candidate BM structure

A Conservative Passport records native quantities that independent mathematics requires to persist even when the active representation does not carry all source information explicitly.

\[ \boxed{ C_{\rm out}(F(x)) = C_{\rm in}(x). } \]

Carries

  • Energy, momentum, charge, normalization, algebraic invariants, topological invariants, or other independently established conserved information when relevant.
Limits
  • The Passport does not create a conservation law.
  • What counts as conserved is determined by the native mathematics.

43. Exact Root Passport Transfer

Role: exact deterministic transfer factorization

Suppose \(P_S\) is the source Passport, \(F\) produces a Root-compatible result, \(P_R\) is the Root Passport, and \(\tau\) translates source-Passport information into Root-Passport information. Exact transfer requires

\[ \boxed{ P_R\circ F = \tau\circ P_S. } \]
Limits
  • The transfer map \(\tau\) must exist on the realized source Passport image and must have the correct native semantics.
Theorems: Interface factorization

44. Passport Promotion

Role: local-to-persistent obligation

Information becomes a persistent Passport component when later mathematics demonstrably depends on that distinction or law context.

\[ \boxed{ \text{local information} \longrightarrow \text{proved downstream dependence} \longrightarrow \text{Passport obligation}. } \]
Limits
  • Promotion is justified by mathematical dependence, not by terminology or frequency of use.

45. Passport Repair

Role: exact refinement repair

When Passport \(P\) is insufficient for a Task, an additional Passport \(Q\) repairs the representation when their joint information makes the Task constant on every refined fibre.

\[ \boxed{ \sim_{P\wedge Q} \subseteq \sim_T. } \]
What it allows
  • Restore exact Task sufficiency by adding only information that is actually required.
  • Localize what missing distinction must be restored.
Limits
  • A repair can increase cost, reduce privacy, alter stability, or introduce new Interface requirements.

47. Serial Passport-Defect Localization

Role: dependency-aware failure theorem

In a two-stage deterministic chain, a failed final Passport must have entered either as an earlier Interface defect or as a later defect pulled back through the upstream map.

\[ D_f = \{a:P_A(a)\land\neg P_B(f(a))\}, \] \[ D_g = \{b:P_B(b)\land\neg P_C(g(b))\}, \] \[ \boxed{ D_{g\circ f} \subseteq D_f\cup f^{-1}(D_g). } \]
What it allows
  • Identify where to repair a serial chain.
  • Recompute downstream Leaves only after the earliest invalid obligation is corrected.
Limits
  • The inclusion can be strict.
  • Non-Boolean Passports need a sector-correct defect notion.
Theorems: Defect localization theorem

Examples: Full-system repair

48. Future-Task Passport

Role: time-indexed Task requirement

A Passport can be judged against a future Task obtained after full evolution.

\[ \Phi_t:X\to X, \qquad T_t=T\circ\Phi_t, \] \[ \boxed{ \sim_P\subseteq\sim_{T\circ\Phi_t}. } \]
Limits
  • A distinction irrelevant now may become relevant later.

49. Passport Continuation Factorization

The current Passport supports the future Task at time \(t\) exactly when that future Task factors through the present Passport representation.

\[ \boxed{ T\circ\Phi_t = \widetilde T_t\circ B_P. } \]
Limits
  • One-time continuation does not imply all-time Dynamic Closure.

50. Passport Dynamic Closure

Role: exact reduced-state evolution

Dynamic Closure asks whether the Passport state itself has a well-defined exact future under the full evolution.

\[ B_P:X\to Q, \qquad \Phi:X\to X, \] \[ \boxed{ B_P\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi_P\circ B_P. } \] Equivalently, \[ B_P(x)=B_P(x') \Longrightarrow \boxed{ B_P(\Phi(x)) = B_P(\Phi(x')). } \]
Limits
  • Current Task sufficiency does not imply Dynamic Closure.
  • Closure does not automatically imply stability or numerical accuracy.
Definitions: Dynamic closure

51. Memory and Hidden-State Failure

Role: dynamic Passport failure

Dynamic Closure fails when states with the same present Passport have different future Passport states.

\[ B_P(x)=B_P(x') \quad\text{but}\quad \boxed{ B_P(\Phi(x)) \neq B_P(\Phi(x')). } \]

Carries

  • Possible hidden causes include omitted memory, Regime, boundary data, control history, extended-domain state, unresolved stochastic dependence, or other latent variables.
Examples: Dynamic closure failure

52. Deterministic Passport Composition

Role: serial deterministic processing

Further deterministic Passport processing cannot reconstruct a distinction already erased by an upstream deterministic representation.

\[ P_1:X\to Y, \qquad P_2:Y\to Z, \] \[ P_1(x)=P_1(x') \Longrightarrow \boxed{ P_2(P_1(x)) = P_2(P_1(x')). } \]

Role: partial-map composition

Passport processing remains subject to ordinary domain restrictions.

\[ P_1:D_1\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad P_2:D_2\subseteq Y\to Z, \] \[ \boxed{ D_{P_2\circ P_1} = \{x\in D_1:P_1(x)\in D_2\}. } \]

54. Passport Re-entry

Role: receiving-domain audit

Exported Passport information may re-enter another structure only through the receiver's actual admissible domain and law/Interface requirements.

65. What a Passport Does Not Do

Role: scope limits

The Passport role is powerful precisely because its limits remain explicit.

Limits
  • A Passport does not override native mathematics.
  • A Passport does not make an invalid quotient valid.
  • A Passport does not create a conservation law.
  • A Passport does not make incompatible Interfaces compatible.
  • A Passport does not make an inadmissible state admissible.
  • A Passport does not turn separate witnesses into one common witness.
  • A Passport does not exchange existential and universal quantifiers.
  • A Passport does not determine a joint coupling from marginals alone.
  • A Passport does not turn stochastic output laws into deterministic point values.
  • A Passport does not make static Task sufficiency equivalent to Dynamic Closure.
  • A Passport does not make successful export equivalent to lawful re-entry.
  • A Passport does not prove external novelty or physical realization.

67. Central Passport Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{retain exactly the information the later mathematics needs;}\\ \text{state the semantic sector explicitly;}\\ \text{keep witness, quantifier, coupling, law, and Interface scope visible;}\\ \text{separate static sufficiency from dynamic closure;}\\ \text{repair the earliest failed obligation before reusing downstream results.} \end{gathered} } \]

A good Passport is neither maximal information retention nor aggressive compression by default. It is a mathematically justified statement of which distinctions must survive for the declared downstream use.
