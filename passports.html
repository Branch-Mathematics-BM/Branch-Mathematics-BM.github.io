Definition

Passport

Active core structure

A Passport is a formal specification of the distinctions, observations, constraints, compatibility requirements, admissibility conditions, or information required for lawful mathematical passage across an interface.

Purpose

Distinction Preservation

A Passport records which distinctions must remain available when mathematical information is transformed, reduced, exported, composed, or re-entered.

Admissibility

A Passport may specify conditions that must hold before an object, operation, Branch, Root, or response may participate in a given mathematical structure.

Compatibility

A Passport may determine whether two mathematical structures are compatible enough to interact or compose lawfully.

Sufficiency

In response problems, a Passport may record whether the available information preserves enough distinctions to support the required task or continuation.

Passport Information

Depending on the mathematical setting, a Passport may contain or refer to:

Observations and observable quantities
Preserved distinctions
Equivalence or quotient conditions
Input and output types
Constraints and admissibility rules
Interface compatibility requirements
Conserved mathematical quantities
Task-specific sufficiency requirements

Passport Semantics

Passport semantics must match the mathematical category in which they operate.

Deterministic

Deterministic Passports may track exact mappings and distinctions.

Stochastic

Stochastic Passports must preserve probability-law semantics rather than being silently reduced to deterministic equivalence.

Nondeterministic or Game-Based

Passports involving multiple possible outcomes must state the relevant existential, universal, path, witness, or strategy quantifiers explicitly.

Approximate

Approximate Passports must identify the metric, divergence, tolerance, or other mathematical notion defining approximation.

Joint Compatibility

Passing several Passport conditions separately does not automatically establish that they can all be satisfied simultaneously.

Joint feasibility requires either a common compatible witness, a nonempty joint feasible set, or an explicitly stated coupling law.

Conservative Passports

Some mathematical reductions remove information from an active representation while still requiring certain quantities to persist.

A conservative Passport may therefore carry quantities such as energy, momentum, charge, invariants, or other conserved structure through a compatible transformation even when those quantities are not represented directly in the reduced object.

Status: Active research structure; exact implementation depends on the mathematical domain.

Passport Registry

Formal Passports may receive stable identifiers in the BM Passport registry.

The registry is intended to record Passport type, domain, required distinctions, interface conditions, admissibility rules, dependencies, and revision status.

Research Status

Passport theory is an active part of Branch Mathematics and GURF. Individual Passport constructions must be proved or validated according to the mathematics of the structures in which they operate.

A Passport does not override the laws of its mathematical domain. Its role is to state and preserve the conditions required for lawful representation, interaction, transformation, or response.
