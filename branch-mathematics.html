Branch Mathematics

Branch Mathematics is a mathematical metaframework for carrying one problem through distinct mathematical Branches while preserving their native mathematics and governing representation, interaction, realization, comparison, export, and reuse.

This page follows the corrected foundations of the 2026-09-10 mathematical edition. Definition, proof, computation, physical interpretation, and historical novelty are treated as different claim types. SSH is retired and supplies no assumption here.

1 · Canonical identity

One problem, multiple native mathematical focuses

BM does not require distinct Branches to be unrelated subproblems, and it does not require them to be the same kind of mathematical object. The point is to let one problem be carried through several lawful mathematical focuses without erasing the native structure that makes each focus mathematically valid.

\[ \boxed{ \text{problem specification} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{typed Branches} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{lawful operations and Interactions} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{realization, response, export, reuse} } \]
No unity assumption. BM does not declare that distinct mathematical descriptions are secretly one object. Any unification has to be established by the native mathematics and the declared interface conditions. If it is not established, the distinctions remain.

2 · Scope before structure

Problem specification

A BM problem begins by fixing the scope, the native mathematical sectors that may participate, terminal obligations, allowable interventions, and the rules under which outputs can be compared. These declarations determine which distinctions matter and which theorem hypotheses are relevant.

Scope

States what mathematical problem is being carried, what counts as inside the problem, and what receiver or terminal obligations must eventually be met.

Native sector

Supplies the actual mathematics: domains, topology, smoothness, measurability, units, algebra, positivity, probability kernels, operator structure, or other required data.

Intervention

An allowed change must be stated in the native sector. BM does not turn an unspecified modification into a lawful operation merely by naming it.

Comparison

Outputs are compared only in a declared common comparison space, on a common domain, using a specified comparison relation or metric.

3 · Typed Branches

Branch

Canonical record

\[ B=(\mathsf S_B,\tau_B,X_B,A_B,\mathcal L_B,\mathcal O_B,\mathcal P_B). \]

\(\mathsf S_B\) is the native sector, \(\tau_B\) the type, \(X_B\) the carrier, \(A_B\) its admissible objects, \(\mathcal L_B\) its declared laws, \(\mathcal O_B\) its operations, and \(\mathcal P_B\) its contracts in context.

A Branch may contain a relation, state space, operator system, complete subsystem, or another mathematically specified focus. The Branch record imposes no universal topology and no universal notion of “information amount.” Those properties come from the native sector when they exist.

Role–carrier firewall. Calling something a Branch does not give it continuity, conservation, linearity, a metric, an energy, an entropy, a probability law, or any other undeclared property.

4 · Lawful action

Operations and Interactions

Operation

An Operation is a specified native action. In a deterministic set sector it is a partial map

\[ f:D_f\subseteq X\longrightarrow Y. \]

Other sectors retain their native objects: relations remain relations, Markov kernels remain kernels, differential operators remain differential operators, and quantum channels remain quantum channels.

Interaction

An Interaction combines declared inputs. In a deterministic set sector:

\[ G:D_G\subseteq A_1\times\cdots\times A_m\longrightarrow W. \]

Individual input admissibility does not establish that an input tuple lies in \(D_G\). Positive proposed side lengths, for example, can each be individually admissible while a triple fails the triangle inequality.

Interaction non-inheritance. An Interaction output inherits topology, algebra, conservation properties, units, or other structure only through an explicit construction or proof. Input structure is not automatically transferred to the output.

Related proof: T08 Structured quotient criterion and T09 Typed composite domain.

5 · Derived mathematics

Leaf

A Leaf is a derived result carried with its native statement, hypotheses, actual derivation, and provenance. A Leaf can be reused only when the hypotheses needed by that result remain true at the new use.

Computed value is not automatically a theorem. A numerical output without a validated domain, model, and error interpretation is a computation, not a universal mathematical statement.

6 · Receiver requirements

Contracts and Passports

Contract and instantiation

\[ \mathcal C^\rho=(\Sigma^\rho,\Phi^\rho), \qquad \mathcal P^\rho_{\theta,c} =\operatorname{Inst}_{\theta,c}(\mathcal C^\rho). \]

A contract is a typed specification. A Passport is that specification instantiated at parameters and context.

In a predicate sector, write a Passport as \((D_{\mathcal P},A_{\mathcal P})\) with \(A_{\mathcal P}\subseteq D_{\mathcal P}\). This produces three different outcomes:

Not applicable

The candidate lies outside \(D_{\mathcal P}\).

Applicable but rejected

The candidate lies in \(D_{\mathcal P}\) but not in \(A_{\mathcal P}\).

Accepted

The candidate lies in \(A_{\mathcal P}\) and satisfies every declared acceptance obligation.

Multiple obligations

Passing one receiver task does not imply that every other receiver condition has been passed.

On a common applicability domain, \(A_2\subseteq A_1\) means Passport 2 is at least as restrictive as Passport 1. If the applicability domains differ, both domain inclusion and acceptance inclusion must be recorded.

Passport pullback

For a total export \(E:X\to Y\),

\[ E^*(D,A)=\bigl(E^{-1}D,E^{-1}A\bigr). \]

For a partial export, the construction must first be restricted to the actual export domain. A general Passport is not automatically a deterministic observation function.

7 · Realization

Configuration and Tree

Configuration

\[ C=(\Sigma_C,\Gamma_C,c). \]

The signature \(\Sigma_C\) declares typed objects and coordination data, \(\Gamma_C\) declares obligations, and \(c\) declares context.

Realization family

\[ \mathfrak F(C;c) = \{\xi\in\operatorname{Int}(\Sigma_C;c): \xi\models_c\Gamma_C\}. \]

Interpretation and satisfaction remain native-sector relative. Merely writing this set does not prove that it is nonempty.

ConditionMeaning
Well-formedness The expressions are meaningful and correctly typed.
Global realizability \(\mathfrak F(C;c)\neq\varnothing\).
Local satisfiability Only the stated local obligations have witnesses; this does not by itself imply a global witness.

Realized Tree

\[ \mathcal T=(C,\xi,\Pi,\omega,\Lambda). \]

Here \(\xi\) is a realization, \(\Pi\) the certified provenance/dependency record, \(\omega\) the declared order or history context, and \(\Lambda\) the active law assignments.

A dependency structure may contain local trees inside a DAG or hypergraph. Naming the structure a Tree does not turn cycles into well-founded recursion.

Related proofs: T17 Local realizability does not imply global realizability, T18 Finite deterministic DAG evaluation, and T44 Join-tree hypergraph gluing.

8 · Receiver-relative export

Response, export, Root, and re-entry

Response

A Response is a declared Tree-level output. It may be value-valued, set-valued, relation-valued, law-valued, or another type supplied by the native sector.

Export

An export translates a Response to the receiver’s declared type. Translation is itself a mathematical map or relation whose domain and obligations must be specified.

Root

A Root is an export accepted under a specified receiver Passport and carrying the information and evidence required by that contract.

\[ \text{Tree} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \text{Export} \xrightarrow[\text{receiver Passport}]{\text{acceptance}} \text{Root}. \]
Root status is receiver-relative. Acceptance by one receiver does not imply acceptance by another receiver, and it does not automatically persist under a later context or translation.

Re-entry

Re-entry is a new mathematical use. A previously accepted Root must satisfy the obligations of the receiving Configuration; old acceptance does not bypass new domain or contract checks.

Candidate Rooting, Rebranching, and Seed constructions remain separately scoped research structures. None is required for the proved foundations on this page.

Related proofs: T21 Root receiver relativity, T22 Root acceptance is not automatically transitive, and T43 Certified Root transfer.

9 · Typed closure

Three different closure universes

BM uses several mathematically different notions of closure. They must not be conflated.

Component dependency closure

\[ \operatorname{Cl}_C:2^{V_C}\to2^{V_C}. \]

Its carrier is the set \(V_C\) of configuration components.

Obligation prerequisite closure

\[ \operatorname{ReqCl}_C:2^{\Omega_C}\to2^{\Omega_C}, \qquad \mathscr Q_P=\operatorname{ReqCl}_C(\mathscr T_P). \]

Its carrier is the obligation universe \(\Omega_C\).

Equivalence refinement

Interface synthesis refines equivalence relations on state carriers. That refinement process is neither component closure nor obligation closure.

Before BM invokes a least closure, the operator being used must actually be extensive, monotone, and idempotent on its declared universe.

10 · Necessity relative to a comparison class

Branch essentiality

A Branch is essential only relative to a fixed, independently specified class of admissible coarsenings or removals. Every candidate that removes the Branch’s distinctions must lose at least one required obligation.

\[ \operatorname{Essential}_{\mathscr Q}(B) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every allowed candidate removing }B \text{ fails some }Q\in\mathscr Q. \]
The comparison class cannot be defined circularly by already requiring preservation of the very obligations used to prove essentiality.

Essentiality need not be absolute. Likewise, raw Branch count is not an invariant of a mathematical object unless an equivalence notion and an irreducibility theorem have first been supplied.

Related proofs: T13 Branch essentiality monotonicity and T15 Branch count need not be invariant.

11 · What a representation must preserve

Representation and admissibility

For functions \(R:X\to Z\) and \(T:X\to W\), exact task sufficiency means that the task factors through the representation:

\[ T=\bar T\,R \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \ker R\subseteq\ker T. \]

Equivalently, whenever \(R(x)=R(x')\), the task must also satisfy \(T(x)=T(x')\). This is the basic fibre test.

Exact set-level sufficiency

Guarantees only that a decoder exists on the realized image of \(R\).

Not automatically inherited

Continuity, computability, numerical stability, positivity, smoothness, causal implementability, or physical realizability need their own hypotheses.

Strict embedding

Preserves whatever embedding properties the native category requires.

Task-sufficient representation

Can be noninjective whenever the receiver tasks do not need the distinctions that are merged.

Structured quotients

A structured quotient must be a congruence for every declared operation. For partial operations, this includes saturation of the operation domain: equivalent inputs must agree on whether the operation is defined, and equivalent defined inputs must produce equivalent outputs.

Law transport must be proved. To transport a law through a representation, name the law map and prove the relevant satisfaction condition. A quotient symbol alone does not transport native mathematics.

Related proofs: T01 Exact fibre factorization, T04 Canonical exact task quotient, T08 Structured quotient criterion, and T36 Sector non-portability.

12 · Continuation through a representation

Exact Dynamic Closure

Present task sufficiency does not guarantee that the retained representation supports future evolution. For a partial evolution \(\Phi_t:D_t\to X\), exact reduced evolution through \(R:X\to Z\) requires both domain saturation and value compatibility:

\[ D_t=R^{-1}(\bar D_t), \qquad R\Phi_t=\bar\Phi_tR \quad\text{on }D_t. \]

Domain saturation means states represented as the same reduced state cannot disagree on whether the requested evolution is available. Value compatibility means defined evolutions from the same reduced state must return to the same reduced successor state.

The theorem assumes \(D_t\) and \(\Phi_t\) are already specified. It does not construct a PDE solution, extend a solution through a singular endpoint, or diagnose the physical reason an evolution became undefined. An availability tag records undefinedness only.

Related proofs: T10 Future task factorization, T11 Exact Dynamic Closure, and T12 Static sufficiency does not imply closure.

13 · Compatibility is typed

Coherence, paths, and global gluing

Coherence compares outputs only when they inhabit a common declared comparison space and are defined on a common domain. If the comparison relation is transitive, pointwise path coherence is transitive.

\[ p(x)\sim q(x),\quad q(x)\sim r(x) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p(x)\sim r(x) \] on the declared common domain.

This must not be confused with global realizability. Local or pairwise compatibility can hold while a cycle of constraints has no simultaneous global solution.

Path coherence

Pointwise comparison of defined path outputs in one comparison space.

Local satisfiability

Each selected local group of constraints has a witness.

Global realizability

One interpretation satisfies all declared obligations simultaneously.

Join-tree gluing

Acyclic running-intersection structure plus compatible separator projections gives a global join.

Related proofs: T24 Path coherence transitivity, T25 Cyclic separator consistency can fail globally, and T44 Join-tree hypergraph gluing.

14 · Information discipline

Distinctions and physical interpretation

In the exact deterministic sector, once a representation identifies two states, later deterministic processing of that representation alone cannot recover a task that separates them.

\[ R(x)=R(x') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S(R(x))=S(R(x')) \] for every subsequent deterministic map \(S\) defined on the retained representation.

Repair can add another lawful observation, retained history, side state, or independently specified source. It cannot invent a distinction that is absent from every supplied input.

Precise persistence claim. Injectivity of a declared total evolution is a mathematically precise persistence condition. Universal physical information conservation is a separate modeling commitment and requires additional physical assumptions.

Stochastic and quantum semantics

BM need not take probability as a primitive of its core. When a stochastic model is imported, however, its probability kernels, couplings, expectations, and total-variation distances retain their ordinary mathematical meanings. A reachable relation does not determine a probability law.

Likewise, density-matrix algebra, observable statistics, and physically admissible quantum channels are different mathematical levels and must not be silently identified.

Related proofs: T07 Deterministic serial information loss, T29 Finite Markov closure, and T53 Mixed deterministic and stochastic continuation.

15 · Failure must name the failed obligation

Defect and repair

A defect records failure of a named obligation \(\Psi\). BM does not assume one universal scalar whose magnitude measures every kind of mathematical failure.

\[ \operatorname{Def}_{\Psi} = \{\text{declared objects or states for which }\Psi\text{ fails}\}. \]

A repair is a declared admissible relation between Configurations. The changed support must be explicit, and obligations whose validity can depend on the change must be revalidated.

Defect Calculus status. Defect Calculus remains an active specialized BM program. The current foundations do not assume a universal defect algebra or universal scalar defect measure.

Related proofs: T27 Exact repair refinement and T28 Repair closure and revalidation.

16 · Why use BM

Where BM adds mathematical work

BM is useful when one problem has several representations, interfaces, admissibility requirements, response tasks, or continuation laws whose compatibility has to be established rather than assumed.

Representation loss

Determine which receiver-relevant distinctions a proposed representation merges.

Continuation

Determine whether a representation that works now remains sufficient under declared evolution.

Interaction descent

Determine whether independently reduced Branches can still participate in a declared Interaction.

Receiver acceptance

Keep task success separate from applicability, other obligations, and receiver-specific acceptance.

Global realization

Keep local compatibility separate from existence of one globally satisfying interpretation.

Repair certification

Identify the lawful new distinction or source that repairs a failure and revalidate affected obligations.

When BM should stay silent. If the native mathematics already answers the question and BM adds no distinction, calculation, interface condition, or certificate, use the native result directly. Adding role names alone does not add mathematical content.
