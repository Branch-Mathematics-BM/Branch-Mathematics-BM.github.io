1. What Branch Mathematics Is

Branch Mathematics (BM) is a mathematical metaframework for carrying one mathematical problem through multiple distinct lawful mathematical Branches while preserving the native mathematics of each Branch.

BM governs the mathematical relations required when different mathematical sectors participate in the same problem: typing, admissibility, Operations, Interactions, Interfaces, constraints, realization, coherence, comparison, export, re-entry, provenance, law/regime context, information distinctions, and repair.

\[ \boxed{ \text{one problem} \longrightarrow \text{multiple lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful interaction and combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{mathematical consequences} } \]

The descriptive phrase meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation describes the level at which BM works. It does not replace the primary definition above.

Limits
  • BM does not make false native mathematics true.
  • BM does not require every problem to use every BM structure.
  • BM does not claim that every mathematical engine used inside BM is historically new.

2. BM Is Plural, Not Single-Track

BM is not one mandatory pipeline. Several mathematical directions may be simultaneously valid and may interact when a lawful Interface exists.

Branch / Interaction
Distinct mathematical focuses and their joint participation.
Operation
Typed transformation, composition, order, feedback, and execution.
Passport / Contract
Typed obligations required for lawful participation.
Representation
Fibres, abstraction, recovery, quotient, and reconstruction.
Realization
Local/global compatibility, Networks, Trees, and obstruction.
Law / Regime
Problems where active law is mathematically relevant.
Defect / Repair
Failure localization and admissible correction.
Export / Re-entry
Receiver-relative reuse of mathematical results.
Refinement / Order
Comparing lawful decompositions or representations.
Scope rule

A specialized direction does not replace another valid direction merely because it is easier to formalize. Separate them when their assumptions differ; connect them when the mathematics supplies a lawful connection.

3. Native Mathematics Integrity

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM does not silently alter or flatten native Branch mathematics.} } \]

A PDE remains governed by PDE analysis. A stochastic Branch retains its kernel semantics. A manifold retains its geometric structure. A quotient retains only the structure that actually descends. A quantum channel is not silently replaced by an ordinary function.

BM may explicitly transform, discretize, approximate, quotient, transport, or re-express a Branch, but that transformation must itself be mathematically defined.

4. Role–Carrier Separation

BM distinguishes a native mathematical object from the BM role it occupies.

\[ \boxed{x\models_C\rho} \]

means that mathematical object \(x\) occupies BM role \(\rho\) in Configuration \(C\).

The same object may be a Leaf, a Response, and a Root for one receiver without becoming three different mathematical objects.

\[ z\models_C \mathrm{Leaf} \land \mathrm{Response} \land \mathrm{Root}_{B_r} \]
Firewall
  • A role name confers no undeclared mathematical property.
  • Root does not imply recurrence.
  • Leaf does not imply terminality.
  • Tree does not imply graph-theoretic tree structure.

5. Problem Scope and Obligation Family

BM claims are relative to the mathematical problem actually being claimed. Let

\[ \boxed{\mathfrak S_P} \]

denote the declared mathematical scope of problem \(P\), and let \(\mathscr T_P\) denote its terminal obligations.

The active obligation family is the dependency closure of those terminal requirements:

\[ \boxed{ \mathscr Q_P = \operatorname{ReqCl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(\mathscr T_P). } \]

The obligations may be functions, predicates, relations, PDE conditions, stochastic laws, convergence requirements, witness conditions, comparison requirements, receiver conditions, or other sector-correct mathematical statements.

No scope gaming.

BM does not permit a representation to be declared sufficient by first deleting every obligation that the representation cannot support. Problem scope must be declared from the mathematical claim, not reverse-engineered solely to validate a favored representation.

6. Branch

A Branch is a mathematically distinct focus participating in the BM problem.

\[ \boxed{ B= \left( \mathsf S_B, \tau_B, X_B, A_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, \mathcal P_B \right). } \]
Field Meaning
\(\mathsf S_B\) Native mathematical sector or structural kind.
\(\tau_B\) Required type information.
\(X_B\) Ambient carrier, state space, or sector-correct analogue.
\(A_B\) Admissible region or admissibility structure.
\(\mathcal L_B\) Native laws, identities, constraints, or invariants.
\(\mathcal O_B\) Native Operations.
\(\mathcal P_B\) Passport / Interface obligations associated with the Branch role.
Limits
  • The seven-field record is an organizational specification, not a universal ontology.
  • Branches need not be disjoint or independent.
  • Different lawful Branch decompositions of the same problem may coexist.

7. When a Branch Earns Separate Status

A mathematical object does not become a Branch merely because it can be isolated and named.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Branch separation earns its place when collapsing it changes required mathematics.} } \]

A proposed Branch distinction is essential relative to \((P,\mathfrak S_P)\) when removing it destroys at least one required type, law, Operation, Interaction, Interface, Passport, provenance requirement, continuation, obstruction, or receiver obligation.

In deterministic factorization form, a proposed coarsening \(q\) fails when some required obligation \(T\) cannot descend:

\[ \boxed{ \nexists\widetilde T \quad\text{such that}\quad T=\widetilde T\circ q\circ R_{\mathcal B}. } \]
No Branch explosion

\(x\), \(x^2\), \(x^3\), and \(\sin x\) do not automatically become four Branches. If every required downstream use factors through the original \(x\)-representation, the extra distinctions are derived quantities or Leaves rather than essential Branches.

8. Operation

An Operation is the general BM role of a typed lawful mathematical transformation or relation.

\[ \boxed{ O:D_O\subseteq X\to Y } \]

is the deterministic form. Other sectors may use relations, kernels, channels, differential operators, optimization operators, rewrite systems, or other native objects.

Serial composition uses the actual domain:

\[ F:D_F\subseteq X\to Y, \qquad G:D_G\subseteq Y\to Z, \] \[ \boxed{ D_{G\circ F} = \{x\in D_F:F(x)\in D_G\}. } \]

9. Interaction

An Interaction is not a second species of map. It is an Operation or sector-correct mathematical relation carrying a declared joint-participation role.

\[ \boxed{ I: D_I \subseteq A_{B_1}\times\cdots\times A_{B_n} \to Z_I } \]

for the deterministic multi-input case.

Product notation does not imply independent inputs. Shared-state identities, couplings, common witnesses, law compatibility, or other constraints belong in the true domain \(D_I\), the relevant Interface, or a Passport.

10. Interaction Non-Inheritance

\[ \boxed{ \text{input structure} \not\Rightarrow \text{output structure} } \]

Topology, metric, order, algebraic structure, symmetry, conservation, gauge structure, probability semantics, or any other native structure transfers only through an explicit induced structure, preservation theorem, quotient, transport law, restriction, completion, or other lawful construction.

11. Leaf

A Leaf is an identified derived result retained for later mathematical use.

\[ z=I(x_1,\ldots,x_n), \qquad \boxed{L=\ell(z)}. \]

The extraction map \(\ell\) may be the identity. A Leaf is a BM provenance/participation role; its underlying mathematics comes from the derivation that produced it.

Limits
  • Not every intermediate symbol is a Leaf.
  • A Leaf is not automatically a Branch.
  • A Leaf is not automatically a Root.

12. Provenance and State History

BM distinguishes:

\[ \boxed{\Pi^{\mathrm{der}}=\text{derivational provenance}} \qquad \boxed{H=\text{state/history context}}. \]

Derivational provenance records how a result was obtained or certified. State/history information records path-dependent data that future mathematics may actually require.

BM retains exactly the provenance/context required for validity, continuation, certification, or receiver use. It does not require every result to carry a full copy of its entire history.

13. Contract and Passport

A Contract is a reusable typed schema of obligations. A Passport is the instantiated obligation family operative in a particular BM situation.

\[ \mathcal C^\rho=(\Sigma^\rho,\Phi^\rho), \qquad \boxed{ \mathcal P^\rho_{\theta,c} = \operatorname{Inst}_{\theta,c}(\mathcal C^\rho). } \]

A problem that needs no reusable schema may use a Passport directly and omit a separate Contract object.

Passport conditions may include admissibility, structural requirements, law/regime compatibility, coupling, shared-witness scope, continuation, reconstruction, approximation bounds, conservative obligations, or receiver requirements.

14. Passport Applicability

A Passport is not a Boolean predicate on every imaginable object. It has an applicability domain \(D_{\mathcal P}\).

\[ \operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P) \subseteq D_{\mathcal P}. \]
Case Meaning
\(x\notin D_{\mathcal P}\) Passport not applicable: type/domain failure.
\(x\in D_{\mathcal P}\setminus\operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P)\) Passport applicable but rejected.
\(x\in\operatorname{Acc}(\mathcal P)\) Passport accepted.
\[ \boxed{ \text{not applicable} \neq \text{applicable but false}. } \]

15. Interface

An Interface is a mathematically specified mechanism through which distinct structures communicate, compare, constrain one another, transport representations, or satisfy common boundary conditions.

An Interface may be a map, relation, span, correspondence, projection, embedding, boundary map, comparison map, transport law, discretization, or another sector-correct connector.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Interface} \not\Rightarrow \text{automatic preservation}. } \]

Specialized Interface subclasses may require additional preservation laws. For example, a Passport-transporting Interface is a specialized class, not a replacement for the general Interface definition.

16. Configuration Status

A Configuration is the coordinated BM specification containing the active native mathematical objects, role assignments, Branches, Operations, Interactions, Interfaces, Passports, laws, dependencies, and provenance relevant to the declared problem.

Status Meaning
Candidate Proposed BM structure; no validity implied.
Well formed Typing, applicability, and declared semantic syntax are coherent.
Locally admissible Every declared local region has at least one lawful realization.
Globally realizable The complete realization family is nonempty.
Realized Tree A particular lawful realization has been selected and recorded.
\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{GR}(C;c) \iff \mathfrak F(C;c)\neq\varnothing. } \]

Global existence and selection are different. A globally realizable Configuration may have many realizations; choosing one requires its own mathematics when the downstream result depends on that choice.

17. Semantic Dependency and Network

A Network is a representation of the problem's semantic dependency/incidence structure.

\[ \boxed{\mathfrak D_C} \]

may be represented by a graph, DAG, hypergraph, factor graph, category, constraint incidence structure, operator network, or another suitable native object.

BM does not assume that an ordinary directed graph captures every dependency.

For a modified region \(S\), the affected closure is

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(S) } \]

rather than merely the naive list of graph descendants.

18. Realization

The global realization family contains all lawful realizations satisfying the complete declared mathematics of the Configuration:

\[ \boxed{\mathfrak F(C;c)}. \]

Applicable requirements may include Branch admissibility, Operation and Interaction domains, Interfaces, Passports, law/regime assignments, shared-witness conditions, coherence, global constraints, and feedback closure.

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F(C;c)\neq\varnothing } \]

is semantic realizability. It is separate from whether realizability has already been certified.

19. Tree

A Tree is a Configuration together with a particular lawful realization and the context required to interpret it.

\[ \boxed{ T= (C,\xi,\Pi,\omega,\Lambda), \qquad \xi\in\mathfrak F(C;c). } \]

Here \(\Pi\) records required provenance, \(\omega\) required order or dependency information, and \(\Lambda\) relevant law/regime context.

Limits
  • A BM Tree need not be a graph-theoretic tree.
  • A diagram without a lawful realization is not yet a realized Tree.
  • Tree is a BM structural role, not a new native species of mathematical object.

20. Response

A Response is the typed output of a declared BM computation under its native semantics.

\[ \boxed{ T\xrightarrow{\mathcal E}Y. } \]

A Response may be single-valued, set-valued, relational, operator-valued, law-valued, stochastic, geometric, or otherwise sector-correct.

A Response is not automatically a Root.

21. Export

Export prepares a Response for a specific downstream receiver.

\[ \boxed{ E_r: D_{E_r}\subseteq\mathcal Y_T \to \mathcal R_r, \qquad r=E_r(Y). } \]

A deterministic receiver obligation \(T_r\) is exactly preserved when

\[ \boxed{ T_r=\widetilde T_r\circ E_r. } \]

Sector-correct alternatives are required for stochastic, relational, approximate, quantum, geometric, or other semantics.

22. Root

A Root is a receiver-admissible exported result.

\[ \boxed{ r\text{ is a Root for }B_r \iff r\in \operatorname{Acc} (\mathcal P_R^{B_r}). } \]

The Root Passport is evaluated only after the export lies in its applicability domain.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root for }B_1 \not\Rightarrow \text{Root for }B_2. } \]

Roothood is receiver-relative and is not automatically transitive through later receivers.

23. Re-entry and Recursive Participation

Re-entry is the general BM mechanism by which an accepted export becomes lawful input to later mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \to \text{Response} \to \text{Export} \to \text{Root} \to \text{receiver} \to \text{updated Configuration / later mathematics}. } \]

Re-entry may require re-Passporting, revalidation, downstream recomputation, repair, or law/regime updates.

A Root entering later mathematics does not automatically create a new Branch; Branch status must separately satisfy the Branch-separation rule.

24. Candidate Rooting, Rebranching, and Seed

Structure Current status
Rooting Candidate specialized attachment operation requiring explicit attachment semantics.
Rebranching Candidate language for new Branch development after lawful attachment/re-entry.
Seed Candidate generative role requiring state space, update rule, admissibility, export condition, and export map.
\[ S_0 \xrightarrow{G} S_1 \xrightarrow{G} \cdots \xrightarrow{\text{export condition}} R \]
Candidate terminology does not become canonical mathematics merely through repeated use. Promotion requires explicit type, domain, semantics, counterexample testing, and mathematical utility.

25. Law Space and Regime Mathematics

Law Space is an active specialized BM direction used when the active law, regime, constitutive family, or rule context itself affects mathematical continuation or interpretation.

\[ \lambda\in\mathscr L, \qquad \mathcal L_\lambda=\text{active law at }\lambda. \]

A law assignment has an explicit domain:

\[ \boxed{ \Lambda: \Omega_{\mathscr L} \rightrightarrows \mathscr L. } \]

If \(|\Lambda(C)|>1\), BM does not silently select one law. The Response must remain set-valued, relational, law-indexed, or unresolved unless an independent lawful selector is defined.

Scope

Law Space is not a mandatory destination of every BM problem. A fixed law may remain an ordinary parameter when separating it performs no mathematical work.

26. Representation, Fibres, and Sufficiency

For deterministic representation

\[ F:X\to Y, \]

the fibre over \(y\in F(X)\) is

\[ \boxed{ F^{-1}(y) = \{x\in X:F(x)=y\}. } \]

Nontrivial fibres identify distinctions removed by the representation. That is not automatically a failure.

A deterministic obligation \(T:X\to W\) is exactly recoverable from \(F\) precisely when

\[ \boxed{ T=\widetilde T\circ F } \]

on the realized image, equivalently when \(T\) is constant on every fibre.

Other sectors use their native notions: lumpability, relational descent, operator sufficiency, approximation bounds, and so on.

27. Structured Quotients

Set-level identification does not automatically preserve the native Operations of a Branch.

For a finitary operation \(\sigma\), an equivalence relation must satisfy the congruence condition

\[ x_i\sim y_i \quad\forall i \Longrightarrow \boxed{ \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

Other mathematical sectors require their own quotient/descent conditions.

28. Dynamic Closure

Static sufficiency and autonomous future evolution are different questions.

Let

\[ \Phi:A\to A, \qquad R:A\to Y, \qquad Y_R=R(A). \]

Exact Dynamic Closure holds iff there exists

\[ \boxed{ \widetilde\Phi:Y_R\to Y_R } \] such that \[ \boxed{ R\circ\Phi = \widetilde\Phi\circ R. } \]

No reduced dynamics outside the realized image \(Y_R\) is required.

29. Convergence and Approximation Firewalls

BM has no universal convergence relation.

\[ \boxed{ x_n\to x \text{ is meaningful only after the native convergence structure is declared.} } \]

Metric, norm, weak, weak-*, operator, order, probabilistic, or other convergence notions are not interchangeable.

Likewise,

\[ \boxed{x\approx y} \]

has no BM meaning by itself. Approximate comparison requires a declared comparison object, error functional or distance/divergence, and tolerance.

30. Coherence

BM coherence has three distinct parts.

30.1 Domain coherence

\[ x\in D_{P_1}\cap D_{P_2}. \]

30.2 Legitimate comparison

\[ q_1:Y_1\to Q, \qquad q_2:Y_2\to Q. \]

The comparison maps must arise from the declared mathematical task. They cannot be invented afterward solely to force agreement.

30.3 Agreement

\[ \boxed{ q_1P_1(x)=q_2P_2(x) } \]

or, when approximate coherence is properly defined,

\[ d_Q(q_1P_1(x),q_2P_2(x)) \le \varepsilon. \]
\[ \boxed{ \text{pairwise coherence} \not\Rightarrow \text{global coherence}. } \]

31. Coupling and Witness Scope

BM distinguishes independent, shared, and coupled witnesses.

Independent

\[ \exists w_1\,\varphi_1(w_1), \qquad \exists w_2\,\varphi_2(w_2). \]

Shared

\[ \exists w\, [ \varphi_1(w) \land \varphi_2(w) ]. \]

Coupled

\[ \boxed{ \exists w_1\exists w_2: R_1(x,w_1) \land K(w_1,w_2) \land R_2(x,w_2). } \]

In finite relational settings this is ordinary conjunctive-query / CSP mathematics. BM uses that mathematics rather than inventing a duplicate coupling theory.

32. Local-to-Global Realization

Local realizability is not sufficient in general:

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F(U_i)\neq\varnothing \ \forall i \not\Rightarrow \mathfrak F(C)\neq\varnothing. } \]

Finite tree-gluing theorem

Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite rooted tree. For every \(v\), let \(F_v\neq\varnothing\) be a local realization set, and for each oriented edge \(u\to v\), let

\[ C_{uv} \subseteq F_u\times F_v \]

be the complete compatibility relation across that edge.

If the global realization set factors exactly as

\[ \mathfrak F_H = \left\{ (x_v): x_v\in F_v,\; (x_u,x_v)\in C_{uv} \ \forall uv\in E \right\}, \]

and every parent state extends across every child edge,

\[ \boxed{ \forall(u\to v)\in E,\; \forall x_u\in F_u,\; \exists x_v\in F_v: (x_u,x_v)\in C_{uv}, } \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak F_H\neq\varnothing. } \]

This is ordinary tree-gluing / CSP-style mathematics. BM's obligation is to prove that the actual problem has been faithfully encoded into the local sets and compatibility relations before using the theorem.

33. Defect Calculus

BM retains Defect Calculus as domain-independent mathematics for representing and localizing failure of declared conditions.

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Def}_{\Psi} } \]

denotes a defect tied to actual mathematical requirement \(\Psi\). Its type may be a set, relation, scalar residual, norm, operator, rank defect, geometric object, cohomology class, or another sector-correct object.

Task-labelled fibre specialization

\[ F_C(c)=C^{-1}(c), \qquad \boxed{ \mathfrak D_T(C;c) = \left( F_C(c), T|_{F_C(c)} \right). } \]

Exact refinement:

\[ \boxed{ F_{(C,W)}(c,w) = F_C(c)\cap W^{-1}(w). } \]

Deterministic coarsening:

\[ \boxed{ F_{P\circ C}(d) = \bigcup_{c:P(c)=d} F_C(c). } \]

The Task-labelled sector is response-specialized and therefore links naturally into GURF.

34. Repair

Repair is not arbitrary modification.

\[ \boxed{ \mathscr R_{\mathrm{adm}} \subseteq \mathsf{Conf}\times\mathsf{Conf} } \]

declares the admissible repair relation. A proposed repaired Configuration \(C'\) is acceptable only when

\[ (C,C')\in\mathscr R_{\mathrm{adm}} \]

and the failed condition is restored without violating the preservation obligations of the repair class.

Repair invalidates affected downstream certification throughout

\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak D_C}(S) } \]

unless the affected results are recomputed or protected by an invariance theorem.

35. Information and Distinctions

BM treats information structurally through distinctions represented by the active mathematics. Different information types are not automatically interchangeable.

For a deterministic representation

\[ F:X\to Y, \]

noninjectivity is permitted:

\[ x_1\neq x_2, \qquad F(x_1)=F(x_2). \]

This means the distinction is absent from the \(Y\)-representation. It does not by itself prove destruction in the modeled total system.

If BM additionally asserts exact deterministic total-system persistence of information type \(\mathcal I\), an extended lawful carrier may be required:

\[ \widehat F:X\to Y\times H, \] \[ \boxed{ \widehat F(x_1)=\widehat F(x_2) \Longrightarrow x_1\sim_{\mathcal I}x_2. } \]

This exact injectivity-style form is scoped to the deterministic exact sector. Other sectors require native preservation semantics.

36. Semantic-Sector Firewall

\[ \boxed{ \text{function} \neq \text{Markov kernel} \neq \text{relation} \neq \text{quantum channel}. } \]

BM coordinates heterogeneous mathematics without silently flattening every Branch into one common Set-valued semantics.

Stochastic example

For Markov kernel \(K\), use the expectation operator

\[ \boxed{ (K\varphi)(x) = \int_Y \varphi(y)\,K(x,dy). } \]

This notation does not presuppose an adjoint structure.

37. Computability Firewall

\[ \boxed{ \text{existence} \neq \text{decidability} \neq \text{effective construction} \neq \text{efficient construction}. } \]

A realization may exist without a known algorithm. An algorithm may exist without being tractable. Complexity claims require an explicit model of computation, input encoding, and resource measure.

38. Refinement and Order

Refinement is defined only after the comparison semantics are declared.

For a common-source deterministic case,

\[ R_1:X_P\to Y_1, \qquad R_2:X_P\to Y_2, \]

and

\[ \boxed{ R_1=A_{21}\circ R_2 } \]

may witness

\[ \mathcal B_1 \preceq_{P,\mathcal T} \mathcal B_2 \]

relative to a declared problem/task class \(\mathcal T\).

This gives a preorder where the comparison data are fixed. A poset requires quotienting by the appropriate mutual-refinement equivalence. A lattice is asserted only when meets and joins actually exist.

Genuinely heterogeneous decompositions may require spans, correspondences, institutions, fibrations, relations, or other sector-correct comparison structures rather than a forced common carrier.

39. Existing-Mathematics Reduction Rule

BM uses established mathematics wherever an existing theory already solves the active mathematical subproblem.

BM problem family Established mathematics often relevant
Representation / abstraction Quotients, factorization, abstract interpretation, Galois connections.
Finite relational witness coupling CSPs and conjunctive queries.
Binary parity consistency Cycle space / signed-graph balance.
Open compositional systems Cospans, structured cospans, operads, wiring diagrams.
Heterogeneous formal logics Institution theory and heterogeneous specification.
Distributed information transport Information-flow / channel-theoretic structures.
\[ \boxed{ \text{known mathematics} \Rightarrow \text{use and credit it, not rename it}. } \]

If only part of a BM construction reduces to known mathematics, the known part is credited and the residual becomes the actual research target.

40. BM and GURF

BM is the broader domain-independent mathematical metaframework. GURF is the response-specialized framework under BM governance.

\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{GURF} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{BM} \quad \text{as a response-specialized framework}. } \]

The arrow is a specialization relation, not literal set containment of mathematical objects.

BM emphasis GURF specialization
Branches, Operations, Interactions, Interfaces Observation, Intervention, response semantics.
General Passport mathematics Task/sufficiency/continuation obligations.
General realization and defect Response-oriented ambiguity, recovery, and certification.
Domain-independent information distinctions Response-oriented distinguishability and inference.

42. Ad Hoc and Pseudo-Math Prohibition

\[ \boxed{ \text{name} \to \text{analogy} \to \text{claim} \quad \text{is not accepted mathematics.} } \]

BM requires the stronger chain:

\[ \boxed{ \text{problem} \to \text{definition} \to \text{type/domain} \to \text{law} \to \text{operation} \to \text{proof/counterexample} \to \text{integration}. } \]

Role-erasure audit

A useful pseudo-math test is to erase BM role words and inspect the remaining mathematics. If a claimed theorem loses its mathematical argument after removing words such as Branch, Leaf, Tree, Root, Passport, or Seed, the theorem has not yet been justified.

No duplicate-theory rule

If an established theory already completely realizes a proposed BM subconstruction under the claimed scope, the BM structure is treated as an application or role of that known mathematics rather than as a separate new theory.

43. Current Core Statement

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Branch Mathematics carries one mathematical problem through}\\ \text{multiple distinct native mathematical Branches.}\\[4pt] \text{It does not require those Branches to share one native language.}\\[4pt] \text{It makes their lawful relationships part of the mathematics.}\\[4pt] \text{Every cross-Branch transformation must have a typed mathematical basis.}\\[4pt] \text{Every required distinction, law, context, witness, and provenance condition}\\ \text{must be preserved, transformed, exported, or lawfully discharged.}\\[4pt] \text{Multiple valid BM directions may coexist and interact.}\\[4pt] \text{Existing mathematics is used where it already solves the problem.}\\[4pt] \text{New BM structure is admitted only when mathematically justified.}\\[4pt] \text{Ad hoc constructions and pseudo-mathematics are excluded.} \end{gathered} } \]
Current architectural status

The core architecture has passed repeated contradiction, scope, decomposition, semantic-sector, local/global, repair, and existing-mathematics reduction tests. Historical novelty and exact prior art remain separate research questions.
