1. What Branch Mathematics Is

Branch Mathematics is meta-structural mathematics of lawful representation and transformation.

BM studies how distinct mathematical structures may participate in one mathematical problem while retaining their own laws, types, distinctions, admissibility conditions, operations, interfaces, and native notions of mathematical validity.

A differential equation, algebraic structure, probability model, geometric construction, dynamical system, operator theory, computational representation, physical model, or other mathematical object may possess a different notion of state, equality, composition, admissibility, observation, transformation, or realization.

BM does not begin by forcing all participating mathematics into one undifferentiated formalism.

Instead, its governing question is

\[ \boxed{ \text{How can distinct lawful mathematical structures participate together without destroying the mathematics that makes them distinct?} } \]

BM is therefore concerned not merely with objects, but with lawful mathematical participation among objects.

2. Governing Structural Direction

A broad BM computation may be represented schematically by

\[ \boxed{ \text{lawful Branches} \longrightarrow \text{lawful Interactions} \longrightarrow \text{combined computation} \longrightarrow \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root} \end{cases} } \]

This is a structural schema, not a universal numerical equation and not a claim that all BM constructions are linear sequences.

A BM computation may instead be parallel, recursive, networked, coupled, categorical, operator-valued, stochastic, nondeterministic, geometric, topological, variational, differential, algebraic, or otherwise structured according to the native mathematics involved.

The essential requirement is lawfulness.

A transformation is not authorized merely because symbols can be written next to one another. Every proposed Interaction, Operation, quotient, composition, realization, interface, export, attachment, or re-entry must satisfy the conditions belonging to the participating mathematical structures.

3. Native Mathematical Authority

BM governs the participation of mathematical structures. It does not replace the mathematics internal to those structures.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM meta-structure} \neq \text{replacement of native mathematical truth}. } \]

If a Branch contains a differential equation, algebra, Hilbert-space construction, probability law, manifold, numerical method, or physical model, correctness inside that Branch remains determined by the mathematics of that subject.

BM cannot make an invalid theorem valid merely by placing the theorem inside a Branch.

4. Branch

A Branch is a typed mathematical carrier of information, distinctions, structure, and lawful mathematical behavior.

The word Branch describes the object's role inside a BM construction. It does not replace the object's native mathematical identity.

A useful organizational representation is

\[ B= \left( X_B, \mathcal L_B, \mathcal O_B, P_B \right), \]
Object Role
\(X_B\) Carrier, state space, object class, or other mathematical domain represented by the Branch.
\(\mathcal L_B\) Laws, identities, constraints, invariants, structural conditions, or native mathematical rules.
\(\mathcal O_B\) Operations or transformations admissible for the Branch.
\(P_B\) Passport or interface information when such information is required.

This four-component record is a working organizational form. BM does not assert that every mathematical structure intrinsically consists of exactly these four components.

Branch Independence

A Branch retains mathematical identity prior to and independently of any particular Interaction.

Participation in a larger Tree or Network does not authorize BM to erase its native structure.

5. Branch Information

Branch information is the mathematically relevant information carried by a Branch.

Depending on the sector, this may include:

  • state variables,
  • parameters,
  • algebraic structure,
  • topological data,
  • geometric data,
  • probability laws,
  • operator data,
  • constraints,
  • boundary or initial data,
  • invariants,
  • observational distinctions,
  • interface requirements,
  • or other typed mathematical information.

BM does not identify Branch information with a single universal information measure.

6. Interaction

An Interaction is a lawful typed mathematical action involving one or more Branches, with multi-Branch interaction being the central case.

For Branches \(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), a working representation is

\[ I: D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \longrightarrow Z_I. \]

Here \(D_I\) is the admissible Interaction domain.

\[ D_I \subseteq X_{B_1}\times\cdots\times X_{B_n} \]

is important because not every formally writable combination of Branch states is necessarily lawful.

Interaction Requirements

Depending on the participating mathematics, admissibility may require:

  • type compatibility,
  • domain compatibility,
  • constraint satisfaction,
  • regularity,
  • boundary compatibility,
  • measurability,
  • continuity,
  • operator-domain conditions,
  • conservation conditions,
  • Passport conditions,
  • or other sector-specific requirements.

7. Leaf

A Leaf is mathematical information extracted from an Interaction result.

If

\[ z= I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \in Z_I \]

and

\[ \ell_j: Z_I \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j, \]

then the individual Leaf value is

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_j = \ell_j(z) = \ell_j \left( I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right) \in \mathcal L_j. } \]

Thus:

  • \(\mathcal L_j\) is the Leaf space or codomain;
  • \(\ell_j\) is the extraction map;
  • \(\lambda_j\) is the extracted Leaf value.

Different extraction maps may expose different information from the same Interaction result.

A Leaf is not merely a synonym for any derived mathematical quantity. Its BM role requires explicit provenance from an Interaction result through an extraction map.

A Leaf is also not automatically a Root.

8. Tree

A Tree is an organized BM structure of Branches, Interactions, interfaces, Leaves, Passports, and intermediate information through which a combined computation is performed.

Its mathematical output is a Response.

\[ \mathcal R_T: D_T \longrightarrow Y_T. \]

In the special case where ordinary sequential composition is appropriate,

\[ \mathcal R_T = I_m \circ I_{m-1} \circ \cdots \circ I_1. \]

But this is not a universal Tree form.

A Tree may instead require:

  • parallel operations,
  • products,
  • pullbacks,
  • equalizers,
  • coequalizers,
  • limits or colimits,
  • coupled systems,
  • operator compositions,
  • recursive constructions,
  • stochastic kernels,
  • relations,
  • or other mathematically appropriate structures.

A BM Tree is therefore not required to be a literal graph-theoretic tree.

9. Response

A Response is the typed mathematical output produced by a Tree or another organized BM computation.

\[ r= \mathcal R_T(x) \in Y_T. \]

A Response may be:

  • a number,
  • a state,
  • a function,
  • a field,
  • a law,
  • a relation,
  • an operator,
  • a classification,
  • a structured object,
  • a family of objects,
  • or another mathematically typed result.

BM does not require every Response to be law-valued.

A Response may terminate the computation or may become a Root if downstream admissibility is established.

10. Root

A Root is a Response or resulting mathematical structure that remains available for lawful participation in additional mathematics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Root role} = \text{Response} + \text{verified downstream admissibility}. } \]

Suppose a Response \(r\) is produced and a subsequent operation has admissible domain

\[ J: D_J \subseteq X_R\times X_C \longrightarrow Z_J. \]

The result may function as a Root only where the relevant typing, law, Passport, interface, and admissibility conditions permit further participation.

Root status does not imply:

  • invertibility,
  • recursion,
  • conservation,
  • physical realization,
  • universal compatibility,
  • or indefinite continuation.

11. Tree, Response, and Root Must Remain Distinct

BM Role Meaning
Tree Organized structure performing combined mathematical computation.
Response Typed output produced by that computation.
Root A Response or resulting structure verified as admissible for additional mathematical participation.
\[ \boxed{ \text{Tree} \xrightarrow{\text{combined computation}} \text{Response} \longrightarrow \begin{cases} \text{terminal result},\\[4pt] \text{Root if downstream admissible}. \end{cases} } \]

12. Passport

A Passport specifies mathematical distinctions, observations, invariants, constraints, law context, admissibility requirements, interface requirements, or task-relevant information that must remain available for a declared mathematical purpose.

A deterministic observational Passport may begin with maps

\[ b_c: X \longrightarrow Y_c, \qquad c\in\mathcal C. \]

A combined Passport map may be written

\[ P(x) = \left( b_c(x) \right)_{c\in\mathcal C}. \]

Passport equivalence is then

\[ \boxed{ x\sim_P x' \iff P(x)=P(x'). } \]

Equivalently,

\[ x\sim_Px' \iff b_c(x)=b_c(x') \quad \forall c\in\mathcal C. \]

Passports are important BM machinery, but BM is not reducible to Passport theory.

13. Fibres and Lost Distinctions

Let

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Z \]

be a deterministic representation.

The fibre over \(z\in F(X)\) is

\[ \mathcal F_F(z) = F^{-1}(z) = \left\{ x\in X: F(x)=z \right\}. \]

States inside the same fibre are indistinguishable to the representation \(F\).

\[ F(x)=F(x') \]

means that the distinction between \(x\) and \(x'\) is no longer available from \(F(x)\) alone.

Whether that loss matters depends on the later mathematics.

14. Exact Recoverability

Let

\[ T: X \longrightarrow W \]

be a later deterministic mathematical task.

The task is exactly recoverable from \(F\) when it factors through the realized image \(F(X)\):

\[ \boxed{ \exists\, \widetilde T: F(X) \longrightarrow W \quad \text{such that} \quad T = \widetilde T \circ F. } \]

Equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ F(x)=F(x') \Longrightarrow T(x)=T(x'). } \]

The domain \(F(X)\), rather than an arbitrary ambient codomain \(Z\), is essential.

Exact factorization over \(F(X)\) does not automatically imply that \(\widetilde T\) extends to every point of \(Z\). Such an extension may require additional mathematical assumptions.

15. Distinction Preservation

A major BM principle is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{Retain distinctions required by later mathematics; identify them only when their loss is mathematically safe.} } \]

This principle motivates Passports, fibres, recoverability, quotient audits, interface audits, continuation analysis, and repair.

It is an important BM principle, but it is not the complete definition of BM.

16. Quotients

A Passport equivalence suggests a set-level quotient

\[ X/{\sim_P}. \]

However, an equivalence relation does not automatically support every mathematical structure originally carried by \(X\).

BM therefore distinguishes:

  • set-level identification,
  • structured quotienting,
  • and downstream law preservation.

17. Structured Quotient Condition

Consider a finitary algebra

\[ A= (X,\Sigma) \]

with operations

\[ \sigma: X^n \longrightarrow X. \]

The quotient operation

\[ \overline{\sigma} \left( [x_1]_P,\ldots,[x_n]_P \right) = \left[ \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \right]_P \]

is well-defined only if representative choice does not change the resulting equivalence class.

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{ x_i\sim_P y_i \quad \forall i \Longrightarrow \sigma(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \sim_P \sigma(y_1,\ldots,y_n). } \]

In ordinary algebraic language, the Passport equivalence must be a congruence for the operations passed to the quotient.

This result is sector-specific. Other mathematical sectors require their own quotient-existence and structure-preservation conditions.

18. Operation

An Operation is a lawful mathematical transformation with declared input type, output type, domain, assumptions, and compatibility conditions.

Operations may be:

  • unary or multi-input,
  • total or partial,
  • deterministic,
  • stochastic,
  • relational,
  • operator-valued,
  • differential,
  • integral,
  • algebraic,
  • geometric,
  • computational,
  • or sector-specific.

An operation's notation alone does not establish that it is admissible.

19. Admissibility

Admissibility is the condition that a proposed mathematical participation is actually allowed by the relevant mathematics.

For an Operation

\[ F: D_F \longrightarrow Y, \]

the admissible domain \(D_F\) may be a proper subset of a larger formally writable carrier.

Admissibility can depend on:

  • typing,
  • regularity,
  • constraints,
  • boundary conditions,
  • domains of unbounded operators,
  • measurability,
  • positivity,
  • causal structure,
  • conservation,
  • Passport compatibility,
  • or other native mathematical requirements.

20. Interface

An Interface governs how mathematical information or structure may pass lawfully between objects.

For maps

\[ F:X_1\longrightarrow X_2, \] \[ P_1:X_1\longrightarrow Q_1, \] \[ P_2:X_2\longrightarrow Q_2, \] \[ G:Q_1\longrightarrow Q_2, \]

a basic deterministic compatibility condition is

\[ \boxed{ P_2\circ F = G\circ P_1. } \]

This expresses agreement between transformation at the original level and transformation at the represented level.

Other sectors may replace equality with an explicitly defined equivalence, approximation, homotopy, equality in distribution, natural isomorphism, weak equality, or other appropriate notion.

21. Composition

Suppose

\[ F: D_F \subseteq X \longrightarrow Y \]

and

\[ G: D_G \subseteq Y \longrightarrow Z. \]

The admissible domain of the composite is

\[ D_{G\circ F} = \left\{ x\in D_F: F(x)\in D_G \right\}. \]

Therefore

\[ G\circ F: D_{G\circ F} \longrightarrow Z. \]

BM does not reduce composition to matching written type labels. The intermediate output must actually lie in the receiving Operation's admissible domain.

Additional Passport, law, interface, or sufficiency requirements may also apply.

22. Deterministic Serial Information Loss

For deterministic maps

\[ X \xrightarrow{F} Y \xrightarrow{G} Z, \]

if

\[ F(x)=F(x'), \]

then necessarily

\[ G(F(x)) = G(F(x')). \]

Thus a later deterministic map cannot recover a distinction that an earlier deterministic map has already identified unless additional information enters through another channel.

This statement is specifically deterministic and must not be silently generalized to every stochastic, nondeterministic, or quantum setting.

23. Parallel Observation and Refinement

Two observations

\[ F:X\longrightarrow Y \]

and

\[ G:X\longrightarrow Z \]

may be combined into

\[ (F,G): X \longrightarrow Y\times Z. \]

The combined equivalence relation is

\[ x\sim_{(F,G)}x' \iff F(x)=F(x') \text{ and } G(x)=G(x'). \]

Hence the combined observation is at least as discriminating as either component separately.

24. Configuration

A Configuration records a simultaneous mathematically admissible arrangement of BM objects, Branch states, interface data, or other active structural information.

A general configuration space may be represented by

\[ \mathcal X_{\mathrm{BM}} \]

with admissible subset

\[ \mathcal A_{\mathrm{BM}} \subseteq \mathcal X_{\mathrm{BM}}. \]

A configuration

\[ \xi\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{BM}} \]

represents a lawfully realizable BM state at the chosen level of description.

The exact internal structure of \(\mathcal X_{\mathrm{BM}}\) is sector-dependent.

25. Network

A Network is a broader organization of Branches, Trees, Roots, Operations, Interfaces, and other BM structures connected through typed lawful relationships.

Networks allow BM to describe mathematics that is not naturally represented by a single Tree.

Depending on the problem, the network may be represented using a graph, directed acyclic graph, hypergraph, category, diagram, dependency network, operator network, or another appropriate structure.

The representation used for the Network does not replace the native mathematics of its nodes and interfaces.

26. Typed Paths

A Typed Path is a sequence of mathematically compatible transformations through a BM structure.

For example,

\[ X_0 \xrightarrow{F_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{F_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{F_n} X_n \]

defines a lawful typed path only when each stage is admissible and each intermediate result is compatible with the next stage.

BM therefore tracks more than the endpoints of a path. The lawfulness of the intermediate route matters.

27. Path Coherence

When multiple lawful paths connect corresponding mathematical objects, BM asks whether those paths agree in the sense required by the sector.

In a strict deterministic diagram, this may mean

\[ F_2\circ F_1 = G_2\circ G_1. \]

In another mathematical setting, path coherence may instead mean agreement up to:

  • canonical isomorphism,
  • natural transformation,
  • homotopy,
  • controlled approximation,
  • equality in law,
  • gauge equivalence,
  • or another explicitly declared relation.

BM does not silently replace these distinct notions of agreement with ordinary equality.

28. Higher Coherence

Pairwise path agreement may not be sufficient when a mathematical system contains many interacting paths, transformations, equivalences, or higher-order interfaces.

Higher coherence refers to compatibility among these compatibility relations themselves.

The exact form of higher coherence depends on the mathematical category involved.

BM therefore treats higher coherence as a general meta-structural requirement rather than imposing one universal equation.

29. Mathematical Realization

Mathematical Realization asks whether an abstract BM specification can actually be constructed inside the mathematical structure in which it is claimed to exist.

If \(S\) is an abstract specification, a realization may be represented schematically by

\[ \rho: S \longrightarrow X \]

subject to the laws defining admissible objects in \(X\).

A syntactically describable object need not possess a mathematical realization.

Mathematical vs. Physical Realization

Mathematical realizability and physical realizability are separate claims.

\[ \boxed{ \text{mathematically constructible} \not\Rightarrow \text{physically realizable}. } \]

30. Export

Export is the lawful production or transfer of a mathematical result from one BM context into a representation or interface intended for another context.

\[ E: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Export may preserve all information, selected information, equivalence classes, invariants, sufficient statistics, interface data, or other declared mathematical content.

What export preserves must be stated rather than assumed.

31. Re-entry

Re-entry is the lawful insertion of exported information into a receiving mathematical structure.

\[ X \xrightarrow{E} Y \xrightarrow{R} X'. \]

Re-entry requires the receiving structure's own admissibility conditions to be satisfied.

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{ \text{successful export} \not\Rightarrow \text{automatic lawful re-entry}. } \]

32. Rooting

Rooting is active candidate BM mathematics describing lawful attachment of a Branch, Root, law family, or other compatible mathematical structure to a mathematically admissible attachment site.

Attachment may occur at a terminal site or at an interior nonterminal site when the mathematics permits it.

Rooting is not merely graphical attachment. It requires:

  • typed compatibility,
  • admissibility,
  • interface compatibility,
  • law compatibility,
  • and any required Passport conditions.
Status: extended Rooting machinery remains active candidate mathematics. It is not treated as a universal law of every BM construction.

33. Rebranching

Rebranching is the candidate BM process by which a lawful attachment site supports new downstream mathematical structure.

Rebranching may produce new Branches, Interactions, Roots, Interfaces, or other typed mathematical structures when the required conditions are satisfied.

It does not imply unrestricted proliferation.

34. Repeated Rooting

Repeated Rooting is not automatically a dynamical system.

If a Root transformation closes on an appropriate state space,

\[ F: X_R \longrightarrow X_R, \]

and the required iterates remain admissible, then one may define

\[ R_{n+1} = F(R_n). \]

Only under such closure and admissibility conditions does ordinary iteration theory apply directly.

Fixed points, stability, cycles, invariant sets, Lyapunov analysis, spectral methods, and asymptotic behavior may then be studied using ordinary mathematics when their assumptions hold.

35. Seed

A Seed is a candidate BM primitive describing information that does not directly export a Root but instead undergoes a lawful generative or self-seeding process until a specified export condition is met.

A generic Seed process may be represented by

\[ S_0 \longrightarrow S_1 \longrightarrow S_2 \longrightarrow \cdots \]

followed, when a declared condition \(C_{\mathrm{exp}}\) is satisfied, by

\[ S_n \xrightarrow{C_{\mathrm{exp}}} R. \]

A Seed is not merely initial data. Its proposed BM identity lies in lawful generative continuation prior to Root export.

Status: Seed remains candidate BM mathematics and is not part of the stable proved-theorem backbone.

36. Law Space

BM may need to distinguish mathematical states not only by their numerical values but also by the law, regime, constraint family, or structural rule under which those states are valid.

A Law Space is a typed mathematical carrier for this law-level information.

Schematically,

\[ \Lambda \in \mathfrak L, \]

where \(\mathfrak L\) denotes an admissible law space and \(\Lambda\) identifies the applicable law data.

The internal mathematics of \(\mathfrak L\) is problem-dependent. BM does not require every law space to possess the same algebraic or geometric structure.

37. Conservation and Persistent Information

Conservation laws belong to the native mathematics of the relevant Branches.

BM's role is to ensure that a representation, Interaction, quotient, export, Rooting operation, or re-entry does not unlawfully discard conserved information that later mathematics still requires.

Candidate Conservative Passports may be used to explicitly track quantities whose persistence is required.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM tracks required conservation information;} \quad \text{BM does not invent the conservation law.} } \]

38. Deterministic, Stochastic, and Nondeterministic Sectors

BM does not assume that every Branch is deterministic.

Deterministic

\[ F: X \longrightarrow Y. \]

Each admissible input has one specified output.

Stochastic

A stochastic transformation may instead be represented by a Markov kernel

\[ M: X \longrightarrow \mathcal P(Y). \]

Acting on a measurable test function \(\varphi\),

\[ M^\dagger\varphi(x) = \mathbb E \left[ \varphi(Y) \mid X=x \right]. \]

Deterministic equality criteria cannot automatically be reused without proving the corresponding stochastic statement.

Nondeterministic / Relational

A nondeterministic relation may be represented by

\[ R \subseteq X\times Y. \]

Existential reachability and universal safety are distinct:

\[ \Diamond_R(U) = \left\{ x\in X: \exists y\in U \text{ with } xRy \right\}, \] \[ \Box_R(U) = \left\{ x\in X: \forall y,\, xRy \Rightarrow y\in U \right\}. \]

BM requires the quantifier structure to remain explicit.

39. Joint Feasibility and Witness Scope

Satisfying several requirements separately does not prove that one mathematical object satisfies them simultaneously.

If condition \(c\) admits feasible set

\[ P_c \subseteq X, \]

then common feasibility requires

\[ \boxed{ \bigcap_{c\in\mathcal C} P_c \neq \varnothing. } \]

If separate witnesses, policies, couplings, or strategies are permitted for different conditions, that quantifier scope must be stated explicitly.

40. Coupling

A Coupling specifies a lawful joint mathematical relationship between objects whose marginal or separately stated behavior does not by itself determine their joint behavior.

This is especially important in probability, stochastic systems, control, optimization, inverse problems, and multi-task feasibility.

BM does not infer a common joint witness merely from separate marginal compatibility.

41. Dynamic Closure

A representation may be sufficient for a task at one instant without being sufficient to continue future lawful mathematics.

Dynamic closure asks whether the retained representation carries enough information to determine the required continuation under the declared law.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{static task sufficiency} \not\Rightarrow \text{dynamic closure}. } \]

This distinction is particularly important when BM is specialized through GURF.

42. Obstruction and Repair

An obstruction identifies why a proposed representation, interface, continuation, quotient, or mathematical participation fails.

A repair augments or changes the mathematical structure so that the required condition becomes satisfiable.

A generic refinement may be represented by

\[ r: X \longrightarrow Y \]

together with additional information

\[ a: X \longrightarrow A, \]

producing

\[ r'(x) = \left( r(x), a(x) \right). \]

Whether the repair is sufficient, minimal, realizable, or dynamically closed is a separate mathematical question.

44. Relationship Between BM and GURF

Branch Mathematics is the broader governing meta-structural mathematics.

GURF specializes BM when a problem contains genuine response semantics.

\[ \boxed{ \text{BM} \longrightarrow \text{GURF when response semantics are present}. } \]

Typical GURF concerns include:

  • system states,
  • observations,
  • tasks,
  • interventions,
  • constraints,
  • regimes,
  • continuation,
  • response ambiguity,
  • obstruction,
  • repair,
  • and future response.

BM may be used without GURF when response semantics are absent.

Likewise, BM itself may remain quiet when ordinary native mathematics completely resolves the question and BM adds no genuine leverage.

46. Portability Rule

A project-level ownership test is used when mathematical machinery appears inside GURF but may actually be domain-independent.

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{l} \text{If a construction can be stated without essential response semantics}\\ \text{and remains meaningful across multiple mathematical sectors,}\\ \text{its portable mathematical kernel belongs under BM.} \end{array} } \]

If the construction's identity fundamentally depends on system response, observation, task, intervention, regime, continuation, or related response semantics, it remains in GURF.

47. What Branch Mathematics Does Not Do

BM does not:

  • replace ordinary mathematical proof;
  • make an invalid algebraic step valid;
  • turn a non-solution of a PDE into a solution;
  • override conservation laws;
  • erase domain or regularity requirements;
  • guarantee physical realizability;
  • make every pair of Branches compatible;
  • make every quotient structured;
  • make every Response a Root;
  • make every Root process iterative;
  • make every observational equivalence physically meaningful;
  • or establish historical novelty merely by introducing BM terminology.

BM is useful only where it provides genuine mathematical leverage.

51. Governing Questions for BM Constructions

A mature BM construction should be able to answer questions such as:

  • What mathematical objects are being carried?
  • What are the Branch types and native mathematical sectors?
  • Which laws, constraints, and invariants remain binding?
  • Which distinctions are preserved?
  • Which distinctions are intentionally identified?
  • Which Operations and Interactions are admissible?
  • What are their actual admissible domains?
  • Which Interfaces connect the structures?
  • Are alternate typed paths coherent?
  • Does higher coherence need to be checked?
  • What information is extracted as Leaves?
  • What information is lost by representation or quotienting?
  • Does a quotient preserve the required mathematical structure?
  • Can required later tasks still be recovered?
  • Is the abstract construction mathematically realizable?
  • Is export lawful?
  • Is re-entry lawful?
  • What Response does the Tree or Network produce?
  • Is that Response terminal or does it satisfy the conditions required to become a Root?
  • If Rooting is proposed, is the attachment site admissible?
  • If iteration is proposed, is the Root state space closed under the proposed iteration?
  • Does the problem genuinely require GURF response semantics?

52. Governing BM Principle

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Each Branch retains the mathematics that makes it lawful;}\\[5pt] \text{distinct structures interact only through admissible mathematical interfaces;}\\[5pt] \text{combined computation must preserve every distinction, law, and structure}\\ \text{required by downstream mathematics.} \end{gathered} } \]

Branch Mathematics is therefore not a demand that mathematics be homogenized.

It is a framework for allowing different mathematics to remain different while still participating lawfully in a larger mathematical construction.
